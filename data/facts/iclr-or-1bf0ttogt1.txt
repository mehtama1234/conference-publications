While recent studies recognize the importance of diversity-enhanced training in objective reasoning, limited attention has been given to subjective tasks. We proposeMultiRole-R1, a diversity-enhanced training framework featuring an unsupervised data construction pipeline that synthesizes reasoning chains incorporating various role perspectives. Training on subjective tasks solely, MultiRole-R1 increases the in-domain and out-of-domain accuracy by 14.1% and 7.64%, and even enhances the performance on advanced math reasoning such as AIME 2024. Notably, this type of model is trained via reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), which induces a diversity degradation in the model generation (Song et al., 2025b; Dang et al., 2025; Zhao et al., 2025; Wu et al., 2025). Recent studies offer several solutions to enhance diversity in RL training (Song et al., 2025a; Yan et al., 2025), but they mostly focus on objective reasoning. In contrast to objective tasks, subjective questions are fundamentally different from objective questions since there are no definitive right or wrong answers (Khurana et al., 2024; van der Meer et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b; Jentzsch & Kersting, 2023; Wu et al., 2024a): This challenge cannot be solved by current diversity-enhanced training approaches in objective domain since they rely on a single ground truth in optimization. To evaluate the effectiveness and generalizability of our approach, we train DeepSeek-R1 series models and Qwen-3-8B using MultiRole-R1 and test them on both subjective and objective questions. Results show that MultiRole-R1 boosts performance by an average of 14.1% on three in-domain (ID) subjective tasks, and 7.64% on four out-of-domain (OOD) tasks that include both subjective and objective questions.
