In this work, we propose a highly data-efficient adaptation paradigm for bimanual control that eliminates the need for prohibitive bimanual pretraining. Consequently, specialist methods, such as Diffusion policy (Chi et al., 2024a) and ACT (Zhao et al., 2023), trained only on target-task demonstrations, struggle on precise, long-horizon tasks. While promising, it is difficult to generalize these methods, as they are often limited to small-scale scenarios, handle only low-dexterity tasks, or backbone constraints (Grotz et al., 2024; Shridhar et al., 2022). To overcome this, prior work trains ‘monolithic’ models, requiring large-scale bimanual data collection and intensive pretraining. In contrast to both monolithic, compute-heavy pretraining and specialized architectural designs, our approach adopts a modular, coordination-centric design. Our method, however, is designed to leverage and scale the existing generalist VLAs. We first train a SingleVLA on large-scale public single-arm data, duplicate to couple them, and then fine-tune it on bimanual tasks—allowing each stage to benefit from the most suitable data (see Figure 2). This composition-based approach avoids bimanual pretraining, requires only a small amount of bimanual fine-tuning, better preserves the strong capabilities of single-arm policies, and significantly improves data and compute efficiency.
