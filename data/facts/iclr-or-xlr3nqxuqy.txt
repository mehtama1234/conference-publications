Given a casual monocular video of such an interaction, our goal is to build a “vid2sim” pipeline for generating human-scene reconstructions. We introduce CRISP, a real-to-sim pipeline that converts monocular human videos into simulation-ready assets by integrating human mesh recovery (HMR), 4D reconstruction, and contact prediction. Unlike most reconstruction works, which yield noisy and non-watertight 2.5D geometry, CRISP explicitly builds geometric outputs ready for physics simulation, by fittingclean, convex and watertightplanar primitives to point cloud reconstructions via a clustering algorithm. Second, we make use of state-of-the-art monocular depth priors to reduce artifacts such as duplicate structures that plague concurrent methods (Allshire et al., 2025). When integrated into RL training pipelines, CRISP supports a 43% faster simulation throughput compared to dense-mesh approaches, while maintaining physically plausible interac- tions. These results highlight that CRISP not only bridges the video-to-simulation gap, but also makes RL training from in-the-wild videos practical and efficient for embodied AI and robotics.
