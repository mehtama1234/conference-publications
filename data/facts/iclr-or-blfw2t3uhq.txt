In SLT, meaning depends on precise grounding in video, and gloss-free models are especially vulnerable because they map continuous signer movements directly into natural language without intermediate gloss supervision that serves as alignment. Our method combines feature-based sensitivity, which measures internal changes when video is masked, with counterfactual signals, which capture probability dif- ferences between clean and altered video inputs. We evaluate the proposed measure on two SLT benchmarks (PHOENIX-2014T and CSL-Daily) with both gloss-based and gloss-free models. Glosses provide strong supervision but limit scalability, since most large-scale SLT datasets are only weakly labeled and lack gloss annotations. Recent advances in SLT increasingly favor gloss-free architectures that rely on large language model backbones (Gong et al., 2024; Chen et al., 2024b; Hwang et al., 2025). A crucial first step toward mitigating and reducing hallucinations is the ability to characterize and detect them, and to assign reliability scores that allow ranking candidate translations by their risk of hallucination. Our experiments across datasets (PHOENIX-2014T (Camgoz et al., 2018) and CSL-Daily (Zhou et al., 2021)) and architectures (T5 (Hwang et al., 2025) and mBART (Chen et al., 2022)) demonstrate that reliability not only surpasses text-only baselines in predicting hallucinations but also generalizes robustly in cross-dataset and cross-architecture transfer. Our findings further reveal that, compared to gloss-based models, gloss-free models hallucinate more often because they systematically under-utilize visual information, defaulting to linguistic priors. SLT systems and datasets are typically categorized as either gloss-based or gloss-free. However, creating gloss annotations is time-consuming, which has led to increased interest in gloss-free SLT models. Examples of such approaches include extending Transformer architectures to SLT (Camgoz et al., 2020), leveraging sentence embeddings instead of glosses (Hamidullah et al., 2024), and integrating spatial and motion visual feature extractors with large language models (LLMs) (Hwang et al., 2025). Ap- proaches now include reinforcement learning to align outputs with factual grounding (Gunjal et al., 2024), fact verification methods that leverage external knowledge bases or retrievers (Yin et al., 2024; Sahu et al., 2024), and tool-augmented strategies that integrate external APIs to assess factual consistency (Chen et al., 2024a).
