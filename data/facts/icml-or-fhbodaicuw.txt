We propose a com- putationally efficient optimal design framework for MNL models that achieves both statistical efficiency and scalability through two comple- mentary approaches: (i) an exact or certified- approximate reformulation of the design oracle as a 0–1 mixed-integer linear program (MILP) with solver-certified early stopping, and (ii) a fully polynomial-time lifted design that replaces the nonlinear objective with a tractable surrogate. As an application, we develop a best assortment identification algorithm for MNL ban- dits with linear utilities and non-uniform revenues, and prove an instance-dependent sample complex- ity of ˜O ` dlogN ∆2 ˘ , where d is the feature dimen- sion, N is the number of arms, and ∆ is the mini- mum revenue gap. Unlike linear or generalized lin- ear models—where the design space consists of individual arms— the design space in MNL models iscombinatorial: Our approach builds onlocallyoptimal design principles to handle the inherent nonlinearity of the MNL model, and leads to a Frank–Wolfe algorithm (Frank et al., 1956; Dunn & Harshbarger, 1978) whose main computational challenge is a linear maximization oracle (LMO) over acombinatorial collection of feasible subsets. To address this bottleneck, we propose two complementary approaches: Leveraging a G-optimal design computed at a nominal parameter, we propose an algorithm, BSI-MNL, that combines an initial exploration phase (which ensures the nominal parameter is uniformly close to the true pa- rameter), design-based sampling, and a principled stopping rule. To the best of our knowledge, this yields the first sample complexity guarantee for best assortment identification in MNL bandits with linear utilities, while remaining computationally efficient via either MILP-based early stopping or the polynomial-time lifted relaxation. • Exact MILP reformulation:To address the primary computational bottleneck of the Frank–Wolfe (FW) algorithm—the linear maximization oracle (LMO) de- fined in (3)—we show that the optimization problem admits anexactreformulation as a 0–1 mixed-integer linear program (Theorem 3.3). Moreover, we show that the FW algorithm retains its ϵ-optimality guarantee even with certified inexact LMO solutions, at the cost of a small additional number of FW iterations (Proposition 3.4). • Polynomial-time lifted surrogate:As an alterna- tive approach, we propose a novel lifted surrogate based on a Schur-complement construction. This re- laxation yields a ratio-of-sums objective that can be optimizedexactlyin polynomial time.
