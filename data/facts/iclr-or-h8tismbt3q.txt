However, most existing Protein Language Models (PLMs) are trained in an unsupervised manner on raw sequences without integrating functional signals. Recent studies have sought to intro- duce supervision by incorporating structural features into language models (Shanker et al., 2024; Barton et al., 2024) or training sequence-based predictors for antigen binding (Jing et al., 2024; Kalemati et al., 2024), yet a comprehensive resource linking arbitrary antibody sequences with de- tailed natural-language functional descriptors remains absent. To address this gap, we introduceAFD-Instruction, a large-scale resource that aligns antibody sequences with concise, machine-readable functional descriptors. Our goal is to embed LLMs with antibody-specific functional annotations, enabling them to infer function from sequence and generate sequences that satisfy specified functional constraints. Across diverse architectures, AFD-Instruction-tuned models consistently outperformed sequence-only baselines in functional classification and target inference, while producing designs that more faithfully reflected desired functional profiles. To construct AFD-Instruction, we first collected antibodies from SabDab (Raybould et al., 2020) and the Protein Data Bank (PDB) (Berman et al., 2000), focusing on those commonly used for antibody design. Each domain’s instruction tasks address essential challenges in antibody research, aiming to advance the field through the training and utilization of LLMs. Antibody DesignThese tasks address sequence generation under functional constraints, cover- ing de novo design of variable regions and targeted design of CDR3 loops. The primary objective is to enable natural language–guided antibody design, thereby enhancing sequence diversity, refining binding specificity, and improving therapeutic potential. The heavy-chain variable regions display a near-unimodal, bell-shaped distribution centered around 110–125 amino acids, reflecting the conserved FR1–FR4 framework architecture of immunoglobulins (Chothia & Lesk, 1987), while longer sequences indicate variability in the CDR- H3 region. Light-chain variable regions are generally shorter and exhibit less variability due to structural constraints ofκ/λchains and typically shorter CDR-L3, with a pronounced peak indicat- ing limited variation outside complementarity-determining regions. We partition AFD-Instruction into training, validation, and test subsets, using the first two for instruction tuning and the last for evaluation.
