A token-wise contrastive excess filtering mechanism then compares the expert against its base backbone to compute token-level excess scores. Using these scores, TITOKfirst per- forms sample filtering and subsequently token selection, retaining only the most informative samples and tokens. When tokenizers differ, masks are aligned prior to training. The resulting filtered data is finally used to train a new LoRA on the target backbone, enabling efficient knowledge transfer.
