Our work is closely related to a family of methods that train graph neural networks (GNN) in an unsupervised, end- to-end differentiable manner. Other methods (Berto et al., 2024; Lin et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2024; Zhou et al., 2024) propose RL-based pretraining-fine-tuning approaches specifically for different variations of vehicle routing prob- lems (VRCs). Unlike these methods, we rely on unsupervised learning with task-specific loss functions, which avoids problems arising from reliance on labels or the large size of the state space in RL. Finally, unlike most of these methods, which do not justify their selection of pretraining tasks theoretically, we aim to use known polynomial reductions to show simi- lar problems transfer effectively between each other, thus guiding our pretraining tasks selection. We first train a GNN encoder, which is trained by a task- specific, unsupervised loss function to generate a vector p whose i-th entry pi =p[v i] is interpreted as the probability that vi is in the set of interest. We then use a sequential, rule-based decoder that strictly enforces the problem constraints to arrive at the final set of interest. GNN Encoder Our GNN encoder relies on the Graph Combinatorial Op- timization Network (GCON) architecture introduced in Wenkel et al. We follow a a common architectural design of a lin- ear layer before and after the GNN layers, as well as GELU (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016) nonlinearities, batch normalization (Ioffe & Szegedy, 2015), and graph size nor- malization after each layer. We additionally concatenate all GNN layer outputs as our input to the final linear layer, which empirically helps performance over most tasks. Objective Functions As objective functions, we rely on the Ising formulations of the CO problems, as provided in Lucas (2014) and dis- played in Table 1. The Ising model and equivalent quadratic unconstrained binary optimization (QUBO) formulations have emerged as an ef- fective framework to unify many CO problems by assigning an energy function (namely the Hamiltonian) to every task such that its optimal solution minimizes the total energy (Lu- cas, 2014; Kochenberger et al., 2014; Glover et al., 2022; Schuetz et al., 2022a).
