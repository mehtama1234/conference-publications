WHY

The Big Picture Problem

A conference this large is hard to understand because the unit of publication hides the shape of the field. Every paper has its own vocabulary, benchmark, loss, dataset, proof, or system. Read one at a time, the papers look like hundreds of unrelated improvements. Read structurally, they expose a smaller number of pressures: how to control models at inference time, how to evaluate behavior that cannot be captured by a single score, how to make data and compute governable, how to make generative models respect structure, how to connect perception to action, and how theory is being pulled toward deployment failure modes.

The atlas should solve that reading problem. It should let a researcher, founder, engineer, or investor move from a theme to its subthemes, from a subtheme to the papers, from a paper to the mathematical object it studies, and from there to adjacent themes. The value is not summarization. The value is compression with structure: fewer words than the conference, but enough conceptual depth to preserve what is actually being attempted.

OUTPUT

What the Finished Site Should Contain

A Strong Field Homepage

The homepage should explain the whole conference as a coherent field movement, then route readers into the major families without marketing language or shallow category labels.

Rich Theme Pages

Each of the 64 theme pages should have a conceptual opening, subtheme sections, and paragraph-depth paper treatment instead of evidence rows alone.

Complete Paper Atlas

All 781 named papers should receive first-principles entries: the core concept, the mathematical object, the problem, the approach, and connected themes.

Mathematical Concept Layer

The math page should grow from a lens list into a cross-paper map of recurring objects: distributions, graphs, verifiers, constraints, spectra, trajectories, latent spaces, and games.

Cross-Theme Navigation

Readers should be able to follow ideas across pages: safety to evaluation, data to optimization, multimodal to robotics, theory to deployment, causality to science.

Public Pages Artifact

The site should remain committed, published, and reviewable through GitHub Pages, with each writing pass traceable in the source repo.

LANES

How to Finish the Full Atlas

1. Evaluation and Safety

Benchmark validity, LLM-as-judge reliability, clinical and legal evaluation, jailbreaks, privacy, calibration, code/security, multimodal, and robotics measurement.

2. Agents and Reasoning

Tool use, long-horizon planning, process diagnostics, test-time scaling, activation steering, coding assistants, and multi-agent coordination.

3. Data and Systems

Open corpora, data valuation, missingness, optimization-time data selection, efficient adaptation, quantization, federated learning, and infrastructure constraints.

4. Multimodal and Embodied AI

Vision-language-action models, robot memory, world models, spatial reasoning, scene graphs, fMRI reconstruction, avatars, and camera-aware perception.

5. Science and Causality

Biomedical agents, causal discovery, neurostimulation, PDEs, neural operators, time series, physical simulation, and scientific reasoning systems.

6. Theory and Generative Structure

Transformer circuits, minimax rates, spectra, quotient spaces, diffusion error, formal language limits, protein generation, flow models, and constrained sampling.
