FIRST PRINCIPLE

Theory Is The Compression Layer

A field with hundreds of methods needs more than leaderboards. It needs objects that make methods comparable: losses, spectra, kernels, games, distributions, manifolds, gradients, equilibria, samplers, and certificates. Theory does not remove engineering detail. It names the structure that engineering keeps rediscovering.

The recurring move in this lane is to replace a recipe with a sharper object. Adam is not just an optimizer but a dynamical system near degenerate minima. Attention is not just a layer but a learned spectrum and association mechanism. Scaling is not just a curve but an interaction among data, context, reuse, and source complexity. A benchmark is not just a score but a finite-sample certification problem.

SUBTHEME 01

Optimization Dynamics

Training is a trajectory through geometry, not a recipe list.

These papers study the motion of optimizers, gradients, curvature, momentum, spectra, and parameterization. The question is not only whether a method reaches low loss, but what structure makes that movement possible and when the same recipe breaks.

ICML 2026

Adam Degeneracy

Shows Adam can achieve local linear convergence on highly degenerate objectives without extra schedulers. The point is that adaptivity changes the local geometry seen by the optimizer. Degenerate flat directions do not automatically imply slow progress if the update rule rescales movement in the right coordinates.

ICLR 2026

DR-Submodular Biased Gradients

Extends Lyapunov analysis to stochastic biased gradients under structured convex constraints. The paper matters because practical gradients are often biased by approximation, sampling, or batching. The theory asks when a descent certificate survives that imperfection instead of assuming ideal gradients.

ICML 2026

Jacobi Spectral Reconstruction

Uses Jacobi-preconditioned gradient descent for condition-number-independent linear convergence in spectral sparse recovery. The method changes the geometry of the problem before optimizing it. Preconditioning is treated as the difference between a hard landscape and one where the relevant sparse spectrum can be recovered efficiently.

ICML 2026

Modern Conservation Laws

Characterizes gradient-flow invariants for GELU, SiLU, SwiGLU, attention, and mixture-of-experts designs. Conservation laws reveal hidden quantities that training preserves. That matters because two architectures can have the same loss surface story but different invariant structure under gradient flow.

ICML 2026

DiReCT

Selects annealing samples whose gradients align with flat validation-Hessian eigendirections while constraining stiff-direction energy. It treats late training as movement through curvature. Good data is not just high quality; it should move the model along directions that improve validation without shaking brittle directions.

ICML 2026

Gradient Flow Through Diagram Expansions

Uses diagram expansions to characterize lazy, rich, NTK, and mean-field learning regimes. The paper gives language for when training mostly changes the readout versus when representation itself moves. That distinction is central to explaining why overparameterized models can either behave like kernels or learn new features.

ICML 2026

SVRG and Beyond via Posterior Correction

Uses richer Gaussian posterior families to introduce stochastic variance-reduced Hessian corrections beyond standard gradient-correction SVRG. The optimization object is the posterior over corrections, not just a variance estimate. That gives second-order information a probabilistic route into stochastic training.

ICLR 2026

FIRE

Uses deviation from isometry and Newton-Schulz iteration to tune reinitialization across vision, language, and RL. It formalizes the stability-plasticity reset problem: new learning should move enough to adapt while preserving geometry that makes old representations usable.

ICML 2026

LR Decay Wastes Your Best Data

Shows that curriculum gains can vanish when high-quality examples arrive after the learning rate has already decayed. The paper's object is the interaction between data order and optimizer schedule. It matters because "better data" is not an absolute property; a useful example has to arrive while the optimizer can still move far enough to use it.

ICML 2026

LoRA Gradient Descent

Proves convergence guarantees for original LoRA gradient descent despite the awkward non-smooth structure created by stacked low-rank adapter factors. The paper turns a widely used engineering trick into an optimization object. That matters because adapter training is now a deployment workhorse, and theory has to explain the thing practitioners actually run.

ICML 2026

Trust-Region LLM RL

Uses a trust-region view to stabilize reasoning and code-generation reinforcement learning while preserving useful inference behavior. The failure mode is familiar: RL can improve a reward and damage the base model's usable policy. A trust region makes the update a controlled movement problem rather than an unconstrained reward chase.

ICML 2026

Polar Express

Applies practical polar-decomposition methods to orthogonalized updates such as Muon and reports validation gains in low-precision GPT-2 training. The mathematical object is not just gradient direction but the matrix geometry of the update. This fits the lane because optimizer design is becoming about which geometry the update should respect under real numeric constraints.

ICML 2026

Beyond Muon

Places orthogonalized updates inside a broader family of matrix updates and shows that the most compressed orthogonal direction is not always better than Adam-style behavior. The paper matters because it refuses to treat a popular optimizer as a black box. It asks which part of the update geometry helps, which part merely stabilizes, and where the recipe stops being universal.

ICML 2026

LoRA-Pre

Reframes exponential-moving-average momentum as online linear regression and compresses optimizer state into low-rank subspaces. The object is optimizer memory: what history has to be retained for stable progress, and what can be projected away? This is theory pulled toward practice because adapter-heavy training is often limited by optimizer state, not parameter count alone.

ICML 2026

SGD RLVR

Shows memory-efficient SGD can match or outperform AdamW in verifiable-reward LLM post-training. The paper attacks a practical assumption: that stronger reasoning training requires heavier adaptive optimizers. Its deeper point is that the reward and verification structure can change the optimizer choice, especially when memory pressure is part of the training problem.

ICML 2026

RACO

Provides convergence guarantees for clipped conflict-averse updates in multi-objective optimization. The paper studies what happens when gradients point toward competing goals rather than one scalar loss. That is increasingly central to post-training, safety, and preference work, where the optimizer must move without letting one objective dominate or destroy another.

ICML 2026

Global Merging

Reinterprets part of decentralized model disagreement as useful signal for matching parallel-SGD convergence rates. The paper changes the meaning of disagreement: it is not always noise to average away. In distributed training, different workers can explore complementary local directions, and theory has to say when merging preserves that benefit rather than merely reducing variance.

ICML 2026

Non-Euclidean EoS

Extends edge-of-stability diagnostics beyond Euclidean gradient descent to optimizer geometries such as normalized, preconditioned, block, spectral, and infinity-norm methods. The contribution is a broader object for training instability. If the optimizer changes the geometry, then sharpness and stability should be measured in that geometry too.

ICML 2026

Constrained Transformers

Uses primal-dual training to enforce monotonic expected loss decrease across Transformer layers. The paper treats depth as an optimization process whose intermediate states should obey a descent constraint. That matters because architecture and optimizer are not separate: the network itself can be designed to satisfy a training-style certificate as information moves through layers.

ICML 2026

WSM

Emulates cosine, linear, and inverse-square-root learning-rate decay through principled model averaging. The paper turns a schedule into an averaging rule over checkpoints. This is useful because it suggests that some benefits attributed to hand-tuned decay may come from where the final weights sit along the optimization trajectory, not only from the instantaneous step size.

ICML 2026

Source Screening

Formalizes source-level data selection for statistically optimal feature-extractor learning. The optimization variable is not a minibatch but the source distribution that will shape the representation. This matters because training dynamics increasingly depend on which data streams are admitted, not merely on how gradients are computed after the data is fixed.

ICML 2026

Dirac-Frenkel-Onsager

Interprets non-unique parameter velocities as gauge freedom for smoother temporal dynamics. The paper looks at learning as a path through parameterized function space where many parameter motions can describe the same functional motion. That matters because optimization theory has to separate real changes in the model's function from arbitrary coordinate movement.

ICML 2026

Phase Retrieval Dynamics

Derives three learning phases governed by spectral decay in nonlinear regression with anisotropic data. The paper studies when fast early alignment gives way to slower late-stage error controlled by low-variance directions. This makes training dynamics visible as a sequence of regimes rather than one smooth convergence story.

ICML 2026

Neural Thickets

Reframes pretrained weights as surrounded by many nearby task-specialist parameter vectors in large, well-pretrained models. The object is the local neighborhood of a foundation model after pretraining. It matters because adaptation may work not by traveling far, but by finding one of many nearby specializations already made reachable by the pretrained basin.

ICML 2026

DMS

Replaces histogram discounting with a smooth model that distinguishes similar high-dimensional measures. The paper belongs in optimization because many search and quality-diversity systems compare distributions while deciding where to allocate effort. A smoother measure lets the optimizer react to meaningful differences without being dominated by brittle bin boundaries.

ICML 2026

FlexRank

Selectively activates nested low-rank components to trade cost for capability across language and vision transformers. The optimization object is rank as a controllable resource. This matters because adaptation and inference often need intermediate capability levels, not a fixed full-rank model or a fixed compressed model.

ICML 2026

DTO-KD

Dynamically balances task and distillation gradients to avoid conflict and dominance. The paper treats knowledge distillation as a multi-gradient negotiation: the teacher can help, but it can also pull the student away from the downstream objective. The method matters because distillation quality depends on when to trust the teacher and when to let the task gradient lead.

ICML 2026

AutoEP

Combines online exploratory landscape analysis with multi-LLM reasoning to adapt metaheuristic hyperparameters. The paper treats search configuration as a live decision problem instead of a fixed recipe. That matters because the best optimizer settings depend on the landscape being encountered, and an automated system needs evidence about that landscape while it searches.

ICML 2026

FlowOptimizer

Learns population-based iterative optimization as velocity fields conditioned on objectives, gradients, and population statistics. The paper turns an optimizer into a learned dynamical system over candidate solutions. This matters because search can be adapted at the level of movement rules, not only through hand-designed mutation or step-size schedules.

ICML 2026

ABOM

Learns evolutionary operator parameters online from target-task populations without handcrafted meta-training tasks. The optimization problem is to adapt the search operator while solving the task, rather than pretraining a general-purpose heuristic. This matters because black-box optimization often faces new landscapes where offline meta-training assumptions are weak.

ICML 2026

Controlled LLM Training on Spectral Sphere

Constrains weights and updates on spectral spheres to align steepest descent with muP-style activation stability. The paper treats stable scaling as a geometric constraint on the training path. It matters because large-model optimization must control both function movement and activation statistics as width and depth change.

ICML 2026

L2Seg

Predicts stable and unstable route segments so iterative solvers can aggregate stable portions into hypernodes. The optimization idea is to reuse local certainty: if part of a solution has stabilized, the solver should treat it as a larger unit. This matters because many combinatorial searches waste effort repeatedly reconsidering substructures that are already reliable.

ICML 2026

PCD

Guides samples toward novel high-performing regions beyond static training data. The paper responds to a common generative-optimization failure: learned generators stay close to the support they have already seen. It matters because discovery tasks need search pressure that can move into new high-value regions without losing the structure learned from past examples.

ICML 2026

SecondOrderSmoothCruiser

Shows SmoothCruiser-style oracle complexity is controlled by the order of the local Taylor remainder. The paper studies optimization difficulty through local smoothness beyond first-order behavior. This matters because higher-order structure can determine whether a method sees a genuinely hard landscape or only a poorly approximated local model.

ICML 2026

HOBIT

Gives a greedy approximation guarantee for an InfoNCE-motivated monotone submodular batching objective. The paper treats batch construction as an optimization problem with structure, not just random sampling. It matters because contrastive and representation learning can depend strongly on which examples share a batch.

ICML 2026

Sequential Data Values

Uses a bipartite graph surrogate for scalable greedy selection with guarantees on ML and LLM fine-tuning data. The paper studies data value as a sequential decision: the value of the next example depends on what has already been selected. This matters because large-scale training cannot afford to score each data point independently of the growing dataset.

SUBTHEME 02

Representation Geometry

Models generalize through the shape of their hidden spaces.

Representation papers ask what geometry is learned by attention, kernels, embeddings, Jacobians, and latent spaces. The output behavior is downstream of those shapes: spectra, rank, alignment, collapse, and equivariance decide what the model can recombine.

ICML 2026

Single-Head Attention in High Dimensions

Links learned query-key singular spectra to test error, interpolation thresholds, and recovery thresholds in a solvable attention model. Attention is reduced to a geometry where singular directions decide what information is recoverable. That makes generalization visible inside the learned spectrum.

ICML 2026

Why Deep Jacobian Spectra Separate

Explains low-rank Jacobian structure through depth-induced singular-value scaling and fixed-gate masked products. The paper studies the derivative of the network as a representation object. Spectral separation says depth can create privileged directions even before downstream labels are considered.

ICML 2026

Gaussian SIM

Proves alternating gradient-flow convergence for jointly learning a projection direction and univariate link. It isolates a simple representation-learning problem: find the direction that makes a nonlinear response predictable. The theory clarifies when projection and link learning cooperate rather than chase each other.

ICML 2026

Uncovering Latent Potential

Uses spectral layer selection and geometric regularization to preserve isotropic, discriminative embedding geometry during fine-tuning. The failure mode is hidden: fine-tuning can improve a task while damaging the latent geometry that made the model broadly useful. The method keeps the embedding space usable.

ICML 2026

Dynamics Reveals Structure

Shows relation composition creates an obstruction for first-order update propagation and may explain editing or reasoning failures. It treats model editing as a dynamical problem inside representation space. If updates cannot propagate through composed relations, local edits will not produce global reasoning changes.

ICLR 2026

Wasserstein GPCA

Lifts Gaussian computations to invertible maps and extends principal-component style geometry to absolutely continuous measures through neural geodesics. The object is not a vector cloud but a distributional manifold. Geometry becomes transport between probability measures.

ICML 2026

NSE Index Models

Identifies an activation-driven exponent governing statistical-to-computational gaps in single- and multi-index models. It studies when the representation is statistically present but computationally hard to learn. The activation function itself changes that gap.

ICML 2026

Stochastic Transformers

Shows random initialization noise prevents deterministic single-cluster collapse and can create antipodal attractors. The result makes randomness part of representation geometry. Noise is not merely nuisance; it can decide whether training collapses or separates into useful structure.

ICLR 2026

InfoNCE Gaussian

Models high-dimensional contrastive representations as approximately Gaussian and derives likelihood, entropy, and mutual-information quantities. The paper matters because contrastive learning often works by geometric intuition but is judged by downstream accuracy. This gives the geometry measurable probabilistic content.

ICML 2026

GraphGlue

Uses adaptive orthogonal frames and prototype tracking to align graph representations across domains. The object is the coordinate system of a graph encoder: two domains may contain similar relational structure but encode it in rotated or distorted bases. Transfer succeeds only if those spaces can be aligned without erasing domain-specific signal.

ICML 2026

Compositional Generalization Requires Linear Orthogonal Representations

Derives linear per-concept decomposition and orthogonality as necessary geometry for compositional generalization. The paper is blunt about what recombination requires: if concepts are tangled in the representation, a model cannot reliably build new combinations from old parts. It connects representation geometry directly to systematic reasoning.

ICML 2026

Neuron-Basis Circuits

Finds small native neuron sets controlling grammatical agreement and multi-hop factual reasoning steps. The method treats neurons as a causal basis rather than interchangeable coordinates. This matters because a representation is more useful when its directions can be tied to mechanisms that can be tested, edited, or ruled out.

ICML 2026

BBP Transitions

Identifies regimes where Hessian outliers reveal planted-signal information in teacher-student models. The paper uses random-matrix structure to explain when a representation starts exposing usable signal rather than only noise. It belongs here because spectra are not just diagnostics; under the right conditions they mark the moment hidden structure becomes recoverable.

ICML 2026

Language Symmetry Geometry

Shows that translation symmetries in co-occurrence statistics can induce circles, lines, and other geometric structures in embeddings. The paper turns language regularity into a geometric cause. It matters because representation shape is not arbitrary decoration: statistical symmetries in data can create the coordinate systems that later models use for analogy and transfer.

ICML 2026

Low-Rank LM Logits

Finds low approximate rank in matrices built from language-model logits across prompts and responses, then gives theoretical predictions for that behavior. The object is the output space itself. If logits live near a low-rank structure, then model behavior may be governed by a smaller set of latent directions than the vocabulary size suggests.

ICML 2026

Temporal Superposition

Shows memory demands can induce dense and sparse RNN feature-packing regimes with angular phase transitions. The paper studies representation under a storage constraint: many temporal features must share limited hidden dimensions. That connects sequence memory to the same superposition problem seen in large language models and sparse feature analyses.

ICML 2026

Riemannian Metric Matching

Learns the carré du champ operator through denoising so diffusion geometry can be recovered without an explicit graph. The paper asks what metric a generative process is implicitly using. That matters because sampling, interpolation, and uncertainty depend on distances in latent space; if the metric is wrong, the model moves through the wrong geometry.

ICLR 2026

Latent Space Dynamics

Extracts prior knowledge from attractors in latent vector fields without requiring input data. The representation is treated as a dynamical system, not a static embedding table. This matters because attractors encode what the model believes is stable, recoverable, or likely, even before a particular observation is supplied.

ICML 2026

Embedding Translation

Analyzes out-of-distribution, mixed-model, and chained translations between embedding spaces through geometric error amplification. The paper studies what happens when two latent coordinate systems are used as if they were interchangeable. It matters because retrieval, model routing, and cross-model tooling increasingly depend on moving meaning between separately trained spaces.

ICML 2026

Detecting the Semantic Fixed Point

Treats Transformer layers as fixed-point iterations and uses trajectory geometry for vocabulary-independent early exit. The object is the path of hidden states through depth. The paper matters because a representation can reveal when computation has stabilized, letting inference stop based on internal convergence rather than a fixed layer budget.

ICML 2026

Which Algorithms Can GNNs Learn

Shows standard message-passing neural networks cannot learn broad algorithm classes and derives more expressive variants. The paper connects representation geometry to algorithmic expressivity: a graph network must preserve the right relational information across rounds to implement an algorithm. If the representation collapses needed distinctions, no amount of training data repairs the architectural limit.

ICML 2026

Discrete Geometry of ReLU Networks

Proves average-degree bounds for connectivity graphs of polyhedral region complexes in ReLU networks. The object is the partition of input space induced by piecewise-linear activations. This matters because expressivity is not only the number of regions; it is also how those regions connect, which controls possible paths through the learned function.

ICLR 2026

RECM

Recovers full equivariance on symmetric distributions while allowing non-symmetric solutions where data require flexibility. The paper treats symmetry as a data-dependent commitment rather than a hard architectural rule. It matters because representations should ignore transformations only when the underlying distribution actually supports that invariance.

ICML 2026

OENN/CENN

Extends equivariant universal approximation to non-invertible compositional categorical symmetries. The paper broadens what it means for a neural representation to respect structure. Many domains have transformations that compose but do not invert cleanly; modeling those symmetries requires more than standard group-equivariant machinery.

ICML 2026

PanoWorld-X

Introduces panoramic Plucker embeddings to control large-motion scene evolution. The representation encodes rays and motion geometry rather than treating panorama generation as ordinary image synthesis. This matters because physical scene generation needs coordinates that preserve how views move through space.

ICLR 2026

DepthLM

Treats pixel reference and cross-dataset camera ambiguity as bottlenecks in vision-language 3D understanding. The paper makes camera geometry part of the representation rather than leaving it as hidden dataset bias. That matters because spatial claims from a multimodal model are only meaningful if the coordinate frame is known.

ICML 2026

Context-Parameter Equivalence

Uses input-output controllability to characterize when context can act like a perfect parameter update. The paper asks whether in-context learning is just a prompt trick or a real movement in function space. It matters because the answer tells us when more context can substitute for adaptation and when actual parameter change is necessary.

ICLR 2026

Symmetry ICL Dynamics

Enforces feature and label permutation equivariance to extract an explicit Transformer layerwise recursion. The paper studies in-context learning through the symmetries a learner should respect across examples and labels. It matters because a representation that preserves the right exchangeability can make the internal learning rule visible.

ICML 2026

Isotropic Gaussian RL

Reduces neuron dormancy and representation collapse by encouraging balanced use of all dimensions. The paper treats representation health as a geometric condition: a policy cannot use information carried by dead or underused coordinates. This matters in reinforcement learning because collapse can hide behind short-term reward gains while damaging future adaptability.

ICML 2026

Emergent Analogical Reasoning

Formalizes analogy as correspondence inference between categories and studies when it emerges under data, scale, and optimization changes. The paper makes analogy a representational alignment problem. It matters because analogical transfer requires the model to map relations across domains, not merely compare surface similarity.

ICML 2026

DeCoDe

Guides denoising to position ligands in pockets before refining detailed molecular structure. The representation is coarse-to-fine: global pose and local conformation are not learned as one undifferentiated vector. This matters because physical generation needs the right hierarchy of coordinates before fine detail can be valid.

ICLR 2026

Camera-Aware MLLM

Shows RGB-only spatial multimodal LLMs overfit camera distributions without explicit camera parameters. The paper identifies camera metadata as a missing coordinate in the representation. It matters because spatial reasoning that changes when the camera distribution changes is not genuine 3D understanding.

ICML 2026

HTI

Uses least-action and manifold priors to infer geodesic behavior paths induced by hyperparameters. The paper treats model behavior as movement on a manifold, where hyperparameters bend the path. This matters because two configurations can reach similar endpoint metrics while taking very different behavioral trajectories.

ICML 2026

Manifold Perturbations

Stabilizes diffusion and flow sampling by reconciling ambient-space training with intrinsic equality constraints. The representation problem is that generated objects often live on a constrained manifold while models train in a larger coordinate space. This matters because sampling errors can come from moving in directions that were never valid degrees of freedom.

ICLR 2026

Depth Anything 3

Uses a single depth-ray prediction target and plain transformer backbone across pose, geometry, and rendering tasks. The paper unifies geometric tasks through a shared representation of rays and depth. It matters because a common coordinate target can let one model serve multiple 3D reasoning roles without task-specific geometric hacks.

ICLR 2026

Diffusion Spacetime

Represents diffusion states as pairs of sample and time to avoid degenerate pullback or terminal-noise geometries. The paper makes time part of the representation rather than a side variable. This matters because diffusion trajectories are paths through a changing geometry, and ignoring time can make the induced metric misleading.

SUBTHEME 03

Games, Markets, and Constraints

Learning systems contain incentives, opponents, and feasible sets.

These papers make optimization social or constrained: agents share constraints, sellers price reusable data, learners face strategic reporting, and algorithms must respect fairness or feasibility. The correct object is often an equilibrium or feasible payoff set, not one loss minimum.

ICML 2026

Asymmetric Perturbation

Perturbs one player's payoff so the perturbed equilibrium coincides with the original equilibrium. The point is to understand how equilibrium can be preserved under asymmetric changes. That matters when learning algorithms or incentives alter only one side of a game.

ICML 2026

RQE Actor-Critic

Provides finite-sample guarantees by targeting a more regular bounded-rational equilibrium concept. Instead of assuming perfectly rational equilibrium behavior, it chooses an object that is smoother and statistically estimable. That makes multi-agent learning more measurable.

ICML 2026

Nash Equilibria with Coupling Constraints

Proves equilibrium existence under playerwise concave shared constraints using feasible-set topology. The shared constraint is the central object: each player's action changes what is feasible for others. Existence theory matters before optimization can claim to find anything.

ICML 2026

FTRL Lower Bounds

Proves FTRL can require exponential time to reach Nash equilibria in two-player potential games. The result warns against equating no-regret style dynamics with fast convergence. Even structured games can hide hard paths for common learning dynamics.

ICML 2026

Data Market Pricing

Uses piecewise-linear convex pricing to recover approximate seller stability in oligopolistic reusable-data markets. Data is non-rival: one dataset can serve many buyers. Pricing theory therefore has to account for strategic sellers and repeated reuse rather than ordinary one-good sale logic.

ICML 2026

Fair OT

Combines fair Sinkhorn, penalized optimal-transport guarantees, and bilevel learned costs for unseen-data fairness. Fairness is encoded as geometry over transported mass. The method asks how to move distributions while preserving fairness constraints that may not be visible in the training sample alone.

ICML 2026

Feasible Payoffs

Estimates the full set of payoffs consistent with observed equilibrium play under Hausdorff error. Instead of guessing one reward function, the paper characterizes the set of payoff structures that could explain behavior. That is the right uncertainty object for inverse game analysis.

ICML 2026

First-Price Auctions

Derives polynomial convergence rates with explicit discretization and approximation tradeoffs. The learning problem is strategic bidding under an auction mechanism. The theory shows how approximation choices affect whether convergence claims remain meaningful.

ICML 2026

Bayesian Truthful Valuation

Proves conditions under which knowledge sources maximize value by submitting truthful information even when coalitions are possible. The object is not a model parameter but an information market. This belongs in the theory lane because data pipelines now depend on incentives: if the mechanism rewards distorted evidence, better learning algorithms cannot repair the source.

ICML 2026

Ranking Feedback Online Learning

Characterizes impossibility and positive regret results when learners receive rankings instead of numeric utilities. The paper asks what can be learned from weak preference structure. That matters because many deployed systems get ordinal human feedback, not calibrated rewards, so the feedback channel itself defines the achievable learning problem.

ICML 2026

Fair Causal Bandits

Studies bandit learning under unknown roundwise counterfactual fairness constraints and gives regret and violation bounds. The central object is a policy that must learn reward while respecting fairness it does not fully observe at the start. This connects games and constraints to deployment: exploration cannot be treated as free when each action affects real subjects.

ICML 2026

Robust Contextual Optimization with Missing Covariates

Builds distributionally robust ambiguity sets directly from partial covariate observations instead of first imputing a clean dataset. The paper treats missingness as part of the feasible decision problem. It matters because a decision rule optimized on completed data can be overconfident exactly where the real observation is incomplete.

ICML 2026

Loss-Aware OT-DRO

Learns decision-focused optimal-transport ambiguity sets through bilevel hypergradient optimization. The paper makes robustness less generic: the uncertainty set should care about the loss and decision it will protect. This is important because worst-case distribution shift is only useful when it stresses the directions that can actually change the decision.

ICML 2026

MAPF via MMOT and Schrodinger Bridges

Uses entropic regularization and Sinkhorn-style solutions to guide reduced linear programs for near-optimal multi-agent paths. The paper treats multi-agent routing as a transport problem under coupling constraints. That matters because many-agent planning is not just independent shortest paths; each trajectory changes the feasible space for the others.

ICML 2026

What Preferences Can and Cannot Predict

Shows that preferential stability can predict dynamic stability for some subgames but fails in general multi-agent learning dynamics. The paper separates what preference observations can certify from what they cannot. It is a useful warning for preference-based alignment: observed rankings are not automatically enough to predict strategic behavior under learning.

ICML 2026

BCO Gradient Variation

Improves dimension dependence and problem-dependent regret bounds for bandit convex optimization. The learner has to optimize with limited feedback, so the geometry of gradient variation becomes the scarce signal. This fits the constraints story because the feedback channel, not only the feasible set, defines what can be learned efficiently.

ICML 2026

Cost-aware FedOpt

Prices random, full, and hybrid client communication strategies in non-convex federated optimization. The object is a learning system whose participants have communication costs, not a centralized optimizer with free gradients. This matters because distributed learning only works in practice when the optimization rule respects the cost of getting information from heterogeneous clients.

ICML 2026

DFN

Generalizes minimum-cost-flow value functions into universal approximators for convex-extendable discrete functions. The paper connects neural approximation to combinatorial structure. It belongs here because some decision problems are not naturally continuous; the theory has to preserve the feasible discrete object while still giving a learnable function class.

ICML 2026

Delayed-Observation RL

Analyzes delayed feedback as MDP dynamics with known structure plus an unknown component. The constraint is temporal: the agent acts before the relevant observation arrives. The paper matters because many real systems do not provide immediate reward or state feedback, so the learning problem must model delay instead of pretending the Markov state is instantly visible.

ICML 2026

MORetro*

Provides admissibility-based guarantees for recovering true Pareto fronts under a fixed single-step model. The paper treats synthesis as multi-objective search rather than one best answer. It matters because scientific and planning tasks often need feasible tradeoff sets, and the guarantee has to cover the front, not just a single scalarized solution.

ICML 2026

Conditional Equivalence of DPO and RLHF

Introduces constrained preference optimization and a soft-margin ranking view to repair pathological DPO regimes. The paper asks when a cheaper preference objective actually matches the constrained RLHF problem people intended. This matters because post-training is full of objectives that are operationally convenient but only useful if their constraint geometry preserves the intended behavior.

ICML 2026

Rare-Update Bandits

Achieves near-minimax regret with only logarithmically many parameter updates while preserving context adaptivity. The constraint is update frequency: learning must remain good even when changing the policy is expensive. This fits practical decision systems where compute, latency, or governance rules make continuous updating impossible.

ICLR 2026

DPO Unchained

Shows that compliant analytical choices can pair with human choice models, including non-convex losses. The paper widens the space of preference objectives while keeping the choice model explicit. It matters because alignment objectives are not just losses to optimize; they encode assumptions about how human comparisons are generated.

ICLR 2026

AuxDPO

Adds auxiliary variables to move direct preference training closer to the local RLHF solution. The paper treats DPO's gap from RLHF as a modeling mismatch that can be corrected with additional latent structure. This belongs in the constraints subtheme because the auxiliary variables are a way of representing the hidden optimization problem that the simpler objective had collapsed.

ICML 2026

Accuracy Auctions

Embeds a truthful auction in classification to optimize welfare under strategic reporting. The paper treats prediction as a mechanism-design problem, where agents may benefit from misreporting unless incentives are designed carefully. What This Lane Says About ICML and ICLR

The theory lane says the conference is no longer satisfied with recipes that work only as folklore. The mathematical work is trying to identify which parts of modern systems are real objects: spectra, equilibria, coverage mass, feasible sets, latent manifolds, and finite-sample certificates.

This is the natural fifth lane after safety, agents, data/systems, and physical generation. Those lanes describe what systems do. This lane asks why those behaviors are possible, when they fail, and what can be guaranteed when engineering constraints are part of the problem.
