## Abstract

Trained attention layers exhibit striking and reproducible spectral structure of the weights, including low-rank collapse, bulk deformation, and isolated spectral outliers, yet the origin of these phenomena and their implications for generalization remain poorly understood. We study empirical risk minimization in a single-head tied-attention layer trained on synthetic high-dimensional sequence tasks generated from the attention-indexed model. Using tools from random matrix theory, spin-glass theory, and approximate message passing, we obtain an exact high-dimensional characterization of training and test error, interpolation and recovery thresholds, and the spectrum of the key and query matrices. Our theory predicts the full singular-value distribution of the trained query–key map—including low-rank structure and isolated spectral outliers—in qualitative agreement with observations in more realistic transformers. Finally, for targets with power-law spectra, we show that learning proceeds through sequential spectral recovery, leading to the emergence of power-law scaling laws.

## Full-Text Upgrade

The full text sharpens the spectrum-generalization link: the learned spectrum is decomposed into useful outliers that correspond to recovered target features and a noisy bulk arising from finite samples. Recovering more outliers improves generalization, while the bulk explains overfitting noise. This makes the singular-value plot a compact diagnostic of what the attention layer has actually learned.

The theory maps ERM in tied attention to an equivalent matrix-sensing problem and derives interpolation and perfect-recovery thresholds. For targets with power-law spectra, the thresholding process recovers dominant spectral modes first, creating emergence-like jumps and power-law scaling of test error with sample size. The paper also argues that weight decay on the factorized query-key parameterization induces a nuclear-norm-like low-rank bias on the attention map.

## Limits and Failure Modes

Limits to watch: the model is single-head and tied-attention; tasks are synthetic attention-indexed problems; the high-dimensional asymptotic theory explains qualitative transformer spectra but does not prove the same exact laws for full multi-layer, multi-head transformers.
