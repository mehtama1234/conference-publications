## Abstract

We study the revenue of approximate correlated equilibrium in discrete first price auctions - the set of allowable bids is $\mathcal{B} = \{0, 1/k, \dots, 1 - 1/k, 1\}$ for some $k \in \mathbb{N}$. We show that the revenue of any $\epsilon$-\textit{approximate} correlated equilibrium is at least $v_2 -  \Theta(1/k)- \Theta(\epsilon k^2)$, where $v_2 \geq 0$ is the second-highest valuation. Our results establish the first polynomial convergence rates on the revenue generated by no-swap regret bidders in first-price auctions.
For instance, if bidders admit the optimal swap regret of $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{k T})$, then the time-averaged revenue is at least $v_2 -  \Theta(1/k) -  \Theta(\epsilon)$ after $\mathcal{O}(k^5/\epsilon^2)$ rounds.

## One-Sentence Claim

No-swap-regret learning in discrete first-price auctions guarantees time-averaged revenue close to the second-highest valuation with explicit polynomial convergence rates.

## Problem

First-price auctions are strategically complex because bidders shade bids rather than truthfully report values. Online-learning models of repeated bidding often converge to equilibrium-like behavior, but revenue guarantees depend on which regret notion is used and how fast approximate equilibrium is reached.

The paper focuses on discrete bid spaces and approximate correlated equilibria. The key problem is to prove a lower bound on seller revenue under no-swap-regret dynamics with rates explicit enough to imply finite-time convergence.

## Core Contribution

The main contribution is a revenue lower bound for any epsilon-approximate correlated equilibrium in discrete first-price auctions: revenue is at least the second-highest valuation minus discretization and approximation terms. The result yields the first polynomial convergence rates for revenue generated by no-swap-regret bidders in this setting.

This turns a qualitative equilibrium-learning story into a quantitative market-design guarantee. It says that if bidders use sufficiently strong no-swap-regret algorithms, then low-revenue outcomes are ruled out after a polynomial number of rounds.

## Method

The analysis works in a discrete bid grid B = {0, 1/k, ..., 1}. It bounds the revenue of approximate correlated equilibria by v2 minus terms scaling with 1/k and epsilon k^2. The discretization parameter k controls how finely bids approximate continuous first-price auctions, while epsilon captures equilibrium approximation error from finite-time learning.

Combining this equilibrium bound with optimal swap-regret rates gives a finite-time guarantee. With swap regret O(sqrt(kT)), the time-averaged revenue reaches v2 minus discretization and epsilon terms after O(k^5 / epsilon^2) rounds.

## Experiments and Evidence

This is a theory paper, so the primary evidence is the theorem and convergence-rate derivation rather than experiments. The abstract states the revenue lower bound and gives the finite-round implication for optimal swap-regret bidders.

The full paper should be inspected for exact constants, assumptions on valuations and bidder populations, and whether the guarantee extends beyond the discrete single-item first-price setting.

## Limits and Failure Modes

The guarantee depends on discretized bids, no-swap-regret behavior, and approximate correlated equilibrium. Real auction systems may include reserves, budgets, pacing, repeated-value uncertainty, asymmetric information, platform interventions, or strategic bidders that do not satisfy no-swap-regret assumptions.

The bound also contains a tradeoff in k: finer bid grids reduce discretization error but increase the epsilon k^2 term and worsen finite-time sample complexity. Practical relevance depends on whether realistic regret rates and grid sizes make the convergence horizon acceptable.

## Deep Themes

- Learning dynamics as market primitives: bidder behavior is modeled through regret minimization rather than static equilibrium selection.
- Finite-time guarantees for economic systems: convergence rate matters as much as limiting equilibrium.
- Approximate equilibrium with explicit error accounting: discretization and regret approximation are both visible in the bound.
- Theory as deployment realism: the paper asks what revenue can be guaranteed when participants learn over time.

## Subthemes

- Second-price-like revenue benchmarks can reappear under first-price learning dynamics.
- Polynomial convergence rates are necessary for translating asymptotic market-learning claims into operational claims.
