## Abstract

Pretraining language models directly on web-scale corpora is the de facto paradigm. We study an alternative where the model is initially exposed to *abstract structured data* to ease the subsequent acquisition of rich semantic knowledge, much like humans learning simple logic and mathematics before higher reasoning. We focus on *procedural data*, generated by formal languages and other simple algorithms, as such abstract data.

We first diagnose the algorithmic skills that different forms of procedural data can improve, often significantly. For example, the accuracy of context recall (Needle-in-a-haystack) jumps from 10 to 98% when a model is pretrained on Dyck sequences (balanced brackets). Second, we study how these gains are reflected in pretraining larger models (up to 1.3B). We find that front-loading as little as 0.1–0.3% procedural data significantly outperforms standard pretraining on natural language, code, and informal mathematics (C4, CodeParrot, and DeepMind-Math datasets). Notably, this also enables the models to reach the same loss value with only 55/67/86% of the original data and thus a comparable reduction in FLOPs. Third, we explore the mechanisms behind the benefits and find that procedural pretraining instills non-trivial structure in both attention and MLP layers. The former is particularly important for structured domains (e.g. code), and the latter for language. Finally, we lay a path for combining multiple forms of procedural data. Our results show that procedural pretraining is a simple, lightweight means of improving performance and accelerating language model pretraining, ultimately suggesting the promise of disentangling knowledge acquisition from reasoning in LLMs.

## One-Sentence Claim

Small amounts of procedural pretraining on abstract algorithmic data can accelerate later language, code, and math pretraining by instilling reusable structure in attention and MLP layers.

## Problem

Standard language-model pretraining begins directly on large natural corpora. This entangles knowledge acquisition with the acquisition of abstract reasoning and algorithmic structure, potentially making training less efficient.

The paper asks whether exposing models first to simple procedurally generated data, such as formal languages and algorithms, can warm up general-purpose pretraining.

## Core Contribution

The paper studies procedural pretraining as a lightweight front-loaded phase. It finds that different procedural data types improve different algorithmic skills, sometimes dramatically. Dyck-sequence pretraining raises needle-in-a-haystack context recall from 10 percent to 98 percent.

At model scales up to 1.3B, adding only 0.1-0.3 percent procedural data before standard corpora improves pretraining on C4, CodeParrot, and DeepMind-Math. Models reach the same loss with 55/67/86 percent of the original data, implying comparable FLOP reductions. Mechanistically, procedural pretraining induces non-trivial structure in attention and MLP layers.

## Method

The method generates abstract procedural datasets from formal languages and simple algorithms, inserts a small procedural phase before normal corpus pretraining, and compares downstream pretraining efficiency and algorithmic skill acquisition.

Mechanistic analyses inspect how attention and MLP layers change, with attention especially important for structured domains such as code and MLPs for language.

## Experiments and Evidence

Evidence reported in the abstract:

- Algorithmic diagnostics across procedural data forms.
- Needle-in-a-haystack context recall improvement from 10 percent to 98 percent after Dyck pretraining.
- Pretraining models up to 1.3B parameters.
- Gains on C4, CodeParrot, and DeepMind-Math with only 0.1-0.3 percent procedural data.
- Same-loss data/FLOP reductions to 55/67/86 percent of original budgets.
- Attention and MLP structure analysis.

## Limits and Failure Modes

- Procedural warmup could overfit to artificial patterns if overused.
- Benefits may depend on matching procedural tasks to later domains.
- Experiments up to 1.3B do not prove behavior at frontier scale.
- Synthetic data quality and mixture scheduling are likely sensitive.

## Deep Themes

**Reasoning substrate can be pretrained separately from knowledge.** The paper explicitly disentangles abstract structure acquisition from semantic corpus learning.

**Tiny curriculum phases can have large downstream effects.** A small procedural fraction changes later data efficiency.

**Mechanistic warmup matters.** Attention and MLP layers absorb different procedural structure that later helps code, language, and math.
