SIMPLE ANSWER

What Are We Doing?

We are turning the ICML / ICLR paper collection into a readable map of the field. The map should answer: what big problems are these papers collectively working on, what mathematical objects keep recurring, what approaches papers use, how subthemes relate to each other, and why the work matters beyond a single paper title.

The site already has the right shape: a homepage, a roadmap, a mathematical concept page, a paper atlas, 64 theme pages, and several standalone lane pages. The next work is not to invent a new infrastructure layer. The next work is to finish the writing at the level where a serious reader can understand the conference without opening hundreds of PDFs one by one.

WHY USEFUL

Why This Is Useful

It compresses the conference without flattening it.

A title list is too shallow and full paper reading is too slow. The atlas sits between them: it keeps named paper evidence visible while explaining the shared problem each cluster is attacking.

It makes themes comparable.

Safety, agents, data, physical AI, theory, and causality can look unrelated. The atlas shows that many papers are working on the same deeper objects: state, action, verifier, distribution, constraint, symmetry, uncertainty, and compute.

It creates a durable research artifact.

A hosted page can be reviewed, linked, revised, and used as a field map. That is more useful than a temporary conversation or a hidden local folder.

It gives us a clear production test.

The site is ready when every major lane has a conceptual spine, every theme has rich subthemes, every named paper has paragraph-depth treatment, and the public pages deploy cleanly.
