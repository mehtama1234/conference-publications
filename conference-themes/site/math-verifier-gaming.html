FIRST PRINCIPLE

The Verifier Is A Channel, Not The Truth

Every benchmark, reward model, proof checker, sandbox, judge, watermark detector, and expert rubric observes only a projection of the real target. The target might be ability, safety, clinical correctness, lawful action, causal validity, privacy deletion, or useful scientific discovery. The verifier is the channel through which evidence about that target is collected.

Gaming appears when the system can increase the verifier's score without increasing the target. Drift appears when the channel used during evaluation stops matching the channel encountered in deployment. The mathematical question is therefore not "did the score go up?" It is: what target does this score identify, under what pressure distribution, and which alternate explanations remain possible?

target Y -> observation channel O -> score S; failure when optimizing S does not improve Y
MAP

Six Failure Chains

Proxy Objective Gap
Reward and verifier signals can point away from the real target.
Process Shortcut
A good final answer can hide a broken trace or missing causal work.
Instrument Drift
A benchmark can measure the wrong latent variable after scale or distribution changes.
Adaptive Pressure
Attackers and users move the evaluated distribution toward weak boundaries.
Certificate Mismatch
The evidence artifact must match the safety, proof, privacy, or provenance claim.
Aggregation Failure
Scalar scores can erase minority risk, value intensity, modality credit, or group constraints.
CHAIN 01

Proxy Objective Gap

The core reward-hacking equation is simple: the learner is optimized against one function, while the human or deployment claim cares about another. The method works only when improving the proxy is informative about improving the target.

RLVepsR -> SafeDPO -> Omni-Reward

Shared object. An update direction induced by an imperfect reward or verifier.

Why it matters. RLVepsR shows that noisy verifiable rewards can create learning, stalling, or anti-learning. SafeDPO exposes the helpfulness-safety frontier instead of hiding it in one reward. Omni-Reward asks a multimodal reward model to see the artifact itself, not a text proxy.

First-principles read. A reward is a derivative of the training objective. If it is biased, underspecified, or blind to the artifact's native state, optimization amplifies that error.

gradient(proxy reward) should align with gradient(target utility)

Evidence Needed

Report proxy-target correlation under held-out pressures, not only training reward. Sweep reward noise, helpfulness-safety control, and modality visibility. Inspect cases where reward increases while expert judgment, execution success, or safety decreases.

Failure outside. The model may learn refusal style, judge preference, proof-like wording, caption similarity, or benchmark habits while the intended capability stays flat.

CHAIN 02

Process Shortcut

A system can reach the right endpoint for the wrong reason. Process-evidence papers ask whether the intermediate variables remain task-conditioned and causally useful.

RAGEN-2 -> TRACE Effort -> Verifying Chain-of-Thought -> CE-Graph

Shared object. A reasoning trace whose internal structure should reduce uncertainty about the answer.

Why it matters. RAGEN-2 detects input-agnostic template reasoning. TRACE Effort asks whether verifier success survives low-effort or truncated reasoning. Verifying Chain-of-Thought turns prose into dependency graphs. CE-Graph checks whether claimed counterexample reasoning changes the final result.

First-principles read. A trace is evidence only if the output depends on it. The mathematical test is causal sensitivity: perturb the process while holding easy shortcuts fixed.

trace is useful evidence when I(input; trace) and effect(trace -> answer) are both nonzero

Evidence Needed

Compare full, truncated, shuffled, template, and corrupted traces. Measure trace-input dependence and final-answer sensitivity. Localize unsupported steps rather than only accepting or rejecting the final answer.

Failure outside. A verifier can reward fluent intermediate text, long traces, or familiar proof shape while the actual computation is missing.

CHAIN 03

Instrument Drift

A benchmark is a measurement instrument. It can be precise and still wrong if the item distribution, judge channel, or latent variable shifts away from the claim readers infer from the score.

ATLAS -> Micro-Benchmarking Reliability -> FRABench/UFEval -> MADQA

Shared object. A noisy observation model from hidden capability to score.

Why it matters. ATLAS spends questions where ability uncertainty is high. Micro-benchmarking asks whether a ranking is statistically credible. FRABench and UFEval keep ability dimensions and uncertainty visible. MADQA separates oracle answerability from agent retrieval and reasoning.

First-principles read. The score identifies the target only through assumptions about items, rubrics, judges, and population coverage. When those assumptions move, the same score means something else.

S estimates theta only under named item distribution, judge, rubric, and population

Evidence Needed

Report confidence intervals, item-response behavior, rubric factor reliability, judge disagreement, and subgroup or domain splits. Show where aggregate ranking reverses under different but defensible measurement channels.

Failure outside. Efficient evaluation can efficiently measure a proxy: benchmark familiarity, prompt-format robustness, judge style, or retrieval luck.

CHAIN 04

Adaptive Pressure

Safety claims are distributional. A model can look safe on ordinary prompts and fail when users, attackers, tools, or environment states push it toward a boundary.

Jailbreak Foundry -> AdvGame -> CSPO -> SandboxEscapeBench

Shared object. A pressure process that searches reachable unsafe states.

Why it matters. Jailbreak Foundry makes attack generation modular and executable. AdvGame lets attacker and defender respond. CSPO uses boundary distance and sensitivity for safe policy movement. SandboxEscapeBench asks whether protected host state is reachable from inside the sandbox.

First-principles read. A boundary claim is only meaningful relative to the transitions allowed by the pressure process. Static tests see points; adaptive tests see reachable paths.

risk = P(reach bad set B under transition process Q)

Evidence Needed

Run adaptive and compositional attacks, not only fixed prompts. Measure distance to constraint violation, reachability of forbidden state, and security-fidelity frontiers. Report the pressure distribution explicitly.

Failure outside. The modeled attacker can miss real channels: social context, repeated interaction, tool side effects, dependency configuration, or off-platform incentives.

CHAIN 05

Certificate Mismatch

A claim needs an evidence artifact with the right shape. The strongest papers stop accepting fluent explanations where execution, proof acceptance, indistinguishability, or detection power is the actual certificate.

CyberGym -> VERINA/WZ-LLM -> Gaussian Unlearning -> Catch-22

Shared object. A certificate function matched to the claim type.

Why it matters. CyberGym requires exploits that run before a patch and fail after it. VERINA and WZ-LLM require checker-accepted proof artifacts. Gaussian unlearning requires indistinguishability from never-trained references. Catch-22 shows watermark detection, stealth, and robustness share one statistical signal budget.

First-principles read. Different claims have different null hypotheses. The verifier must reject the right alternative explanation.

certificate C rejects null explanation H0 for the specific claim being made

Evidence Needed

Execute artifacts, run proof kernels, compare deleted models to retraining references, and plot watermark detection against quality and attack survival. State the null hypothesis each certificate rules out.

Failure outside. A certificate can be too narrow: an exploit that only works in a toy image, a proof for an irrelevant lemma, an unlearning test with weak attackers, or a watermark detector calibrated on friendly transformations.

CHAIN 06

Aggregation Failure

Many failures are caused by premature scalarization. A single number can erase which group was harmed, which value was intense, which modality caused the answer, or which actor had incentive to distort the evidence.

VALUEFLOW -> Fair OT -> Accuracy Auctions -> Data Market Pricing -> MoCA

Shared object. An aggregation rule over values, groups, private utilities, data contributions, or evidence channels.

Why it matters. VALUEFLOW estimates value intensity instead of binary labels. Fair OT represents group constraints through couplings. Accuracy Auctions makes prediction value user-dependent. Data Market Pricing studies truthful contribution incentives. MoCA separates perception failure from reasoning failure.

First-principles read. Aggregation is a mathematical choice. It decides which losses can cancel, which actors can manipulate reports, and which failure source remains visible.

aggregate score A(x_1,...,x_n) is valid only if lost coordinates are truly irrelevant

Evidence Needed

Report subgroup and aspect losses, not only the mean. Test truthful reporting incentives, value-intensity calibration, and modality-credit interventions. Inspect cases where the aggregate improves while a protected, rare, or high-consequence coordinate worsens.

Failure outside. A single score can reward majority preference, average accuracy, cheap data, or text-prior answers while hiding the exact coordinate where deployment harm appears.

SYNTHESIS

The Overall Problem

What The Papers Are Trying To Solve

The shared problem is aligning three things: the real target, the observed evidence, and the optimization pressure. The target is what users or science care about. The evidence is what the system can measure. The pressure is what training, evaluation, deployment, or adversaries push the model to optimize.

Why The Approaches Work

They work when they make the proxy-target gap smaller or visible. Some make the verifier executable. Some decompose the score. Some estimate tails directly. Some expose frontiers. Some preserve causal routes. Some state the null hypothesis a certificate rules out.

What Remains Hard

No verifier is complete. A stronger measurement channel can still drift, be gamed, omit a population, miss a rare path, or reward a proxy. The first-principles discipline is to state exactly what the evidence can identify and what alternative explanations remain.
