FIRST PRINCIPLE

A Generated Object Has To Live In The Right Space

A molecule is not a list of atom coordinates. The same molecule can be moved or rotated without becoming a new molecule. A robot action is not any list of numbers; it must pass through joints, contact, timing, and safety margins. A physical-equation solution is not a picture on a grid; it is a whole field whose slopes and boundary rules matter. A video is not a stack of unrelated images; it is a path through one continuing world.

The strong papers in this lane work by naming the real object before training starts. They group together descriptions that mean the same thing, separate variables that play different roles, steer probability through allowed paths, keep the same object under harmless coordinate changes, or add measurements that expose hidden state. Once the state space is right, learning becomes a smaller and better-posed problem.

FORMULA PASS

Paper-Specific Math Boxes

Depth Anything 3

Object. Like looking at a photo of a hallway and asking how far away the door really is, the object is the missing distance behind each pixel.

Why it works. A picture shows color and shape from one view, but not the full room. What must stay the same is the 3D surface that produced the image. What may change is the camera view, scale, or scene. Depth helps because it forces the model to say where the visible surface sits in space rather than only naming what it looks like. Names used later: camera ray for the line from the camera through a pixel, and depth for how far along that line the surface lies.

pixel u = projection_K(X); learn depth z(u) so X = z(u) * ray_K(u)

Evidence check. Test metric depth, relative ordering, camera changes, cross-scene scale, and failures where recognition succeeds but distance is wrong.

Stable Video Infinity

Object. Like writing a long scene where each new paragraph must continue from the paragraph you actually wrote, the object is a video that keeps using its own previous frames as the starting point.

Why it works. Long-video drift happens because a model may train on clean beginnings but later has to continue from imperfect frames it created itself. What must stay the same is the scene identity, motion logic, and event continuity. What may change is the next frame and the small errors already present. Training on self-generated states teaches the model how to repair the kinds of mistakes it will actually face.

next state = model continuation from current state; train on the errors this creates

Evidence check. Measure error growth over horizon, compare clean-prefix and self-prefix training, and check temporal identity, motion, and scene consistency after long rollouts.

FlashWorld And VectorWorld

Object. Like a driving simulator where turning the wheel should change the road scene in a lawful way, the object is the world record before and after an action.

Why it works. Interactive generation works only if the action changes the state, not just the prompt. What must stay the same is the underlying world: actors, road layout, positions, timing, and physical possibility. What may change is the action and the next world that follows from it. Vector or graph state makes the changeable variables explicit, so different actions from the same state can produce different lawful futures. Name used later: world state.

current world + action -> next world -> rendered observation

Evidence check. Run counterfactual action tests, topology changes, actor-interaction checks, and compare state-structured generation to pixel-only continuation.

Safety-Guided Flow

Object. Like guiding people through a building before they reach a blocked hallway, the object is the route possible outputs follow while they are still forming.

Why it works. Rejecting unsafe samples after generation is late: the sample has already traveled through unsafe territory. Steering the route changes where forming outputs go. What must stay the same is usable output quality and the safety boundary. What may change is the direction of movement during generation. A safety push moves forming samples away from unsafe regions, while a validity push keeps them near usable outputs. Name used later: velocity field for the rule that gives each forming sample its next movement.

sample movement = ordinary generation step - unsafe-region push + valid-region pull

Evidence check. Report boundary-crossing rates, quality-safety frontiers, sensitivity to boundary misspecification, and paths showing whether samples are redirected before rejection.

DMPO For Diffusion LLMs

Object. Like judging finished drafts and then making future drafting paths more likely to end in the better drafts, the object is reward-guided text generation without a left-to-right action list.

Why it works. Diffusion language models form text by repeatedly cleaning and revising a whole noisy state, not by choosing one next token at a time. What must stay the same is the generation process the model actually uses. What may change is how likely high-reward finished samples become. DMPO helps by matching the update to the denoising path that produced the sample instead of pretending each step is an ordinary token action. Name used later: diffusion policy optimization.

make high-reward completed samples more likely under the denoising process

Evidence check. Compare to autoregressive RL imports, measure reward-quality movement, and audit whether improvements persist across denoising schedules.

Quotient-Space Diffusion Model

Object. Molecular identity after grouping together coordinate files that describe the same molecule.

Why it works. Rotations and translations create different coordinate files for the same molecule. Treating those files as one object lets denoising spend its effort on physically meaningful geometry: bonds, angles, clashes, pockets, and conformations. The formal phrase equivalence class means this same-object group.

molecule state = structure after ignoring arbitrary rigid pose

Evidence check. Test same-pose behavior, pose-invariant likelihood, bond/angle validity, docking sensitivity, and cases where orientation is physically meaningful.

Protein Autoregressive Modeling

Object. Like sketching the building shape before placing every brick, the generator chooses the protein's overall fold first, then fills in local pieces that must fit it.

Why it works. What may change is the local amino-acid or backbone detail. What must stay consistent is the larger structure that makes the protein plausible. Protein structure has nested constraints, so choosing the global scaffold first narrows the allowed local choices and prevents incompatible fragments. Name used later: coarse-to-fine factorization.

P(structure) = P(coarse fold) * product_i P(local_i | coarse fold, previous local states)

Evidence check. Report fold consistency, local geometry validity, long-range contact accuracy, and ablations that reverse or flatten the generation order.

DeCoDe And Chamaileon/Complexa

Object. A molecular design broken into target context, binding site, shape change, sequence, and atom-level rules.

Why it works. Binding and conformation are coupled but not identical variables. Separating them gives the sampler control over where interaction happens and how the molecule can move, while contextual data keeps the allowed set anchored to biological state.

design = target context + binding site + shape + sequence + atom-level constraints

Evidence check. Test binding-site accuracy, conformational diversity, clash rates, target-state generalization, and experimental or structural validation where available.

IRNO

Object. A scientific prediction that is repaired step by step until it obeys the governing rule closely enough.

Why it works. One-shot prediction hides accumulated rule error. Like checking a forecast against the rule it should obey, the method finds the leftover mismatch and learns a correction that moves the state closer to consistency. What must stay the same is the governing physical relation. What may change is the correction step. Names used later: residual for leftover mismatch, operator for the physical rule, and fixed point for a state that no longer changes under the rule.

next prediction = current prediction + correction(leftover rule mismatch)

Evidence check. Track residual norms across iterations, high-frequency error, boundary-condition changes, and whether repeated correction converges or oscillates.

HDFlow

Object. A robot policy split into two questions: which nearby outcome should the robot aim for, and what body motion can reach it?

Why it works. High-level intent can have several possible answers, while low-level motion must be smooth and physically allowed. Splitting them avoids forcing one vector to represent both the chosen outcome and the movement toward it. Latent means hidden: the latent subgoal is the internal chosen aim before the final action path is produced.

choose hidden subgoal, then move the body along a feasible action path

Evidence check. Measure subgoal quality, local trajectory feasibility, multimodal action coverage, contact failures, and performance when the subgoal is correct but actuation is hard.

MAPF Via MMOT And Schrodinger Bridges

Object. Many agents moving together without collisions.

Why it works. Collision-free planning fails when each route is solved alone and conflicts are repaired later. These methods move a whole crowd of possible paths together, while the constraints keep agents from occupying incompatible places at the same time. The formal transport and bridge terms name ways to move probability from starts to goals while respecting those constraints.

move start positions toward goal positions while preserving dynamics and avoiding collisions

Evidence check. Test collision rate, path cost, scalability with agent count, identity swaps, and robustness when starts, goals, or obstacles shift.

Mind-Omni And PRISM

Object. Human neural or perceptual signals as noisy clues about hidden concepts, attributes, and relations.

Why it works. Brain measurements are not images. A structured middle code creates a bridge: first estimate which hidden concept or relation best explains the measurement, then map that estimate to image, language, or relation output. Latent means hidden and inferred from clues, not directly observed.

body signal -> estimated hidden concept -> generated image, text, or relation

Evidence check. Compare reconstruction through latent codes to direct decoding, test object-attribute-relation accuracy, and perturb modalities to see which concepts survive.

TEDBench, MiAE, FLIP2, And RealPDEBench

Object. A benchmark split that asks whether the model learned the scientific rule rather than memorizing nearby examples.

Why it works. Scientific generalization requires held-out examples to differ along the real structural variable: fold, physical-equation boundary condition, physical regime, or transfer domain. A split that keeps near-duplicates nearby measures memory, not law.

valid split separates by structural variable, so train-test similarity cannot explain success alone

Evidence check. Report leakage checks, structural-distance distributions, regime shifts, transfer baselines, and whether performance drops where the scientific law is hardest.

OBJECT 01

Coordinate State

Depth Anything 3

Problem. Recognition can succeed while metric geometry is wrong. A model may name objects correctly and still fail at reconstruction, navigation, or manipulation.

Object. Like knowing not only that a chair is in the room but how far away it is and which direction each pixel points, the object is scene distance tied to camera rays.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the 3D layout the camera observed. What may change is the image appearance or camera setup. Depth gives distances; rays connect pixels to directions in space, so the model must represent geometry rather than only labels. Name used later: joint depth-and-ray state.

Connection. This is the visual analog of quotient diffusion: remove ambiguity that comes from an under-specified coordinate system.

Camera-Aware MLLM

Problem. A multimodal model can confuse object relations with viewpoint artifacts if the camera is treated as hidden.

Object. Camera parameters as observed variables in the conditioning state.

Why it works. Geometry answers are conditional claims. Left, right, size, distance, and visibility depend on projection. Supplying the camera reduces confounding between scene structure and image formation.

Connection. It matches causal-route papers in spirit: expose the variable that changes the observed signal before asking the model to reason.

3ViewSense

Problem. A single perspective hides occluded surfaces and makes some spatial relations unstable.

Object. Standard straight-on views plus a separated simulation-and-reasoning loop.

Why it works. Straight-on views remove perspective distortion and make comparisons closer to measuring with a ruler. The reasoning model sees a description where the needed relation is easier to infer.

Connection. This is a coordinate change that makes the answer less fragile.

Holi-Spatial

Problem. Spatial language needs supervision over masks, boxes, relations, and question-answer pairs, but hand labels are expensive.

Object. A renderable 3D Gaussian Splatting scene as a data generator.

Why it works. If the scene state is explicit and renderable, labels can be derived from geometry instead of guessed from pixels. The generator becomes a measurement instrument for spatial supervision.

Connection. It links generative scenes to evaluation: the same structured state can create examples and verify answers.

TideGS And RadioGS

Problem. Generic visual fields often look right but miss physical light, wave behavior, or time-varying structure.

Object. Small local field pieces with position, size, opacity, and signal strength that can be rendered and inspected.

Why it works. A splat is one compact local piece of a continuous field. It lets the model build the whole signal from parts whose position, extent, opacity, and emitted signal can still be checked.

Connection. These papers share the same idea as field-basis methods: choose building blocks that make the physical signal easier to represent.

WAFT And SplAttN

Problem. Spatial reasoning fails when the model picks one match too early even though the image is still ambiguous.

Object. Like keeping several possible locations circled on a map until more clues arrive, the object is a set of possible visual matches tied to image locations.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the real match the evidence supports. What may change is which candidate remains plausible as more context arrives. Keeping doubt localized in space lets later evidence resolve it instead of forcing an early brittle assignment. Name used later: spatial attention over candidate matches.

Connection. This is the same mathematics as sampler coverage: do not discard plausible states before the observation has enough information.

OBJECT 02

Temporal World State

Stable Video Infinity

Problem. Long generated videos drift because each locally plausible step shifts the next state the model must continue from.

Object. Error paths produced by the generator itself.

Why it works. Training on self-generated failures changes the model's practice data from clean prefixes to the states it will actually visit. Drift becomes a correctable error pattern rather than an unobserved buildup.

Connection. This is temporal error repair, close in shape to IRNO's repeated repair of physical-rule mismatch.

SANA-Video

Problem. Minute-long high-resolution video needs memory of the past without quadratic growth.

Object. Like carrying a short running summary while watching a long movie, the generator keeps just enough past video state for later frames to stay coherent without memory growing forever.

Why it works. What may change is the number of frames. What must stay preserved is identity, motion, scene layout, and facts later frames depend on. Long video is a state-compression problem: keep the information that still changes future predictions and drop detail that no longer matters. Name used later: constant-memory state.

Connection. It is the generative counterpart to long-context cache allocation: keep the state that still changes future predictions.

FlashWorld And VectorWorld

Problem. A world model must respond to action. Static prompt-to-video is too passive for simulation or driving.

Object. Like a driving simulator where turning the wheel changes the road situation, the object is a world state that updates after an action.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the underlying world: lanes, actors, road connections, timing, and action consequences. What may change is the action, view, or graph representation used instead of pixels. The model can update a structured world after an action instead of regenerating unrelated frames. Name used later: interactive state-change system.

Connection. This is agents in physical form: action changes the state, and the next observation must depend on that changed state.

SceneSmith

Problem. Robot environments must be physically and functionally usable, not merely visually dense.

Object. A simulation-ready scene assembled by designer, critic, and orchestrator roles.

Why it works. Generation is decomposed into proposal, critique, and coordination. The artifact is checked for affordances, object placement, and downstream use, so scene validity becomes part of the loop.

Connection. The same control structure appears in multi-agent reasoning: decomposition helps only when critics evaluate the right state variables.

Control Consistency Losses And dWorldEval

Problem. A generated trajectory can look plausible while violating the control signal that supposedly produced it.

Object. Like checking whether a video of a car turning left actually follows from a left-turn command, the object is agreement between action, world rule, and visual future.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the cause-effect link between action and later state. What may change is the generated frame sequence. The model is penalized for paths whose causes do not match their effects, so the generated sequence becomes a claim about change, not only a plausible video. Name used later: control consistency.

Connection. It is the world-model version of proof verification: check whether each later state follows from the allowed transition.

OBJECT 03

Sample Paths

Safety-Guided Flow

Problem. A generator can put probability near unsafe regions even when samples look high quality.

Object. Like steering a crowd away from a blocked hallway while still getting them to valid exits, the object is the movement rule used while samples are forming.

Why it works. The sampler's path matters. What must stay the same is usable output quality and the safety boundary. What may change is the direction of movement before the sample is finished. If the movement rule is changed early, probability avoids regions that a post-hoc classifier might only reject after generation. Name used later: guided flow.

Connection. This is constraint handling inside the motion, like Pi-net moves decisions into allowed regions.

DMPO For Diffusion LLMs

Problem. Reasoning RL recipes from left-to-right language models do not directly apply when generation is diffusion-style.

Object. Like improving a blurry draft by repeated cleanup instead of writing one word at a time, the object is the path that turns noise into a completed language sample.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the final sample quality being rewarded. What may change is the denoising route that produced it. The update follows the process the model actually uses to make text, so training does not pretend that cleanup steps are ordinary next-token actions. Name used later: diffusion policy optimization.

Connection. The lesson is general: training must respect the generation process that actually defines the sample.

FALCON

Problem. Molecular equilibrium estimates depend on probability and coverage of important shapes, not only visually plausible conformations.

Object. Like keeping a receipt for how a simple starting shape was stretched into a molecule, the object is a sampler that knows how much probability weight each generated state carries.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the probability needed for the physical estimate. What may change is the generated molecular shape. The model can move probability mass while tracking how its weight changes, so samples become weighted evidence rather than only plausible candidates. Name used later: density.

Connection. It links generative modeling to statistical mechanics: the sample is useful only if its probability means something.

SRMC

Problem. Rare cases can dominate estimates even when most samples look good.

Object. Like checking a bridge by deliberately inspecting stress points instead of only walking across the middle, the object is a sampling rule that visits important rare regions often enough to measure them.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the quantity being estimated. What may change is where samples are drawn and how their weights are corrected. Missing an important region creates a biased measurement, not a cosmetic defect, because the final estimate depends on places ordinary sampling may barely visit. Name used later: rare-region sampler.

Connection. This explains why benchmark averages can lie: rare regions may contain the scientific quantity of interest.

Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridges

Problem. Medical acoustic data can have noise and jumps that ordinary bell-shaped noise underrepresents.

Object. Like allowing for sudden pops in an audio recording instead of treating every corruption as gentle hiss, the object is a sample path that permits abrupt deviations.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the acoustic signal being reconstructed or modeled. What may change is the noise pattern along the route. Heavy-tailed noise assigns more chance to large jumps, so the sampler can model corruption regimes that a light-tailed process would treat as impossible outliers. Name used later: Cauchy noise.

Connection. The noise rule is part of the model's geometry; choosing the wrong one changes which observations are considered plausible.

OBJECT 04

Molecules And Proteins

Quotient-Space Diffusion Model

Problem. Rigid rotations and translations change coordinates without changing the molecule.

Object. A space where all same-molecule poses are treated as one structure.

Why it works. Like recognizing the same key after turning it in your hand, learning no longer wastes probability on fake variants. What must stay the same is bond geometry and relative arrangement. What may change is arbitrary rigid-body pose. Name used later: quotient space for this same-object grouping.

Connection. This is the cleanest example of the lane's central rule: first identify what counts as the same object.

state = structure after ignoring arbitrary rigid-body pose

Protein Autoregressive Modeling

Problem. A protein backbone has constraints across local residues, secondary motifs, and global fold.

Object. Like sketching the whole bridge before choosing each bolt, the object is a generation order that chooses broad structure before fine details.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the physically valid fold. What may change is the next local residue or structural detail. The method helps when early coarse choices rule out impossible later completions, so local generation is guided by the global shape it must fit. Name used later: autoregressive hierarchy.

Connection. The same idea appears in HDFlow: solve a high-level shape first, then fill in feasible local motion.

DCFold

Problem. Fast protein prediction can output plausible folds without enough structural consistency.

Object. Like checking that a building sketch from the front and side describes the same building, the object is agreement between two predicted views of one protein structure.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the underlying fold. What may change is the view or structural summary used to predict it. If two views of the same fold disagree, the model has exposed a geometry violation. Name used later: dual consistency.

Connection. Consistency is the scientific analog of verifier checks in agents: speed is acceptable only when structural constraints still hold.

Chamaileon And Complexa

Problem. Binder design is not one sequence against one static target.

Object. Like designing a key for a lock that can flex into several shapes, the object is the sequence and 3D structure tied to target state, contact points, and atom-level rules.

Why it works. What must stay the same is biological binding that can exist in the measured system. What may change is sequence, target state, contact geometry, or generated candidate. Synthetic scale gives many possible designs, but experimental multimer evidence anchors that wide search to real biology. Name used later: target-state-conditioned binder design.

Connection. This is the data version of same-object geometry: broad generated variation must collapse onto physically valid structure.

DeCoDe

Problem. Binding location and molecular shape are coupled but not identical.

Object. Like choosing where a flexible key touches the lock separately from how the key bends, the object is two linked choices: binding position and molecular conformation.

Why it works. What must stay the same is chemically valid binding. What may change is where the molecule acts or how it flexes. Separating the two choices keeps one source of variation from having to explain both location and shape. Name used later: factorized diffusion variables.

Connection. This is the molecular form of decomposed control in agents and robotics.

FIDIA

Problem. Protein design often evaluates the best candidate from a generated set, not the average sample.

Object. Like baking ten loaves and serving only the best one, the object is the best candidate found within a finite batch.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the downstream selection rule. What may change is the mix of generated candidates. Training should match deployment: improving the best sampled candidate is different from improving the average sample. Name used later: best-of-N objective.

Connection. This is the same frontier logic as ParetoPO and MORetro*: what matters is the selected feasible outcome under budget.

mCLM, ReaSyn, And MORetro*

Problem. Chemically attractive molecules are not enough if they cannot be synthesized or if their route is too costly or toxic.

Object. Like designing furniture only from parts the workshop can actually buy and assemble, the object is the molecule plus the route, cost, and feasibility of making it.

Why it works. What must stay the same is chemical validity and makeability. What may change is the building block, reaction path, route cost, or toxicity tradeoff. The model generates paths through chemical operations rather than isolated strings that must be repaired later. Names used later: synthetic tree for the making route and Pareto front for non-wasteful tradeoffs among route criteria.

Connection. These papers make feasibility constructive: the route is evidence that the molecule belongs to the real design space.

OBJECT 05

Scientific Fields

IRNO

Problem. Cheap learned physical solvers can accumulate small high-frequency errors over repeated prediction.

Object. A repeated repair rule: check the current prediction, find the leftover mismatch, and correct it.

Why it works. Like adjusting a forecast after seeing the leftover mismatch, the model is trained to repair the error left by the current approximation. What must stay the same is the physical rule being solved. What may change is the correction at each step. Name used later: fixed point for the state that no longer needs correction.

Connection. It shares a mathematical shape with Stable Video Infinity: learn from the states your own model creates.

MERLIN

Problem. Scientific fields are observed through sparse measurements and queried at changing resolutions.

Object. Like mapping temperature across a room from a few thermometers, the object is the whole hidden field plus the rule that says what each sensor reading tells us about it.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the underlying field being measured. What may change is the sensor location, resolution, or query grid. The model represents the field separately from any one grid, so a new measurement is a question asked of the same object rather than a new object. Name used later: measurement operator.

Connection. This is a precise version of multimodal grounding: observations are not the state, they are measurements of the state.

Dirac-Frenkel-Onsager

Problem. Momentum can speed learning but can also push physical-equation parameters through directions that break the equation constraints.

Object. Like accelerating a train only along the track, the object is the set of movement directions that stay inside the physical rule.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the equation constraint. What may change is speed along directions that remain valid. Momentum is useful only after it is projected onto the local surface of allowed motion; directions that would leave the track are blocked. Name used later: variational constraint geometry.

Connection. This is the optimizer form of safe steering: change the state without crossing the boundary that defines validity.

LoRFS And Modified SINNs

Problem. High-dimensional physical equations are too large for naive coordinate-wise fitting.

Object. A smaller set of field-building pieces plus losses that check the physics rule.

Why it works. Like describing a song with a few notes that repeat instead of recording every vibration separately, smooth physical fields can often be built from fewer useful pieces than raw coordinates suggest. What must stay the same is satisfaction of the physical equation. What may change is the set of pieces used to build the field. Names used later: spectral and separable bases for two ways to choose those pieces.

Connection. Basis choice plays the same role as token choice in mCLM: it decides which operations become simple.

ReViT

Problem. Rotated physical fields should obey the same dynamics under transformed coordinates.

Object. Like turning a weather map without changing the storm, the object is the same physical rule seen under rotated coordinates.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the physical law. What may change is the coordinate orientation. The model's output rotates consistently when the input rotates, so it does not need to relearn the same law for every orientation. Name used later: rotational equivariance.

Connection. It is the field version of quotient-space thinking: separate real dynamics from coordinate accidents.

Walrus And RealPDEBench

Problem. Simulation-trained scientific models may fail on measured systems.

Object. Like testing a flight simulator against real flight logs, the object is a rule for physical change checked on both clean simulation and messy measurements.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the physical process being claimed. What may change is domain, sensor noise, resolution, or boundary condition. Walrus searches for reusable change rules; RealPDEBench asks whether those rules survive the measurement limits of real systems. Name used later: dynamics transfer.

Connection. The pair explains why pretraining and benchmarking are inseparable in scientific ML: transfer claims need measured stress tests.

OBJECT 06

Embodied Action

HDFlow

Problem. High-dimensional robot action cannot be represented well by one deterministic output.

Object. Like deciding which shelf to reach before choosing every joint movement, the object is a high-level aim plus a detailed body-motion path.

Why it works. What must stay the same is feasible progress toward the task. What may change is the sampled aim or the local motion that realizes it. The model first chooses a coarse region of intent, then samples feasible local motion, keeping several possible action choices alive. Name used later: latent subgoal with trajectory.

Connection. It is protein hierarchy applied to control: coarse structure constrains fine motion.

MomaGraph

Problem. Pixels do not directly say what a robot can do with objects.

Object. A scene graph that records object states and what actions each object allows.

Why it works. Like labeling a drawer as openable and a cup as graspable, the graph records action-relevant facts instead of only appearance. What must stay the same is which objects matter and how they can change. What may change is camera view or object layout. Name used later: affordance for an action the environment makes possible.

Connection. It mirrors Graph-then-Plan agent systems, but with physical state rather than web or code state.

EgoTactile

Problem. Vision cannot always observe contact, slip, pressure, or grasp stability.

Object. Egocentric video paired with full-hand pressure measurements.

Why it works. Like feeling a glass start to slip before seeing it fall, touch reveals physical state that pixels can hide. What must stay the same is the contact fact that decides action success. What may change is the sensor channel used to observe it. Name used later: latent variable for a hidden state inferred from measurements.

Connection. This is the sensor version of hidden-state diagnostics: add the measurement that makes the hidden variable visible.

JoSE

Problem. Muscle and actuator spaces have many dimensions, but useful behavior often lies in a much smaller set of coordinated movements.

Object. A smaller action surface that keeps behavior-relevant variation and removes redundant actuator movement.

Why it works. Like steering by reach, turn, and grip rather than every tiny motor command, the controller searches in coordinates that preserve movements that change behavior and remove actuator changes that do the same job. What must stay the same is functional movement. What may change is raw actuator detail. Name used later: manifold for that smaller reachable surface.

Connection. It is same-object grouping for action: many actuator vectors can express the same functional movement.

RodriNet

Problem. Articulated motion has rotation rules that ordinary vector layers do not naturally respect.

Object. A learnable movement rule built around known body-rotation math.

Why it works. Like using a hinge rule when modeling a door, known rotation structure is built into the transformation before learning the leftover behavior. What must stay the same is lawful joint and body motion. What may change is the learned correction around that law. Name used later: kinematics for the rule connecting joints, rotations, and body position.

Connection. It belongs with ReViT and Dirac-Frenkel-Onsager: the architecture respects a geometric law before fitting.

MAPF Via MMOT And Schrodinger Bridges

Problem. Multi-robot path finding must coordinate many agents without collisions, often before identities matter.

Object. A crowd-level movement plan with collision-free bridge constraints.

Why it works. Like routing many people through hallways without crashes, planning becomes movement of many possible routes under constraints. What must stay the same is collision-free progress from starts to goals. What may change is which route each agent takes. Names used later: Markov for choosing the next move from current state, and transport for moving mass from starts to goals.

Connection. It is the embodied counterpart of fair matching and sampler bridge methods.

BehaviorVLA, PACT, And EcoVLA

Problem. Robot policies must transfer, accept new constraints, and run inside wall-clock control loops.

Object. Reusable behavior patterns, bounded policy movement, and computation schedules that fit real-time control.

Why it works. BehaviorVLA attacks sample efficiency, PACT limits how far a correction can move the policy, and EcoVLA fits computation into unused latency gaps. Each method preserves something concrete: task behavior, closeness to trusted behavior, or timing.

Connection. Robotics makes the whole atlas concrete: a policy fails if the math ignores data, constraints, or systems time.

OBJECT 07

Human Signals Beyond Images

Mind-Omni And PRISM

Problem. Brain signals are noisy measurements, not images or text.

Object. Structured brain codes or object-attribute-relation descriptions that bridge neural and visual spaces.

Why it works. The intermediate code gives the model a structured variable between fMRI and media. Reconstruction becomes a problem of estimating hidden concepts from measurements, then mapping concepts to images or language.

Connection. This is MERLIN's measurement logic in neuroscience form.

Hibiki-Zero, EmotionThinker, And EEmo-Logic

Problem. Speech and affect depend on timing, prosody, and emotional state, not just transcript content.

Object. Hidden timing and emotion variables aligned with language or audio.

Why it works. Like hearing the same sentence as a joke, warning, or apology depending on tone and timing, the model separates what was said from how it was expressed. What must stay the same is meaning plus the human state supported by the signal. What may change is transcript wording, rhythm, tone, or expression. Name used later: latent variable for a hidden cause inferred from the signal.

Connection. It is the human-signal version of factorized diffusion: separate variables that are coupled in the observation but distinct in the cause.

DLMR And Causal Route Gating

Problem. Multimodal systems can remember or route through the wrong evidence source.

Object. A memory or routing variable that assigns credit to modalities and intermediate causes.

Why it works. Explicit routing reduces hidden confounding. The system has to decide which signal changed the belief state instead of merging all evidence into one opaque vector.

Connection. This matches Camera-Aware MLLM: expose the causal variable before interpreting the output.

LIMSSR

Problem. Action quality may need multimodal evidence when some modalities are missing.

Object. Like grading a sports move when one camera angle is missing, the object is a score built from the evidence that is present while keeping the missing view marked as unknown.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the action quality being judged. What may change is which sensor channels are available. The model should not hallucinate a complete record just because training had all modalities; it must score from observed evidence and carry doubt about the missing parts. Name used later: partial observability.

Connection. This is partial observability, the same state problem as agents and robotics.

OBJECT 08

Verification And Benchmarks

TEDBench, MiAE, And FLIP2

Problem. Protein benchmarks can reward leakage, near-duplicate memory, or scaling that does not improve the intended generalization.

Object. Like testing whether a student can solve a new kind of problem instead of a memorized homework variant, the object is a split where training examples are separated from test examples by meaningful protein structure and transfer difficulty.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the biological capability being claimed. What may change is which folds, tasks, or related examples are withheld. The benchmark works only if high scores require structure-sensitive transfer, not recognition of near duplicates or leaked labels. Name used later: structure-aware evaluation split.

Connection. This is scientific benchmark hygiene: if the split is wrong, the mathematical claim is wrong.

Seizure-Semiology-Suite And VideoKR

Problem. Medical and knowledge-rich video tasks require domain outcomes, not generic visual plausibility.

Object. Like asking an expert what event happened, when it happened, and which evidence supports it, the object is the expert-visible state in the video.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the clinically or knowledge-relevant event. What may change is the clip, wording, or visual surface. The evaluation asks whether the model preserved the state variables experts use, not whether the video merely looks plausible or the label sounds likely. Name used later: domain-grounded observation.

Connection. This extends control consistency from physics to expert domains: the output must follow the real rules of the system.

MetamerGen And OmniVerifier

Problem. Generated media can fool one perceptual path or one judge while failing broader consistency.

Object. Like asking several inspectors to check whether an edit looks right, follows the instruction, and agrees across media, the object is a set of checks over different failure views.

Why it works. A single metric sees one view of the generated object. What must stay the same is the intended perceptual or instruction-level claim. What may change is which checker reads which view. Multiple checks reduce the chance that the model exploits an evaluation blind spot. Names used later: verifier and perceptual equivalence.

Connection. Verification closes the lane: native state spaces matter because they define what should be checked.

SYNTHESIS

The Cross-Paper Shape

Coordinate To Constraint To Sampler

Depth Anything 3, Camera-Aware MLLM, Quotient-Space Diffusion, DeCoDe, Safety-Guided Flow, and FALCON all show the same sequence. First choose coordinates that identify the real object. Then state the constraints that define valid objects. Then design the sampler so probability mass moves inside that space instead of being filtered after the fact.

World Model To Action To Verification

Stable Video Infinity, VectorWorld, SceneSmith, Control Consistency Losses, MomaGraph, HDFlow, and MAPF papers treat generation as a closed loop. The output is not done when it looks plausible. It must remain valid after an action changes the world and a verifier checks the transition.

Scientific Prior To Measurement To Benchmark

MERLIN, IRNO, ReViT, LoRFS, Walrus, RealPDEBench, TEDBench, MiAE, FLIP2, Mind-Omni, and PRISM connect prior structure to measurement. A useful model says what the hidden state is, how observations are produced from it, and what benchmark would distinguish real structure from memorized surface patterns.

FOCUSED CHAIN

Native State: From Quotient Geometry To Fields To Action

Quotient-Space Diffusion: Remove Fake Coordinates

Problem. A molecule or protein complex has physical identity before it has an arbitrary pose in a coordinate file. Rotating or translating every atom should not create new scientific evidence.

Plain object. Like recognizing the same chair after the camera moves, the model groups coordinate files that describe the same structure.

Why it works. What must stay the same is molecular identity and relative geometry. What may change is arbitrary pose. The learner stops spending probability on nuisance copies and can concentrate on bonds, pockets, clashes, and conformational variation. Name used later: equivalence class under a symmetry group.

Failure boundary. The removed variation must truly be nuisance. Chirality, view-dependent sensors, docking orientation, or asymmetric environments can make a pose-like variable physically meaningful.

structure = coordinates after grouping arbitrary rigid poses

TideGS, RadioGS, And Spatial Fields: Use A Basis The Medium Understands

Problem. A continuous physical signal is not naturally a flat image. It has locality, smoothness, visibility, attenuation, and measurement geometry.

Plain object. Like rebuilding a landscape from many small smooth patches, the model stores a field as local pieces with position, size, opacity, and signal strength.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the continuous signal the sensor or renderer would measure. What may change is the local piece used to approximate it. Rendering, interpolation, sensor simulation, and spatial reasoning become operations on a field state instead of guesses over pixels. Name used later: basis.

Failure boundary. A convenient basis can smooth over discontinuities, hard boundaries, long-range coupling, sensor artifacts, or sharp physical events that are not well expressed by local blobs.

observations = measurements taken from a field built out of local pieces

IRNO And Scientific Operators: Predict By Residual Correction

Problem. In scientific prediction, a small local error can be amplified by the next step, especially in high-frequency patterns or boundary-sensitive regimes.

Plain object. Like checking a calculation, finding the leftover mismatch, and correcting it again, the prediction is repeatedly compared with the physical rule.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the governing rule the system should obey. What may change is the current prediction after each correction. The model trains on the error left by its own approximation and moves toward consistency. Names used later: residual, operator, and fixed point.

Failure boundary. A correction process can settle and still be wrong if the mismatch is measured with the wrong ruler, if the boundary conditions changed, or if the missing physics sits outside the learned correction class.

state[t + 1] = state[t] + correction(leftover rule mismatch)

HDFlow: Split Global Intent From Local Motion

Problem. Robot action mixes two different uncertainties: which outcome should be pursued and which low-level movement can realize it.

Plain object. Like first choosing which shelf to reach for and then planning the arm path, the policy separates the intended subgoal from the feasible body motion.

Why it works. What must stay linked is intent and physical reachability. What may change is the exact motion. The high-level state keeps possible intents alive; the local flow handles continuous feasible motion once an intent is selected. Name used later: latent subgoal distribution.

Failure boundary. A bad subgoal can make the low-level controller precisely execute the wrong plan. Contact timing, friction, occlusion, and actuator limits can still break a locally smooth trajectory.

policy = choose subgoal, then move the body toward it

MomaGraph, EgoTactile, JoSE, And RodriNet: Add The Missing Body State

Problem. Action is not text and not only vision. A valid movement depends on object affordances, pressure, slip, rotations, joints, actuator redundancy, and body-specific constraints.

Plain object. Like planning with both a scene map and the feeling in your hand, the policy needs the body-world facts that decide which movements are allowed.

Why it works. What must stay the same is physical feasibility. What may change is graph state, touch state, action surface, or body-rotation rule. Each representation exposes a variable that raw pixels or token commands hide. Names used later: affordance graph, action manifold, and kinematic operator.

Failure boundary. If the sensor omits hidden physical state, if the graph is stale, or if the learned smaller action surface excludes a needed recovery move, the policy reasons inside the wrong body-world model.

valid action = body state + world relation + constraint-respecting transition

The Native-State Chain

Shared object. A representation whose coordinates match what the domain needs to keep true: same-object identity for molecules, useful field pieces for media, correction states for physical rules, and embodied states for contact.

First-principles read. Learning works best after the valid state has been named. If validity is absent from the representation, the loss must rediscover it indirectly from examples, which makes the method brittle under new poses, resolutions, sensors, bodies, or boundaries.

Across papers. Quotient-Space Diffusion removes fake state. TideGS and RadioGS choose renderable field coordinates. IRNO learns a correction process instead of a one-shot answer. HDFlow splits intent from motion. MomaGraph, EgoTactile, JoSE, and RodriNet add the body variables that make action physically meaningful.

raw observation -> native state -> constrained transition -> verifier
FOCUSED CHAIN

Grounded World Models: Action To Transition To Evidence

The Core Mathematical Problem

Problem. A generated world is useful only if it keeps the same state under action. Looking plausible at each frame is too weak, because independent plausibility does not prove that the later frame follows from the earlier state and chosen control.

Plain object. Like a toy town where turning the steering wheel should change the same car's next position, the future must follow from this state and this action.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the world state being acted on. What may change is the action and next observation. This turns generation into a conditional claim, not a request for any likely future. Name used later: controlled transition model.

(state[t], action[t]) -> state[t + 1] -> observation[t + 1]

FlashWorld: Keep Interaction In The State

Problem. A prompt-to-video model can change the scene in ways that ignore the user's intervention, because the prompt is not a physical control variable.

Plain object. Like a simulator that remembers which car, road, and action are active, the world state must include the variables an action can change.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the action-sensitive world. What may change is the chosen intervention. The generated continuation depends on the intervention that caused it, so different actions should produce different futures from the same state. Name used later: interactive latent world state.

Failure boundary. Interaction fails when the hidden state does not contain the variables the action actually changes, such as contact, hidden objects, intent, or physical constraints.

VectorWorld: Use State Variables Instead Of Pixels Alone

Problem. Driving and simulation worlds contain lanes, actors, routes, right-of-way, and timing. Pixels show those facts indirectly, often with occlusion and irrelevant texture.

Plain object. Like a traffic map that names cars, lanes, routes, and motion instead of storing only photographs, the state names the facts that constrain driving.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the world relation that controls motion. What may change is the coordinate format. Lane layout can restrict feasible motion; actor state can carry velocity and intent; graph edges can express who can affect whom. Name used later: structured state.

Failure boundary. A graph is only better than pixels if it preserves the relevant world variables. Missing unusual actors, weather effects, construction changes, or hidden intent turns the structured state into a false simplification.

dWorldEval And Control Consistency: Test The Causal Link

Problem. A generated trajectory can match the visual distribution while breaking the action-state relation. It can look like driving while ignoring steering, braking, object persistence, or collision constraints.

Plain object. Like checking whether this steering action could really lead to that next road position, the test links action, state change, and rendered future.

Why it works. What must stay true is that causes constrain effects. What may change is the action and future being checked. This is stronger than frame quality because it asks whether the generated future is an allowed consequence of the control.

Failure boundary. The evaluator must know enough about the transition law. If the check only sees superficial motion, the generator can satisfy the metric while violating the real simulator or task dynamics.

valid if observed future is compatible with allowed transition

OmniVerifier And VideoKR: Verify The Right Projection

Problem. A world can be wrong in many views: visual consistency, instruction satisfaction, object relation, temporal ordering, factual knowledge, or domain meaning. One scalar score rarely sees all of them.

Plain object. Like inspecting a generated scene through several windows, each checker looks at the part where a different failure would show up.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the hidden world fact the task depends on. What may change is which readable view the verifier uses. OmniVerifier checks media and instruction constraints; VideoKR checks whether video reasoning preserves knowledge-rich event state.

Failure boundary. Verification is only as strong as the views it measures. A model can still exploit blind spots if no verifier sees the variable that matters for the downstream decision.

The Grounded World-Model Chain

Across papers. FlashWorld puts intervention into the transition. VectorWorld chooses state coordinates that expose actors and road connectivity. dWorldEval and control-consistency losses test whether actions actually constrain generated futures. OmniVerifier and VideoKR broaden the evidence so visual plausibility cannot hide semantic or instruction failure.

First-principles read. A world model is a claim about lawful change under partial observation. The model must choose a state, update it under action, render observations from it, and then face tests that inspect the same variables the task depends on.

Big-picture consequence. The useful direction is not bigger video alone. It is generation with an accountable transition: the future should be traceable to the prior state and the action, and the verifier should observe the part of the future where failure would matter.

state + action -> transition -> observation -> verifier view
FOCUSED CHAIN

Tail-Sensitive Sampling: Modes, Boundaries, And Selected Outcomes

The Core Mathematical Problem

Problem. Many scientific and safety quantities are controlled by small regions of probability space. Average sample quality can be high while the method misses a rare conformer, crosses an unsafe boundary, underweights an abrupt corruption, or fails to produce one exceptional design in a finite batch.

Plain object. Like checking the few rare cases that decide a safety report, the sampler must preserve probability where the task actually reads it.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the downstream quantity: rare-case chance, boundary crossing, weighted estimate, or best candidate in a batch. What may change is how samples are drawn. The question is not only whether samples look typical; it is whether probability is placed correctly where the task is sensitive.

useful sampling = preserve probability where the task quantity is large or fragile

SRMC: Missing A Mode Is Biased Measurement

Problem. If a rare region contributes strongly to an estimate, a sampler that rarely visits it gives the wrong scientific answer even when common samples are excellent.

Plain object. Like polling only easy-to-reach neighborhoods, the estimate is biased if important regions are rarely visited.

Why it works. What must stay the same is coverage of regions that change the estimate. What may change is the route used to sample them. SRMC treats sampling as measurement: improving coverage reduces bias and doubt, not just visual sameness.

Failure boundary. If the method discovers visually distinct but low-value regions while missing the physically important one, apparent coverage still fails the task.

Safety-Guided Flow: Move Mass Before It Reaches The Boundary

Problem. A generator can be safe on most samples yet place enough probability near unsafe regions that rare failures remain likely under pressure or repeated use.

Plain object. Like redirecting traffic before cars reach a closed road, the sampler moves forming outputs away from unsafe regions early.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the safety boundary. What may change is the path the sample cloud follows. Instead of sampling first and rejecting later, the dynamics changes how mass moves, reducing boundary crossings before final artifacts appear. Name used later: guided flow.

Failure boundary. The boundary model must match real harm. If the unsafe set is misspecified, the flow can steer away from measured danger while entering unmeasured danger.

sample movement = useful direction - boundary push

Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridges: Put Probability On Jumps

Problem. Some observation processes contain abrupt deviations. A standard bell-curve noise law treats large jumps as extremely implausible, which can make the model smooth away the event it must explain.

Plain object. Like allowing a trip log to include sudden detours instead of forcing every route to be smooth, the path model lets jumps be plausible.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the real abrupt event if the data-generating process contains one. What may change is the noise law used for paths. Name used later: heavy-tailed noise.

Failure boundary. Heavy tails help only when abrupt movement is real structure or realistic noise. If the task needs smooth dynamics, they can over-explain ordinary error as jumps.

FALCON: Density Makes Samples Numerically Meaningful

Problem. A molecular sample can look valid while its probability under the equilibrium distribution is unknown, making physical estimates unstable.

Plain object. Like keeping a receipt for how a simple starting point was stretched into a molecular shape, the sampler tracks probability weight during transformation.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the probability weight needed for the scientific estimate. What may change is the generated conformation. Tracking stretching turns samples into weighted evidence, not just opaque candidates. Name used later: Jacobian.

Failure boundary. Inspectable density is useful only if the flow family can represent the target distribution. A wrong density with precise arithmetic is still wrong science.

sample probability changes by how much the transformation stretches local volume

FIDIA: Optimize The Candidate That Deployment Selects

Problem. Protein design often samples many candidates and tests the best one. Training for average likelihood can improve the middle of the distribution while leaving the deployable winner unchanged.

Plain object. Like baking many loaves and serving only the best one, deployment cares about the best candidate in the batch, not the average candidate.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the selection rule used downstream. What may change is the distribution of generated candidates. Best-of-N selection changes the target from mean quality to upper-tail quality under a finite budget. Name used later: best-of-N objective.

Failure boundary. Pushing the upper tail can create brittle candidates if the score is incomplete. The selected best sample is only meaningful when the scoring function sees stability, synthesis, and biological constraints.

deployment value = expected score of the best candidate in the batch

The Tail-Sensitive Sampling Chain

Across papers. SRMC preserves rare cases for estimation. Safety-Guided Flow changes movement near unsafe boundaries. Cauchy-driven bridges choose a noise law that can represent jumps. FALCON makes probability weight inspectable. FIDIA optimizes the selected best sample rather than the average sample.

First-principles read. Sampling is a measurement procedure over possible outputs. The right method depends on which part of the distribution the task reads: common mass, rare cases, boundary events, abrupt corruption, likelihood-weighted expectations, or the best item in a finite batch.

Big-picture consequence. The page's broader theme becomes sharper: generative quality is not one property. A sampler is good only relative to the quantity that will be computed from its samples.

possible outputs -> quantity the task reads -> sampler design -> estimate or selected artifact
