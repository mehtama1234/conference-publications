Research lane

Safety and evaluation as measurement design

This lane asks what kind of evidence should count when models become deployed systems. ICML and ICLR 2026 are not treating evaluation as a scoreboard after the real research is done. They treat measurement as a design choice that shapes what models learn, what failures remain invisible, and what kinds of systems the field is willing to trust.

FIRST PRINCIPLE

Evaluation Is a Control Surface

A benchmark is not a neutral mirror. It tells researchers what to optimize, tells companies what to ship, and tells users what to trust. If the benchmark is static, models can memorize its style. If the score is a single number, models can improve the number while hiding the failure. If the judge is an LLM, the evaluation inherits that judge's blind spots. If rare harms are not sampled, they disappear from development even though they return at deployment scale.

The safety and evaluation papers are therefore about measurement design. They ask what object should be measured: final answer, action trace, executable outcome, internal activation, clinical span, legal source choice, visual evidence re-check, policy recovery path, tail probability, uncertainty interval, or human-verifiable consequence. The deeper argument is that modern AI systems need measurements that see process, pressure, uncertainty, and context rather than only final response quality.

SUBTHEME 01

Benchmark Validity

The central problem is whether the score is measuring the ability it claims to measure.

A modern benchmark can fail even when every example has a correct answer. It can reward shortcuts, collapse several skills into one number, hide score variance, or test an ability that matters only inside the benchmark's artificial format. The strongest papers in this subtheme make the benchmark itself the object of scientific scrutiny: what is the construct, what evidence supports it, how stable is the ranking, and what failures are invisible to the score?

ICLR 2026

FRABench / UFEval

Treats fine-grained multimodal evaluation as an aspect taxonomy problem. Instead of asking whether a judge prefers output A or B overall, it asks whether evaluation criteria can transfer across text, image understanding, image generation, and interleaved generation.

ICML 2026

Micro-Benchmarking Reliability

Asks how small a benchmark can be before pairwise model rankings become unreliable. The concept is not benchmark compression alone; it is confidence in the ordering decision created by a reduced test set.

ICML 2026

ATLAS

Recasts benchmark use as psychometric ability estimation. Item-response theory and Fisher-information item selection replace fixed test administration, allowing fewer questions while estimating latent model ability more directly than raw accuracy.

ICML 2026

Prescriptive Scaling

Turns evaluation into a compute-to-capability frontier. Instead of reporting one model's score, it estimates high-quantile downstream capability boundaries as a function of pretraining FLOPs under current post-training practice.

SUBTHEME 02

Judge Reliability

A judge is a measurement instrument, not ground truth.

The field increasingly uses LLMs, multimodal models, expert panels, and hybrid evaluators to score open-ended outputs. That creates a second-order evaluation problem: how do we know the judge is reliable? These papers focus on calibration, aspect generalization, disagreement, rationale quality, and situations where judges are especially likely to overrate plausible but unsafe answers.

ICLR 2026

WebDevJudge

Evaluates LLM, multimodal, and agentic judges on web implementation comparisons. The web page is a difficult object because correctness includes layout, interaction, responsiveness, and functional behavior that source-only judging can miss.

ICLR 2026

CounselBench

Uses mental-health professionals to evaluate open-ended counseling responses. The paper exposes a core judge failure: models and LLM judges can reward comforting language while missing unsafe clinical advice or boundary violations.

ICML 2026

Pressure Reveals Character

Validates an LLM judge against human annotations while evaluating multi-turn pressure scenarios. The key measurement problem is not only whether a model refuses bad behavior, but whether scoring rationales remain faithful under honesty, safety, and manipulation pressure.

ICML 2026

AGREE

Shows that overall aesthetic judgments can hide conflicts between underlying attributes. It treats subjective scoring as multidimensional measurement rather than one scalar preference.

SUBTHEME 03

High-Stakes Evaluation

High-stakes tasks require measuring the constraints around the answer.

In medicine, law, education, finance, and compliance-heavy workflows, a useful answer can still be unacceptable if it violates role boundaries, source rules, privacy constraints, or professional standards. The common move in this subtheme is to evaluate behavior inside the pressure of the domain rather than treating the domain as a source of prompts.

ICML 2026

Copyright-Bench

Evaluates whether agents select lawful sources in realistic commercial workflows. Its important variable is pressure: user preferences and time constraints can push models away from compliant behavior even when the rule is known.

ICML 2026

Seizure-Semiology-Suite

Tests medical temporal-report faithfulness in seizure videos. The measurement target is not generic video understanding but laterality, symptom sequence, temporal localization, and clinically meaningful reporting.

ICML 2026

DR Tulu

Evaluates long-form research-agent answers across science, healthcare, and general benchmarks. Its focus is attributed research behavior: whether a model can produce sustained, sourced, domain-sensitive work rather than a short answer.

ICML 2026

M-CBE

Studies the design space between accuracy and interpretability using linear and symbolic expert functions. In high-stakes decisions, a correct prediction may be insufficient if the reason cannot be understood or governed.

SUBTHEME 04

Adversarial Safety

Safety measurement has to assume the model is being pressured, attacked, or bypassed.

Static refusal tests are too weak for deployed systems. Attacks can appear through hidden text channels, multimodal inconsistencies, fine-tuning access, linguistic triggers, or internal harmful features that are not visible in the prompt. The papers here move safety evaluation from checking a final answer toward monitoring internal state, attack diversity, and strategic interaction.

ICML 2026

Jailbreak Foundry

Turns jailbreak papers into executable modules in a shared harness. Its contribution is not just another attack list; it is infrastructure for making red-team evaluation reproducible and extensible.

ICML 2026

AdvGame

Frames attacker and defender models as a non-zero-sum game. Instead of testing a frozen defense against a frozen attack set, it co-evolves both sides so safety training faces adaptive pressure.

ICML 2026

Activation Oracles

Uses internal activations to detect malign propensities absent from visible text. The measurement object is hidden computation, not surface content.

ICML 2026

FlowGuard

Detects multimodal jailbreaks by tracking redundancy, synergy, and modality dominance between unimodal and fused predictions. It treats abnormal cross-modal information flow as the safety signal.

ICML 2026

Robust Harmful Features

Finds safety-relevant attention heads and activation features that remain informative under jailbreaks. The idea is to monitor robust internal features when visible refusals can be manipulated.

ICLR 2026

Invisible Safety Threat

Shows that malicious fine-tuning can hide harmful prompts and responses inside benign cover text. The failure is a monitoring failure: surface classifiers see safe stegotext while decoded interactions remain unsafe.

ICML 2026

Trojan-Speak

Treats fine-tuning access as a safety attack surface. It shows adversarial fine-tuning can preserve reasoning capability while bypassing content classifiers, and that activation probes can reveal harmful internal representations.

ICML 2026

Stable-GFN

Uses GFlowNet training to find diverse effective red-team attacks. Pairwise comparison, noisy-reward masking, and fluency stabilization turn attack generation into a distributional search problem instead of a single best jailbreak.

ICML 2026

Consistent Adversarial Attacks

Separates label-preserving vulnerability from finite-data or expressivity artifacts. The paper asks whether adversarial vulnerability remains a real property when the perturbation preserves the underlying label.

ICML 2026

SecFid

Frames prompt-injection defense as a frontier between security and fidelity. The evaluation separates executing injected text, ignoring it, and faithfully processing benign spans, so defenses are judged by their deployment cost as well as blocked attacks.

ICLR 2026

SGF

Recasts safety guidance for diffusion and flow models as energy-based negative guidance against unsafe samples. MMD potentials and barrier analysis identify the early denoising window where intervention most changes the final sample.

SUBTHEME 05

Uncertainty, Calibration, and Tail Risk

Deployment risk often lives in probability mass the benchmark barely samples.

Many harmful behaviors are rare per query but common at platform scale. Other failures come from overconfident wrong answers, reward models that are uncertain in hidden ways, or calibration procedures that fail under online shift. These papers treat uncertainty as a first-class output of evaluation, not an appendix after the score.

ICML 2026

Rare Event Analysis of LLMs

Defines rare-event analysis for model behavior through event setup, probability estimation, and structure exploration. The key move is to estimate tail probabilities with error analysis instead of waiting for rare harms to appear in ordinary samples.

ICML 2026

Tail Risks in LM Outputs

Uses importance sampling from unsafe model variants to estimate low-probability harmful-output rates. It is a deployment-scale answer to events around one in ten thousand queries.

ICML 2026

Know More Know Clearer

Aligns subjective certainty with objective accuracy. The paper targets the practical failure where a model's confidence presentation diverges from the evidence supporting its answer.

ICML 2026

UP-OCP

Reduces online conformal prediction to universal portfolio selection. The purpose is finite-time miscoverage control without fragile manual tuning under sequential distribution shift.

ICML 2026

BNRM

Uses sparse non-negative latent factors and Bayesian reward modeling to reduce reward over-optimization. It treats reward uncertainty and factor interpretability as defenses against reward hacking.

ICML 2026

Binary RAR

Uses a binary retrieval-augmented factuality reward: a response is rewarded only if it contains no contradictions with retrieved evidence. The design avoids the generic-claim reward hacking that can appear with continuous factuality scores.

ICML 2026

Reward Redistribution for CVaR MDPs

Reformulates static CVaR reinforcement learning with dense per-step rewards and a contracting Bellman operator. Tail-risk safety becomes trainable through dynamic programming rather than a sparse terminal penalty.

SUBTHEME 06

Executable Code, Security, and Agent Evaluation

For agents and code systems, evidence should often be executable.

Some capabilities cannot be graded by reading the model's explanation. A security agent should produce an exploit or patch evidence. A compiler optimizer should preserve semantics and improve runtime. A UI or code-edit agent should act in context. The papers in this subtheme turn evaluation into an environment with state, actions, tools, and verifiers, even when the final product is a research benchmark rather than a deployed system.

ICLR 2026

CyberGym

Validates generated proof-of-concept tests against pre-patch and post-patch code versions. The score is not whether the model describes a vulnerability; it is whether executable evidence distinguishes vulnerable from fixed code.

ICML 2026

SandboxEscapeBench

Measures whether an agent can escape nested VM/container sandboxes and reach protected host evidence. The benchmark makes the security boundary itself the object under test.

ICML 2026

CVE-Factory

Builds LiveCVEBench and more than a thousand executable training environments for code-security agents. It makes security evaluation live enough to avoid relying only on stale examples.

ICML 2026

CIRBench

Combines compiler verifier checks, equivalence checking, and runtime measurement for LLVM IR optimization. The correct answer must preserve semantics and produce useful performance change.

ICML 2026

DRPBench

Benchmarks data-race prediction over concurrent programs. The object is program semantics across possible interleavings, not local code style.

ICML 2026

Harnessing Non-Adversarial Robustness

Studies robustness to semantically neutral prompt changes rather than only adversarial strings. It identifies perturbation-induced module bias and gives per-example and population-level certification framings for random prompt perturbations.

SUBTHEME 07

Multimodal Grounding

Multimodal evaluation asks whether the model uses the evidence channel it claims to use.

A vision-language model can answer from priors, text context, or earlier conversational assumptions without actually grounding in the current image or video. Multimodal evaluation therefore needs interventions that separate seeing from guessing: swapped images, situated egocentric views, perceptual attributes, emotion dimensions, visual outcome verification, and medical temporal reports.

ICLR 2026

OmniVerifier

Builds a universal visual verification benchmark and verifier across generation, editing, and visual reasoning tasks. It treats verification as reusable infrastructure for visual outcomes.

ICML 2026

VISUALSWAP

Swaps visually similar but semantically different images to test whether VLMs genuinely re-check visual evidence. It directly attacks conversational inertia.

ICML 2026

UniPercept

Defines perceptual image understanding across aesthetics, quality, structure, and texture. It broadens image evaluation beyond object recognition into perceptual judgment.

ICML 2026

SAW-Bench

Evaluates observer-centric spatial reasoning from smart-glasses videos. The key measurement target is situated awareness from the viewer's physical perspective.

ICML 2026

EEmo-Logic

Builds fine-grained image-evoked emotion assessment across five dimensions. It treats affective perception as structured reasoning rather than a single sentiment label.

ICML 2026

DOUBT

Separates object recognition from relational reasoning before scoring multimodal hallucination trustworthiness. Its von Mises-Fisher trust metric is a way to avoid unstable small-sample semantic-entropy estimates.

ICML 2026

Geometric Flow Grounding

Uses manifold-flow projection residuals as zero-shot signals for deepfake video detection. The safety signal is whether a video follows learned geometric dynamics rather than merely whether frames look plausible.

ICML 2026

DGS-Net

Fine-tunes CLIP for AI-generated image detection while preserving transferable priors through distillation-guided gradient decomposition. It targets cross-generator generalization rather than detector success on one generator family.

SUBTHEME 08

Robotics and Embodied Evaluation

Embodied models have to be measured through memory, state, and action over time.

Robotics and agent evaluation are hard because the model's answer changes the state it will later observe. Success depends on temporal memory, spatial memory, procedural memory, user coordination, and the ability to rank policies without always running expensive real-world rollouts. These papers define evaluation around trajectories and state evolution rather than isolated perception.

ICML 2026

RoboMME

Benchmarks memory-augmented VLA variants across temporal, spatial, object, and procedural memory. Its main claim is that memory representation effectiveness is task-dependent.

ICML 2026

dWorldEval

Ranks robot policies by coupling future observation generation with progress-as-text success indicators. It evaluates action-centric world models as policy-assessment tools.

ICML 2026

tau2-bench

Tests conversational agents in a shared Telecom Dec-POMDP where user and AI both use tools. The benchmark separates reasoning failures from communication and user-guidance failures.

ICML 2026

MemoryBench

Benchmarks service-time LLM memory through accumulated simulated user feedback. The object is not recall of a static context window but continual learning from interaction history.

SUBTHEME 09

Privacy, Unlearning, and Formal Guarantees

Some safety claims need guarantees, not only empirical rates.

Privacy, unlearning, robustness, and fairness often require stronger evidence than average-case benchmark performance. The common mathematical move is to define the property as a constraint over distributions, mechanisms, output configurations, or deletion events, then prove or verify when the system satisfies it.

ICLR 2026

Gaussian Certified Unlearning

Introduces epsilon-Gaussian certifiability and studies deletion through one Newton step plus calibrated Gaussian noise. The core object is a prediction-level guarantee after removing training data.

ICML 2026

High-Dimensional Gaussian Mechanism

Proves when Gaussian additive noise is asymptotically optimal for differential privacy and when other radial mechanisms may improve finite-regime utility.

ICML 2026

OC-space

Frames robustness and fairness verification for tree ensembles as search over output configurations. It unifies multiple property checks through one verification space.

ICML 2026

CSPO

Evaluates safe RL recovery through time-to-safety, violation frequency, and reward preservation. The safety property is dynamic behavior near a constraint boundary, not final return alone.

ICML 2026

Catch-22

Frames watermarking as a trilemma among robustness to edits, stealth from keyless observers, and reliable verification. Its information-theoretic analysis shows why provenance signals can become distinguishable as they become robust.

ICLR 2026

Spherical Watermark

Embeds watermark bits into diffusion latent Gaussian noise while preserving visual quality and the latent prior. The mathematical object is a distribution-preserving transformation of latent noise for media provenance.

ICML 2026

SlaClip

Uses slack already induced by DP-SGD clipping to adapt thresholds without extra private gradient-statistic queries. It keeps the same privacy accountant while improving utility through better clipping control.

ICML 2026

Biased Generalization

Shows that diffusion test loss can improve while generated samples become unusually close to training data. It challenges test-loss early stopping as a privacy-safe metric for generative models.

ICML 2026

GEM

Uses contrastive velocity matching to erase unwanted concepts in Rectified Flow models. It treats generative unlearning as trajectory shaping, bridging teacher-guided erasure with flow-matching geometry.

ICML 2026

Concept Removal Guidance

Estimates unwanted concept presence from diffusion noise predictions and adaptively calibrates negative guidance during denoising. It suppresses concepts without fine-tuning or external classifiers while preserving benign fidelity.

SUBTHEME 10

Scaling, Transfer, and Measurement Economics

Evaluation also has a budget and a transfer problem.

Large model evaluation is expensive. Human labels are expensive. New tasks arrive faster than full benchmark suites can be built. These papers ask how much evidence is needed, how public benchmarks transfer to new tasks, how subjective constructs should be measured, and how to estimate capability frontiers without exhaustively testing every model on every task.

ICML 2026

Hedging on the Frontier

Uses public benchmark dominance relations as side information for few-sample new-task learning. It treats benchmark tradeoffs as geometry that can guide model selection under sparse evidence.

ICML 2026

VALUEFLOW

Uses repeated rankings and Plackett-Luce aggregation to estimate value intensity. It avoids direct scalar ratings for subjective constructs where people may not share a stable numeric scale.

ICML 2026

Ranking Time Series

Replaces noisy absolute time-series labels with pairwise comparisons and ideal-point modeling. The measurement insight is that relative judgments can be more reliable than unstable direct labels.

ICML 2026

Consequence-Based Utility

Scores hard math solutions by downstream consequences when direct verification is unavailable. Evaluation becomes a question of whether an output improves related verifiable work.

ICML 2026

Surgery

Uses attention-sink divergence regularization to reduce harmful pattern learning during fine-tuning. It is measurement economics at the internal level: a cheap mechanistic signal stands in for expensive post-hoc safety discovery.

ICML 2026

Buffer-and-Reinforce

Protects refusal behavior during fine-tuning-as-a-service adaptation with BufferLoRA and ReinforceLoRA. The safety object is the gradient path during user adaptation, not only the final adapted model.
