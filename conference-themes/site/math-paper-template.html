FIRST PRINCIPLE

A Paper Entry Should Preserve The Claim

The problem with a weak paper summary is that it compresses away the thing that makes the method mathematically meaningful. Saying "better benchmark", "new agent", "efficient training", or "robust generation" does not say what changed, what stayed fixed, what evidence identifies the claim, or what failure remains possible.

The template below forces one discipline: before describing the approach, explain the thing whose behavior must be tracked. Start with the everyday version: a saved work record, a boundary the agent must not cross, a pile of possible explanations, a route through time, or the real artifact being judged. Any later math name should come only after the plain object is clear.

WORKED FORMS

How The Template Changes By Theme

Agents

Trace As Controlled Evidence

Problem. A final answer can be right for the wrong reason, or long because the policy learned a template.

Object. Like a lab notebook for work: what the system saw, what it tried, what tools it used, what changed, and what the checker said.

Why it works. The wording, tool order, and number of steps may change. What must stay the same is dependence on the actual question and evidence that each expensive step helped. RAGEN-2, h1, Skill-Pro, CyberGym, ParetoPO, and OMAC differ in implementation, but each treats reasoning as a watched process rather than a static answer. Formal label if needed: trajectory, or trace.

a trace is useful when it changes with the real input and its extra cost buys a better outcome

Evidence check. Perturb the input, measure trace dependence, tool-call marginal value, verifier agreement, and whether reusable skills preserve correctness outside their source episode.

Evaluation

Benchmark As Instrument

Problem. A score can confuse ability with item choice, judge error, shortcut behavior, or sampling noise.

Object. Like a thermometer for a hidden skill: the score is only the reading, not the skill itself.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the capability being claimed. What may change is which question, judge, artifact, or domain evidence produces the reading. ATLAS, FRABench, UFEval, WebDevJudge, CounselBench, MADQA, and domain-specific suites improve evaluation by matching the test evidence to the capability claim. Formal label if needed: measurement channel.

an observed score mixes real capability, sampled-item luck, judge bias, and shortcut artifacts

Evidence check. Report uncertainty, component scores, judge calibration, artifact-native checks, ranking reversals, and domain-expert disagreement.

Safety

Risk As Boundary And Tail Mass

Problem. Average behavior can be safe while rare or pressured behavior crosses the line.

Object. Like marking a forbidden zone on a map, the object is the bad event and the line the system must not cross.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the safety rule. What may change is the prompt, attack, pressure, or route by which the system approaches the line. CSPO, Rare Event Analysis, SandboxEscapeBench, Jailbreak Foundry, AdvGame, FlowGuard, and watermarking papers make the forbidden region explicit instead of assuming random tests find it. Formal labels if needed: harmful event set, feasible boundary, and adversarial game.

risk means the chance of a named bad event, or whether useful behavior stays inside the safety boundary

Evidence check. Estimate tail probabilities, boundary distance, adaptive attack success, false positives, robustness to paraphrase or editing, and utility loss.

Data / Systems

Data As Parameter Movement

Problem. Examples are useful only relative to current model state, training time, hardware limits, and target distribution.

Object. Like one lesson given to a student at a specific moment, the object is the push that example gives the model when the model sees it.

Why it works. What must stay the same is useful deployed behavior. What may change is when the example arrives, how much compute it costs, what memory is kept, and how much the model is still able to move. Midtraining Bridges, DiReCT, Sequential Data Values, OPUS, Common Corpus, ThinKV, LiftQuant, RePAIR, and sparse/IO-aware systems ask what information survives the path from data to deployed function. Formal label if needed: marginal update.

an example's value depends on useful target gain, damage to other behavior, compute cost, and source-record cost at that training moment

Evidence check. Measure timing sensitivity, useful training push, cache sufficiency, compression error, source records, data duplication, and real hardware behavior.

Physical / Generative

Generation As Native-State Sampling

Problem. A generated artifact can look plausible while violating the space where the object actually lives.

Object. Like building a molecule, video, robot move, or weather field using the rules of that object, not just using pixels or tokens that look right.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the real validity rule: same molecule under rotation, valid route, physical residual, feasible body movement, or consistent world state. What may change is the coordinate frame, sample path, rendering, or candidate design. Quotient-Space Diffusion, Protein Autoregressive Modeling, FALCON, IRNO, TideGS, FlashWorld, HDFlow, and molecule-design papers choose the native object before optimizing the sample.

generate in the object's real space while preserving same-object rules and physical or chemical constraints

Evidence check. Test same-object behavior, route validity, field residuals, temporal consistency, rare-mode coverage, and verifier projections in the native artifact.

Theory / Optimization

Theorem As A Budget Statement

Problem. A method may work only under hidden budgets: how many directions may move, how sharp the local surface is, how much precision remains, how many examples are available, or how long regret can last.

Object. Like a recipe that works only with enough heat, time, and ingredients, the object is the budget that makes the promise true.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the claim being guaranteed. What may change is the rank, step size, precision, sample size, time horizon, or visible signal strength. Adam Degeneracy, LoRA theory, Alignment-Sensitive Minimax Rates, Single-Head Attention, Transformer Circuits, Language Generation in the Limit, and finite-certification papers explain when a recipe is allowed to succeed.

claim holds only when signal, samples, precision, rank, or time exceed the relevant threshold

Evidence check. State assumptions, show threshold behavior, separate what can be represented from what can be learned, and test whether deployment preserves the promised regime.

Causality / Multimodal

Claim As Identified Evidence

Problem. Correlation, fluent explanation, or fused modality output can overstate what the evidence distinguishes.

Object. Like a detective keeping all possible stories that still fit the clues, the object is the claim that every surviving story agrees on.

Why it works. What must stay the same is agreement across the evidence-supported stories. What may change is the graph, hidden state, modality swap, or causal route. Local Covariate Selection, DiCoLa, Distributional Equivalence, CausalGame, PRISM, Mind-Omni, VISUALSWAP, MINT, and DLMR restrict claims to what interventions, swaps, graph assumptions, or measurement channels can support. Formal labels if needed: identified effect, equivalent story set, and sufficient hidden state.

a claim is valid only when every still-possible story allowed by the evidence agrees on it

Evidence check. Report adjustment validity, still-possible story groups, intervention response, modality swaps, remove-one-memory tests, expert labels, and remaining alternatives the evidence cannot separate.

STYLE RULE

Write The Object Before The Method

The atlas should avoid paper prose that starts with a method name and then tries to infer why it matters. The better order is: problem, object, what must stay the same, allowed movement, method, evidence, boundary, connection. This keeps the writing conceptual without becoming vague.

The result should let a reader compare papers across conferences by the object being controlled. A benchmark paper and a causal-discovery paper may both be about measurement validity. A diffusion paper and a safety paper may both be about tail mass. A tokenizer paper and a video-compression paper may both be about sufficient information under a fixed budget. The template makes those shared problems visible.
