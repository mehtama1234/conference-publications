This Is The Claim-Check Layer

A paper can name the right object and still fail to show that this object caused the improvement. This audit asks the stricter question: what promise is the method making, and what observation would prove that promise instead of only making the result look good?

Use this page when expanding lane cards. If a method says a score measures skill, it needs doubt ranges and calibration. If it says outputs stay inside an allowed region, it needs boundary cases. If it says signal is visible in model directions, it needs stable directions that actually predict the target.

BIG PICTURE

What This Audit Adds

The table turns "why it works" into an evidence demand. A paper that treats wrapper changes as irrelevant needs same-object tests. A paper that studies rare bad cases needs stress sampling and doubt ranges. A paper that changes model weights needs movement evidence under the right ruler. A paper that makes a cause claim needs explicit assumptions and unresolved alternatives.

That gives the atlas a stronger standard for future expansion: every paper card should state not only the object, but also the plain promise it makes and what observation would make that promise credible.
