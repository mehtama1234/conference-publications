Efficient adaptation through representation bridges

The problem is not simply making training cheaper. In scientific and healthcare domains, adaptation is often blocked by scarce labels, expensive sensors, privacy limits, and large foundation models that are too costly to duplicate. BioX-Bridge asks whether a small bridge between existing representations can transfer useful knowledge across biosignal modalities.

FIRST PRINCIPLES

Why efficient adaptation matters

A foundation model can encode useful structure, but real deployment rarely asks for the exact same modality, sensor, task, or population that the model was trained on. In biosignals, the same physiological state may appear through different measurements: signals can differ in cost, comfort, fidelity, sampling conditions, and label availability. The adaptation question is therefore not "can we train another large model?" It is "can we reuse what an existing model knows when the new modality has little labeled data?"

Classical teacher-student distillation can transfer knowledge, but it often requires running a large teacher during training. That is expensive in compute and memory, especially when the teacher is a modern foundation model. BioX-Bridge explores a more local idea: connect intermediate representation spaces with a lightweight bridge network. If two biosignal modalities reflect related physiology, useful information may not need to pass through final outputs. It can pass through a carefully chosen internal alignment point.

Modality gap

The source and target signals may measure related body state through different sensors, costs, and noise patterns.

Label scarcity

The target modality may not have enough labeled data to train a strong task model from scratch.

Bridge point

The useful transfer surface is an intermediate representation, not necessarily the teacher's final prediction or the student's full parameter set.

SUBTHEME

Lightweight cross-modal bridges

BioX-Bridge transfers biosignal knowledge by aligning model interiors

biosignal transfer

BioX-Bridge starts from the fact that biosignal modalities are heterogeneous but physiologically coupled. A wearable signal, a clinical sensor, or another physiological stream may differ in convenience and quality, yet still reflect overlapping human-body state. That opens the door to cross-modal transfer: use a stronger or better-supported modality to help a weaker or less-labeled modality. The paper's method avoids heavy teacher-student distillation by training a lightweight bridge network that aligns intermediate representations across foundation models and modalities. It also introduces a strategy for selecting where inside the models the bridge should be built, because the alignment location determines whether the transferred information is still too raw, too task-specific, or just right for reuse.

The reported result is the main efficiency claim: across multiple biosignal modalities, tasks, and datasets, BioX-Bridge reduces trainable parameters by 88-99 percent while maintaining or improving transfer performance relative to state-of-the-art methods. The first-principles lesson is that adaptation cost can fall sharply when the system identifies the correct interface. Instead of moving all weights, labels, or outputs, the method moves information through a compact connector between representation spaces. This is especially relevant in healthcare, where accessibility and usability depend on adapting to cheaper, more comfortable, or more available sensing modalities without rebuilding the full stack.

FIELD READ

What this means for benchmarks and gyms

An efficient-adaptation benchmark should record more than final accuracy. It should name the source modality, target modality, available labels, frozen components, trainable bridge or adapter, alignment positions, parameter budget, memory budget, and transfer metric. Without those fields, a benchmark cannot tell whether a system adapted efficiently, merely used a larger hidden teacher, or shifted cost into an unreported component.

For our agent-data normalization work, BioX-Bridge is a reminder that not every gym is about tool actions in a browser or codebase. Some gyms are about controlled adaptation actions over model components. The action may be choosing an alignment layer, training a bridge, freezing a backbone, or evaluating transfer under sensor shift. The verifier needs to check both task performance and resource use, because efficiency is part of the scientific claim.
