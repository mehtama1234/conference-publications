Scientific and physical-domain generation

The central problem is generating objects that obey the world, not just the training distribution. A molecule, protein, PDE field, weather state, robot trajectory, traffic world, CAD object, or physical video can look plausible and still be scientifically useless if it violates constraints the experiment cares about.

Problem

Scientific generation is not ordinary content generation. The sample is valuable only if it satisfies geometry, dynamics, energy, causality, measurement, synthesizability, safety, or downstream objective constraints.

Approaches

The papers use quotient spaces, equivariance, coarse-to-fine construction, diffusion and flow models, learned PDE solvers, inverse-problem priors, active sensing, scientific benchmarks, and domain-specific verifiers.

Connecting read

The shared ambition is to make generative models into scientific instruments: propose candidates, reduce search, respect constraints, and expose uncertainty before a real experiment, simulation, or deployment spends the expensive budget.

READ

The Big Picture

Scientific and physical-domain generation is where generative AI has to leave the world of plausible artifacts and enter the world of accountable candidates. A generated protein must fold and bind. A molecule must be synthesizable, stable, and useful. A PDE forecast must respect dynamics over time. A weather latent must preserve enough pressure-variable structure to support downstream forecasts. A driving world model must remain closed-loop stable when agents react. A physical video must obey motion and contact, not only look convincing frame by frame. In these domains, the model is not making media for inspection; it is proposing something that may drive an experiment, simulation, design loop, or control policy.

That changes the role of generation. The useful model is not the one that merely matches the training distribution. It is the one that narrows a hard search space while preserving the constraints that make a candidate real. The papers on this page attack that from several directions: bake symmetries into the state space, split coarse placement from fine structure, learn solvers that carry physical laws, use diffusion and flow as samplers over energy landscapes, build benchmarks that punish physical inconsistency, and add active sensing or uncertainty when the model cannot know enough from passive data. The connecting question is: can generation reduce expensive scientific search without laundering invalid guesses into confident outputs?

STRUCTURE

What Makes A Scientific Sample Useful

Constraint Satisfaction

The generated object has to land inside a feasible set: physical geometry, safety boundaries, equality constraints, synthesizable chemistry, valid CAD, or stable dynamics.

Search Reduction

The model should shrink a large candidate space before expensive simulation or experiment. Protein binders, molecular docking, retrosynthesis, and black-box optimization all live here.

Dynamics

Scientific fields and physical worlds evolve. PDEs, weather, wavefunctions, driving scenarios, sensor fields, and robot contacts require temporal consistency rather than isolated samples.

Measurement

Inverse problems and data assimilation ask what hidden state explains limited observations. The model must respect the measurement process instead of hallucinating a pretty reconstruction.

Uncertainty

Scientific outputs often guide action under incomplete evidence. Mode coverage, hypothesis coverage, rare-disease pathways, posterior hypergraphs, and credible alternatives matter.

Evaluation

A scientific generator needs domain checks: nonredundant benchmarks, physical video tests, paired measured/simulated PDE data, docking speed, synthesis objectives, or human/MLLM physical consistency ratings.

CLUSTERS

The Main Research Moves

1. Physical validity starts with the right state space

Several papers make the state space itself carry scientific structure. Quotient-Space Diffusion Model models scientific structures on quotient spaces so rigid-body symmetries do not have to be relearned from data. Efficient Diffusion via Landing treats feasible-set generation as the central object, aiming at molecular, robotics, and engineering domains where satisfying constraints is not optional. Manifold Perturbations perturbs equality-constrained distributions while retaining intrinsic geometry, and Geometric Flow Grounding keeps learned dynamics on the tangent bundle of the data manifold. ReViT enforces discrete rotational equivariance for PDE fields. The shared point is that scientific generation improves when impossible degrees of freedom are removed before sampling begins.

2. Molecules and proteins require generation plus selection

Protein and molecular design papers treat generation as one step inside a search loop. Protein Autoregressive Modeling generates protein backbones coarse-to-fine and decodes them with a flow model, supporting motif and conditional generation. Complexa builds an atomistic binder prior from synthetic binder-target pretraining and experimental multimers. Chamaileon models sequence and structure jointly across target contexts so one binder can work across states. FIDIA optimizes inverse-folding policies for Best-of-N high-fitness selection, making inference-time candidate ranking part of the design objective. Quantized Consistency Docking compresses co-folding diffusion into a sub-second docking primitive. FLIP2 and TEDBench/MiAE provide the necessary caution: protein benchmarks must test real generalization and avoid leakage, because a generator that exploits redundancy is not a scientific instrument.

3. Chemistry needs synthesizable paths, not isolated molecules

Chemical generation has to respect how objects can actually be made. ReaSyn projects molecules into synthesizable space through bidirectional synthetic-tree generation and discrete flow edits. mCLM tokenizes molecules into synthesis-compatible functional building blocks paired with natural-language function descriptions, making the language of chemistry closer to the process of making compounds. MORetro* recovers Pareto fronts for retrosynthesis over cost, sustainability, toxicity, yield, and user-defined criteria. Insertion Process argues that left-to-right construction is often the wrong order for molecule and planning domains. TD3B designs allosteric binders by optimizing transition direction, so the molecule is judged by induced behavior rather than static resemblance.

4. PDEs and physical fields need solvers that remember dynamics

The PDE and field-modeling papers turn generation into numerical modeling. Walrus pretrains a Transformer over 19 fluid-like physical scenarios, aiming at a foundation model for continuum dynamics. Modified SINNs combines spectral-domain PINNs, coefficient decay scaling, and basis embeddings for high-dimensional PDEs. LoRFS represents high-dimensional PDE solutions as separable Fourier sums with analytic physics losses. Flowers builds PDE solvers from learned feature warps rather than Fourier, attention, or convolutional mixing. MERLIN learns observation functionals with near-linear progression for resolution-free reconstruction, and CoEvol-NO keeps a persistent latent state while co-evolving mesh coordinates through predictor-corrector updates. These are not generic generators; they are learned solvers whose errors accumulate over physical time.

5. Sampling methods must cover modes without exploding cost

Scientific targets are often unnormalized, multimodal, and expensive to sample. Autoregressive Boltzmann Generators replace flow-based Boltzmann proposals with exact-likelihood autoregressive proposals for molecular equilibrium sampling. FALCON pressures continuous flows toward invertibility so Boltzmann-generator likelihoods become practical in few steps. Reinforced SMC connects maximum-entropy RL samplers with sequential Monte Carlo proposals and twist functions. SRMC improves mode coverage and estimator variance with constant memory. Flow Sampling learns samplers for unnormalized densities from energy-constructed denoising drifts. High-Accuracy Sampling and Dimension-Free Diffusion Sampling clarify when diffusion-style sampling can reach high accuracy without paying an explicit ambient-dimension tax.

6. Inverse problems need measurement-aware priors

Inverse problems are where a generator is most tempted to hallucinate. Weak Diffusion Priors shows mismatched priors can still reconstruct well when measurements are sufficiently informative, clarifying when the data dominates the prior. Lottery Prior uses lightweight random-network compression priors and ensemble refinement for zero-shot inverse problems. FFDP scales multimodal biomedical registration by adapting distributed systems to convolution-heavy pipelines. Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridges uses heavy-tailed bridge models for robust biomedical signal restoration. EgoTactile adapts video diffusion priors with physical rectification to infer hidden grasp pressure. Prism handles the biological version: multimodal epigenomic integration needs proximal signals and backdoor adjustment, not just correlation-rich fusion.

7. Scientific world models support sensing, forecasting, and control

Some work uses generation to decide what to observe or how to act next. LASER combines a latent world model with a POMDP policy to move sensors toward high-information regions of a continuum field. Generative Filtering scales data assimilation for nonlinear chaotic and atmospheric dynamics by reusing generative system models. WLA/ERA5-Latent compresses full pressure-variable weather data from hundreds of terabytes to a much smaller latent space. OSM+ provides city-scale traffic prediction and policy-control datasets, while VectorWorld generates streaming vector-graph driving worlds for real-time closed-loop autonomous simulation. In these papers, generation is useful because it supports decisions under evolving physical state.

8. Scientific hypotheses and uncertainty need coverage, not just one answer

Scientific search often has many admissible explanations. HypoSpace shows that models can stay valid while collapsing coverage as hypothesis spaces grow combinatorial, which is dangerous when the goal is exploration rather than a single plausible answer. Risk Hypergraphs uses posterior hypergraph inference for calibrated pathway uncertainty in rare disease estimation. SI-VAE applies spatially informed autoencoders to protein organization in human cells, supporting downstream statistical analysis rather than only reconstruction. FacRNN factorizes neural population dynamics into independent low-rank groups for interpretability. Information Shapes Koopman Representation balances mutual information and von Neumann entropy so latent dynamics do not collapse while preserving diverse modes.

9. Physical media generation needs physics-aware evaluation

Several papers sit between media generation and physical simulation. PhyWorldBench tests whether generated videos obey physical principles, using humans and zero-shot MLLM evaluation rather than relying on photorealism. PanoWorld-X uses spherical attention and panoramic representation to generate explorable 360-degree scene videos. Active Mind Avatars fuses audio, image, and text with a pseudo last frame to keep avatar motion semantically coherent. RadioGS supervises unobserved-view radiance by matching Gaussian primitive radiance against physically based renders. cadrille generates editable Python CAD code from point clouds, images, or text, which matters because engineering output must be inspectable and editable, not only rendered. Motive improves motion smoothness and physical plausibility by curating high-influence video clips.
