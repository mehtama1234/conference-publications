READ FIRST

The Formula Is A Boundary

In this atlas, a formula usually says where a claim begins and ends. It states what is optimized, what is preserved, what is allowed, what is measured, or what uncertainty remains. The formula is not meant to make the prose look technical; it is a compact way to prevent vague claims.

Read each equation as a contract: if the assumption is true and the evidence checks the right quantity, the approach has a mathematical reason to work. If not, the formula marks the failure boundary.

Constrained Objective
Best allowed action, not best action anywhere.
State Transition
The next state must preserve task information.
Sufficient Statistic
Compress history without changing the target.
Measurement Channel
Observed score as noisy evidence.
Tail Probability
Rare event estimation under deployment.
Transport Path
Moving probability mass through time.
Spectral Gap
Signal direction visible above noise.

FORM 01

Constrained Objective

Optimization Only Means Something After The Allowed Set Is Named

maximize reward(x) subject to x in C, or minimize cost(x) subject to g_k(x) <= 0

Plain read. The system is not free to choose any high-scoring output. It must choose inside the set C: legal actions, safe policies, chemically valid molecules, feasible robot motions, fair couplings, or robust decisions under missing covariates.

Papers

CSPO, Pi-net, Copyright-Bench, Fair OT, ReaSyn, MORetro*, Robust Contextual Optimization, PACT, HDFlow.

Why It Works

The optimizer no longer learns to put mass on invalid regions and hope a filter fixes it later.

Evidence

Violation rate, margin to boundary, objective-feasibility frontier, and stress tests near the constraint.

FORM 02

State Transition

The Path Matters Because Errors Propagate

state_{t+1} = F(state_t, action_t, observation_t, noise_t)

Plain read. The method is about how the system changes, not just where it ends. Reasoning traces, robot motions, world models, PDE solvers, samplers, and agent workflows all depend on whether each state keeps enough information for the next step.

Papers

RAGEN-2, tau2-bench, HDFlow, IRNO, FlashWorld, VectorWorld, Mamba Markov ICL, Thinking in Flow.

Why It Works

Once transition is explicit, drift, collapse, stale memory, and residual error become measurable instead of appearing as isolated mistakes.

Evidence

Stepwise ablations, long-horizon drift, residual reduction, state perturbations, and final-state sensitivity.

FORM 03

Sufficient Statistic

A Compressed State Is Valid Only If It Keeps The Conditional Target

P(y | history_t) = P(y | s_t)

Plain read. The summary s_t can replace the whole history only for a specified target y and task family. This is the mathematical test behind memory compression, cache retention, skill summaries, and routed multimodal memory.

Papers

ThinKV, STAR-KV, EntroKV, DLMR, Skill-Pro, VideoKR, Mamba Markov ICL, Linear Recurrent Memory.

Why It Works

The method reduces compute or context length without erasing the variables needed later.

Evidence

Compare full-history and compressed-state decisions, perturb rare evidence, and measure failures caused by dropped information.

FORM 04

Measurement Channel

A Score Is A Noisy Observation Of A Hidden Target

score = target signal + item noise + judge bias + shortcut artifact + omitted evidence

Plain read. The benchmark result is produced by an instrument. The instrument has item choices, rubrics, judges, sample sizes, and blind spots. The formula asks whether the observed score identifies the capability being claimed.

Papers

ATLAS, FRABench, UFEval, JudgeBench, WebDevJudge, CounselBench, MADQA, Seizure-Semiology-Suite, Omni-Reward.

Why It Works

Better evaluation comes from reducing or exposing measurement error, not from pretending the score is the target.

Evidence

Confidence intervals, item information, expert agreement, rendered-artifact judging, component scores, and ranking stability.

FORM 05

Tail Probability

Rare Failures Need Their Own Estimator

P(E_bad) = E_Q[1{Y in E_bad} * p(Y) / q(Y)]

Plain read. Sample from Q to see more rare failures, then reweight by p/q so the estimate still refers to deployment distribution P. This is useful only if Q covers the bad event and weights are not too unstable.

Papers

Rare Event Analysis, Tail Risks, Control Consistency Losses, SandboxEscapeBench, Jailbreak Foundry, FALCON, SRMC, FlowGuard.

Why It Works

It spends evaluation budget where the failure indicator is informative while preserving the original probability claim.

Evidence

Event definitions, proposal sensitivity, effective sample size, confidence intervals, and near-boundary examples.

FORM 06

Transport Path

A Sampler Is A Route Through Distributions

p_t -> p_{t+1} by denoiser, vector field, bridge, proposal, or reward tilt

Plain read. The output distribution is built step by step. If the path loses modes, accumulates quantization error, or moves through unsafe regions, the final artifact inherits that failure.

Papers

DMPO, FALCON, Safety-Guided Flow, Cauchy Bridges, GWF, MMOT/Schrodinger Bridges, Quantized Diffusion, FIDIA.

Why It Works

Controlling intermediate distributions controls likelihood, diversity, safety, and mode coverage more directly than endpoint filtering.

Evidence

Mode recall, likelihood calibration, path stability, guidance bias, and accumulated error across steps.

FORM 07

Spectral Gap

Signal Must Separate From Noise To Be Reliable

recover signal when leading singular/eigen direction separates from the noise bulk

Plain read. High-dimensional models contain many directions. A direction is useful when it is stable enough to estimate and aligned with the target, not merely because it has a large parameter count behind it.

Papers

Alignment-Sensitive Minimax Rates, Single-Head Attention, BBP Transitions, Low-Rank LM Logits, SVD Interpretability, Jacobian Spectra.

Why It Works

Finite data recovers separated directions more reliably. Alignment with those directions lowers sample burden.

Evidence

Spectral gaps, bootstrap stability, target alignment, perturbation tests, and sample-size scaling.

FORM 08

Update Geometry

An Update Is A Vector In A Metric

theta_{t+1} = theta_t - eta * M_t^{-1} g_t, with movement judged by curvature or trust region

Plain read. Gradient size alone does not say what changes. The metric, Hessian, preconditioner, rank constraint, or KL radius decides which directions are cheap, stiff, safe, or destructive.

Papers

Adam Degeneracy, DiReCT, LoRA Theory, Trust-Region LLM RL, Beyond Muon, Non-Euclidean Edge of Stability, RAIN-Merging.

Why It Works

The method permits useful movement while avoiding directions where small changes create large damage.

Evidence

Curvature energy, projected update loss, trust-region violations, optimizer ablations, and off-target capability audits.

FORM 09

Low-Rank Movement

Adaptation Works If The Needed Change Lives In Few Directions

Delta W = A B, rank(Delta W) <= r

Plain read. Low-rank adaptation assumes the task does not need arbitrary movement in parameter space. It needs a small set of directions that capture most useful change.

Papers

LoRA Theory, FlexRank, RAIN-Merging, Low-Rank LM Logits, COMPACT, LiME, MetaEmbed.

Why It Works

Restricting movement reduces memory and overfitting when the task signal is concentrated in a small subspace.

Evidence

Rank-energy spectra, projection loss, adapter direction alignment, and performance versus rank.

FORM 10

Quotient Space

Equivalent Encodings Should Collapse To One Object

[x] = {g x : g in G}; learn p([x]) rather than p(x)

Plain read. The bracket [x] means the object represented by x after ignoring nuisance transformations. The model should not treat every rotated molecule, relabeled graph, or changed viewpoint as a new object.

Papers

Quotient-Space Diffusion, Camera-Aware MLLM, GraphGlue, protein generation, TideGS, RadioGS.

Why It Works

It removes fake degrees of freedom before learning, so capacity is spent on the real object.

Evidence

Invariance tests, equivalent-input consistency, and downstream validity under transformed coordinates.

FORM 11

Identified Claim

A Claim Is Identified Only If All Plausible Worlds Agree

c is identified if c(w) is constant for all w in W(data, assumptions)

Plain read. W is the set of worlds, graphs, or mechanisms consistent with the observed evidence and assumptions. A causal or scientific claim is justified only when the target does not change across that set.

Papers

Distributional Equivalence, Local Covariate Selection, Structure Learning CI, DiCoLa, Unpaired Causal IV, BRCD, CausalGame.

Why It Works

The method refuses to claim what the evidence cannot distinguish.

Evidence

Equivalence classes, adjustment validity, CI calibration, intervention stress tests, and explicit non-identification cases.

FORM 12

Verifier

A Check Must Observe The Artifact Where Correctness Lives

V(artifact, assumptions, observation channel) -> pass, fail, score, or uncertainty

Plain read. The verifier is an external measurement procedure. It may execute code, render a page, check a proof, inspect a visual outcome, or test statistical indistinguishability.

Papers

CyberGym, VERINA, WZ-LLM, PaperBench, WebDevJudge, OmniVerifier, Gaussian Certified Unlearning, Finite Test Certification.

Why It Works

It makes correctness harder to fake because the claim must survive a procedure outside the generator's text.

Evidence

Verifier scope, false positives, execution logs, formal checker failures, reproduction success, and adversary view.

FORM 13

Game Or Mechanism

Other Actors Change The Objective By Responding

agent i chooses policy pi_i with payoff u_i(pi_1,...,pi_n), constraints, and information

Plain read. A policy does not act in an inert world. Attackers adapt, users report private values, data sellers respond to pricing, and agents change one another's information.

Papers

AdvGame, OMAC, Accuracy Auctions, Data Market Pricing, Constrained Nash, Fair Causal Bandits, MASPOB, Value of Variance.

Why It Works

The model includes response and incentives inside the problem instead of treating them as noise.

Evidence

Adaptive attacks, truthful reporting, equilibrium distance, convergence time, and payoff-sensitivity tests.

FORM 14

Tradeoff Frontier

One Scalar Score Hides What Was Sacrificed

frontier = {(a,b): no feasible solution improves a without worsening b}

Plain read. A frontier is the boundary of possible tradeoffs: accuracy versus tool cost, safety versus helpfulness, security versus fidelity, compression versus quality, detection power versus false positives.

Papers

ParetoPO, SecFid, Catch-22, SafeDPO, VALUEFLOW, Accuracy Auctions, Fair OT, LiftQuant, FlexRank.

Why It Works

It prevents hidden losses from being averaged away. The reader can see what was paid to improve the reported objective.

Evidence

Frontier curves, sensitivity to weights, subgroup tradeoffs, false-positive costs, and examples where scalar rankings reverse.

BIG PICTURE

How To Read The Equations

When a formula appears in the atlas, ask what it protects. A constraint protects validity. A state equation protects history. A measurement equation protects the meaning of a score. A tail estimator protects rare risk. A spectral condition protects recoverability. A verifier protects the claim from fluent imitation.

That is the connecting tissue across the conferences: the equations name what must survive while the system reasons, learns, compresses, samples, acts, or gets evaluated.
