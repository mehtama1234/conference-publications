Scientific and healthcare ML as modeling under biological constraint

The central problem is that scientific and medical data is noisy, scarce, structured by mechanisms, and high stakes. The model must preserve physiology, time, uncertainty, and expert meaning rather than only fit labels.

Problem

Biomedical data is rarely clean i.i.d. data. It may be fMRI, EEG, ECG, bowel sounds, genomics, connectomes, peptides, diagrams, or expert rankings, each with its own measurement process.

Approaches

The papers use mechanistic simulators, diffusion bridges, biosignal-language alignment, phenotype-conditioned graph routing, dynamic chunking, saliency, relative ranking, and discovery-oriented agents.

Connecting read

The field is pulling foundation-model methods into science, but the useful work keeps the domain's structure visible instead of flattening it into generic tokens.

FIRST PRINCIPLES

The core problem

In healthcare and science, the target is often not a label; it is an underlying process. A neuron responds to stimulation through biophysics. A connectome routes signals through phenotype-dependent structure. A sleep study contains multiple synchronized physiological channels. A peptide has an energy landscape. The model is useful only if it respects the process enough to generalize.

That is why these papers combine learned models with mechanistic assumptions, time-series structure, cross-modal signals, and explanation. The repeated pattern is not "apply a bigger model to medicine." It is choosing an interface between ML and a domain: tokens for fMRI, chunks for DNA, bridges for noisy sounds, core tokens for biosignal channels, or diagrams for scientific methodology.

CORE

The model is reading a measurement, not reality itself

A hospital trace, brain scan, genome, peptide sample, or methodology diagram is not the thing itself. It is an instrumented view of the thing.

That distinction is the first-principles key to the whole page. An fMRI scan is delayed, noisy evidence about brain activity. A sleep recording is a bundle of sensors watching a hidden body state. A retinal stimulation response is a measurement of a biophysical system under an intervention. A DNA sequence is a long string whose meaningful units may not align with fixed token boundaries. A peptide sample is evidence about an energy landscape. A scientific diagram is a compressed representation of a method, not decoration.

So the useful question is not only whether a model predicts well. The useful question is what object it preserves while translating the measurement. Does the fMRI token keep the neural concept? Does the biosignal bridge keep the physiological state across modalities? Does the imputer keep feature identity? Does the saliency method explain the signal in the time scale a clinician would inspect? Does the generative sampler preserve the physical distribution rather than just output plausible structures?

TOPIC 01

Mechanism is stronger than label fit

In science and medicine, a label can be right for the wrong biological reason.

Learning Biophysical Models for Neurostimulation is the clean example. The task is not merely to predict a response from recordings. The model has to preserve the Hodgkin-Huxley mechanism well enough that new electrode patterns still make sense. Autoregressive Boltzmann Generators has the molecular version: a generated peptide sample is valuable only if it respects the energy landscape, not just sequence plausibility. TTT-Discover has the search version: discovery needs feedback from the task while solving, not only a final answer that sounds scientific.

The deeper principle is that scientific ML should keep the causal or physical object visible. A model that hides the mechanism behind a high score may work as a predictor but fail as a scientific instrument.

TOPIC 02

Biomedical tokens must preserve physiological units

Tokenization is not neutral when the input is a brain signal, DNA sequence, sleep study, or multi-channel biosignal.

Mind-Omni turns fMRI into discrete brain tokens. dnaHNet learns dynamic genomic chunks. CoTAR summarizes EEG and ECG dependencies through core tokens. SleepLM aligns sleep signals with language. These methods are all choosing an interface: what becomes a unit, what gets compressed, what cross-modal relation is allowed, and what signal identity survives.

If the interface cuts through the wrong unit, the model can lose the domain meaning before learning begins. A DNA motif can be fragmented, a physiological channel can be blurred into another channel, a sleep event can lose timing, or a brain token can encode scanner artifacts rather than neural content. That is why representation design is part of the scientific claim.

TOPIC 03

Clinical time is evidence, not just sequence length

Medical traces carry timing, missingness, repeated motifs, and expert disagreement; flattening them into generic sequences loses the clinical object.

HELIX treats missing features as named physiological channels rather than anonymous blanks. Time Series Saliency Maps asks whether an explanation points to the right temporal evidence for heart, EEG, or forecasting tasks. Ranking Time Series uses relative judgments because experts may disagree on absolute labels while still agreeing on comparisons. Universal Redundancies in TSFMs identifies motif parroting and seasonality bias because time-series models can confuse repeated pattern with reliable evidence.

The common principle is that clinical time series need accountable temporal reasoning. The model should know which feature is missing, when an event happened, why a time region mattered, and when a recurring motif is a shortcut rather than a signal.

TOPIC 04

Cross-modal alignment must come from the body, not from text alone

Biosignal modalities are related because the body creates coupled measurements, not because their descriptions share words.

BioX-Bridge transfers across physiological modalities by using cross-modal correlations. SleepLM connects polysomnography streams to natural language while still needing signal reconstruction and event localization. Mind-Omni connects brain, image, and language tasks through brain tokens. PhenoBrain conditions connectome routing on phenotype so subject context changes how brain-graph evidence should move.

This matters because fluent text can create a false sense of alignment. A model can describe EEG, ECG, or fMRI in medical language while failing to preserve the physiological state. The evidence has to show that the aligned representation still reconstructs, transfers, predicts, or routes the body signal correctly.

TOPIC 05

Scientific communication is also a checked artifact

A scientific diagram, explanation, or generated method artifact has to preserve the structure of the work, not just look professional.

PaperBanana is useful because methodology diagrams are part of how researchers reason and communicate. A diagram that reverses a pipeline, hides a key dependency, or decorates the wrong relationship is scientifically misleading even if it looks polished. Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridges has the same artifact lesson in a different form: a denoised bowel-sound signal is useful only if the heavy-tailed acoustic assumption matches the clinical interference problem.

The broader rule is that scientific artifacts need domain-native checks. Figure quality, signal denoising, and discovery workflows are not proven by generic plausibility. They are proven by whether the artifact preserves the method, source signal, or task feedback it claims to represent.

SYNTHESIS

Four ways the papers handle scientific structure

1. Biological mechanisms should constrain the learned model

mechanistic science

This cluster keeps the physical or biological process in the loop. The point is not to replace science with black-box prediction, but to learn pieces of a mechanism that can still be tested against unseen conditions.

ICML 2026Learning Biophysical Models for Neurostimulation
Learning Biophysical Models for Neurostimulation fits Hodgkin-Huxley retinal models from extracellular recordings to predict unseen multi-electrode stimulation responses. It matters because stimulation response is not just a statistical label. The model has to infer biophysical parameters that make sense under new electrode configurations.
ICML 2026Autoregressive Boltzmann Generators
Autoregressive Boltzmann Generators introduces Robin, a transferable model for peptide systems with improved zero-shot energy-distance behavior. The scientific target is an energy landscape, not ordinary sequence likelihood. The model is useful if generated samples preserve physically meaningful distributions across related molecular systems.
ICML 2026TTT-Discover
TTT-Discover reports discoveries across mathematics, GPU kernels, algorithm design, and single-cell denoising. Its role in this theme is not one domain result, but the idea of test-time training as a scientific search procedure. The model adapts while solving, using task feedback as part of discovery rather than only post-hoc evaluation.

2. Physiological foundation models need cross-modal alignment

biosignal interfaces

Medical signals come from many instruments, each seeing a different part of the body. These papers build interfaces that let fMRI, EEG, ECG, sleep channels, and language share information without pretending they are the same signal.

ICML 2026Mind-Omni
Mind-Omni tokenizes fMRI into discrete brain tokens and trains one diffusion model across brain, vision, and language tasks. The conceptual move is to make brain activity enter a generative foundation-model interface. If the tokens preserve neural information, one model can support both encoding and decoding across modalities.
ICLR 2026CoTAR
CoTAR replaces decentralized attention with core-token aggregation and redistribution for EEG and ECG channel dependencies. Biosignal channels are not independent, but full pairwise attention can be wasteful and noisy. Core tokens act as a shared coordination space where cross-channel dependencies can be represented compactly.
ICML 2026SleepLM
SleepLM aligns multimodal polysomnography with natural language through contrastive alignment, captioning, and signal reconstruction. Sleep data is inherently multimodal and clinical interpretation is linguistic. The model tries to connect raw physiological streams to descriptions clinicians can inspect and reason about.
ICLR 2026BioX-Bridge
BioX-Bridge uses cross-modal physiological correlations to transfer knowledge across biosignal modalities when labeled target data is scarce. The method uses the body itself as the bridge: modalities are correlated because they measure related physiological states. That helps when one signal has labels and another has few.

3. Time-series medicine needs explanation, imputation, and expert-compatible labels

clinical time series

Clinical time series are noisy and often hard for experts to label consistently. These papers focus on preserving signal identity, explaining temporal evidence, correcting biases, and using relative judgments when absolute labels are unreliable.

ICML 2026HELIX
HELIX assigns learnable feature identities to stabilize cross-feature time-series imputation. In clinical data, missing values are not interchangeable blanks; each feature has a physiological meaning. Feature identity helps the imputer know what kind of signal is absent and how it relates to observed channels.
ICML 2026Time Series Saliency Maps
Time Series Saliency Maps explains wearable heart-rate extraction, EEG seizure detection, and forecasting with domain-appropriate attribution. The paper matters because explanation has to match the signal. A saliency method for images is not automatically meaningful for periodic, noisy, or event-driven physiological traces.
ICML 2026Ranking Time Series
Ranking Time Series targets low-agreement expert annotation, especially in medical time series, through relative judgments and elastic similarity. It recognizes that experts may disagree on absolute labels while still agreeing that one trace is more severe, cleaner, or more similar than another. Ranking can be a more stable supervision format.
ICML 2026Universal Redundancies in TSFMs
Universal Redundancies in TSFMs identifies heads responsible for motif parroting and seasonality bias using stable-rank intrinsic ablation. Time-series foundation models can overuse repeated motifs or seasonal shortcuts. The paper provides a way to locate and remove redundant behavior that looks useful until distribution changes.

4. Scientific inputs need domain-specific tokenization and communication

representation design

Generic tokenization and generic diagrams are often the wrong interface for science. These papers build representations that preserve biological motifs, phenotype-conditioned communication, noisy acoustic separation, and scientific-method diagrams.

ICML 2026dnaHNet
dnaHNet uses differentiable dynamic chunking to model raw genomic sequences without fragmenting biological motifs through fixed tokenization. Genomic meaning does not necessarily align with subword-like fixed chunks. A dynamic chunker can preserve motifs as units when they matter for prediction.
ICML 2026PhenoBrain
PhenoBrain injects phenotype information into long-range connectome routing and attention regulation for neuropsychiatric prediction. Functional brain networks differ by subject and phenotype. The model conditions communication on clinical context so long-range routing is not one-size-fits-all.
ICML 2026Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridges
Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridges uses heavy-tailed bridge drivers to isolate bowel sounds from speech interference. The heavy-tailed assumption matters because interference is not always mild Gaussian noise. The representation of noise should match the clinical acoustic setting.
ICML 2026PaperBanana
PaperBanana benchmarks methodology-diagram generation for AI scientists through PaperBananaBench. Scientific communication often depends on diagrams that compress method structure. The benchmark asks whether models can produce figures that explain an experimental or algorithmic pipeline, not merely fluent prose.
BOUNDARY

Healthcare claims need native clinical evidence

The direct 15-paper set is about scientific and biomedical modeling, but the nearby atlas rows show the same first-principles rule from the evaluation side: a clinical model is only useful if the evidence checks the clinical object, not just the model's fluency.

CounselBench is the mental-health version of this rule. The relevant object is not an open-ended answer that sounds caring. It is a response judged by mental-health professionals for safety, appropriateness, and unauthorized medical-advice risk. Seizure-Semiology-Suite is the video-reporting version. The relevant object is not a generic medical caption. It is temporal clinical faithfulness: laterality, symptom sequence, localization, and the hierarchy of semiological features. SurvDiff is the data-generation version. The relevant object is not a plausible synthetic table. It is a survival dataset whose event-time distribution and right-censoring mechanism remain useful for downstream survival analysis. MedAgentGym is the agent version. The relevant object is not biomedical prose. It is executable biomedical data-science work with sandbox state, feedback, ground truth, and trajectories.

These nearby papers are not counted in the direct paper set below because the local theme index maps them primarily to evaluation, governance, agents, or scientific discovery. They still matter for understanding this page because they define the boundary of acceptable evidence. A healthcare foundation model must preserve the biological or clinical object, and the verifier must be native to that object: expert safety review, temporal-report faithfulness, censoring preservation, executable analysis, intervention response, signal reconstruction, or physiologically meaningful attribution.

EVIDENCE

What would prove the biomedical model preserved the object

The proof has to match the measurement. A biomedical model is not proven by generic accuracy if the domain mechanism, sensor meaning, time structure, or expert boundary was lost.

Intervention response

Mechanistic models should predict what happens under new stimulation, molecular conditions, or task feedback, not only fit familiar observations.

Signal reconstruction

Biosignal models should reconstruct, caption, localize, retrieve, or transfer signals while preserving channel identity, timing, and physiological meaning.

Cross-modal consistency

Brain, vision, language, EEG, ECG, and sleep interfaces should agree because they carry related state, not because the model learned a loose text association.

Clinical time faithfulness

Imputation, saliency, and ranking methods should preserve feature identity, event timing, expert disagreement, and uncertainty across the time trace.

Domain-unit preservation

Genomic chunks, phenotype-conditioned routes, heavy-tailed sound bridges, and method diagrams must keep the scientific unit they were built to represent.

Native expert checks

The strongest evidence uses the right verifier: held-out interventions, energy-distance behavior, clinical review, physiological reconstruction, executable analysis, or method-faithful diagram evaluation.

DEEPER PRINCIPLE

What this theme says about ICML and ICLR

The scientific and healthcare papers point toward domain-respecting foundation models. The interesting work is not just scale; it is the interface between learned representations and mechanisms, instruments, expert labels, and physiological constraints.

The deeper principle is measurement accountability. A model should be judged by whether it preserves the thing the instrument was supposed to reveal. For mechanistic biology, that means response under new interventions. For biosignals, it means the hidden body or brain state survives translation into tokens, captions, or embeddings. For clinical time series, it means missingness, timing, and expert disagreement remain visible. For scientific communication, it means the diagram or generated artifact keeps the method structure intact.

This is why final text is too weak as evidence. A medical explanation can sound clinical while ignoring sensor timing. A generated protein can look plausible while missing the physical distribution. A diagram can look clean while misrepresenting the experiment. The right verifier has to inspect the domain object: intervention response, energy behavior, signal reconstruction, cross-modal transfer, imputation quality, motif preservation, or method-faithful diagrams.
