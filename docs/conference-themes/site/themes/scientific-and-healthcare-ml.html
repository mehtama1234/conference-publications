Scientific and healthcare ML as modeling under biological constraint

The central problem is that scientific and medical data is noisy, scarce, structured by mechanisms, and high stakes. The model must preserve physiology, time, uncertainty, and expert meaning rather than only fit labels.

Problem

Biomedical data is rarely clean i.i.d. data. It may be fMRI, EEG, ECG, bowel sounds, genomics, connectomes, peptides, diagrams, or expert rankings, each with its own measurement process.

Approaches

The papers use mechanistic simulators, diffusion bridges, biosignal-language alignment, phenotype-conditioned graph routing, dynamic chunking, saliency, relative ranking, and discovery-oriented agents.

Connecting read

The field is pulling foundation-model methods into science, but the useful work keeps the domain's structure visible instead of flattening it into generic tokens.

FIRST PRINCIPLES

The core problem

In healthcare and science, the target is often not a label; it is an underlying process. A neuron responds to stimulation through biophysics. A connectome routes signals through phenotype-dependent structure. A sleep study contains multiple synchronized physiological channels. A peptide has an energy landscape. The model is useful only if it respects the process enough to generalize.

That is why these papers combine learned models with mechanistic assumptions, time-series structure, cross-modal signals, and explanation. The repeated pattern is not "apply a bigger model to medicine." It is choosing an interface between ML and a domain: tokens for fMRI, chunks for DNA, bridges for noisy sounds, core tokens for biosignal channels, or diagrams for scientific methodology.

SYNTHESIS

Four ways the papers handle scientific structure

1. Biological mechanisms should constrain the learned model

mechanistic science

This cluster keeps the physical or biological process in the loop. The point is not to replace science with black-box prediction, but to learn pieces of a mechanism that can still be tested against unseen conditions.

ICML 2026Learning Biophysical Models for Neurostimulation
Learning Biophysical Models for Neurostimulation fits Hodgkin-Huxley retinal models from extracellular recordings to predict unseen multi-electrode stimulation responses. It matters because stimulation response is not just a statistical label. The model has to infer biophysical parameters that make sense under new electrode configurations.
ICML 2026Autoregressive Boltzmann Generators
Autoregressive Boltzmann Generators introduces Robin, a transferable model for peptide systems with improved zero-shot energy-distance behavior. The scientific target is an energy landscape, not ordinary sequence likelihood. The model is useful if generated samples preserve physically meaningful distributions across related molecular systems.
ICML 2026TTT-Discover
TTT-Discover reports discoveries across mathematics, GPU kernels, algorithm design, and single-cell denoising. Its role in this theme is not one domain result, but the idea of test-time training as a scientific search procedure. The model adapts while solving, using task feedback as part of discovery rather than only post-hoc evaluation.

2. Physiological foundation models need cross-modal alignment

biosignal interfaces

Medical signals come from many instruments, each seeing a different part of the body. These papers build interfaces that let fMRI, EEG, ECG, sleep channels, and language share information without pretending they are the same signal.

ICML 2026Mind-Omni
Mind-Omni tokenizes fMRI into discrete brain tokens and trains one diffusion model across brain, vision, and language tasks. The conceptual move is to make brain activity enter a generative foundation-model interface. If the tokens preserve neural information, one model can support both encoding and decoding across modalities.
ICLR 2026CoTAR
CoTAR replaces decentralized attention with core-token aggregation and redistribution for EEG and ECG channel dependencies. Biosignal channels are not independent, but full pairwise attention can be wasteful and noisy. Core tokens act as a shared coordination space where cross-channel dependencies can be represented compactly.
ICML 2026SleepLM
SleepLM aligns multimodal polysomnography with natural language through contrastive alignment, captioning, and signal reconstruction. Sleep data is inherently multimodal and clinical interpretation is linguistic. The model tries to connect raw physiological streams to descriptions clinicians can inspect and reason about.
ICLR 2026BioX-Bridge
BioX-Bridge uses cross-modal physiological correlations to transfer knowledge across biosignal modalities when labeled target data is scarce. The method uses the body itself as the bridge: modalities are correlated because they measure related physiological states. That helps when one signal has labels and another has few.

3. Time-series medicine needs explanation, imputation, and expert-compatible labels

clinical time series

Clinical time series are noisy and often hard for experts to label consistently. These papers focus on preserving signal identity, explaining temporal evidence, correcting biases, and using relative judgments when absolute labels are unreliable.

ICML 2026HELIX
HELIX assigns learnable feature identities to stabilize cross-feature time-series imputation. In clinical data, missing values are not interchangeable blanks; each feature has a physiological meaning. Feature identity helps the imputer know what kind of signal is absent and how it relates to observed channels.
ICML 2026Time Series Saliency Maps
Time Series Saliency Maps explains wearable heart-rate extraction, EEG seizure detection, and forecasting with domain-appropriate attribution. The paper matters because explanation has to match the signal. A saliency method for images is not automatically meaningful for periodic, noisy, or event-driven physiological traces.
ICML 2026Ranking Time Series
Ranking Time Series targets low-agreement expert annotation, especially in medical time series, through relative judgments and elastic similarity. It recognizes that experts may disagree on absolute labels while still agreeing that one trace is more severe, cleaner, or more similar than another. Ranking can be a more stable supervision format.
ICML 2026Universal Redundancies in TSFMs
Universal Redundancies in TSFMs identifies heads responsible for motif parroting and seasonality bias using stable-rank intrinsic ablation. Time-series foundation models can overuse repeated motifs or seasonal shortcuts. The paper provides a way to locate and remove redundant behavior that looks useful until distribution changes.

4. Scientific inputs need domain-specific tokenization and communication

representation design

Generic tokenization and generic diagrams are often the wrong interface for science. These papers build representations that preserve biological motifs, phenotype-conditioned communication, noisy acoustic separation, and scientific-method diagrams.

ICML 2026dnaHNet
dnaHNet uses differentiable dynamic chunking to model raw genomic sequences without fragmenting biological motifs through fixed tokenization. Genomic meaning does not necessarily align with subword-like fixed chunks. A dynamic chunker can preserve motifs as units when they matter for prediction.
ICML 2026PhenoBrain
PhenoBrain injects phenotype information into long-range connectome routing and attention regulation for neuropsychiatric prediction. Functional brain networks differ by subject and phenotype. The model conditions communication on clinical context so long-range routing is not one-size-fits-all.
ICML 2026Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridges
Cauchy-Driven Diffusion Bridges uses heavy-tailed bridge drivers to isolate bowel sounds from speech interference. The heavy-tailed assumption matters because interference is not always mild Gaussian noise. The representation of noise should match the clinical acoustic setting.
ICML 2026PaperBanana
PaperBanana benchmarks methodology-diagram generation for AI scientists through PaperBananaBench. Scientific communication often depends on diagrams that compress method structure. The benchmark asks whether models can produce figures that explain an experimental or algorithmic pipeline, not merely fluent prose.
FIELD READ

What this theme says about ICML and ICLR

The scientific and healthcare papers point toward domain-respecting foundation models. The interesting work is not just scale; it is the interface between learned representations and mechanisms, instruments, expert labels, and physiological constraints.

That matters for benchmark design too. A scientific or medical gym cannot be judged only by final text. It needs evidence about signals, units, uncertainty, mechanisms, and whether the model's reasoning respects the domain object being measured.
