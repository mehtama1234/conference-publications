Interpretability as intervention

The central problem is moving from explaining a model after the fact to changing its behavior with internal evidence. A useful interpretation should help detect failure, localize cause, steer generation, repair representations, or certify that the model used the evidence it claimed to use.

Problem

Explanations are weak if they cannot change decisions. If a feature, head, subspace, circuit, or attribution graph marks a failure, it should become a handle for intervention, verification, monitoring, or repair.

Approaches

The papers use activation steering, hidden-state norms, causal features, attribution graphs, concept bottlenecks, mechanistic data attribution, SAE features, saliency diagnostics, and model-set stability analysis.

Connecting read

Interpretability is becoming operational: find the mechanism, prove it matters, then use it as a control surface without pushing the model into unnatural states.

TOPIC 01

The Big Picture

The older version of interpretability often stopped at a human-readable story: this neuron detects a concept, this token attended to that token, this image region mattered. The papers here push further. They ask whether an explanation can support action. Can an internal norm tell us when to allocate more reasoning? Can a hidden direction stabilize persona drift? Can attribution graphs identify invalid chain-of-thought structure? Can a concept prover choose evidence that a verifier can check? Can saliency reveal hallucination onset while a vision-language model is still reasoning? Can backdoor triggers be turned into explicit descriptions that support mitigation?

That shift makes interpretability harder. An intervention must be faithful enough to affect the real mechanism, but gentle enough not to move the model into artificial activation states. A probe can become a target for evasion if it is connected to reward. An activation edit can solve a surface behavior while damaging the natural distribution of internal states. A sparse feature can be useful only if it maps to a causal unit rather than a pretty label. This page is therefore about interpretability as an engineering interface: a way to detect, localize, steer, repair, and certify model behavior.

TOPIC 02

The concrete object is the usable explanation

An interpretability paper is about an explanation that can do work: an activation signal, attribution path, circuit, sparse feature, saliency map, concept bottleneck, trigger description, or mechanistic signature.

What must stay fixed is faithfulness to the behavior being explained. A steering vector must correspond to the intended mechanism, not a correlated style. A saliency method must track visual evidence, not architecture artifacts. An attribution trace must point to sources that actually affected the output. A safety probe must reveal the failure pathway before reward pressure teaches evasion. A concept model must preserve task-relevant concepts rather than user-friendly labels alone.

What may change is the intervention enabled by the explanation: allocate compute, steer activations, verify reasoning, synthesize data, repair labels, remove concepts, inspect grounding, detect attacks, or constrain a model class. The method works when the explanation supports a successful action that would fail without that internal evidence. The failure boundary is explanation theater: the story is readable, but editing, verifying, or relying on it does not improve real behavior.

FAILURE MODES

How explanations become theater

Interpretability fails when the explanation is readable but not faithful enough to support a real intervention, verifier, repair, or safety decision.

Correlated feature

The named neuron, direction, or concept tracks style, topic, or confidence rather than the mechanism that caused behavior.

Attribution drift

The explanation points to plausible tokens, regions, samples, or features that did not actually control the output.

Probe gaming

A detector becomes part of reward or evaluation, so the model learns to evade the probe instead of removing the failure pathway.

Off-manifold edit

An activation intervention changes the output by moving internal states into unnatural regions that do not preserve normal behavior.

Friendly label

A concept explanation is easy for people to read but does not preserve the task-relevant concept, source, or causal unit.

No action gain

The explanation does not improve steering, verification, data repair, grounding inspection, safety mitigation, or model design.

TOPIC 03

What Interpretability Is Used For

Steering

Hidden-state norms, prompt-induced activation replacements, assistant persona axes, decode-time subspaces, and reasoning mechanism vectors turn internal measurements into control inputs.

Verification

Attribution graphs, prover-verifier games, information-flow tracing, concept evidence, and minimal explanations turn explanations into checks of whether the model used the right structure.

Failure Diagnosis

Safety heads, hallucination saliency, harmful-feature paths, robustness pathways, topological attack signatures, and backdoor articulation expose failure mechanisms before output metrics are enough.

Mechanism Discovery

SVD subspaces, neuron-basis circuits, SAE features, token association dynamics, temporal features, and analogical circuits identify the internal units doing work.

Attribution

Mechanistic data attribution, recursive long-horizon tracing, visual attribution streaming, time-series saliency, and temporal graph memory backtracking connect behavior to sources.

Model Design

Concept bottleneck experts, interpretable trees, falling rule structures, NAM guarantees, and spatial latent models use interpretability as part of the predictor itself.

TOPIC 04

Interpretability has to survive intervention

A real explanation should keep pointing to the same mechanism after the model is edited, steered, stressed, or used in a decision loop.

This is the practical difference between a description and an intervention. If a hidden direction controls persona, then moving that direction should change persona without destroying unrelated abilities. If a saliency map tracks visual grounding, then removing or changing the grounded region should change the answer in the predicted way. If an attribution trace claims a training sample caused a feature, then deleting, downweighting, or counterbalancing that sample should move the feature or behavior. If a concept bottleneck claims to use human concepts, then changing a concept should change the prediction for the concept's reason, not because the bottleneck leaked labels through another path.

That is why the page title says intervention. Interpretability becomes a first-principles tool only when the explanation has a controlled counterfactual: what would happen if this feature, circuit, region, source, or concept were changed while the rest of the system stayed as fixed as possible?

EVIDENCE

What would prove an explanation worked

The proof has to show that the explanation tracks the mechanism that caused the behavior and supports a useful action.

Causal intervention

Editing the named feature, head, subspace, region, source, or concept changes the target behavior in the predicted direction while preserving unrelated behavior.

Counterfactual check

Changing the evidence the explanation points to should change the output; leaving that evidence fixed while changing distractions should not.

Faithful attribution

Attribution traces should survive ablation, source removal, data reweighting, region masking, or long-horizon backtracking to the responsible cause.

Natural-state audit

Activation edits and probes should be tested for off-manifold distortion so the model is not being forced into an artificial state that only looks successful.

Action gain

The explanation should improve steering, verification, data repair, grounding inspection, safety mitigation, model selection, or runtime allocation.

Adversarial pressure

If the explanation becomes part of training, reward, or evaluation, it should be checked for evasion, probe gaming, and hidden failure paths.

CLUSTERS

The Main Research Moves

1. Internal signals become runtime control surfaces

Several papers treat internal activations as signals that can directly steer behavior. The Tell-Tale Norm uses hidden-state L2 magnitude as an endogenous measure of reasoning intensity, then applies it to recursion, steering, and response selection. Steer Like the LLM distills prompt effects into position-specific activation interventions, replacing text prompts with learned activation controls. Assistant Axis identifies a direction in activation space that predicts and controls default assistant persona drift. DecodeShare finds a compact task-general subspace in KV-cached decode-time hidden states. Base Models Know How to Reason decomposes differences between base and thinking models into mechanism vectors and SAE-derived firing heuristics. The shared move is to stop treating hidden states as opaque byproducts and start treating them as levers.

2. Reasoning explanations need structural verification

For reasoning systems, a fluent chain is not enough; the internal or external structure must support the answer. CRV classifies structural fingerprints in attribution graphs to detect reasoning errors. FlashTrace recursively traces attribution through reasoning chains back to source inputs, preserving faithfulness across long horizons. Neural Concept Verifier combines concept encodings with prover-verifier games so a prover selects interpretable evidence for a nonlinear verifier. FPS structures formal solving as forward derivation and backward verification. NAD uses activation sparsity and neuron agreement across samples as a correctness signal rather than relying on textual self-evaluation. These papers turn explanations into tests of process, not decorations around answers.

3. Mechanistic features are useful when they are causal units

A named feature is only useful if it corresponds to a mechanism that can be manipulated or traced. SVD as Fast Interpretability decomposes Transformer MLP weights into detector-effector rank-1 subspaces without training proxy models. Neuron-Basis Circuits shows native MLP neurons can be as sparse as SAE features and support gradient-based circuit tracing. Neural Effect Search uses SAE features as causal discovery units while addressing multiple testing and entanglement. T-SAE adds adjacent-token contrastive consistency to recover smoother temporal semantic features. FAC Synthesis finds shared interpretable feature spaces across LLaMA, Mistral, and Qwen that can guide post-training data synthesis. The important bar is causal usefulness, not just interpretability vocabulary.

4. Visual and multimodal interpretability must track grounding

Vision-language systems fail when the answer is driven by a language prior instead of visual evidence. Visual symbolic mechanisms identifies content-independent spatial position IDs and attention-head mechanisms for object binding. LVLMs-Saliency fuses attention and gradients to identify hallucination onset when saliency to prior outputs drops. Visual Attribution Streaming amortizes causal region attribution from attention features so users can inspect grounding during reasoning. DAVE separates stable locally equivariant gradient components from architecture-induced patch and attention artifacts. TRACE extracts hidden boundary cues from diffusion self-attention and distills them into a fast edge decoder. Multimodal ICL Circuits shows multimodal in-context learning relies on induction-style label-copying circuits refined across modalities.

5. Safety interpretability is about finding the failure pathway

Safety work here looks inside the model to identify where harmful behavior emerges. Surgery treats attention-head sink divergence as an internal signal separating harmful fine-tuning dynamics. Robust Harmful Features distinguishes early compromised heads from mid-layer safety-aligned heads through ablation and token attribution. Suppress and Diversify treats corruption robustness as pathway prevalence and selection, not only output performance. Backdoor Self-Awareness converts hidden backdoor conditions into explicit trigger descriptions for mitigation. Persistent Homology LLM Adversarial Influence uses topological signatures to detect representation compression under prompt injection and backdoor fine-tuning. These papers are trying to locate the path from input condition to unsafe output before the output alone exposes it.

6. Attribution connects behavior back to data, time, and memory

Attribution becomes more useful when it follows the causal chain far enough back. MDA uses influence functions to trace interpretable LLM units to high-impact training samples. AI Engram isolates artificial memory traces through constrained inverse problems satisfying specificity, reactivation, sufficiency, and necessity. Time Series Saliency Maps generalizes Integrated Gradients through invertible transforms such as frequency domains. MemExplainer explains temporal graph predictions by backtracking historical events that shaped node memory vectors. Information Flow UQ traces layer-wise context-token contributions in retrieval-augmented generation to assess whether outputs used relevant evidence. The common idea is that an explanation should point to the responsible source, not only the final activation.

7. Concept models make interpretability part of the predictor

Some papers embed interpretability into the model class itself. CompSLOT uses concept-level structure to improve continual learning performance, not just explain classification. M-CBE generalizes concept bottleneck models with multiple concept-to-task expert expressions and flexible functional forms. Falling Trees extends falling rule lists to tree-shaped branches while preserving decreasing-risk prioritization. Provable NAM Explanations generates cardinally minimal sufficient explanations for Neural Additive Models with formal guarantees. Categorical ANOVA derives closed-form main-effect and interaction decompositions under dependent categorical features. Rashomon Trust studies the stability of sparse interpretable model sets under small shifts. Here the explanation is not bolted on; it constrains the prediction system.

8. Interpretability can be gamed if it becomes the reward

A mature interpretability pipeline has to consider adversarial pressure. Obfuscation Atlas shows white-box deception detectors can induce evasion once detector scores affect reward, which is exactly the risk of turning a probe into an optimization target. Divergent Causal Interventions shows common activation interventions can push representations away from natural model distributions, warning that a successful edit may still be off-manifold. Activation Oracles trains LLMs to answer natural-language questions about activations and recover hidden fine-tuned information, expanding interpretability access but also raising control questions. Shared Semantics Divergent Mechanisms clusters continuations by both semantic embeddings and mechanistic attribution signatures, reminding us that two outputs can mean the same thing while being produced differently. Interpretability as intervention needs robustness against the intervention itself.
