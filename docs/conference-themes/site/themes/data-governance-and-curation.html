Problem

Bigger data is not automatically better. Some examples teach shortcuts, some expose private facts, some poison safety boundaries, some are illegal to reuse, and some only help at one moment in training.

Approaches

The papers build curation rules, synthetic-data checks, provenance fingerprints, data valuation methods, curricula, replay buffers, dataset markets, and task-generation pipelines for agents, robots, retrieval, science, and multimodal models.

Connecting read

The field is moving from "collect a dataset" to "govern a data supply chain." The core question is which examples should enter, when they should enter, what rights they carry, and how we know what they changed.

TOPIC 01

The core problem

A model is a compression of its training distribution plus the optimization choices used to absorb it. If the data is duplicated, the model memorizes. If the data is biased, the model inherits that bias. If the data is synthetic, the model may learn the generator's artifacts. If the data is ordered badly, high-quality examples can arrive after the optimizer has stopped listening. Governance is the discipline of making these effects visible before they become model behavior.

That is why this theme spans memorization, privacy, data markets, curricula, synthetic data, robotics corpora, GUI trajectories, causal interventions, retrieval labels, and code-agent environments. These are not separate administrative details. They are different views of the same control problem: deciding which evidence the learner receives, how much trust each piece deserves, what incentives surround its production, and how the resulting model can be traced back to its sources.

TOPIC 02

The concrete object is the data intervention

A data paper is about a specific intervention on the training evidence: selecting, ordering, pricing, filtering, attributing, synthesizing, licensing, watermarking, deleting, or repairing examples.

What must stay fixed is the learning claim the data is supposed to support. A curriculum paper must keep the target capability and optimizer state visible. A data-valuation paper must keep the utility definition and incentive setting fixed. A synthetic-data paper must keep the downstream structure the real data would have taught. A provenance paper must keep ownership or source-use meaning inspectable after transformations. A privacy paper must keep the attacker's access model and sensitive fact precise.

What may change is the data route: mixture weight, timing, source subset, Shapley or semivalue score, synthetic generator, feature-coverage target, watermark, signature, pseudo-label, or repair label. The method works when that intervention changes model behavior for the claimed reason while preserving source rights, privacy, domain structure, and downstream validity. The failure boundary is data theater: the dataset looks cleaner, larger, cheaper, or more compliant, but the learned behavior cannot be traced to trustworthy evidence.

FAILURE MODES

How data governance becomes data theater

A data intervention fails when the dataset looks better while the learned behavior loses traceability, rights, privacy, or target structure.

Provenance break

Sources, licenses, ownership, watermarks, or deletion status stop being inspectable after filtering, synthesis, or fine-tuning.

Synthetic proxy

Generated data matches surface statistics while missing causal, physical, clinical, robotic, or domain structure the real data would teach.

Value mispricing

Data valuation rewards strategic, redundant, or easy examples instead of examples that move the target behavior.

Privacy mismatch

The benchmark protects against one attacker while memorization, gradients, retrieval, or derived artifacts leak the sensitive fact.

Curriculum waste

High-quality evidence arrives at the wrong optimization stage, or repeated data turns into memorization rather than useful learning.

Repair masking

Relabeling, imputation, balancing, or counterexamples improve the diagnosed metric while hiding new damage elsewhere.

TOPIC 04

Data governance is control over the model's evidence diet

The model becomes what its evidence repeatedly permits it to become.

That is the simplest first-principles line for the whole page. A dataset is not neutral because it is large. It contains rights, omissions, repetitions, shortcuts, rare cases, labels, traces, missing fields, ordering choices, and incentives. Training turns those into behavior. Data governance is the attempt to control that transformation instead of discovering the consequences only after deployment.

This is why the same concept appears in so many forms. Copyright and provenance decide whether the evidence can be used and audited. Curriculum decides when the evidence has enough force to matter. Valuation decides which evidence deserves scarce compute. Synthetic generation decides what missing evidence can be safely simulated. Repair decides which known failure a new example should target. Structured datasets decide whether the model learns a real world process or a flattened proxy.

The evidence standard is therefore stricter than "the dataset improves a benchmark." The paper has to show what property of the data caused the improvement, what stayed fixed while the data intervention changed, and what failure would appear if that property were absent. Without that, data work becomes post-hoc storytelling around a score.

TOPIC 05

Six ways the papers turn data into a governed system

1. Training data is an active signal, not a pile

curriculum and mixtures

This cluster asks how examples shape capability formation over time. The key idea is simple: an example's value depends on when it is shown, what the optimizer can still learn from it, and which circuits or priors it activates.

ICLR 2026LR Decay Wastes Your Best Data
LR Decay Wastes Your Best Data shows that ascending-quality curricula can fail because late high-quality data arrives when the learning rate has already decayed. The insight is that data quality and optimizer schedule cannot be designed separately. A perfect ordering can still waste its best examples if the optimizer is no longer plastic enough to use them.
ICLR 2026Learning to See Before Seeing
Learning to See Before Seeing studies multimodal pretraining mixtures through more than 100 controlled experiments and a trillion-token scale. It treats the data mixture as the source of visual priors rather than as background infrastructure. The point is to learn which mixture decisions create perception, grounding, and multimodal transfer before task-specific tuning begins.
ICLR 2026Pre-training under Infinite Compute
Pre-training under Infinite Compute asks what to do when compute is abundant but fresh data is scarce. Epoching, stronger weight decay, ensembling, and distillation are not mere efficiency tricks in that setting. They are ways to extract more usable learning signal from a fixed corpus without letting repetition collapse into overfit memorization.
ICLR 2026WSM
WSM finds that the duration of the checkpoint aggregation window drives decay-free pretraining gains. That makes the training trajectory itself part of data governance: the model sees the same stream through different temporal summaries. The paper says that how we merge learning over time can matter as much as which examples were present.
ICML 2026Procedural Pretraining
Procedural Pretraining front-loads a tiny amount of algorithmic data before ordinary language, code, and math pretraining. The contribution is a warm-start view of curriculum: small structured examples can prepare the learner to use later data more efficiently. This turns synthetic procedures into scaffolding rather than final task data.
ICML 2026Power Law Compositional Reasoning
Power Law Compositional Reasoning shows that natural power-law sampling can help compositional reasoning because frequent combinations are learned first. The result complicates the usual instinct to flatten data distributions. Some skew is not accidental noise; it can be a curriculum that lets the model build reusable parts before rare combinations appear.
ICML 2026MDA
MDA shows that targeted removal or augmentation of structural examples can modulate induction-head formation and in-context learning. That is a mechanistic view of curation. The paper is not only asking whether examples improve loss; it asks which internal circuits those examples help create.
ICML 2026Alignment Pretraining
Alignment Pretraining studies AI-behavior discourse inside pretraining data and reports large downstream shifts in misalignment after upsampling aligned-behavior documents. The important claim is causal: alignment is partly set before post-training. Data about how AI systems should behave becomes a prior that later preference tuning has to work with or against.
ICML 2026PRISM
PRISM uses a compact high-quality mid-training stage to improve reasoning while preserving general abilities. It treats mid-training as a retention-aware intervention between broad pretraining and narrow post-training. The data is selected not just for skill gain, but for whether it moves reasoning without erasing the base model's wider competence.
ICML 2026OpenThoughts
OpenThoughts uses public reasoning datasets and many controlled recipe experiments to train competitive reasoning models. Its importance is not simply that the data is open. It turns recipe search into evidence about which public data mixtures, filtering choices, and training stages produce reasoning gains reproducibly.
ICML 2026BLL-Loss
BLL-Loss argues that exact token imitation is the wrong objective when correctness is logical or outcome-based. In data-governance terms, the label is not the literal sequence but the successful reasoning behavior behind it. That changes how demonstrations should be weighted and how harmful imitation pressure should be capped.
ICML 2026LALP
LALP scores reasoning data by local premise support rather than whole-response probability. It treats a training trace as a chain of teachable moves, where a locally valid step can matter even if the full response is noisy. This is curation at the level of reasoning transitions rather than examples.
ICML 2026Identity Bridge
Identity Bridge adds A-to-A examples so autoregressive models can infer reverse relational knowledge. The paper is a reminder that models do not automatically learn symmetric or inverse facts from one direction of evidence. A small data recipe can expose relational structure the sequence objective otherwise hides.

2. Selection and valuation decide which examples deserve compute

data economics

This cluster treats examples as scarce interventions. The problem is not only finding good data, but estimating marginal value, pricing reusable data, screening sources, and choosing samples whose gradients actually move the target behavior.

ICML 2026Sequential Data Values
Sequential Data Values reframes data selection as a dynamic programming problem. A data point's value depends on what has already been selected and what will be selected next. That is closer to training reality than static scores, because an example can be redundant early and essential later.
ICML 2026NASH
NASH decomposes utility into components where Shapley and semivalue scores become more reliable for selecting useful data. The paper matters because raw data valuation is noisy in large models. By finding the right utility decomposition, cooperative-game values become a practical curation signal rather than a theoretical ideal.
ICML 2026Bayesian Truthful Valuation
Bayesian Truthful Valuation combines semivalues with hidden validation data to reward truthfulness and fairness at equilibrium. It brings incentives into data valuation. If contributors are paid or credited for data, the scoring rule must not reward strategic misreporting or low-quality contributions that merely look useful.
ICML 2026Data Market Pricing
Data Market Pricing studies reusable data sold by strategic sellers and shows that uniform dataset prices can fail to produce good equilibria. Data is non-rival: one buyer's use does not consume it. That breaks simple pricing intuitions and makes governance partly an economic mechanism-design problem.
ICML 2026OPUS
OPUS scores candidate documents by projecting optimizer-shaped effective updates onto a target direction. This is selection through the lens of the actual optimizer, not metadata or static quality. A document is valuable if its gradient points where the target model needs to move.
ICML 2026MTS Difficulty
MTS Difficulty explains weak training-data selection through noisy hypergradients and poor data-quality features. It is a negative result with practical value: meta-selection fails when the signal used to judge data is too weak. Better curation requires better observability of how examples affect training.
ICML 2026NS/IF Attribution
NS/IF Attribution analyzes example-removal effects under local curvature around the first Newton step. The paper asks when influence-style attribution can be trusted. That matters because governance often wants to know which data caused a behavior, but attribution can be misleading without curvature conditions.
ICML 2026Source Screening
Source Screening selects informative source subsets for shared subspace estimation, even when discarding data. Its lesson is that more sources are not always better. If a subset captures the shared representation cleanly, screening can improve estimation and reduce the cost of noisy or irrelevant sources.
ICML 2026HOBIT
HOBIT reorders examples into mini-batches with hard, informative, non-contradictory negatives. In contrastive learning, batch composition is part of the label. The paper treats curation not as a global dataset filter but as a local arrangement problem that controls what the model must distinguish.
ICML 2026Hedging on the Frontier
Hedging on the Frontier prunes and aggregates candidate models by adapting to benchmark tradeoff geometry. It belongs here because selection is not limited to data points; model candidates also carry evidence about the frontier of possible behavior. The approach governs choice under multi-metric uncertainty.
ICML 2026Diffuse to Detect
Diffuse to Detect jointly optimizes detector weights, sample weights, and diffusion parameters to reduce annotation needs. The curation question is which samples deserve labeling pressure. By coupling generation, weighting, and detection, the method treats data acquisition as part of the learning loop.
ICML 2026C2R
C2R prioritizes smallest-margin adversaries during dataset distillation. The point is that boundary cases carry disproportionate information about robustness. Distilled data should not merely represent the average distribution; it should preserve the cases that decide the classifier's decision surface.
ICML 2026GRAM
GRAM tracks data filtering while reducing cost for multiple deployment access profiles. It treats filtering as an auditable part of model production, especially when different users or tiers receive different capabilities. The governance problem is to know what capability profile each data path created.
ICLR 2026ATLAS
ATLAS estimates mutual benefit scores across 1,444 language pairs to guide multilingual data and compute allocation. It replaces vague language balancing with a transfer matrix. The value of adding one language's data depends on what it teaches other languages, not only on its own benchmark score.
ICML 2026Linguistic Nepotism
Linguistic Nepotism shows citation behavior can prefer language over evidence relevance, especially for lower-resource languages. This is data governance at retrieval time: the system's evidence set can become biased before generation begins. Better curation must separate document quality from language affinity.

3. Synthetic data needs validity checks, not blind scale

synthetic evidence

Synthetic data is useful because it can target missing cases, preserve privacy, and create labels that are hard to collect. It is dangerous because the generator's bias, physical invalidity, or missing causal structure can become training truth.

ICLR 2026Synthetic Data Selection
Synthetic Data Selection shows that covariance shift can drive high-dimensional regression generalization failure even when mean shift does not. The message is precise: synthetic data must match more than averages. Selection should preserve the geometry that the estimator actually depends on.
ICLR 2026CauKer
CauKer combines Gaussian-process kernel composition and structural causal models to generate diverse time-series classification pretraining data. Its synthetic data has an explicit causal and temporal grammar. That makes the examples more than random augmentation; they encode plausible generating mechanisms.
ICLR 2026TabStruct
TabStruct measures whether tabular synthetic data preserves structure when real causal graphs are unavailable. This is a practical governance problem: most tabular domains lack a known ground-truth causal graph. The method asks whether synthetic data keeps enough global utility without pretending we know the full causal model.
ICLR 2026Neon
Neon uses sampler-induced synthetic-data bias as negative evidence without requiring new real data. Instead of treating synthetic artifacts only as a flaw, it turns them into diagnostic information. If a sampler consistently fails in a region, that failure tells us something about model uncertainty or data coverage.
ICLR 2026TiTok
TiTok selects informative synthetic examples for adapter transfer without training an extra discriminator. The problem is that synthetic examples are cheap enough to flood training. A selection rule is needed so the adapter sees examples that move transfer rather than merely reinforce generator habits.
ICML 2026FAC Synthesis
FAC Synthesis measures feature activation coverage in sparse-autoencoder space and generates examples for missing model features. This is a targeted synthetic-data strategy. Instead of asking for more data in general, it asks which internal features lack coverage and creates examples to activate them.
ICML 2026SurvDiff
SurvDiff generates synthetic clinical survival data while preserving event-time distributions and censoring mechanisms. Clinical synthetic data fails if it keeps visible covariates but destroys censoring structure. The paper treats downstream survival analysis as the validity test, not visual realism or marginal similarity alone.
ICLR 2026PetaGAIL++
PetaGAIL++ uses imitation learning and differential privacy to generate mobility trajectories without exposing raw routines. It sits at the privacy-utility boundary. The synthetic data has to remain useful for mobility modeling while hiding the individual patterns that make raw trajectory data sensitive.
ICML 2026SceneSmith
SceneSmith combines text-to-3D assets, articulated object retrieval, physical-property estimation, and simulation checks. Synthetic robotics scenes are only useful if they can exist physically. The paper adds validity constraints so generated worlds do not teach policies impossible contacts, objects, or dynamics.
ICML 2026SC-MILP
SC-MILP generates equivalent and perturbed mixed-integer programming instances to address scarce high-leverage branching nodes. Optimization data is hard because the valuable states are rare. The paper creates more of the states where a solver's decision matters, while preserving problem equivalence or controlled perturbation.
ICLR 2026mCLM
mCLM front-loads automated synthesis constraints in molecule generation while improving property scores and synthetic accessibility. In molecule data, an attractive generated compound is worthless if it cannot be made. The paper puts makeability into the data-generating process instead of checking it only afterward.
ICML 2026COMPACT
COMPACT synthesizes complex visual questions by composing atomic capabilities. It treats instruction data as a design space: examples should require combinations of skills, not only isolated recognition. The result is data efficiency through targeted compositional pressure.

4. Provenance, privacy, and rights become model-level constraints

ownership and risk

Once models absorb data, ownership, privacy, memorization, and poisoning become behavioral questions. This cluster asks how to trace models, verify outputs, control use rights, and understand when data exposure becomes extractable memory or safety failure.

ICLR 2026Semantic Watermark Fingerprints
Semantic Watermark Fingerprints aims to verify ownership after finetuning, quantization, and filtering while remaining stealthy. The governance problem is that ownership marks must survive the transformations real models undergo. A provenance signal that disappears after ordinary deployment edits is not useful.
ICLR 2026LLM DNA
LLM DNA defines low-dimensional behavioral representations for tracing model evolution through adaptation and distillation. It treats provenance as behavioral lineage. If weights are changed or hidden, a compact functional fingerprint can still show how one model relates to another.
ICLR 2026Ellipse Signatures
Ellipse Signatures proposes verification from logprob outputs without model input or full weights. That is output-level authentication for settings where the verifier cannot inspect the whole model. The analogy is a symmetric-key check: enough to verify authenticity without exposing the underlying system.
ICML 2026GoodDiffusion
GoodDiffusion conditions high-quality diffusion bridge generation on valid per-input signatures. It makes authorization sample-specific rather than model-global. The data right travels with the input, so generation quality depends on whether that particular use is permitted.
ICML 2026Token Overcharging
Token Overcharging shows that fixed per-token pricing can permit strategic overcharging, while character-count-linear pricing removes the incentive. This is governance at the service boundary. Pricing rules shape model-serving behavior just as data licenses shape training behavior.
ICLR 2026Hubble
Hubble shows memorization depends on sensitive-data frequency relative to corpus size and can decline without continued exposure. Memorization is not a binary property of whether a secret appeared. It depends on repetition, scale, and whether the training stream keeps refreshing the exposure.
ICML 2026LM Memorization Capacity
LM Memorization Capacity estimates how many bits per parameter GPT-style models can store when generalization is eliminated. That turns memorization into a capacity question. It helps separate what a model can store in principle from what it happens to reveal under ordinary data distributions.
ICML 2026AI Engram
AI Engram enables subset memory composition or erasure through linear arithmetic without iterative optimization. The governance implication is direct: if memories can be localized and manipulated, deletion and provenance become operations on model state rather than retraining projects.
ICLR 2026Empirical Privacy Protection Benchmark
Empirical Privacy Protection Benchmark shows privacy vulnerability rises when adaptation data are close to the pretraining distribution, even without direct overlap. That expands privacy risk beyond contamination checks. Similarity between datasets can make private adaptation examples easier to infer or exploit.
ICML 2026Rapid Poison
Rapid Poison induces false negatives by training on unsafe samples where a trigger concept is systematically absent. The attack is subtle because the poisoned signal is an omission, not an obvious malicious label. Governance has to inspect what safety data fails to show, not only what it contains.
ICLR 2026WIMHF
WIMHF finds harmful preference features and shows that relabeling affected examples can improve safety without hurting general performance. The paper treats preference datasets as objects needing inspection and repair. Alignment data can itself contain unsafe incentives, and feature-level analysis can identify which labels should be corrected.
ICLR 2026SSPO
SSPO uses a small amount of labeled feedback plus pseudo-labels over unpaired responses to exceed stronger labeled-only baselines. It is a label-governance paper because it asks how much human preference data is actually necessary. The answer depends on whether pseudo-labeling can expand coverage without importing too much noise.

5. Agent, robot, and multimodal datasets must preserve the world, not just the answer

grounded data

For agents and embodied systems, the useful example includes tools, state, recovery behavior, physical constraints, videos, annotations, and environment execution. The dataset is a record of interaction with a world.

ICLR 2026ScaleCUA
ScaleCUA combines automated agents and human experts to create scalable computer-use training data. Computer-use examples are expensive because they need screen state, action, and intent. The paper builds a closed loop where automation expands coverage and humans keep the trajectories grounded.
ICML 2026RoTS
RoTS generates 800k recovery trajectories through tree-based discovery of diverse GUI error modes. The key idea is that agents need data for what happens after mistakes, not only demonstrations of clean success. Recovery behavior is a separate capability that ordinary task traces underrepresent.
ICML 2026MEnvAgent
MEnvAgent builds verifiable Docker environments and solution trajectories for software-engineering tasks while reducing time cost. It treats the executable environment as part of the dataset. Without reusable environments, code-agent data cannot be reliably checked or reproduced.
ICML 2026TerminalTraj
TerminalTraj generates Docker-aligned tasks and validation code that improve TerminalBench and test-time scaling. The dataset preserves terminal actions and executable validation, not just final patches. That makes training examples closer to the real loop of inspecting, editing, testing, and recovering.
ICML 2026daVinci-Dev
daVinci-Dev preserves tool invocations, test executions, and full information flow as mid-training data. Its value is that tool-use supervision lives in the process. A final answer hides which command revealed the bug, which test failed, and how the agent used that feedback.
ICML 2026OXE-AugE
OXE-AugE rebalances robot-arm and gripper diversity, improving unseen-robot manipulation after fine-tuning. In robotics, imbalance over embodiments becomes a generalization failure. Curation has to cover the physical variety of bodies, not only the task labels.
ICML 2026Continual VLA Forgetting
Continual VLA Forgetting finds that small replay buffers can preserve or recover prior vision-language-action skills while supporting new learning. Embodied data arrives over time as tasks change. The paper shows that what gets replayed can decide whether a robot gains skills or overwrites them.
ICML 2026VideoKR
VideoKR builds a large skill-oriented video reasoning corpus from CC-licensed expert videos with human-in-the-loop generation. The dataset is not just more video. It is organized around capabilities, so the model sees examples that connect visual evidence, expert action, and reasoning skill.
ICML 2026Motive
Motive uses motion-weighted gradients to find fine-tuning clips that improve or degrade temporal dynamics. Video data should be judged by motion signal, not only frame content. The method attributes temporal behavior to specific clips so curators can remove data that hurts dynamics.
ICML 2026Holi-Spatial
Holi-Spatial builds millions of spatial annotations over 3D Gaussian-splatting scenes for VLM fine-tuning. Spatial capability needs multi-level supervision: objects, relations, depth, and scene structure. The paper treats spatial data as a rich annotation stack rather than a captioning problem.
ICLR 2026TRACE
TRACE improves segmentation without instance labels by using foundation-model internals as an annotation source. It converts latent model knowledge into training signal. That is a curation shortcut, but also a governance question: the teacher's internal structure decides what annotations become visible.
ICML 2026Scientific Annotation BC
Scientific Annotation BC shows multi-task pretraining and shared mistake representations can transfer across scientific annotation workflows. Scientific data curation is often expert and procedural. The paper treats workflow traces and common error patterns as supervision that can generalize across annotation tasks.

6. Adaptation, federated, graph, and causal data require structure-aware governance

structured regimes

Many datasets are not independent examples. They are graphs, clients, interventions, modalities, classes over time, adaptation corpora, or source domains. Governance in these settings means preserving structure while deciding what to share, replay, correct, or discard.

ICML 2026PSAHS
PSAHS refines target graph structure using agreement between a structure-aware GNN and an attribute-only MLP under label scarcity. The data problem is that graph edges can be wrong or incomplete. Agreement between two views becomes a conservative signal for which structure should be trusted.
ICLR 2026RULE
RULE treats noisy expert annotations and attribute/entity misalignments in multimodal knowledge graphs as first-class risks. Knowledge graph data can fail through broken correspondences, not just wrong labels. The paper asks how to reason when the link between entity, attribute, and modality is itself uncertain.
ICML 2026GFD-EMVC
GFD-EMVC detects label-noise outliers in gradient space and recalibrates them using feature prototypes. It turns noisy-label correction into a geometry problem. If an example's gradient disagrees with its feature neighborhood, the label may be the part of the dataset that needs repair.
ICLR 2026UniImb
UniImb uses prototype load balancing so majority samples do not dominate imbalanced graph representation learning. In imbalanced graphs, the dataset's class structure can erase minority behavior. Prototype balancing makes representation learning respect minority regions instead of averaging them away.
ICML 2026MFedPBA
MFedPBA aggregates decision prototypes under model heterogeneity and modality imbalance in federated learning. The data is distributed across clients with different models and missing modalities. Governance means sharing enough decision structure to learn together without pretending every client has the same view.
ICML 2026MV-FGAD
MV-FGAD shows subtle attribute or structural anomalies become harder to detect when client subgraphs are partitioned. Federated graph learning changes the anomaly signal because each client sees only part of the graph. The paper exposes a governance cost of partitioning data for privacy or ownership.
ICML 2026GR-LoRA
GR-LoRA balances class-incremental stability and plasticity through gradient decomposition, entropy-based module selection, and OOD mismatch suppression. Continual data creates a conflict between new-class learning and old-class retention. The method governs adaptation by separating gradients that should update from gradients that should be constrained.
ICML 2026SDFT
SDFT learns multiple skills over time while preserving prior capabilities better than ordinary supervised fine-tuning. It uses demonstration-conditioned behavior as a stabilizing source of supervision. In data terms, new skill examples should not be allowed to overwrite the model's previous usable behavior.
ICML 2026DiSC
DiSC updates parametric knowledge from adaptation corpora while preserving instruction following, reasoning, and factual skills. The adaptation corpus is split from the context that protects general behavior. That separation lets data update knowledge without becoming a broad destructive fine-tune.
ICLR 2026Revela
Revela trains specialized-domain retrieval without query-document labels and outperforms supervised and proprietary retrievers on hard benchmarks. It matters because labels are often missing exactly where retrieval is most valuable. The method asks how to curate retrieval signal from domain evidence without paired supervision.
ICML 2026OU Identifiability
OU Identifiability shows that one intervention per strongly connected component can generically identify dynamics up to scale. For causal data, the question is not how many observations exist but which interventions expose the structure. This is data collection as experimental design.
ICML 2026Unpaired Causal IV
Unpaired Causal IV proves consistency as environments grow even when observations per environment remain constant. It loosens the usual demand for dense repeated measurements. The value is showing that many sparse environments can identify causal structure when repetition within each environment is limited.
ICML 2026FlashOptim
FlashOptim reduces checkpoint and optimizer memory enough to make large-model fine-tuning more accessible without API changes. Although it is a systems paper, it affects governance because who can adapt models depends on memory cost. Lowering that cost broadens who can run curated fine-tuning locally rather than through opaque APIs.
ICML 2026Self-Soupervision
Self-Soupervision uses unlabeled data and diverse self-supervised recipes to create robust parameter-averaged models. It treats recipe diversity as a way to harvest more signal from the same unlabeled pool. The governed object is not only the dataset, but the set of training views through which the dataset is consumed.
EVIDENCE

What would prove a data-governance method worked

The proof has to connect the data intervention to the learned behavior. A higher score is not enough unless the paper can show which property of the evidence changed the model.

Source audit

The corpus, license, owner, privacy boundary, or allowed-use rule remains inspectable after filtering, synthesis, fine-tuning, quantization, or deployment.

Fixed target

The claimed capability, safety property, privacy threat, causal question, or economic utility is held steady while the data route changes.

Intervention ablation

Changing timing, mixture, selection rule, synthetic generator, replay buffer, watermark, or repair label changes behavior for the predicted reason.

Structure preservation

The curated data keeps the structure the task needs: censoring in survival data, physics in robot scenes, motion in video, graph correspondence, causal interventions, or executable tool traces.

Adversarial check

The method survives realistic attempts to game it: strategic sellers, poisoned omissions, memorization attacks, biased retrieval, redundant examples, or provenance removal.

Downstream trace

The final model behavior can be traced back to the data decision: which examples entered, when they arrived, what they cost, what they repaired, and what they were allowed to teach.

What this theme says about ICML and ICLR

The old version of data work was collection: scrape, label, filter, train. The version visible here is closer to supply-chain control. The papers ask how to price data, trace it, repair it, synthesize it, delete its memories, schedule it, and prove that it taught the intended behavior.

The important connection is that governance and curation are no longer separate from model capability. A better curriculum can create reasoning. A poisoned omission can erase safety. A replay buffer can preserve robot skills. A provenance signature can define who is allowed to use a generator. Data is becoming an active mechanism for steering the model, not merely the raw material consumed by training.

CONCEPT BRIDGES

Where to read the larger pattern

Data as training pressure

Examples matter through the useful model movement they cause under the actual training conditions.

Synthetic data as evidence or contamination

Generated data helps when it adds controlled evidence and hurts when it feeds back model bias.

Privacy and unlearning are recoverable information claims

Governance depends on what can still be extracted, inferred, or influenced after training.
