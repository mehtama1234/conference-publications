Representation geometry

The theme is about the shape of learned information. Models do not only store facts or fit labels; they carve data into directions, manifolds, spectra, clusters, circuits, symmetries, and latent coordinates that determine what transfers, what composes, and what breaks.

Problem

Accuracy tells us whether a system worked on a test set, but it does not tell us what internal structure made the success possible. The same score can hide brittle shortcuts, unused dimensions, collapsed features, overfit camera priors, or a representation that cannot compose new concepts.

Approaches

The papers use spectra, ranks, manifolds, equivariances, graph metrics, hyperbolic and Wasserstein geometry, feature circuits, latent dynamics, topology, and controlled interventions to make the model's internal coordinate system observable.

Connecting read

Read this page as the geometry layer underneath many other themes. It explains why some models transfer, why some reason, why some agents generalize, and why some safety edits or modality alignments fail despite high benchmark numbers.

The first-principles bridge for positives, negatives, hard examples, triplet limits, grounding, and pair-governed representation geometry.

TOPIC 01

The Big Picture

Representation geometry starts from a simple observation: a model's behavior is constrained by how it has organized information internally. If similar objects are near each other for the wrong reason, the model will generalize along the wrong axis. If concepts are entangled, editing one concept will disturb another. If a graph model stores environment-specific distortions instead of invariant structure, it will fail out of distribution. If a language model's logits or hidden states have low-rank structure, that structure may explain both efficiency and blind spots. The geometry is not decoration around the model; it is the working surface on which learning, transfer, reasoning, and control happen.

The important production question is what can be done once the geometry is visible. Some papers diagnose: they measure spectra, topology, fixed points, phase transitions, collapse, or confounding. Some papers prescribe: they add equivariance, orthogonality, metric structure, hyperbolic uncertainty, manifold constraints, or Wasserstein aggregation. Some papers exploit geometry directly: they route experts, align modalities, upsample features, edit concepts, recover correspondences, or generate physical structure. The connecting idea is that the representation should expose the variables the task actually depends on and suppress variables that are incidental, private, unstable, or impossible to transfer.

TOPIC 02

The concrete object is the learned coordinate system

A representation-geometry paper is about the coordinate system the model uses: directions, subspaces, spectra, manifolds, metrics, invariances, topology, factors, bindings, or physical coordinates.

What must stay fixed is the task-relevant relation. Concept factors should preserve compositional meaning. Transfer maps should preserve shared structure across domains. Invariances should preserve what should not change under viewpoint, coordinate, language, or noise transformations. Safety edits should remove sensitive directions while preserving task cues. Physical representations should preserve the geometry of objects, poses, forces, or trajectories.

What may change is the basis, metric, alignment, projection, equivariant layer, latent path, topology diagnostic, or control direction. The method works when the new geometry makes behavior more predictable, transferable, editable, or physically coherent. The failure boundary is beautiful geometry without behavioral authority: the plot, rank, or manifold looks clean but does not preserve the relation the task actually uses.

FAILURE MODES

How clean geometry can be the wrong geometry

A representation-geometry claim fails when the coordinate system looks interpretable but does not control the relation the task depends on.

Axis illusion

A direction, factor, or concept appears meaningful in plots but is not causal for behavior under intervention or transfer.

Invariant erasure

The method removes variation that the task actually needed, such as camera geometry, syntax, pose, timing, or domain-specific structure.

Alignment drift

Two spaces appear aligned by average metric while rare domains, chained translations, local definitions, or physical coordinates break.

Collapse masking

Rank, spectrum, or topology diagnostics look stable while usable dimensions, minority concepts, or long-tail distinctions disappear.

Unsafe edit

A privacy, fairness, or safety projection removes the measured direction while preserving a recoverable proxy or damaging task cues.

Physical mismatch

A latent geometry produces plausible samples while breaking object pose, force, depth, material, trajectory, or molecular constraints.

TOPIC 03

What The Geometry Explains

Composition

Compositional generalization needs separable factors. Papers on orthogonal concept factors, multiplicative binding, analogy, grammar substructure, and reasoning dimensionality ask when a representation can recombine parts instead of memorizing whole examples.

Transfer

Transfer depends on which directions are stable across domains. Graph pretraining, domain adaptation, embedding translation, modality alignment, and federated personalization all become questions about whether two systems share enough geometry to communicate.

Invariance

Many failures come from encoding coordinates that should have been quotiented out. Equivariance, camera awareness, physics-compatible attention, depth-ray targets, and noise-coordinate invariance are all ways to control what transformations should or should not change the representation.

Collapse

Representations can lose usable dimensions, homogenize spikes, over-specialize experts, or flatten hierarchy. Geometry gives a vocabulary for seeing collapse before it appears as a downstream failure.

Mechanism

Some papers look for causal internal units: neuron-basis circuits, continuation clusters, relation algebra, fixed-point trajectories, attractors, and latent vector fields. The goal is to connect coordinates to mechanisms, not just visualization.

Physical Structure

In 3D, robotics, protein, motion, maps, and rendering, the learned space has to respect real geometry. Here representation quality is not abstract; it determines whether a generated object, pose, map, or trajectory is physically coherent.

TOPIC 04

The Main Research Moves

1. Features become useful when they factor the world

A recurring claim in this theme is that generalization requires the model to represent the right parts separately. Compositional Generalization Requires Linear Orthogonal Representations makes this explicit by deriving linear per-concept decomposition and orthogonality as necessary geometry for recombining concepts. Concept Binding studies the complementary operation: once factors exist, a model still needs a low-complexity way to bind them together, and multiplicative binding gives a controlled route. Emergent Analogical Reasoning treats analogy as correspondence inference between categories, so the representation has to support mapping structure from one category to another rather than matching surface features. Grammar Substructure and T-SAE show the language version of the same idea: useful representations separate high-level semantic structure from local syntax and make grammar-aligned internal features easier to reuse.

2. Spectra, rank, and dimensionality reveal hidden constraints

Several papers use linear algebra as a microscope. Low-Rank LM Logits finds low approximate rank in prompt-response logit matrices, suggesting that language model behavior often lives in a smaller subspace than the vocabulary size implies. Embedding Collapse proves that too-small Euclidean embeddings can violate a large fraction of realizable contrastive triplets, which turns dimensionality from a tuning detail into a hard geometric bottleneck. Reasoning Dimensionality connects lower intrinsic dimensionality of reasoning strategies with better in-distribution and out-of-distribution GSM8K performance. Phase Retrieval Dynamics shows why late-stage training can be governed by spectral tails, where low-variance directions dominate the remaining error after fast initial alignment. BBP Transitions brings the same spectral view to initialization, identifying when Hessian outliers reveal planted signal information.

3. Transfer works when two spaces can be aligned without lying

The transfer papers ask whether two learned coordinate systems preserve the same task-relevant relations. GraphGlue uses adaptive orthogonal frames and prototyping to measure and improve cross-domain graph transfer. Embedding Translation studies the hazards of translating between embedding spaces, especially out-of-distribution inputs, mixed-model paths, and chained translations where geometric error compounds. Diffract shows that continual pretraining can preserve singular spectra while rotating singular vectors and changing domain-specific heads, which explains how two models can look spectrally similar but behave differently. FedARC handles the federated version, where fine-grained feature heterogeneity across clients has to be corrected without erasing useful personalization. AnyUp is the practical vision case: arbitrary vision features can be upsampled at inference time only if the method respects enough generic structure across encoders.

4. Invariance is a promise about what should not matter

Many representation failures are failures of invariance. Camera-Aware MLLM and DepthLM show that RGB-only spatial models can overfit camera distributions unless camera geometry is made explicit. RECM relaxes equivariance layer by layer, treating symmetry as something to calibrate from data rather than impose uniformly. ENGNN enforces equivariance to nodewise noise transformations so arbitrary coordinate choices do not inflate sample complexity. OENN/CENN extends equivariant approximation to non-invertible compositional categorical symmetries, broadening the class of transformations that neural networks can respect. ReViT, RodriNet, and Depth Anything 3 bring the principle into physics and geometry tasks: representations should not violate the transformation rules of the world they are modeling.

5. Manifolds and metric spaces make latent movement meaningful

A model's latent space is useful only if movement inside it corresponds to meaningful change. Riemannian Metric Matching learns the metric itself through denoising, giving diffusion a graph-free way to reason about local geometry. Wasserstein GPCA looks for principal modes of variation in probability measures as geodesic curves rather than ordinary linear directions. HTI uses least-action and manifold priors to infer geodesic behavior paths induced by hyperparameters. Diffusion Spacetime argues that diffusion states should be represented as both sample and time so the geometry does not degenerate at terminal noise. Flow Sampling and Manifold Perturbations make this operational for generative models: sampling and perturbation need to respect intrinsic constraints, not just ambient coordinates.

6. Geometry can expose mechanisms, not just embeddings

A stronger use of representation geometry is to identify what internal mechanism causes a behavior. Neuron-Basis Circuits finds small sets of native neurons controlling subject-verb agreement and multi-hop city-state-capital reasoning, treating neurons as a causal feature basis rather than interchangeable units. Shared Semantics Divergent Mechanisms uses a rate-distortion view to cluster continuation modes while trading off semantic coherence and mechanistic consistency. Detecting the Semantic Fixed Point treats Transformer layers as fixed-point iterations and uses trajectory geometry for early exit. Dynamics Reveals Structure studies negation, converse, and composition as relation algebra, making logical propagation a geometric constraint. Latent Space Dynamics extracts prior knowledge from attractors in latent vector fields even without input data.

7. Geometry is also a safety and control surface

When a representation separates sensitive or unsafe directions from task-relevant directions, intervention becomes more precise. OCE separates concept direction from neuron magnitude and angular geometry for scalable multi-concept erasure. OPL/G-OPL removes privacy-sensitive subspaces while preserving task cues through projection and cosine alignment. Fair Posthoc Control optimizes feature space so post-processing fair classifiers get a better fairness-accuracy frontier. LM Geometry Diagnostics is an important caution: effective rank can reflect training hyperparameters more than downstream performance, so geometric diagnostics have to be validated against behavior. Persistent Homology LLM Adversarial Influence adds a nonlinear topology lens for detecting attack-induced changes across layers, architectures, and model sizes.

8. Physical and multimodal models need coordinates that correspond to the world

In embodied and generative systems, bad representation geometry shows up as bad physical structure. PanoWorld-X uses panoramic Plucker embeddings so large-motion scene evolution is controlled by 3D motion geometry. RoSE estimates intermediate normal maps from shading variation to reduce 3D misalignment. Generative Human Geometry Distribution uses staged flow training for pose-conditioned avatars and novel poses. RadioGS uses Gaussian surfels and ray tracing for relighting and material disentanglement. DeCoDe, TEDBench/MiAE, and TD-JEPA show the same idea in molecules, proteins, and control: the representation has to encode the stable geometry of objects and dynamics before higher-level behavior can transfer.

Compression preserves or destroys capability
