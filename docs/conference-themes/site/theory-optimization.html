This lane is about the mathematical objects underneath modern ML practice. The theory papers are not separate from deployment. They ask why optimizers move, why attention generalizes, why scaling laws appear, when games converge, how samplers behave, what representations identify, and which guarantees survive the constraints of real systems.

FIRST PRINCIPLE

Theory Is The Compression Layer

A field with hundreds of methods needs more than leaderboards. It needs objects that make methods comparable: losses, spectra, kernels, games, distributions, manifolds, gradients, equilibria, samplers, and certificates. Theory does not remove engineering detail. It names the structure that engineering keeps rediscovering.

The recurring move in this lane is to replace a recipe with a sharper object. Adam is not just an optimizer but a dynamical system near degenerate minima. Attention is not just a layer but a learned spectrum and association mechanism. Scaling is not just a curve but an interaction among data, context, reuse, and source complexity. A benchmark is not just a score but a finite-sample certification problem.

SUBTHEME 01

Optimization Dynamics

Training is a trajectory through geometry, not a recipe list.

These papers study the motion of optimizers, gradients, curvature, momentum, spectra, and parameterization. The question is not only whether a method reaches low loss, but what structure makes that movement possible and when the same recipe breaks.

ICML 2026

Adam Degeneracy

Shows Adam can achieve local linear convergence on highly degenerate objectives without extra schedulers. The point is that adaptivity changes the local geometry seen by the optimizer. Degenerate flat directions do not automatically imply slow progress if the update rule rescales movement in the right coordinates.

ICLR 2026

DR-Submodular Biased Gradients

Extends Lyapunov analysis to stochastic biased gradients under structured convex constraints. The paper matters because practical gradients are often biased by approximation, sampling, or batching. The theory asks when a descent certificate survives that imperfection instead of assuming ideal gradients.

ICML 2026

Jacobi Spectral Reconstruction

Uses Jacobi-preconditioned gradient descent for condition-number-independent linear convergence in spectral sparse recovery. The method changes the geometry of the problem before optimizing it. Preconditioning is treated as the difference between a hard landscape and one where the relevant sparse spectrum can be recovered efficiently.

ICML 2026

Modern Conservation Laws

Characterizes gradient-flow invariants for GELU, SiLU, SwiGLU, attention, and mixture-of-experts designs. Conservation laws reveal hidden quantities that training preserves. That matters because two architectures can have the same loss surface story but different invariant structure under gradient flow.

ICML 2026

DiReCT

Selects annealing samples whose gradients align with flat validation-Hessian eigendirections while constraining stiff-direction energy. It treats late training as movement through curvature. Good data is not just high quality; it should move the model along directions that improve validation without shaking brittle directions.

ICML 2026

Gradient Flow Through Diagram Expansions

Uses diagram expansions to characterize lazy, rich, NTK, and mean-field learning regimes. The paper gives language for when training mostly changes the readout versus when representation itself moves. That distinction is central to explaining why overparameterized models can either behave like kernels or learn new features.

ICML 2026

SVRG and Beyond via Posterior Correction

Uses richer Gaussian posterior families to introduce stochastic variance-reduced Hessian corrections beyond standard gradient-correction SVRG. The optimization object is the posterior over corrections, not just a variance estimate. That gives second-order information a probabilistic route into stochastic training.

ICLR 2026

FIRE

Uses deviation from isometry and Newton-Schulz iteration to tune reinitialization across vision, language, and RL. It formalizes the stability-plasticity reset problem: new learning should move enough to adapt while preserving geometry that makes old representations usable.

SUBTHEME 02

Representation Geometry

Models generalize through the shape of their hidden spaces.

Representation papers ask what geometry is learned by attention, kernels, embeddings, Jacobians, and latent spaces. The output behavior is downstream of those shapes: spectra, rank, alignment, collapse, and equivariance decide what the model can recombine.

ICML 2026

Single-Head Attention in High Dimensions

Links learned query-key singular spectra to test error, interpolation thresholds, and recovery thresholds in a solvable attention model. Attention is reduced to a geometry where singular directions decide what information is recoverable. That makes generalization visible inside the learned spectrum.

ICML 2026

Why Deep Jacobian Spectra Separate

Explains low-rank Jacobian structure through depth-induced singular-value scaling and fixed-gate masked products. The paper studies the derivative of the network as a representation object. Spectral separation says depth can create privileged directions even before downstream labels are considered.

ICML 2026

Gaussian SIM

Proves alternating gradient-flow convergence for jointly learning a projection direction and univariate link. It isolates a simple representation-learning problem: find the direction that makes a nonlinear response predictable. The theory clarifies when projection and link learning cooperate rather than chase each other.

ICML 2026

Uncovering Latent Potential

Uses spectral layer selection and geometric regularization to preserve isotropic, discriminative embedding geometry during fine-tuning. The failure mode is hidden: fine-tuning can improve a task while damaging the latent geometry that made the model broadly useful. The method keeps the embedding space usable.

ICML 2026

Dynamics Reveals Structure

Shows relation composition creates an obstruction for first-order update propagation and may explain editing or reasoning failures. It treats model editing as a dynamical problem inside representation space. If updates cannot propagate through composed relations, local edits will not produce global reasoning changes.

ICLR 2026

Wasserstein GPCA

Lifts Gaussian computations to invertible maps and extends principal-component style geometry to absolutely continuous measures through neural geodesics. The object is not a vector cloud but a distributional manifold. Geometry becomes transport between probability measures.

ICML 2026

NSE Index Models

Identifies an activation-driven exponent governing statistical-to-computational gaps in single- and multi-index models. It studies when the representation is statistically present but computationally hard to learn. The activation function itself changes that gap.

ICML 2026

Stochastic Transformers

Shows random initialization noise prevents deterministic single-cluster collapse and can create antipodal attractors. The result makes randomness part of representation geometry. Noise is not merely nuisance; it can decide whether training collapses or separates into useful structure.

SUBTHEME 03

Games, Markets, and Constraints

Learning systems contain incentives, opponents, and feasible sets.

These papers make optimization social or constrained: agents share constraints, sellers price reusable data, learners face strategic reporting, and algorithms must respect fairness or feasibility. The correct object is often an equilibrium or feasible payoff set, not one loss minimum.

ICML 2026

Asymmetric Perturbation

Perturbs one player's payoff so the perturbed equilibrium coincides with the original equilibrium. The point is to understand how equilibrium can be preserved under asymmetric changes. That matters when learning algorithms or incentives alter only one side of a game.

ICML 2026

RQE Actor-Critic

Provides finite-sample guarantees by targeting a more regular bounded-rational equilibrium concept. Instead of assuming perfectly rational equilibrium behavior, it chooses an object that is smoother and statistically estimable. That makes multi-agent learning more measurable.

ICML 2026

Nash Equilibria with Coupling Constraints

Proves equilibrium existence under playerwise concave shared constraints using feasible-set topology. The shared constraint is the central object: each player's action changes what is feasible for others. Existence theory matters before optimization can claim to find anything.

ICML 2026

FTRL Lower Bounds

Proves FTRL can require exponential time to reach Nash equilibria in two-player potential games. The result warns against equating no-regret style dynamics with fast convergence. Even structured games can hide hard paths for common learning dynamics.

ICML 2026

Data Market Pricing

Uses piecewise-linear convex pricing to recover approximate seller stability in oligopolistic reusable-data markets. Data is non-rival: one dataset can serve many buyers. Pricing theory therefore has to account for strategic sellers and repeated reuse rather than ordinary one-good sale logic.

ICML 2026

Fair OT

Combines fair Sinkhorn, penalized optimal-transport guarantees, and bilevel learned costs for unseen-data fairness. Fairness is encoded as geometry over transported mass. The method asks how to move distributions while preserving fairness constraints that may not be visible in the training sample alone.

ICML 2026

Feasible Payoffs

Estimates the full set of payoffs consistent with observed equilibrium play under Hausdorff error. Instead of guessing one reward function, the paper characterizes the set of payoff structures that could explain behavior. That is the right uncertainty object for inverse game analysis.

ICML 2026

First-Price Auctions

Derives polynomial convergence rates with explicit discretization and approximation tradeoffs. The learning problem is strategic bidding under an auction mechanism. The theory shows how approximation choices affect whether convergence claims remain meaningful.

SUBTHEME 04

Sampling and Inference

Inference is controlled approximation under uncertainty.

Probabilistic theory here asks how to sample, infer latent state, compare distributions, and quantify uncertainty when exact computation is unavailable. The focus is not only correctness in the limit, but memory, dimension, variance, and finite-sample behavior.

ICML 2026

RED-HDP-HMM

Uses Gibbs inference for nonstationary state durations, outperforming sticky HDP-HMM variants. The hidden-state model is made more realistic by letting durations vary. Inference improves because the latent process better matches temporal persistence rather than forcing stationarity.

ICML 2026

Mixtures Closest To A Given Measure

Uses a relaxation framework to infer cluster count and initialize local clustering methods. The target is not merely fitting a mixture but finding the mixture closest to a measure under a tractable relaxation. It turns model selection into a geometric approximation problem.

ICML 2026

Dimension-Free Diffusion Sampling

Combines low-degree approximation and collocation for polylogarithmic sampler complexity in accuracy. The paper asks when diffusion sampling can avoid direct dependence on ambient dimension. That matters because high-dimensional coordinates often exaggerate the true difficulty of the support.

ICML 2026

FlashSinkhorn

Streams optimal-transport tiles through SRAM with fused Triton kernels to reduce memory traffic and scale transport optimization. It is inference theory meeting hardware reality: Sinkhorn iterations are only useful at scale if memory movement is controlled as carefully as arithmetic.

ICML 2026

Bulk-Calibrated Credal Sets

Translates upper expectation over credal sets into tractable mean-plus-sup distributionally robust objectives. The uncertainty object is a set of plausible distributions, not one posterior. The method makes that set usable inside optimization without discarding ambiguity.

ICML 2026

KDE Kernel Algebra

Improves epsilon dependence while proving hardness limits for related kernel access problems. Kernel density estimation becomes an algebraic access problem: which queries can be answered efficiently, and which require real work regardless of implementation?

ICML 2026

LC-PB-SCM

Uses probability generating functions and trie representations to identify Poisson branching causal patterns under latent confounding. The latent branching process is encoded in a representation that makes identification possible. This turns causal inference into structured symbolic-probabilistic recovery.

ICLR 2026

Hawkes Representer Theorem

Converts RKHS triggering-kernel estimation into finite transformed-kernel expansions. Temporal point-process inference becomes finite-dimensional without abandoning nonparametric structure. That is the value of a representer theorem: it tells us where the infinite problem actually lives.

SUBTHEME 05

Scaling, Data, and Generalization

More is not one variable.

Scaling papers split "more" into data size, context length, data reuse, source complexity, high-quality response coverage, and sample selection. They ask when extra material is useful and when the bottleneck is the structure of the data or objective.

ICLR 2026

Length Generalization Bounds

Compares infinity-norm versus average error and softmax versus finite-precision attention settings. Length generalization depends on what error notion is being bounded and how attention is implemented. The paper makes extrapolation a concrete mathematical property rather than a vague hope.

ICML 2026

MTS Difficulty

Analyzes normalized data-weight dynamics and improves multi-task selection with larger batches and distribution/training features. The problem is that data difficulty is dynamic: a sample's value depends on the current training state and the other tasks it competes with.

ICLR 2026

Difficult Examples Hurt Unsupervised Contrastive Learning

Shows difficult examples can worsen unsupervised contrastive generalization and that removal can improve downstream classification despite smaller sample size. The result challenges the instinct to keep all hard data. Difficulty can inject noise rather than reusable structure.

ICLR 2026

Coverage Principle

Defines coverage as pretrained probability mass on high-quality responses needed for Best-of-N and post-training. The model can only amplify what pretraining made reachable. Coverage turns post-training success into a property of the base distribution, not just the reward procedure.

ICLR 2026

Intrinsic Entropy

Argues training dataset size can dictate an optimal context length and bound context-length scaling. Context is not free capacity. If data entropy is too small, longer context can exceed what training statistics support, making context length a data-dependent quantity.

ICLR 2026

Multi-Epoch Scaling

Defines a comparison between K-epoch reuse and one-pass fresh-data scaling. The question is what repeated exposure buys when fresh data is scarce. The paper separates compute scaling from data novelty and asks when reuse behaves like new evidence.

ICML 2026

Language Generation in the Limit

Shows computable generation in the limit can still be sample-infeasible for regular, context-free, and stricter formal-language classes. Learnability and practical sample complexity diverge. A generator may be possible in principle while requiring unrealistic data.

ICLR 2026

Shallow NN Scaling Laws

Derives excess-risk exponents for quadratic and diagonal networks as sample complexity and weight decay vary. Scaling laws become analyzable in simple networks where the role of regularization can be isolated. That provides a baseline for interpreting larger empirical curves.

SUBTHEME 06

Verification and Interpretability

A claim about a model needs an object that can be checked.

This subtheme covers lower bounds, certification, attribution, constraints, and explanation. The common thread is accountability: a theorem, verifier, or interpretable object must say what has been proven, identified, or ruled out.

ICML 2026

Secret Memory Lower Bounds

Introduces a multi-player communication-game technique for lower bounds on private medians, quantiles, and max-select. The contribution is a proof tool for unavoidable memory costs. Privacy requirements are not only policy constraints; they can force more space.

ICML 2026

BTT Algorithms

Relates BTT and correlation-clustering optima within a 3/2 factor on complete graphs and proves shared hardness. It clarifies what approximation can be expected before engineering begins. If two objectives share hardness, switching names does not remove the barrier.

ICML 2026

TRECA

Handles nuisance-parameter error and possible monotonicity or unconfoundedness violations in responder classification. The paper is about robustness of a causal classification claim. It asks what can still be inferred when the assumptions are imperfect rather than pretending the nuisance model is exact.

ICML 2026

SC-MILP

Learns depth-aware branch-and-bound node representations through dynamic stratified contrastive training. MILP solving is made observable through the search tree. The learned representation is judged by whether it helps choose nodes in a verifiable combinatorial process.

ICLR 2026

Distributional Equivalence

Uses rank constraints to traverse equivalence classes and recover models up to identifiable structure. The important word is equivalence: some details cannot be identified from data. The theory separates recoverable structure from arbitrary parameterization.

ICLR 2026

Pi-net

Enforces convex output constraints through operator-splitting projections and implicit differentiation. The verifier is built into the architecture: outputs are projected into the feasible set. That is stronger than penalizing violations after the fact.

ICML 2026

FFOLayer

Computes approximate hypergradients for optimization layers using near-constant first-order information. It makes differentiable optimization layers cheaper to use inside learning systems. The question is how much gradient information is enough to tune a layer without solving a full second-order problem.

ICLR 2026

Provable NAM Explanations

Exploits neural additive model structure to reduce sufficient-explanation search to logarithmically many verification queries. The explanation is not just a visualization; it is a query-efficient certificate over an additive model. Interpretability becomes a property that can be verified.

FIELD READ

What This Lane Says About ICML and ICLR

The theory lane says the conference is no longer satisfied with recipes that work only as folklore. The mathematical work is trying to identify which parts of modern systems are real objects: spectra, equilibria, coverage mass, feasible sets, latent manifolds, and finite-sample certificates.

This is the natural fifth lane after safety, agents, data/systems, and physical generation. Those lanes describe what systems do. This lane asks why those behaviors are possible, when they fail, and what can be guaranteed when engineering constraints are part of the problem.
