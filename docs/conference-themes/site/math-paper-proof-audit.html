This Is The Claim-Check Layer

A paper can name the right object but still fail to prove that the object is the one driving performance. This audit asks the stricter question: which proof sketch is the paper using, and what evidence would make that sketch credible?

Use this page when expanding lane cards. If a row says the proof sketch is measurement, the paper needs calibration or uncertainty evidence. If the sketch is feasibility, it needs violation and boundary evidence. If the sketch is spectral visibility, it needs stable directions above noise.

BIG PICTURE

What This Audit Adds

The table turns "why it works" into an evidence demand. A paper that relies on invariance needs invariance tests. A paper that relies on tail sampling needs proposal and variance checks. A paper that relies on update geometry needs directional movement evidence. A paper that relies on identification needs explicit assumptions and alternatives.

That gives the atlas a stronger standard for future expansion: every paper card should state not only the mathematical object, but also which proof sketch it uses and what observation would make that sketch credible.
