Evaluation and safety as measurement under pressure

This page expands the evaluation and safety lane at paper level. The common mathematical problem is that a score is only a reading from an instrument. The real target may be ability, risk, compliance, behavior under pressure, privacy, provenance, value, or trust, and those targets are not directly visible. The hard work is designing the measuring instrument.

FIRST PRINCIPLE

The Score Is Not The Capability

A benchmark score is produced by a task distribution, item set, judge, rubric, sampling plan, stopping rule, and failure taxonomy. It is not the capability itself. A high score can mean real ability, benchmark overfit, judge bias, shortcut use, lucky sampling, or hidden reliance on a modality that should not have mattered.

Safety has the same structure. A model can look safe under ordinary samples and fail under user pressure, prompt injection, fine-tuning access, rare bad cases, hidden internal channels, or deployment constraints. The mathematical move across these papers is to choose what will be observed and define the harmful event, forbidden boundary, checker, remaining-doubt set, or tradeoff surface before making the safety claim.

FORMULA PASS

Paper-Specific Math Boxes

ATLAS

Object. A model skill we cannot see directly, estimated by choosing questions whose answers would reduce real doubt.

Why it works. A teacher learns little from giving a student a question that is obviously too easy or impossibly hard. The useful question is near the student's current boundary, where different still-possible skill levels would answer differently. What must stay the same is the skill being measured. What may change is which question comes next as doubt narrows. Names used later: latent ability for the hidden skill, Fisher information for how much a question can separate still-plausible skill levels, and posterior for revised belief after answers.

choose the next question that most reduces doubt about the model's current ability estimate

Evidence check. Report remaining doubt, item-information gain, agreement with held-out full tests, and whether the adaptive path changes when the initial ability estimate is perturbed.

FRABench And UFEval

Object. Like a report card with separate grades, the evaluation shows grounding, factuality, instruction following, coherence, style, and task-specific quality separately.

Why it works. One final grade can hide the failure that matters. What must stay the same is that each claimed ability is measured on its own. What may change is how those abilities are weighted for a user or task. Splitting the rubric prevents a model from hiding a severe local failure behind high unrelated scores. Name used later: ability vector.

score(m) = w dot q(m), but validity needs q_k(m) stable and separately measured for each aspect k

Evidence check. Show aspect reliability, inter-rater agreement per component, aggregate-ranking reversals under plausible weights, and failure examples where the total score hides the broken dimension.

Micro-Benchmarking Reliability

Object. A leaderboard rank treated as a claim made from a small sample of questions.

Why it works. If two runners finish a short race almost together, one race is weak evidence about who is faster. A benchmark has the same problem. What must stay the same is the capability comparison being claimed. What may change is the sampled items. The method works by asking whether the ordering would likely survive another draw of similar questions. Name used later: finite-sample uncertainty.

claim A > B only when P(S_A - S_B > 0 | sampled items) is high under resampling

Evidence check. Include bootstrap intervals, item-count sensitivity, pairwise reversal rates, and the smallest benchmark size that preserves the claimed ordering.

Rare Event Analysis And Tail Risks

Object. The chance of a named harmful event, not the average benchmark score.

Why it works. A rare dangerous driving mistake can matter more than thousands of normal turns. Ordinary random testing may almost never visit that case. What must stay the same is the definition of the harmful event. What may change is how often the test deliberately visits risky-looking regions. The estimate is then corrected so it still describes deployment frequency. Names used later: importance sampling for targeted-then-corrected measurement, and tail for the rare edge of outcomes.

failure risk = chance of named bad event, estimated by oversampling the risky region and correcting back

Evidence check. State the event grammar, report confidence intervals, compare several sampling rules, and show sensitivity to the boundary of the harmful-event set.

CSPO, SecFid, And SandboxEscapeBench

Object. Like a marked safety line on a factory floor, the evaluation defines which actions are allowed and how close the system gets before crossing.

Why it works. What may change is the policy update, prompt-injection attempt, or tool trajectory. What must stay the same is the deployed safety boundary. Safety becomes concrete when the evaluator asks how close the system is to violation and which movement would cross the line. Name used later: allowed set or forbidden state.

maximize useful behavior while keeping every checked action inside the allowed set

Evidence check. Measure distance to violation, security-fidelity frontiers, reachable forbidden states, and attacks that approach the boundary through different action routes.

RLVepsR And Weak-Strong Verification

Object. A checker whose mistakes become training instructions.

Why it works. A coach helps only when the advice points toward better play. If the coach rewards the wrong move, practice makes the player worse. What must stay the same is real task success. What may change is the checker quality and whether expensive review is used. Name used later: gradient for the training push created by the checker signal.

useful update needs checker push to point in the same direction as true task success

Evidence check. Sweep verifier false positives and false negatives, measure learn/stall/anti-learn regimes, audit disagreement cases, and calibrate when the weak verifier should defer.

Gaussian Certified Unlearning

Object. A deletion claim tested by whether the released model looks like one that never saw the deleted data.

Why it works. Noise creates overlap between the after-deletion model and the retrained-without-that-data reference. The question is not whether parameters changed, but whether an attacker can distinguish the post-unlearning model from the retrained reference through the allowed observation channel.

deletion is credible when attacker observations cannot tell unlearned model from retrained-without-data model

Evidence check. Compare to retraining references, run membership and reconstruction attacks, report utility loss, and state exactly which observation channel the certificate protects.

Catch-22 And Watermarking

Object. A tradeoff between mark strength, false accusations, edit survival, and generation quality.

Why it works. A hidden stamp must be strong enough to find later, but not so strong that it ruins the page or falsely marks clean pages. What must stay the same is the ability to tell marked from unmarked output under stated edits. What may change is mark strength, detector threshold, and output quality. Name used later: KL, one way to measure how much the marked output pattern differs from the clean one.

choose mark strength so detection rises while false positives and quality loss stay bounded

Evidence check. Report ROC curves, edit robustness, false-positive rates on clean and human data, generation-quality movement, and detector transfer across domains.

VALUEFLOW And SafeDPO

Object. Human values as several scored concerns with different strength, not one global number.

Why it works. Ranking alone says which answer won, not how much was at stake or which value moved. Intensity and frontier measurements preserve the tradeoff: helpfulness may improve by spending safety, fairness, or user-group value. Optimization can then avoid making hidden sacrifices look like improvement.

improve helpfulness while explicitly tracking safety risk and how strongly each value moved

Evidence check. Show preference-strength calibration, helpfulness-safety curves, subgroup disagreement, and cases where scalar reward hides a high-intensity value loss.

Omni-Reward, VISUALSWAP, And FlowGuard

Object. Like judging a movie by watching the scene, not only reading the caption, the object is the evidence channel the judge or guard actually sees.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the evidence the claim depends on: text, image, video, action trace, causal route, or fused state. What may change is which channel is swapped, hidden, or scored. A model cannot be credited for using evidence the evaluator never observes. Multimodal rewards and route interventions test whether the decision depends on the intended signal rather than a shortcut through captions, priors, or spurious features. Name used later: observation channel.

validity needs score f(o_text, o_image, o_trace) to change under causal intervention on the relevant evidence channel

Evidence check. Compare native-artifact judging to text-only judging, run swap interventions, ablate evidence routes, and measure whether scores change only when the relevant channel changes.

OBJECT 01

Measurement Instruments

ATLAS

Problem. Fixed benchmarks waste items that add little information about the model being tested.

Object. The next question chosen because it can still change the evaluator's belief about the model's skill.

Why it works. A useful test item sits where the answer would separate skill levels that are still plausible. What must stay the same is the skill being measured. What may change is the next item after earlier answers. Names used later: item-response theory and Fisher information.

Connection. This is test-time compute allocation applied to measurement.

FRABench And UFEval

Problem. One overall preference score can hide which skill failed.

Object. A rubric that separates ability into parts and reports remaining doubt in each comparison.

Why it works. Aspect-level scoring separates visual grounding, instruction following, generation quality, and other hidden components. Reporting doubt prevents small noisy gaps from becoming false rankings.

Connection. It is splitting a benchmark into the variables where failure actually happens.

Micro-Benchmarking Reliability

Problem. Small benchmarks can reverse model rankings by sampling noise.

Object. Confidence that one model really beats another under finite sampled questions.

Why it works. The paper treats model order as a statistical claim. It asks how many examples preserve the comparison with acceptable doubt instead of assuming fewer items preserve the leaderboard.

Connection. It pairs with finite certification: both ask how much evidence a claim needs.

WebDevJudge

Problem. A web implementation cannot be judged reliably from source text alone.

Object. Like reviewing a website by opening it in the browser, the object is rendered layout, interaction, responsiveness, and task intent.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the user-facing behavior. What may change is the source code route used to produce it. A judge that sees only code misses state and visual behavior that determine correctness. Name used later: rendered artifact judge.

Connection. It is the web version of executable evaluation.

CounselBench

Problem. Counseling responses can sound supportive while violating clinical safety boundaries.

Object. Expert judgment over professional constraints, risk, and response quality.

Why it works. Mental-health evaluation has hidden variables ordinary text preference misses: escalation, boundaries, harm minimization, and appropriate doubt. Human experts supply the domain measurement channel.

Connection. High-stakes judging is not just preference; it is constrained measurement.

MADQA

Problem. Document QA benchmarks can confuse retrieval, reading, oracle access, and reasoning.

Object. A gap among human, agent, and oracle performance under document evidence.

Why it works. Classical Test Theory separates what is answerable from what an agent actually recovers. The benchmark estimates whether failure came from missing evidence, weak navigation, or reasoning over the evidence.

Connection. It is measurement decomposition over a research workflow.

OBJECT 02

Pressure And Boundaries

Copyright-Bench

Problem. Legal compliance can fail when user pressure rewards fast or convenient source choice.

Object. Like a worker who must finish the job using only licensed materials, the object is the menu of lawful workflow actions under commercial constraints.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the legal and provenance boundary. What may change is which source, workflow step, or answer path the agent chooses inside that boundary. The benchmark measures whether the agent completes the task without crossing the allowed menu. Name used later: feasible set.

Connection. It is high-stakes feasible-set design.

CSPO

Problem. A safe-RL policy needs to recover near constraint boundaries, not merely satisfy average constraints.

Object. Distance to violation plus whether the next policy movement gets closer to or farther from violation.

Why it works. The update accounts for how close the policy is to failure and how movement changes that distance. Safety becomes a boundary problem, not an average-score problem.

Connection. This matches PACT in robotics and trust-region RL in post-training.

Jailbreak Foundry

Problem. Red-team evaluation is weak when each attack has its own script and assumptions.

Object. A modular executable attack harness.

Why it works. Shared modules make attacks reproducible and comparable. The defense is tested against transformations that others can run, extend, and combine.

Connection. It makes adversarial safety closer to benchmark infrastructure than anecdotal prompting.

AdvGame

Problem. Static defenses can overfit historical attacks.

Object. A setting where attacker and defender each respond to the other's move.

Why it works. Each side responds to the other. The measurement process moves toward the boundary where safety claims are weakest.

Connection. This is mechanism design with an adversary.

SecFid

Problem. Prompt-injection defense can be secure by ignoring too much or faithful by executing unsafe instructions.

Object. The tradeoff boundary between blocking attacks and preserving benign task content.

Why it works. The method separates attack blocking from benign-content preservation. Safety is reported as a tradeoff surface, not a single score.

Connection. This is the same tradeoff-boundary logic as watermarking and SafeDPO.

SandboxEscapeBench

Problem. Tool agents can cross security boundaries through shells, containers, runtimes, or host misconfiguration.

Object. Whether protected host evidence can be reached from inside a sandbox.

Why it works. The benchmark defines a forbidden state and tests whether agent actions can reach it. This turns safety into a checked path problem.

Connection. It is executable boundary testing.

OBJECT 03

Tail Risk

Rare Event Analysis Of LLMs

Problem. Harmful behavior can be invisible in ordinary testing and still appear often at platform scale.

Object. The chance of a specified bad event.

Why it works. The event is defined first, then sampled or estimated with reported doubt. This prevents average benchmark draws from deciding whether rare deployment harms exist.

Connection. It is sampler theory for safety events.

Tail Risks In LM Outputs

Problem. Events around one in ten thousand queries require far more targeted estimation than ordinary evaluation runs.

Object. Sampling that deliberately visits unsafe-looking regions more often, then corrects back to deployment frequency.

Why it works. Waiting for a rare bad case can take too long. The method buys information by visiting the risky region on purpose, while keeping a correction receipt so the final number still refers to normal deployment. What must stay the same is the bad-event definition. What may change is how samples are gathered. Name used later: importance sampling.

Connection. It is coverage of rare failure regions.

Reward Redistribution For CVaR MDPs

Problem. Worst-slice risk is hard to train when the penalty appears only at the end of a trajectory.

Object. Bad-ending risk sent back to the earlier steps that helped cause it.

Why it works. If a trip ends in a crash, the useful lesson may be an earlier lane change, not only the final second. What must stay the same is focus on the worst slice of outcomes. What may change is how blame is assigned to earlier actions so the policy can learn. Name used later: CVaR.

Connection. It mirrors h1's solution to sparse long-horizon rewards.

UP-OCP

Problem. Online prediction intervals must maintain coverage under sequential data.

Object. Prediction ranges that keep containing the truth often enough as data arrives over time.

Why it works. A weather forecast range has to keep adjusting as new readings arrive, while still being honest about how often the truth should fall inside it. What must stay the same is the promised miss rate. What may change is the range after each new observation. Name used later: conformal coverage.

Connection. It is safety as online statistical control.

ROCP, CreDRO, And CRC

Problem. A calibrated prediction set can still be useless for the decision it supports.

Object. A set of plausible outcomes shaped by the cost of the downstream action.

Why it works. A delivery driver needs to know whether rain is possible if rain changes the route; a broad weather score is not enough. What must stay visible is the uncertainty that can change the action. What may change is the size or shape of the plausible-outcome set. Names used later: robust set or credal set.

Connection. It is robust contextual optimization in prediction-set form.

RLVepsR

Problem. Verifiable-reward RL can learn, stall, or anti-learn depending on verifier noise.

Object. Training behavior under noisy yes/no checking.

Why it works. Checker quality controls whether the training push points toward skill or away from it. Evaluation noise becomes a training variable, not just a reporting problem.

Connection. This closes the loop: a bad measurement can create a bad model.

OBJECT 04

Executable Verifiers

CyberGym

Problem. Security ability is not demonstrated by describing a vulnerability.

Object. Executable proof-of-concept behavior against pre-patch and post-patch code.

Why it works. The exploit must distinguish vulnerable from fixed code. That gives the benchmark behavioral evidence rather than persuasive text.

Connection. It is the cleanest example of artifact-first evaluation.

CIRBench And DRPBench

Problem. Compiler and concurrent-code claims require meaning checks, not style judgments.

Object. Same-behavior checks, runtime, and possible execution orderings.

Why it works. CIRBench checks whether optimization preserves meaning and improves performance; DRPBench checks race risk across schedules. Both use program behavior as the verifier.

Connection. Code evaluation is strongest when it runs or reasons over all relevant executions.

WZ-LLM, VERINA, And AlgoVeri

Problem. Proof and verified-code tasks are easy to fake in prose.

Object. Like a key that must actually open the lock, the object is a generated proof or program that an external checker accepts.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the rule of the proof or verified-code system. What may change is the wording, search path, or repair attempt. The verifier rejects unsupported steps, so correctness is no longer a matter of fluency. Name used later: kernel-checked proof artifact.

Connection. This is proof as executable evaluation.

BrokenMath And 2-SAT Robustness

Problem. Reasoning models can agree with false claims or depend on formatting while appearing mathematically competent.

Object. Controlled false statements and families of logic-preserving changes.

Why it works. The benchmark changes premise validity, clause order, renamings, redundancy, and implication structure to see whether the model follows the underlying logic.

Connection. It tests whether the reasoning object stays the same under harmless surface changes, not surface familiarity.

TerminalTraj And SwingArena

Problem. Real software work unfolds through commands, tests, files, review, and CI state.

Object. Like a saved work session: commands run, files changed, tests passed or failed, review comments answered, and the final project state.

Why it works. The exact command order may differ, but the endpoint must be verifiable and the intermediate work must explain how the endpoint was reached. The benchmark records process and checks state changes, so a patch or command sequence has to survive the same feedback loops developers use. Name used later: trajectory of environment-changing actions.

Connection. It links agent evaluation to controlled inference over a mutable state.

Weak-Strong Verification

Problem. Strong verification is costly, while cheap verifiers are unsure.

Object. Calibrated triage among accept, reject, and defer.

Why it works. Expensive checking is spent where weak-verifier doubt or risk justifies it. Verification becomes a budget allocation problem.

Connection. It is the measurement analog of test-time scaling.

OBJECT 05

Distinguishability

Gaussian Certified Unlearning

Problem. Removal claims need an auditor-visible guarantee, not a statement that retraining was approximated.

Object. Whether an observer can tell the unlearned model apart from one that never saw the deleted data.

Why it works. A deletion promise matters only if the allowed auditor cannot still see the removed record's trace. What must stay the same is the auditor's view. What may change is whether the deleted record was ever included. Name used later: indistinguishability.

Connection. Privacy wants indistinguishability; watermarking wants distinguishability.

Exact RL Unlearning

Problem. RL data affects policies through trajectories, rewards, and state visitation, so supervised deletion logic is insufficient.

Object. Policy-level stability after removing user data.

Why it works. The guarantee compares the learned decision process to one trained without the user's trajectories. It treats deletion as a property of behavior, not only parameters.

Connection. It extends unlearning into agents and control.

Catch-22

Problem. Watermarks must survive edits, remain hidden, and still be detectable.

Object. Like invisible ink that must survive copying without making innocent pages look guilty, the object is the tradeoff among detection, stealth, false accusation risk, and quality loss.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the authorized test's ability to tell marked output from clean output under stated edits. What may change is mark strength, detector threshold, and output quality. The paper names the impossibility boundary instead of hiding it in a single watermark score. Name used later: KL-based hypothesis testing for measuring how separable the two output patterns are.

Connection. It is SecFid's frontier logic in provenance form.

Spherical Watermark And Ellipse Signatures

Problem. Media and model provenance signals must survive transformations without ruining quality.

Object. A hidden source mark placed where normal generation still looks normal but authorized tests can detect it.

Why it works. A good source mark is like invisible ink that survives ordinary handling without making the page strange. What must stay the same is natural-looking output plus detectability by the authorized test. What may change is editing, compression, or generation details. Names used later: latent signature and logprob signature.

Connection. Provenance is controlled distinguishability.

LM Memorization Capacity And Hubble

Problem. Memorization risk cannot be measured if sensitive exposure is unknown.

Object. Bits of recoverable training information and controlled sensitive insertions.

Why it works. Known insertions and exposure frequencies let researchers separate useful generalization from unintended copying. Capacity estimates make leakage quantitative.

Connection. This is benchmark design for privacy risk.

PRISM And SlaClip

Problem. Private adaptation can waste privacy budget through parameterization or clipping choices.

Object. Privacy noise placed where the model is actually moving, plus unused clipping room already paid for by the privacy budget.

Why it works. PRISM makes the protection insensitive to how a low-rank update is written; SlaClip improves utility using slack already paid for by the privacy accountant. Both align the protection with the actual training movement.

Connection. Privacy is controlled distinguishability under training movement.

OBJECT 06

Values And Governance

VALUEFLOW

Problem. Values are not binary labels and cannot be captured by one global reward.

Object. Like asking many people not only which answer they prefer, but how strongly the change matters, the object is a strength estimate for each value.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the value evidence in the rankings. What may change is the answer, user group, or value being steered. Ranking data estimates relative intensity without assuming everyone shares one stable numeric scale. Name used later: value-intensity profile.

Connection. It is measurement of plural values for alignment.

Omni-Reward

Problem. Reward models must judge text, image, video, audio, and 3D artifacts under free-form preferences.

Object. Like judging a film by watching it, not just reading its synopsis, the judge must see the actual artifact plus the user's preference.

Why it works. What may change is the medium: text, image, video, audio, or 3D. What must stay the same is that the evidence channel matches where failure happens. The reward model should inspect native evidence rather than a caption that may omit the error. Name used later: multimodal reward state.

Connection. It links values to grounding.

SafeDPO

Problem. Helpfulness and safety cannot be collapsed without hiding the tradeoff.

Object. A preference-training rule with an explicit knob for helpfulness versus safety.

Why it works. The knob exposes movement along the tradeoff boundary rather than burying it in reward-model weights. Training can state what it is sacrificing.

Connection. It is SecFid for preference optimization.

Accuracy Auctions

Problem. Average accuracy ignores that users value correct predictions differently.

Object. A rule for collecting private value reports and using them to weight outcomes.

Why it works. Auction design asks whether people can be made better off by reporting values honestly, then using those values to judge predictions. The metric becomes social utility rather than mean correctness.

Connection. It turns evaluation into mechanism design.

Fair OT And Fair Posthoc Control

Problem. Fairness audited after optimization may arrive too late or force retraining.

Object. A rule for matching or adjusting outcomes while keeping group constraints visible.

Why it works. Fair optimal transport puts fairness into the matching of distributions; posthoc control keeps a controllable fairness-accuracy boundary after training. Both make fairness part of the decision, not an after-the-fact report.

Connection. Governance is allowed-set design over groups and outcomes.

Data Market Pricing And Bayesian Truthful Valuation

Problem. Data contributors may distort evidence if incentives reward distortion.

Object. Like paying weather stations for useful reports, the payment rule should make honest useful information more rewarding than exaggeration or strategic hiding.

Why it works. What may change is the price, buyer, seller, and uncertainty about usefulness. What must stay the same is that evidence quality is rewarded in the direction of truth. The training data pipeline becomes part of the evaluated system because incentives shape which examples appear. Name used later: truthful valuation.

Connection. Data quality is strategic, not only statistical.

OBJECT 07

Grounded Evidence

VISUALSWAP

Problem. A VLM can answer from prior conversation or language bias without rechecking the current image.

Object. Like swapping the photo in a report and checking whether the conclusion changes, the object is a test of whether image evidence controls the answer.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the question and surrounding text. What may change is the visual evidence. If the answer does not change when the visual evidence changes, the model was not using the image as claimed. Name used later: causal dependence on perception.

Connection. It is modality-credit assignment as evaluation.

FlowGuard And Causal Route Gating

Problem. Multimodal hallucination can arise from abnormal fusion or text-prior dominance.

Object. A check of which evidence channel actually changed the answer.

Why it works. FlowGuard detects whether channels repeat, add, or dominate evidence; route gating suppresses prior-heavy text paths. Both ask which channel caused the answer.

Connection. This is causal inference inside multimodal evaluation.

Seizure-Semiology-Suite

Problem. Medical video evaluation needs clinically meaningful temporal reports, not generic action labels.

Object. Like a clinician watching which side of the body moves first, which symptoms appear, in what order, and for how long, the object is the event pattern doctors use.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the medically meaningful event structure. What may change is clip, wording, or model explanation. The benchmark measures whether a model preserves the variables clinicians use. Name used later: semiology, the clinical language for symptom signs and patterns.

Connection. High-stakes evaluation must inherit the domain's measurement grammar.

PhyWorldBench And RealPDEBench

Problem. Generated or predicted physical systems can look plausible while violating dynamics.

Object. Physical consistency over motion, fields, and measured processes.

Why it works. The benchmark checks whether outputs obey the rules of the world being modeled. A visual score alone cannot certify physical validity.

Connection. This is control consistency as evaluation.

RoboMME And dWorldEval

Problem. Embodied policies depend on memory, future prediction, and action consequences.

Object. Robot performance separated by memory type and by whether generated futures help choose actions.

Why it works. RoboMME decomposes memory into temporal, spatial, object, and procedural forms; dWorldEval asks whether generated futures help rank policies. Both evaluate the state a robot uses to act.

Connection. Embodied evaluation is state evaluation.

CausalGame And MoCA

Problem. A system may fail because it observed badly, reasoned badly, or inferred the wrong cause-and-effect story.

Object. A diagnosis that separates data collection, perception, reasoning, and causal explanation.

Why it works. CausalGame tests whether agents design evidence when ordinary correlations are confusing; MoCA routes feedback to perception or reasoning depending on failure type. Both make the failure source explicit.

Connection. Good evaluation explains why the answer failed, not only that it failed.

SYNTHESIS

The Cross-Paper Shape

Score To Instrument

ATLAS, FRABench, UFEval, Micro-Benchmarking Reliability, WebDevJudge, CounselBench, and MADQA all replace a leaderboard number with a measuring process. The hidden target is capability or quality; the observed score is noisy evidence produced by questions, judges, rubrics, artifacts, and task samples.

Average To Tail

Rare Event Analysis, Tail Risks, CVaR MDPs, UP-OCP, robust prediction sets, RLVepsR, CSPO, and SandboxEscapeBench focus on boundary regions where ordinary samples are weakest. They work by changing the harmful event, sampling rule, remaining-doubt set, or allowed boundary so the dangerous region becomes measurable.

Claim To Certificate

CyberGym, CIRBench, WZ-LLM, VERINA, unlearning papers, Catch-22, watermarking, Fair OT, and behavior-under-change checks all require a certificate: execution, proof acceptance, indistinguishability, detection power, or constrained feasibility. The paper's value comes from matching the certificate to the claim.

FOCUSED CHAIN

Measurement Validity: From Score To Instrument

The central question is not whether a benchmark has a number. It is whether that number is a valid observation of the capability being claimed. These papers make evaluation mathematical by naming the hidden target, the observation channel, the noise source, and the population over which the claim is supposed to generalize.

ATLAS: Items Are Information Purchases

Plain object. Like a teacher choosing the next question where the student's level is still unclear, the test spends each item where an answer could reduce real doubt.

Why it matters. What must stay the same is the skill being measured. What may change is which question comes next. Adaptive evaluation works because it spends test budget where belief about ability is still uncertain, rather than treating every item as equally diagnostic. Names used later: Fisher information and latent ability.

Remaining failure. Information can be high for the wrong hidden target. If items measure benchmark familiarity, prompt format sensitivity, or judge preference, adaptive selection becomes efficient measurement of a proxy.

choose the item expected to reduce ability doubt the most

FRABench And UFEval: One Score Hides A Vector

Plain object. Like a report card with separate grades, quality is split into parts so a strong score in one part cannot hide failure in another.

Why it matters. Many failures are invisible after one-number averaging. What must stay separate is the ability that can fail: grounding, instruction following, fidelity, safety, or style. What may change is how a user weights those abilities. Name used later: factored rubric.

Remaining failure. A rubric is itself a model. If the chosen dimensions omit the real task variable or judges cannot distinguish them reliably, factorization gives a clean but wrong coordinate system.

quality = grounding + instruction + fidelity + safety + remaining doubt

Micro-Benchmarking Reliability: Ranking Is A Statistical Claim

Plain object. Like deciding whether one runner is truly faster after only a few races, the benchmark asks whether the observed gap is bigger than the noise in the test.

Why it matters. A leaderboard order is not just sorted means. What must stay fixed is the item population the claim is about. What may change is the finite sample drawn from it. Micro-benchmarking works when it reports whether the observed gap is larger than the doubt introduced by small item sets.

Remaining failure. Statistical confidence over the tested items does not prove the item set is representative. A ranking can be stable and still narrow.

rank(A > B) needs score gap greater than measurement doubt

WebDevJudge And CounselBench: The Judge Must See The Right Artifact

Plain object. Like judging a website by opening it in a browser, or judging counseling by risk handling rather than warm wording, the judge must see the place where failure lives.

Why it matters. The output string is not always the evaluated object. What must stay the same is the real domain standard. What may change is the artifact being inspected: rendered page, expert-risk scenario, interaction, or answer. The judge works when its observation channel includes the variable users or experts care about.

Remaining failure. A judge can still observe the right artifact with the wrong rubric, or observe one session while missing delayed harm, accessibility, edge layouts, or user-specific clinical context.

score = judge(domain artifact), not judge(text description of artifact)

MADQA: Separate Evidence Access From Reasoning

Plain object. Like separating "the file was missing" from "the file was found but misread," the benchmark keeps evidence access and reasoning failure apart.

Why it matters. Document QA failure can come from missing evidence, bad navigation, weak reading, or wrong inference. What must stay separate is which link failed. What may change is the agent, human baseline, or oracle view used for comparison. MADQA makes the gap observable instead of reducing every miss to one accuracy loss.

Remaining failure. The decomposition depends on how oracle access and human comparison are defined. If the oracle is too strong or the human baseline too narrow, the gap can misattribute failure.

agent error = missing evidence + retrieval error + reasoning error + scoring noise

The Measurement Chain

Shared object. A measurement process that maps hidden capability to observed score through items, judges, rubrics, artifacts, and reported doubt.

First-principles read. A score is evidence only after the instrument is named. The right benchmark is not the largest benchmark; it is the one whose observation channel matches the hidden claim and whose remaining doubt is visible enough to prevent overclaiming.

hidden target -> task sample -> artifact -> judge/rubric -> score with doubt

Tail-Pressure Safety: From Average Score To Boundary Evidence

Safety fails where ordinary sampling is weakest: rare events, adaptive attackers, constraint boundaries, noisy verifiers, repeated use, and reachable infrastructure states. These papers work by changing the object from average behavior to the event, path, or boundary where loss concentrates.

Rare Event Analysis And Tail Risks: Define The Event Before Sampling

Plain object. Like testing for black ice instead of only counting normal drives, the method names the harmful event before deciding how to look for it.

Why it matters. A platform can see a one-in-ten-thousand failure many times. What must stay fixed is the event and pressure process being claimed. What may change is the sampling route used to find enough cases. Name used later: tail probability.

Remaining failure. The estimate is conditional on the pressure process. A new user strategy, repeated interaction, or tool-augmented attack can move failure mass outside the sampled rare region.

risk = chance of event E under named pressure, with doubt reported

CVaR MDPs, UP-OCP, ROCP, CreDRO, CRC: Keep Uncertainty Operational

Plain object. Like keeping the bad-weather cases on the desk when choosing a driving rule, the decision must preserve the bad-case slice or the still-plausible outcomes.

Why it matters. Average reward and ordinary coverage can be useless for safety decisions. What must stay visible is the set of outcomes that would change the action. What may change is how cautious the decision is. Names used later: CVaR, conformal set, and robust set.

Remaining failure. A robust set can omit the real shift or become too broad to act on. A tail objective can protect the wrong loss if downstream harm is misspecified.

decide using plausible outcomes and bad-case losses, not only average label error

Jailbreak Foundry And AdvGame: Pressure Must Adapt

Plain object. Like testing a lock against someone trying new keys after seeing the old defense, the attack process must be allowed to change.

Why it matters. Static attack lists test yesterday's boundary. What must stay the same is the defense claim. What may change is the attacker's route, tool, or response to the defense. Executable attack modules and adaptive games move evaluation toward the region where defenses are currently weak.

Remaining failure. The game only contains the actions it models. Hidden channels, social coordination, new tools, or off-platform incentives can create attacks outside the harness.

defense claim depends on response path attacker <-> defender

SecFid, CSPO, SandboxEscapeBench: Boundaries Are Geometry

Plain object. Like a safety line on a factory floor, the system needs to know which actions are allowed, how close it is to crossing, and whether a route can reach the forbidden area.

Why it matters. Prompt-injection defense, safe policy updates, and sandbox security all ask whether the system remains inside an allowed set under pressure. What must stay fixed is the deployed boundary. What may change is direction and distance of the next action. A small update near the boundary is riskier than a large update deep inside the safe region.

Remaining failure. The formal boundary can omit real constraints: usability, dependency configuration, host topology, policy ambiguity, or tool side effects.

safe action requires state remains inside allowed set under allowed transitions

RLVepsR And Weak-Strong Verification: Bad Measurement Can Train Bad Behavior

Plain object. Like training from a coach whose advice is sometimes wrong, the model improves only when the checker points behavior toward the real task.

Why it matters. Checkers are not neutral scorekeepers when they feed training. What must stay the same is the connection between checker signal and true skill. What may change is when a stronger checker is used. If checker noise points the update the wrong way, a model can anti-learn.

Remaining failure. Calibration can drift after the model learns to target the verifier. Defer policies can also hide hard cases if budgets are too tight.

training signal = true skill signal + checker noise; direction matters

The Tail-Pressure Chain

Shared object. A safety measurement process over rare events, adaptive pressure, allowed boundaries, and checker feedback.

First-principles read. Safety evidence is not a global property of a model. It is evidence about an event under a pressure process, with a boundary model and a checker. The claim weakens when any one of those objects changes.

event definition -> pressure process -> boundary model -> checker -> risk estimate
