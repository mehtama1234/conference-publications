Evaluation and safety as measurement under pressure

This page expands the evaluation and safety lane at paper level. The common mathematical problem is that a score is only an observation of a hidden target: ability, risk, compliance, robustness, privacy, provenance, value, or trust. The hard work is designing the measuring instrument.

FIRST PRINCIPLE

The Score Is Not The Capability

A benchmark score is produced by a task distribution, item set, judge, rubric, sampling plan, stopping rule, and failure taxonomy. It is not the capability itself. A high score can mean real ability, benchmark overfit, judge bias, shortcut use, lucky sampling, or hidden reliance on a modality that should not have mattered.

Safety has the same structure. A model can look safe under ordinary samples and fail under user pressure, prompt injection, fine-tuning access, low-probability tails, hidden activation channels, or deployment constraints. The mathematical move across these papers is to choose the right observation channel and define the event, boundary, verifier, uncertainty set, or tradeoff frontier before making the safety claim.

OBJECT 01

Measurement Instruments

ATLAS

Problem. Fixed benchmarks waste items that add little information about the model being tested.

Object. Latent model ability estimated through item-response theory and Fisher information.

Why it works. Each item is valuable only if it reduces uncertainty about ability near the current estimate. Adaptive item choice turns evaluation into efficient estimation instead of static scoring.

Connection. This is test-time compute allocation applied to measurement.

FRABench And UFEval

Problem. One overall preference score can hide which skill failed.

Object. A factored rubric over ability dimensions and uncertainty in comparisons.

Why it works. Aspect-level scoring separates visual grounding, instruction following, generation quality, and other hidden components. Uncertainty reporting prevents small noisy gaps from becoming false rankings.

Connection. It is representation factorization for benchmarks.

Micro-Benchmarking Reliability

Problem. Small benchmarks can reverse model rankings by sampling noise.

Object. Pairwise ranking confidence as a finite-sample estimator.

Why it works. The paper treats model order as a statistical claim. It asks how many examples preserve the comparison with acceptable uncertainty instead of assuming fewer items preserve the leaderboard.

Connection. It pairs with finite certification: both ask how much evidence a claim needs.

WebDevJudge

Problem. A web implementation cannot be judged reliably from source text alone.

Object. A judge over rendered layout, interaction, responsiveness, and task intent.

Why it works. The artifact must be observed in the form users experience it. A judge that sees only code misses state and visual behavior that determine correctness.

Connection. It is the web version of executable evaluation.

CounselBench

Problem. Counseling responses can sound supportive while violating clinical safety boundaries.

Object. Expert judgment over professional constraints, risk, and response quality.

Why it works. Mental-health evaluation has hidden variables ordinary text preference misses: escalation, boundaries, harm minimization, and appropriate uncertainty. Human experts supply the domain measurement channel.

Connection. High-stakes judging is not just preference; it is constrained measurement.

MADQA

Problem. Document QA benchmarks can confuse retrieval, reading, oracle access, and reasoning.

Object. A gap among human, agent, and oracle performance under document evidence.

Why it works. Classical Test Theory separates what is answerable from what an agent actually recovers. The benchmark estimates whether failure came from missing evidence, weak navigation, or reasoning over the evidence.

Connection. It is measurement decomposition over a research workflow.

OBJECT 02

Pressure And Boundaries

Copyright-Bench

Problem. Legal compliance can fail when user pressure rewards fast or convenient source choice.

Object. A feasible set of lawful workflow actions under commercial constraints.

Why it works. The benchmark measures whether the agent stays inside the allowed action set while still completing the task. Compliance is evaluated as constrained action, not as a refusal phrase.

Connection. It is high-stakes feasible-set design.

CSPO

Problem. A safe-RL policy needs to recover near constraint boundaries, not merely satisfy average constraints.

Object. Signed distance to violation plus local sensitivity of the constraint to policy movement.

Why it works. The update accounts for how close the policy is to failure and how movement changes that distance. Safety becomes boundary geometry.

Connection. This matches PACT in robotics and trust-region RL in post-training.

Jailbreak Foundry

Problem. Red-team evaluation is weak when each attack has its own script and assumptions.

Object. A modular executable attack harness.

Why it works. Shared modules make attacks reproducible and comparable. The defense is tested against transformations that others can run, extend, and combine.

Connection. It makes adversarial safety closer to benchmark infrastructure than anecdotal prompting.

AdvGame

Problem. Static defenses can overfit historical attacks.

Object. A non-zero-sum adaptive game between attacker and defender.

Why it works. Each side responds to the other. The measurement distribution moves toward the boundary where safety claims are weakest.

Connection. This is mechanism design with an adversary.

SecFid

Problem. Prompt-injection defense can be secure by ignoring too much or faithful by executing unsafe instructions.

Object. A frontier between security and task fidelity.

Why it works. The method separates attack blocking from benign-content preservation. Safety is reported as a tradeoff surface, not a single score.

Connection. This is the same frontier logic as watermarking and SafeDPO.

SandboxEscapeBench

Problem. Tool agents can cross security boundaries through shells, containers, runtimes, or host misconfiguration.

Object. Reachability of protected host evidence from inside a sandbox.

Why it works. The benchmark defines a forbidden state and tests whether agent actions can reach it. This turns safety into a state-space reachability problem.

Connection. It is executable boundary geometry.

OBJECT 03

Tail Risk

Rare Event Analysis Of LLMs

Problem. Harmful behavior can be invisible in ordinary testing and still appear often at platform scale.

Object. Tail probability over a specified event set.

Why it works. The event is defined first, then sampled or estimated with uncertainty. This prevents average benchmark draws from deciding whether rare deployment harms exist.

Connection. It is sampler theory for safety events.

Tail Risks In LM Outputs

Problem. Events around one in ten thousand queries require far more targeted estimation than ordinary evaluation runs.

Object. Importance sampling from unsafe model variants.

Why it works. Sampling is biased toward the rare region and corrected statistically. The method buys information about low-probability failures without waiting for them to appear naturally.

Connection. It is mode coverage in the failure distribution.

Reward Redistribution For CVaR MDPs

Problem. Tail-risk objectives are hard to train when the penalty appears only at the end of a trajectory.

Object. CVaR risk converted into dense per-step dynamic-programming signal.

Why it works. Redistribution turns a sparse tail criterion into a contracting Bellman-style update. The policy can learn which early actions affect downstream tail loss.

Connection. It mirrors h1's solution to sparse long-horizon rewards.

UP-OCP

Problem. Online prediction intervals must maintain coverage under sequential data.

Object. Conformal coverage controlled through universal portfolio selection.

Why it works. The method adapts calibration while preserving finite-time miscoverage control. Uncertainty is governed over time, not recalibrated only after failure.

Connection. It is safety as online statistical control.

ROCP, CreDRO, And CRC

Problem. A calibrated prediction set can still be useless for the decision it supports.

Object. Robust or credal prediction sets shaped by downstream decision loss.

Why it works. The set is optimized to protect actions under plausible uncertainty, not only to contain the label. This keeps uncertainty operational.

Connection. It is robust contextual optimization in prediction-set form.

RLVepsR

Problem. Verifiable-reward RL can learn, stall, or anti-learn depending on verifier noise.

Object. Phase behavior under noisy binary verification.

Why it works. The verifier quality controls whether gradients point toward skill or away from it. Evaluation noise becomes a training-dynamics variable.

Connection. This closes the loop: a bad measurement can create a bad model.

OBJECT 04

Executable Verifiers

CyberGym

Problem. Security ability is not demonstrated by describing a vulnerability.

Object. Executable proof-of-concept behavior against pre-patch and post-patch code.

Why it works. The exploit must distinguish vulnerable from fixed code. That gives the benchmark behavioral evidence rather than persuasive text.

Connection. It is the cleanest example of artifact-first evaluation.

CIRBench And DRPBench

Problem. Compiler and concurrent-code claims require semantic checks, not style judgments.

Object. Equivalence, runtime, and possible interleaving behavior.

Why it works. CIRBench checks whether optimization preserves meaning and improves performance; DRPBench checks race risk across schedules. Both use program semantics as the verifier.

Connection. Code evaluation is strongest when it runs or reasons over all relevant executions.

WZ-LLM, VERINA, And AlgoVeri

Problem. Formal proof and verified-code tasks are easy to fake in prose.

Object. Kernel-checked proof artifacts across Lean, Dafny, Verus, or related systems.

Why it works. The verifier rejects unsupported steps. The generated artifact must satisfy a formal checker, so correctness is no longer a matter of fluency.

Connection. This is proof as executable evaluation.

BrokenMath And 2-SAT Robustness

Problem. Reasoning models can be sycophantic or format-sensitive while appearing mathematically competent.

Object. Controlled false statements and logical perturbation families.

Why it works. The benchmark changes premise validity, clause order, renamings, redundancy, and implication structure to see whether the model follows the underlying logic.

Connection. It tests invariance of the reasoning object, not surface familiarity.

TerminalTraj And SwingArena

Problem. Real software work unfolds through commands, tests, files, review, and CI state.

Object. A trajectory of environment-changing actions with verifiable endpoints.

Why it works. The benchmark records process and checks state changes. A patch or command sequence must survive the same feedback loops developers use.

Connection. It links agent evaluation to controlled inference over a mutable state.

Weak-Strong Verification

Problem. Strong verification is costly, while cheap verifiers are uncertain.

Object. Calibrated triage among accept, reject, and defer.

Why it works. Expensive checking is spent where weak-verifier uncertainty or risk justifies it. Verification becomes a budget allocation problem.

Connection. It is the measurement analog of test-time scaling.

OBJECT 05

Distinguishability

Gaussian Certified Unlearning

Problem. Removal claims need an auditor-visible guarantee, not a statement that retraining was approximated.

Object. Indistinguishability between an unlearned model and one that never saw the deleted data.

Why it works. The guarantee is a hypothesis test under calibrated noise and high-dimensional geometry. Deletion is meaningful only relative to what an observer can distinguish.

Connection. Privacy wants indistinguishability; watermarking wants distinguishability.

Exact RL Unlearning

Problem. RL data affects policies through trajectories, rewards, and state visitation, so supervised deletion logic is insufficient.

Object. Policy-level stability after removing user data.

Why it works. The guarantee compares the learned decision process to one trained without the user's trajectories. It treats deletion as a property of behavior, not only parameters.

Connection. It extends unlearning into agents and control.

Catch-22

Problem. Watermarks must survive edits, remain hidden, and still be detectable.

Object. A KL-based hypothesis-testing frontier.

Why it works. Detection power, stealth, and robustness trade through the same statistical signal. The paper names the impossibility boundary instead of hiding it in a single watermark score.

Connection. It is SecFid's frontier logic in provenance form.

Spherical Watermark And Ellipse Signatures

Problem. Media and model provenance signals must survive transformations without ruining quality.

Object. Distribution-preserving latent or logprob signatures.

Why it works. The signature is embedded in a space where normal generation statistics are preserved enough for quality, while authorized tests can still detect the mark.

Connection. Provenance is controlled distinguishability.

LM Memorization Capacity And Hubble

Problem. Memorization risk cannot be measured if sensitive exposure is unknown.

Object. Bits of recoverable training information and controlled sensitive insertions.

Why it works. Known insertions and exposure frequencies let researchers separate useful generalization from unintended copying. Capacity estimates make leakage quantitative.

Connection. This is benchmark design for privacy risk.

PRISM And SlaClip

Problem. Private adaptation can waste privacy budget through parameterization or clipping choices.

Object. Tangent-space privacy noise and DP-SGD clipping slack.

Why it works. PRISM makes privacy noise invariant to low-rank factorization; SlaClip improves utility using slack already paid for by the privacy accountant. Both align the mechanism with the actual training geometry.

Connection. Privacy is optimization geometry under a distinguishability constraint.

OBJECT 06

Values And Governance

VALUEFLOW

Problem. Values are not binary labels and cannot be captured by one global reward.

Object. Calibrated value-intensity profiles from repeated rankings.

Why it works. Ranking data estimates relative intensity without assuming everyone shares a stable numeric scale. Values become structured variables that can be compared and steered.

Connection. It is psychometrics for pluralistic alignment.

Omni-Reward

Problem. Reward models must judge text, image, video, audio, and 3D artifacts under free-form preferences.

Object. A cross-modal reward state combining artifact evidence and preference language.

Why it works. The reward model must condition on the artifact's native evidence, not only a caption or summary. This makes preference evaluation multimodal.

Connection. It links values to grounding.

SafeDPO

Problem. Helpfulness and safety cannot be collapsed without hiding the tradeoff.

Object. A direct preference objective with an explicit helpfulness-safety control parameter.

Why it works. The knob exposes movement along the frontier rather than burying it in reward-model weights. Training can state what it is sacrificing.

Connection. It is SecFid for preference optimization.

Accuracy Auctions

Problem. Average accuracy ignores that users value correct predictions differently.

Object. A welfare mechanism with private values.

Why it works. Auction design asks whether values can be elicited truthfully and used to weight outcomes. The metric becomes social utility rather than mean correctness.

Connection. It turns evaluation into mechanism design.

Fair OT And Fair Posthoc Control

Problem. Fairness audited after optimization may arrive too late or force retraining.

Object. A fairness-constrained coupling or adjustable fairness-accuracy frontier.

Why it works. Fair OT puts fairness into distribution matching; posthoc control keeps a controllable frontier after training. Both make fairness part of the decision geometry.

Connection. Governance is feasible-set design over groups and outcomes.

Data Market Pricing And Bayesian Truthful Valuation

Problem. Data contributors may distort evidence if incentives reward distortion.

Object. A market mechanism over reusable information.

Why it works. Stability and truthfulness conditions align private incentives with useful evidence sharing. The training data pipeline becomes part of the evaluated system.

Connection. Data quality is strategic, not only statistical.

OBJECT 07

Grounded Evidence

VISUALSWAP

Problem. A VLM can answer from prior conversation or language bias without rechecking the current image.

Object. Contrastive visual dependence under swapped images.

Why it works. If the answer does not change when the visual evidence changes, the model was not using the image as claimed. The benchmark tests causal dependence on perception.

Connection. It is modality-credit assignment as evaluation.

FlowGuard And Causal Route Gating

Problem. Multimodal hallucination can arise from abnormal fusion or text-prior dominance.

Object. Information flow and causal route contribution across modalities.

Why it works. FlowGuard detects redundancy, synergy, and dominance patterns; route gating suppresses prior-heavy text paths. Both ask which channel caused the answer.

Connection. This is causal inference inside multimodal evaluation.

Seizure-Semiology-Suite

Problem. Medical video evaluation needs clinically meaningful temporal reports, not generic action labels.

Object. Laterality, symptom sequence, timing, and semiology structure.

Why it works. The benchmark measures whether a model preserves the variables clinicians use. Domain vocabulary and temporal order are part of the target, not decoration.

Connection. High-stakes evaluation must inherit the domain's measurement grammar.

PhyWorldBench And RealPDEBench

Problem. Generated or predicted physical systems can look plausible while violating dynamics.

Object. Physical consistency over motion, fields, and measured processes.

Why it works. The benchmark checks whether outputs obey the rules of the world being modeled. A visual score alone cannot certify physical validity.

Connection. This is control consistency as evaluation.

RoboMME And dWorldEval

Problem. Embodied policies depend on memory, future prediction, and action consequences.

Object. Memory-typed VLA performance and world-model-based policy ranking.

Why it works. RoboMME decomposes memory into temporal, spatial, object, and procedural forms; dWorldEval asks whether generated futures help rank policies. Both evaluate the state a robot uses to act.

Connection. Embodied evaluation is state evaluation.

CausalGame And MoCA

Problem. A system may fail because it observed badly, reasoned badly, or inferred the wrong causal mechanism.

Object. Diagnostic decomposition over data collection, perception, reasoning, and causal explanation.

Why it works. CausalGame tests whether agents design evidence under confounding; MoCA routes feedback to perception or reasoning depending on failure type. Both make the failure source explicit.

Connection. Good evaluation explains why the answer failed, not only that it failed.

SYNTHESIS

The Cross-Paper Shape

Score To Instrument

ATLAS, FRABench, UFEval, Micro-Benchmarking Reliability, WebDevJudge, CounselBench, and MADQA all replace a leaderboard number with a measurement model. The hidden variable is capability or quality; the observed score is noisy evidence produced by items, judges, rubrics, and task distributions.

Average To Tail

Rare Event Analysis, Tail Risks, CVaR MDPs, UP-OCP, robust prediction sets, RLVepsR, CSPO, and SandboxEscapeBench focus on boundary regions where ordinary samples are weakest. They work by changing the event, sampling distribution, uncertainty set, or constraint geometry so the dangerous region becomes measurable.

Claim To Certificate

CyberGym, CIRBench, WZ-LLM, VERINA, unlearning papers, Catch-22, watermarking, Fair OT, and formal robustness checks all require a certificate: execution, proof acceptance, indistinguishability, detection power, or constrained feasibility. The paper's value comes from matching the certificate to the claim.
