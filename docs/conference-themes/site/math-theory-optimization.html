Theory and optimization as hidden budgets

This page expands the theory lane at paper level. The common mathematical problem is to name what a method is spending or protecting: how far the model is allowed to move, which signal must stay visible above noise, how much evidence is enough, how much memory or precision is available, how costly it is to move probability mass, how much checker work is needed, how much private information can leak, or how long interacting systems may take to settle.

FIRST PRINCIPLE

A Recipe Works Only In A Geometry

Think of training a model like steering a heavy cart through a crowded room. A push is not enough by itself. You need to know which wheels can turn, which floor patches are slippery, which walls cannot be crossed, and which nearby objects must not be hit. In these papers, an optimizer update, attention layer, benchmark score, market rule, or sampler is the steering rule. It says what can move, what must stay fixed, what evidence counts, and which cost is being ignored.

The big picture across these papers is a shift from surface performance to structural explanation. Instead of saying that a method works because it is larger, deeper, or better tuned, the papers ask simpler questions first: where is the useful signal, what kind of movement would damage it, what rule keeps the method inside the allowed region, what evidence would rule out a false explanation, and when does a guarantee stop applying?

FORMULA PASS

Paper-Specific Math Boxes

Adam Degeneracy

Object. Training as repeated pushes where each model knob gets its own ruler for how large a push should feel.

Why it works. Some places in training are like flat ground, while others are like a cliff edge. If every direction uses the same ruler, the trainer may barely move where it should move or jump too far where it should be careful. What must stay the same is progress toward lower error without unstable jumps. What may change is the per-knob step size learned from recent pushes. Formal labels if needed: adaptive metric for the learned movement ruler, and curvature for the hard flat-or-steep shape.

new model = old model - step size adjusted by the learned ruler

Evidence check. Show when this learned ruler changes the path compared with one fixed ruler for all pushes, especially near nearly flat places and under different memory lengths.

DiReCT

Object. A late training example as a proposed push on the model, checked against places where already-learned behavior is forgiving or easy to break.

Why it works. Near the end of training, not every useful-looking example is useful. One example may nudge the model toward better held-out behavior; another may touch a brittle habit where a tiny move breaks many answers. What must stay the same is broad useful behavior. What may change is which examples are used late. Formal labels if needed: gradient for the example's push, and Hessian for the map that marks brittle directions where small moves sharply change held-out behavior.

prefer an example when its push helps the target and avoids fragile directions

Evidence check. Compare selected examples by the held-out behavior movement they cause, the fragile-direction budget they spend, and whether the same example helps at one training time but hurts at another.

LoRA Theory

Object. Adaptation where the model is not allowed to move everywhere, only inside a small family of update shapes.

Why it works. A full model has too many knobs to turn. LoRA says the useful change for a new task may fit inside a smaller set of coordinated knob movements. What must stay the same is the behavior the base model should keep. What may change is the small adapter movement added for the new task. Formal label if needed: low-rank subspace for the small allowed family of update directions.

success means the needed task change fits inside the small adapter family

Evidence check. Measure how much full fine-tuning change is lost when squeezed into the small family, and whether adapter directions keep matching useful task pushes over training.

Alignment-Sensitive Minimax Rates

Object. Learning difficulty as a question of whether the answer signal sits where the model can see it clearly.

Why it works. Imagine trying to hear one singer in a noisy room. If the singer is near the microphone, fewer recordings are needed. If the singer is buried behind noise, more recordings may still not help. What must stay the same is the target pattern. What may change is the representation used to expose it. Formal labels if needed: spectral alignment for signal sitting in visible directions, and effective dimension for how many such directions matter.

risk depends on how much target signal lies in directions v_j whose strength lambda_j is above noise

Evidence check. Report where the target signal sits, how that changes with sample size, and cases where two models have similar complexity but different signal visibility.

Single-Head Attention In High Dimensions

Object. Attention as finding a real association inside a large table of mostly accidental matches.

Why it works. When many tokens and features are compared, random coincidences create background noise. Attention works reliably only when the true query-key relationship creates a repeated pattern stronger than that background. What must stay the same is the real association. What may change is dimension, noise, or sample size. Formal label if needed: a separated singular value, meaning one pattern in the matching table rises above the noise crowd.

recover association when the leading singular value of QK^T stands apart from the noise spectrum

Evidence check. Track when the association becomes visible, how dimension and noise hide it, and where recovery fails at the predicted threshold.

Transformer Circuits Can Realize Clustering Algorithms

Object. A transformer layer stack as an implementation of assignment, aggregation, centroid update, and iteration.

Why it works. Clustering is not magic pattern matching; it is a repeated map from points to clusters and back to centroids. Attention can represent assignment and aggregation, while feed-forward blocks can implement the update.

Lloyd step: c_k <- average{x_i : k = argmin_j ||x_i-c_j||^2}; circuit realizes assignment plus averaging

Evidence check. Verify each algorithmic suboperation, test iteration depth, compare to k-means trajectories, and inspect where learned variants depart from Lloyd updates.

Language Generation In The Limit

Object. Finite examples as evidence for one rule among many possible language rules.

Why it works. The examples `2, 4, 6` do not prove one rule; they could mean even numbers, counting by two up to six, or many other rules. Language samples have the same issue. What must stay the same is the future rule being claimed. What may change is the rival rule that still fits the finite evidence. Formal labels if needed: expressivity for "can represent it" and learnability for "the evidence can identify it."

a rule can exist even when finite examples cannot prove which rule it is

Evidence check. State the language class, construct rival rules that match finite samples, and report the evidence barrier separately from representation power.

FTRL Lower Bounds

Object. The time it takes interacting learners to stop chasing each other's changes.

Why it works. Two drivers can each make locally sensible moves and still keep cutting each other off. In learning games, low average regret can coexist with a joint system that has not settled. What must stay the same is the rule for a stable joint response. What may change is each learner's local update over time. Formal label if needed: a settled point where no player improves by changing alone, or Nash equilibrium.

small regret over T does not imply distance(x_T, Nash set) shrinks quickly

Evidence check. Exhibit hard game families, measure distance to stable joint behavior over time, and distinguish average regret from last-iterate convergence.

Fair Optimal Transport

Object. A matching table that moves weight from one group to another while obeying fairness rules.

Why it works. Imagine matching riders to drivers. If the cheapest matching violates the fairness rule, checking fairness after the match is too late. What must stay the same is the fairness rule. What may change is which acceptable matches are chosen to reduce cost. Formal label if needed: optimal transport for cheapest allowed mass movement.

choose the lowest-cost matching table that still obeys fairness rules

Evidence check. Compare unrestricted and rule-bound matching tables, report cost-fairness tradeoffs, and test whether fairness survives cases unlike the tuning data.

Mamba Markov ICL

Object. In-context prediction as remembering enough recent transition evidence to guess what comes next.

Why it works. If a route usually goes from station A to station B, the next useful fact is often the current station plus recent transition counts, not the whole travel diary. What must stay the same is the next-step pattern. What may change is how much old history is compressed. Formal labels if needed: hidden Markov transition law for the next-step rule, and smoothed sufficient statistics for compact counts that avoid overtrusting tiny evidence.

estimate P(x_{t+1}|x_t) with smoothed transition counts; recurrence stores the needed evidence

Evidence check. Compare against the best counting-and-smoothing estimators, vary sequence uncertainty and length, and test whether hidden state tracks transition evidence.

Finite Test Certification

Object. A limited test set as a sample of the worlds where a claim is supposed to hold.

Why it works. A bridge inspection cannot check every future load and storm. It can certify only a named range beyond what was tested. A benchmark has the same limit. What must stay the same is the family of worlds the claim covers. What may change is the unseen test mix inside the allowed range. Formal label if needed: chi-square radius for measuring how far the unseen mix can move from tested evidence.

needed test size grows with the allowed unseen-world distance chi^2(Q || P) and the desired error probability

Evidence check. Report the assumed family of worlds, the allowed distance from tested evidence, the confidence target, and which shifts remain outside the certificate.

Certified Unlearning And Privacy Bounds

Object. A deletion or privacy claim as a promise that an observer cannot reliably tell whether one record was present.

Why it works. Removing a file is not enough if the public output still carries its fingerprint. What must stay the same is the observer's allowed view: outputs, parameters, logs, or samples. What may change is whether one record was included, removed, or forgotten. Formal label if needed: making the two possible histories look too similar for the allowed observer to reliably tell apart, or indistinguishability.

bound how well an attacker can distinguish M(D) from M(D \\ {z}) under the chosen view

Evidence check. State the attacker view, run membership and reconstruction tests, compare with retraining where possible, and report utility loss beside the certificate.

OBJECT 01

Update Geometry

Adam Degeneracy

Problem. Some training landscapes look too flat or too uneven for a one-ruler update rule.

Object. Adam as a rule that learns a separate step-size ruler for different coordinates from recent pushes.

Why it works. The same raw push can be too small in one parameter and too large in another. Adam changes how each coordinate is measured before taking the step, so the optimizer may still make progress near places where ordinary gradient descent stalls. This is the plain meaning behind adaptive metric and degenerate curvature.

Connection. This is the base pattern for the lane: a training push matters only after the allowed movement ruler is named.

DiReCT

Problem. Late training data can help or harm depending on where it pushes the already-trained model.

Object. Each example as a proposed movement, checked against places where held-out behavior is forgiving or fragile.

Why it works. Late in training, the model has already settled into many behaviors. Some pushes can still improve it; others touch brittle habits and cause damage. What must stay the same is broad held-out behavior. What may change is the selected late example. Formal labels if needed: gradient for the push and validation Hessian for the fragile-direction map, meaning the map of directions where a small push changes held-out behavior a lot.

Connection. It links theory to curriculum and systems: an example matters through the movement it causes at that moment.

LoRA Gradient Descent And LoRA-Pre

Problem. Fine-tuning every parameter is expensive and gives the model too many ways to drift.

Object. Training inside a small allowed family of update shapes, while keeping a compressed memory of past useful pushes.

Why it works. LoRA works when the new task does not need arbitrary movement across the whole model. It needs a smaller coordinated movement that can be stored cheaply. LoRA-Pre asks whether the trainer's memory can also be compressed without forgetting the push information needed to keep improving. Formal label if needed: low-rank subspace for the smaller movement area made from a limited number of update directions.

Connection. Adapter theory is compression as constrained movement.

Beyond Muon And Polar Express

Problem. For matrix-shaped weights, a raw update can pour too much movement into a few directions and starve others.

Object. A matrix update rewritten into its main directions and sizes before the step is taken.

Why it works. Normalizing or orthogonalizing an update is like straightening a set of handles before pulling. It can spread movement across useful matrix directions and make low-precision arithmetic easier, but only if those directions match the task. What must stay the same is the intended training push. What may change is how that push is split across the matrix. Formal labels if needed: the main independent directions inside the matrix, or singular directions; and splitting a matrix move into direction plus stretch, or polar decomposition.

Connection. Optimizer design becomes a question of how to distribute a training push, not just how large the push is.

Trust-Region LLM RL

Problem. Reinforcement learning can increase reward while moving the policy away from useful base-model behavior.

Object. Like letting a driver improve a route without leaving familiar roads, the update can improve reward only while behavior remains close to the trusted model.

Why it works. What may change is the response pattern that earns better reward. What must stay the same is the base behavior users still rely on. The boundary keeps reward training from making one large jump into behavior that scores well but breaks hidden capability. Formal label if needed: a stay-nearby boundary around trusted behavior, or a trust region.

Connection. The same boundary idea appears in CSPO, PACT, DPO variants, and safe generation.

Non-Euclidean Edge Of Stability

Problem. A training run can look unstable if measured with the wrong ruler.

Object. Stability measured with the same ruler the optimizer uses to take its steps.

Why it works. A mountain road looks longer on a flat map than it feels to a car using the road. Some optimizers normalize, rescale, or constrain their steps, so ordinary straight-line distance can misdescribe stability. What must stay the same is the actual update rule. What may change is the ruler used to describe it. Formal label if needed: distance measured by the optimizer's own ruler rather than ordinary straight-line distance, or non-Euclidean distance.

Connection. It prevents false explanations of training dynamics by measuring the wrong space.

OBJECT 02

Visible Signal Directions

Alignment-Sensitive Minimax Rates

Problem. A model can have many features and still miss the part of the data that predicts the answer.

Object. The target signal's location: whether it sits in the model's clear, strong feature directions or in weak noisy ones.

Why it works. Learning is easier when the needed clue is already in a place the model can hear above the noise. Then fewer examples are needed because each one points toward the same visible direction. What must stay the same is the answer-carrying signal. What may change is how strongly the model's representation exposes it. Formal labels if needed: the target clue sitting in directions the model can repeatedly see, or spectral alignment; and the number of visible directions that matter, or effective dimension.

Connection. This is the statistical form of DiReCT's update-direction logic.

Single-Head Attention In High Dimensions

Problem. Attention can find real matches, but large feature spaces also create many accidental matches.

Object. The query-key table checked for one matching pattern that stands above background noise.

Why it works. A repeated beat can be heard only when it is louder than the room noise. If the true association is strong enough, it appears as a dominant pattern in the table. What must stay the same is the token relationship. What may change is the amount of background variation. Formal label if needed: the ordered list of pattern strengths in the matching table, or a singular spectrum.

Connection. It links attention behavior to the same visible-signal logic used in sample-efficiency theory.

Why Deep Jacobian Spectra Separate

Problem. Loss curves say whether training improved, but not which input changes the network became sensitive to.

Object. A map of how tiny input or layer changes are magnified or muted as they pass through depth.

Why it works. A trained network can become like a sound board that raises a few meaningful sliders and lowers many irrelevant ones. What must stay the same is the small input change being tested. What may change is how much each layer amplifies it. Formal labels if needed: Jacobian for the table of tiny-change effects, and spectrum for the ordered list of which effects are large or small.

Connection. This explains the local shape of the function learned by training.

BBP Transitions

Problem. A useful hidden pattern can exist in the data before the training algorithm has a clean direction to follow.

Object. The moment when a hidden signal becomes strong enough to separate from random background variation.

Why it works. Before separation, the signal is like a faint mark inside static. After separation, it creates a visible direction that the learner can use. What must stay the same is the hidden pattern. What may change is signal strength, sample size, or noise. Formal labels if needed: Hessian spectral outlier for a direction that sticks out from noise on the fragile-direction map, and phase transition for the before/after visibility threshold.

Connection. This gives a first-principles account of when hidden structure becomes visible to training.

Low-Rank LM Logits

Problem. The model scores an enormous vocabulary, but those scores may be controlled by a much smaller number of shared causes.

Object. Prompt-response score tables checked for a small set of directions that explain most score changes.

Why it works. If many token preferences move together, the raw vocabulary size exaggerates how complicated the behavior is. Low rank means many scores can be rebuilt from a few shared score factors. Latent directions are those hidden shared causes.

Connection. It connects representation shape to compression and steering.

SVD As Fast Interpretability

Problem. A full cause audit is slow, but an auditor still needs a first map of which parts drive predictions.

Object. A table split into the strongest directions of variation and their sizes.

Why it works. SVD is like sorting the main ways outputs vary from largest to smallest. It does not prove cause by itself, but it tells the auditor where to look next and which smaller direction families may carry most of the behavior.

Connection. The method turns visible signal directions into an audit instrument.

OBJECT 03

Architecture As Computation

Transformer Circuits Can Realize Clustering Algorithms

Problem. Saying "attention layer" does not say what step-by-step procedure the layer is carrying out.

Object. A transformer stack broken into assign, gather, update, and repeat steps.

Why it works. Clustering asks each point which center it belongs to, averages points inside each group, moves the centers, and repeats. Attention can perform the matching and gathering pieces; feed-forward blocks can update the centers. What must stay the same is the clustering procedure. What may change is which transformer parts implement each step.

Connection. It is the cleanest answer to why theory matters: architecture becomes a computation whose parts can be inspected.

Softmax Turing

Problem. A model may be capable of a kind of computation in principle without learning that computation from the data we give it.

Object. A proof about what softmax-attention transformers can simulate if their pieces are arranged correctly.

Why it works. Owning enough parts to build a calculator is not the same as having built one from examples. This result answers the parts question: can the model family represent general computation at all? What must stay the same is the intended step-by-step computation. What may change is the arrangement of attention pieces that simulates it. Formal labels if needed: expressivity for what the model family can represent if arranged correctly, and learnability for whether examples can identify the right arrangement.

Connection. It pairs naturally with Language Generation In The Limit: possible is not the same as learnable.

HATSolver

Problem. Some problems are built from nested pieces, and flat sequence matching loses that structure.

Object. Attention arranged so smaller subproblems can be solved and then combined.

Why it works. A long equation is easier when parentheses and subexpressions stay visible. The model should not treat all tokens as one undifferentiated pile. What must stay the same is which parts belong together. What may change is the attention route used to solve and combine those parts. Formal label if needed: nested problem structure where smaller pieces must be solved before larger pieces, or algebraic hierarchy.

Connection. This is the theory analog of coarse-to-fine generation: preserve the parts that must later fit together.

Rational Transductors

Problem. A sequence model can memorize short examples but fail when the same rule is used at a longer length.

Object. Like reading a turn-signal pattern and carrying a small note of "what state am I in now?", the object is a rule update that can keep running as more symbols arrive.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the rule being followed. What may change is sequence length. Length generalization needs a reusable state update, not a table of positions seen during training. Formal labels if needed: machines that carry a small state while reading symbols, or finite automata; and sequence-to-sequence rule machines with that reusable state, or rational transductors.

Connection. It links sequence modeling to explicit state updates instead of treating extrapolation as a scaling accident.

Exact GNN Algorithms

Problem. A graph neural network may fit training graphs while still being unable to run the intended graph algorithm.

Object. Conditions under which graph structure, model size, and number precision are enough to recover the algorithm exactly.

Why it works. The paper turns "can the network learn it?" into a bounded engineering question: how many neighbors can each node have, how large must the network be, and how much numeric precision is required? What must stay the same is the intended graph procedure. What may change is the graph size and numeric representation. Formal label if needed: a finite learning proof that studies training through a simplified large-network lens, or NTK-style theory.

Connection. It belongs with Floating-Point Neural Networks: computation claims must survive numeric reality.

Mamba Markov ICL

Problem. A recurrence is useful only if it remembers the facts that matter for the next prediction.

Object. Hidden state as a compact record of observed transitions.

Why it works. If the next word mostly depends on the current situation, the model needs a running note of what has followed what so far. Rare observations should count, but not make the model wildly overconfident. What must stay the same is the next-step rule implied by the useful history. What may change is the compact state used to summarize that history. Formal labels if needed: a state that carries enough history for the next step, or Markov; and cautious counting rules that revise belief without overreacting, or Bayes/minimax estimators.

Connection. It links architecture, inference, and memory in one object.

OBJECT 04

Games And Constraints

Nash Equilibria With Coupling Constraints

Problem. Several players may share the same limited resource, so one player's move changes what remains possible for the others.

Object. A joint allowed-action space plus a stable point where no player wants to move alone.

Why it works. Before an algorithm can claim it will settle, the stable point must actually exist inside the shared limits. Topology is the formal study of the allowed space's shape; Nash equilibrium is the formal stable point under everyone else's choices.

Connection. This is the strategic version of physical feasibility.

FTRL Lower Bounds

Problem. A learning rule can avoid repeated regret and still take far too long to settle into stable joint behavior.

Object. The time needed for follow-the-regularized-leader updates to reach equilibrium in games.

Why it works. Low regret means each player is not doing badly on average compared with a fixed alternative. It does not mean the players have stopped chasing each other's changing actions. The lower bound shows that even structured games can force very long learning paths.

Connection. It is a warning for multi-agent AI: interaction can make settling time the real bottleneck.

Fair Optimal Transport

Problem. The cheapest way to match two groups can create unfair pairings.

Object. A matching table that says how much weight moves from each source group to each target group, with fairness rules built in.

Why it works. If fairness is checked only after the cheapest matching is chosen, the unfair assignment has already happened. What must stay fixed is the fairness rule on who may be paired or moved together. What may change is the matching table chosen inside that rule. Formal label if needed: optimal transport for moving probability mass at minimum cost while obeying the rules.

Connection. It shares probability-moving math with planning and robust decisions.

Data Market Pricing And Bayesian Truthful Valuation

Problem. People who sell or contribute data may exaggerate value, hide flaws, or respond to payment rules.

Object. A market rule that turns private evidence and incentives into prices and reports.

Why it works. The rule must make honest reporting the easier winning move, or the learning system receives distorted evidence. Bayesian means the buyer updates uncertainty from reported evidence; truthful valuation means the payment rule makes lying unattractive under the stated assumptions.

Connection. Governance becomes mathematical when the evidence source is strategic.

Robust Contextual Optimization With Missing Covariates

Problem. A decision can become overconfident when missing facts are filled in as if they were known.

Object. Like choosing while some form fields are blank, the object is a set of still-plausible worlds that agree with the facts we actually observed.

Why it works. Instead of pretending the missing values are facts, the optimizer chooses an action that survives the remaining plausible completions. What must stay visible is doubt about the blank fields. What may change is which completion of those fields turns out to be true. Formal labels if needed: the set of possible worlds still consistent with the evidence, or an ambiguity set; and the background facts used to choose the action, or covariates.

Connection. This is partial observation in decision-optimization form.

MORetro*

Problem. A synthesis route can be cheap but toxic, high-yield but unsustainable, or safe but slow.

Object. A menu of routes where improving one criterion would require worsening another.

Why it works. There is no single universal score for every deployment. A lab may prefer safer, cheaper, greener, or faster routes depending on the situation. What must stay the same is that no listed route is simply worse on every important criterion. What may change is which priority the user chooses. Formal label if needed: the menu boundary where no route is simply worse than another on every criterion, or a Pareto front.

Connection. The same tradeoff-menu idea explains tool-use efficiency, safety-fidelity tradeoffs, and protein Best-of-N selection.

OBJECT 05

Sampling And Inference

GWF

Problem. Two objectives can give similar final scores while moving the whole probability cloud along different routes.

Object. Like moving piles of sand from one shape to another, the loss does not just say the shapes differ; it says where the mass should move next.

Why it works. What must stay clear is the target comparison between the old probability cloud and the desired one. What may change is the route used to move probability. A mismatch score becomes an instruction for movement once it is optimized. Formal label if needed: probability movement that pays for travel distance, or Wasserstein flow; and a different kind of mismatch ruler, or divergence.

Connection. This is the theory behind flow and diffusion samplers as controlled probability movement.

Path-Dependent DAI

Problem. In a construction process, the same next choice can mean different things depending on the path already taken.

Object. A sampler that treats the unfinished object and its history as the current state.

Why it works. If a molecule route or agent trace has a prefix, that prefix changes what moves remain sensible. The method does not pretend each next choice is independent of history. What must stay the same is the current unfinished object plus the facts from the route that still constrain it. What may change is the next action sampled. Formal labels if needed: a rulebook for current state, allowed action, and next state, or a Markov decision process; state that must carry the route so far, or path-dependent state; and hidden route facts that shape the next move, or latent dynamics.

Connection. It matches molecule synthesis routes and agent traces: prefixes carry state.

CreDRO And CRC

Problem. A prediction set can have the right statistical coverage and still be useless for the decision it supports.

Object. A set of possible answers shaped around the cost of the downstream action.

Why it works. If one mistake is harmless and another is dangerous, the uncertainty report should keep the dangerous doubt visible. What must stay the same is the decision-relevant uncertainty. What may change is which plausible answer distribution is true. Formal labels if needed: sets that keep several plausible answer distributions alive instead of collapsing them too early, or credal and robust sets.

Connection. It is evaluation uncertainty made actionable.

Weak Diffusion Priors

Problem. A generative model used as a prior may know useful image or signal structure but still be wrong about the exact scientific source.

Object. Like using a sketch to help restore a damaged photo while letting the actual measurements overrule the sketch, the object is a reconstruction rule that balances both.

Why it works. The model's guess is helpful only where measurements are incomplete. What must stay the same is obedience to observed evidence. What may change is the reconstructed image or signal. Measurements correct the sketch where it is wrong, while the sketch fills in shape where evidence is thin. Formal labels if needed: the revised belief after measurements, or posterior; and a helpful starting guess that is not exactly true, or prior mismatch.

Connection. It is the backbone for using diffusion models in science and medicine without letting them overrule evidence.

Linear Recurrent Memory

Problem. When an agent cannot see the whole world, memory helps only if it keeps the facts needed for future choices.

Object. Like carrying a small notebook that records only the facts still needed for the next decision, the object is a compact memory updated after each observation.

Why it works. What must stay the same is the decision the full history would support. What may change is the compressed note that replaces the full history. Remembering everything is expensive, and remembering the wrong summary is useless; the paper states when a linear recurrent state is enough for the next optimal decision. Formal label if needed: sufficient statistic, meaning a shorter saved record that keeps every past fact needed for the next decision.

Connection. It connects agents, Mamba-style models, and state estimation.

OU Identifiability And Latent Hawkes Causal Discovery

Problem. Scientific time series may be generated by hidden forces or event triggers that are only partly observed.

Object. Tests for whether the observed times, intervals, or interventions rule out rival hidden process stories.

Why it works. A fitted curve is not enough. Two different hidden process stories can draw the same curve while disagreeing about what would happen after an action. What must stay the same is the observed timing evidence. What may change is the hidden mechanism story. Formal labels if needed: evidence strong enough to pick one mechanism, or identifiability; and mechanism state that affects events but is not directly observed, or latent state.

Connection. This is causal discovery as a boundary on what the evidence can prove.

OBJECT 06

Scaling And Learnability

Language Generation In The Limit

Problem. A correct generator may exist, but finite data may not reveal which generator it is.

Object. The amount of evidence needed to rule out rival language rules that fit the same examples.

Why it works. The sequence 2, 4, 6 could mean many rules, not only "add two." Seeing a few strings from a language has the same problem: many rules agree now and disagree later. What must stay the same is the true rule being claimed. What may change is the number and variety of examples. Formal labels if needed: how much data is needed, or sample complexity; and whether the rule could be represented at all, or expressivity.

Connection. It is the caution paired with Softmax Turing.

Coverage Principle

Problem. Post-training cannot reliably select an answer the base model almost never produces.

Object. The amount of probability the base model already assigns to good responses.

Why it works. A chooser cannot pick the good option if it almost never appears on the menu. Rewarding, reranking, or taking the best of many samples helps only when the base model can already produce enough good candidates. What must stay the same is the target answer quality. What may change is how many candidates are sampled or selected. Formal label if needed: enough reachable good probability mass, or coverage.

Connection. This is the reachable-answer condition for reasoning outputs.

Intrinsic Entropy And MrRoPE

Problem. A longer context window helps only when the data contains useful long-range clues and the position system preserves their relationships.

Object. The amount of genuinely different information in the data, plus a coordinate rule for positions beyond the trained range.

Why it works. More pages help only if they add new clues, not if they repeat the same clue many times. And a long address system helps only if old and new positions still mean the right things when stretched. What must stay the same is the relation between a fact and the place where it appears. What may change is context length. Formal label if needed: how many real alternatives the data contains, or entropy.

Connection. Long context is a state-recovery problem under data and coordinate constraints.

Power Law Compositional Reasoning

Problem. A model cannot combine reusable skills later if training rarely shows the pieces in useful variety.

Object. The frequency pattern of simple pieces and their combinations in the training data.

Why it works. Some pieces must appear often enough to be learned, while rarer combinations must still appear enough to teach recombination. A power law is a formal frequency shape with many common pieces and many rare ones. Data volume alone is not the key; the shape of exposure controls what can be reused.

Connection. It links data distribution to reasoning structure.

Hallucination Rate-Distortion

Problem. A model with limited memory cannot store every factual distinction at full detail.

Object. A tradeoff between compact factual storage and the errors caused by compression.

Why it works. If a notebook has too little space, some details get merged: two names, dates, or sources may become hard to separate later. The theory asks which false answers become likely when the stored record is too small or too blurry. What must stay the same is the factual distinction needed by the question. What may change is the storage budget. Formal label if needed: the tradeoff between storage size and reconstruction error, or rate-distortion.

Connection. It links factuality to information theory rather than surface confidence.

QAT Scaling And WaterSIC

Problem. A smaller-number model fails when rounding damage hits behavior-critical quantities.

Object. Numeric precision treated as a budget assigned across weights and activations.

Why it works. Quantization replaces high-detail numbers with a smaller grid. The question is which rounding errors the function can tolerate and which destroy useful directions. Quantization-aware training and related scaling laws turn low-bit deployment into controlled approximation rather than blind shrinking.

Connection. This is the systems version of visible signal directions: preserve the quantities that carry behavior.

OBJECT 07

Certificates

Finite Test Certification

Problem. Benchmark claims often talk about broad deployment behavior while measuring only a finite set of tasks.

Object. A calculation of how many tested environments are needed for the broader claim.

Why it works. A finite test can support only a bounded promise: it says how far the unseen world may be from the tested world before the claim no longer follows. What must stay fixed is the allowed difference between test and deployment. What may change is which finite tasks are sampled. Formal labels if needed: the allowed distance between the tested mix and the deployment mix, or chi-square radius; and how many tests are needed, or sample complexity.

Connection. It is the theory version of UFEval and micro-benchmark reliability.

Pi-net

Problem. A neural network can output an answer that violates a hard rule, even if violations are penalized during training.

Object. A layer that maps the network's proposed answer back into the allowed set before it is used.

Why it works. If a rule must never be broken, the model should not merely be scolded after breaking it. The architecture repairs the proposed answer before use and trains through that repair. What must stay the same is membership in the allowed set. What may change is the model's first proposal. Formal labels if needed: moving to the nearest allowed answer, or projection; breaking repair into simpler repair steps, or operator splitting; and learning through the repair operation, or implicit differentiation.

Connection. It is feasible-set theory turned into architecture.

Provable NAM Explanations

Problem. An explanation can sound plausible without proving it is enough to support the model's output.

Object. A small set of feature contributions that is sufficient to justify the prediction under a structured model.

Why it works. Neural additive models split the prediction into pieces, one per feature or feature group. That structure makes it possible to ask fewer targeted questions and still prove which pieces carry the answer. Query-efficient means needing few checks; sufficient explanation means the named pieces are enough.

Connection. It shows why architecture choice affects what can be explained.

Gaussian Certified Unlearning And Exact RL Unlearning

Problem. Deleting data is not proven if the trained model still behaves differently because that data was once present.

Object. Like checking whether a removed ingredient can still be detected in the finished dish, the object is whether the released model reveals that the deleted record was ever used.

Why it works. The guarantee compares two histories: one where the record appeared and was removed, and one where it never appeared. What must stay the same is what the auditor is allowed to observe. What may change is the hidden training history. In RL, the removed data may affect whole action paths and policies, not just labels. Formal labels if needed: a test for telling two histories apart, or hypothesis testing; and the largest observable behavior difference between them, or total variation.

Connection. Unlearning is privacy, optimization, and decision-process certification at once.

Floating-Point Neural Networks

Problem. A proof that assumes perfect real numbers may not describe the model that runs on actual hardware.

Object. Learning guarantees checked under finite-width numbers and finite arithmetic operations.

Why it works. Computers round numbers and can represent only a limited set of values. That changes which parameters exist and how operations behave. A guarantee that survives floating point is closer to the system people actually deploy.

Connection. It makes numerical representation part of learning theory.

Catch-22

Problem. A watermark cannot be maximally hidden, hard to remove, and easy to detect all at once.

Object. The tradeoff between making watermarked text look ordinary and making it separable by a detector.

Why it works. Detection compares two possible sources: marked and unmarked text. If the two are too similar, the detector misses the mark; if they are too different, the mark is visible or fragile. What must stay the same is the source signal after ordinary transformations. What may change is the detector's tolerance for false alarms and missed marks. Formal label if needed: how separable the two text distributions are under the test, or KL.

Connection. It links safety evaluation to classical decision theory.

SYNTHESIS

The Cross-Paper Shape

Movement Ruler Before Descent

Adam Degeneracy, DiReCT, LoRA theory, Beyond Muon, Non-Euclidean EoS, Alignment-Sensitive Minimax Rates, and Jacobian-spectra papers all say that a training push is meaningful only after the movement ruler is named. The same push can help, stall, or break a model depending on which directions are allowed, which are fragile, and which tiny-change effects are being measured.

Can Represent Is Not Can Learn

Transformer Circuits, Softmax Turing, HATSolver, Rational Transductors, Exact GNN Algorithms, and Language Generation in the Limit separate two questions: can the model family hold the rule at all, and can finite data plus training actually find it? That distinction blocks the lazy assumption that a bigger system will automatically discover the right algorithm.

Guarantees Before Claims

Finite Test Certification, Pi-net, unlearning papers, Floating-Point Neural Networks, Catch-22, Fair OT, and mechanism-design work all force the claim to name its checker. The big-picture problem is not proving everything; it is refusing to claim more than the allowed state space, sample size, arithmetic, and incentive rule can support.
