FIRST PRINCIPLE

A Recipe Works Only In A Geometry

An optimizer update, attention layer, benchmark score, market mechanism, or sampler is never just an implementation detail. It defines what can move, what can be observed, what is counted as equivalent, what evidence is enough, and what cost is ignored. Theory becomes useful when it exposes that hidden object and states the regime where the method should work.

The big picture across these papers is a shift from surface performance to structural explanation. Instead of saying that a method works because it is larger, deeper, or better tuned, the papers ask: which directions contain signal, which directions are stiff or noisy, which transformations preserve the object, which equilibrium is reachable, and which finite test could actually certify the claim?

OBJECT 01

Update Geometry

Adam Degeneracy

Problem. Degenerate objectives can look too flat for ordinary local convergence stories.

Object. An adaptive optimizer dynamical system near degenerate minima.

Why it works. Adam rescales coordinates using gradient history, so flat or ill-conditioned directions are not seen in the same geometry as plain gradient descent. The paper explains when degeneracy is not fatal because the update rule changes the effective metric.

Connection. This is the base pattern for the lane: the optimizer's geometry decides which movement is possible.

DiReCT

Problem. Late training data can improve validation or damage brittle directions depending on its gradient.

Object. Alignment with flat validation-Hessian eigendirections under a stiff-direction energy budget.

Why it works. Good late data moves parameters along directions where validation can improve without crossing high-curvature regions. The method makes data selection a curvature-controlled update, not a quality ranking.

Connection. It links theory to curriculum and systems: an example matters through the movement it induces at that moment.

LoRA Gradient Descent And LoRA-Pre

Problem. Adapter training uses low-rank factorizations and compressed optimizer state, but generic smooth optimization theory does not describe the actual object.

Object. Movement inside factorized low-rank parameter subspaces plus projected optimizer memory.

Why it works. Low-rank updates restrict the degrees of freedom, reducing memory and focusing adaptation. The hard part is proving that the factorized path still descends and that projected momentum keeps useful history.

Connection. Adapter theory is compression as constrained movement.

Beyond Muon And Polar Express

Problem. Matrix updates can be normalized, orthogonalized, or decomposed, but it is unclear which geometry helps.

Object. The singular structure of optimizer updates.

Why it works. Orthogonalization controls how update energy is distributed across matrix directions. Polar-decomposition methods make that control practical under low precision, but the theory also shows when compressed orthogonal movement is not automatically superior.

Connection. Optimizer design becomes representation geometry applied to gradients.

Trust-Region LLM RL

Problem. Reinforcement learning can increase reward while moving the policy away from useful base-model behavior.

Object. A bounded neighborhood around the current policy.

Why it works. The update is allowed to improve reward only inside a region where the old policy still constrains the new one. This turns post-training into controlled displacement rather than an unconstrained reward chase.

Connection. The same boundary idea appears in CSPO, PACT, DPO variants, and safe generation.

Non-Euclidean Edge Of Stability

Problem. Sharpness measured in ordinary Euclidean coordinates can misdescribe optimizers that normalize, precondition, or constrain updates.

Object. Stability in the metric induced by the optimizer.

Why it works. A method can look unstable under one geometry and stable under the geometry it actually follows. The theory asks whether the update remains on a controlled boundary in its own coordinates.

Connection. It prevents false explanations of training dynamics by measuring the wrong space.

OBJECT 02

Spectra

Alignment-Sensitive Minimax Rates

Problem. Kernel complexity alone does not say whether the target is easy to learn.

Object. Effective span dimension: target signal in the leading learned spectral span above noise.

Why it works. Risk improves when the representation's strong directions align with the target. Fewer noisy directions need to be estimated, so generalization depends on signal placement, not only sample count.

Connection. This is the statistical form of DiReCT's update-direction logic.

Single-Head Attention In High Dimensions

Problem. Attention generalization is hard to explain from layer diagrams or parameter counts.

Object. Query-key singular spectra and recovery thresholds.

Why it works. Singular directions determine which associations can be recovered from high-dimensional noise. The paper turns attention into a measurable spectral estimator rather than a black-box matching mechanism.

Connection. It links attention behavior to the same signal-noise geometry used in minimax rates.

Why Deep Jacobian Spectra Separate

Problem. Deep networks often form low-rank derivative structure, but loss curves hide why.

Object. Singular values of layerwise Jacobian products.

Why it works. Depth and gating can amplify some derivative directions while suppressing others. That separation explains how networks become sensitive along recoverable directions and insensitive elsewhere.

Connection. Jacobian spectra are the local geometry of the function learned by optimization.

BBP Transitions

Problem. A representation may contain signal statistically before it becomes recoverable algorithmically.

Object. Hessian spectral outliers in teacher-student models.

Why it works. Random-matrix phase transitions identify when planted signal separates from the noise bulk. Once an outlier appears, the learner has a direction worth following.

Connection. This gives a first-principles account of when hidden structure becomes visible to training.

Low-Rank LM Logits

Problem. A vocabulary-sized output space appears enormous, but model behavior may live in fewer directions.

Object. Approximate rank of prompt-response logit matrices.

Why it works. If logits concentrate near a low-rank subspace, many output preferences are governed by shared latent directions. This makes behavior more compressible and more interpretable than the raw vocabulary suggests.

Connection. It connects representation geometry to compression and steering.

SVD As Fast Interpretability

Problem. Full mechanistic analysis is expensive, but audits still need to know which subspaces drive predictions.

Object. Subspace contributions from singular-vector decompositions.

Why it works. SVD exposes dominant linear directions that account for variation in outputs. It is not a complete mechanism, but it can rapidly identify candidate subspaces for deeper causal checks.

Connection. The method turns spectra into an audit instrument.

OBJECT 03

Architecture As Computation

Transformer Circuits Can Realize Clustering Algorithms

Problem. Saying "attention layer" does not name the algorithm being executed.

Object. A transformer circuit implementing assignment, aggregation, update, and iteration.

Why it works. Lloyd-style clustering can be decomposed into operations that attention and feed-forward blocks can represent. The proof gives a bridge between architecture and algorithmic primitive.

Connection. It is the cleanest answer to why theory matters: architecture becomes a checkable computation.

Softmax Turing

Problem. Long reasoning requires knowing what computation a model class can express in principle.

Object. Softmax-attention transformers as simulators of formal computation.

Why it works. The result separates expressivity from learnability. A model class may be able to simulate general computation, but that does not prove data and optimization will find the algorithm.

Connection. It pairs naturally with Language Generation in the Limit: possible is not the same as learnable.

HATSolver

Problem. Some tasks require hierarchical algebraic structure rather than flat sequence matching.

Object. Attention arranged around algebraic hierarchy.

Why it works. The architecture matches the decomposition of the mathematical problem. Subexpressions can be solved and combined with structure instead of relying on one undifferentiated hidden state.

Connection. This is the theory analog of protein coarse-to-fine generation.

Rational Transductors

Problem. Length generalization fails when a sequence model lacks a stable recurrence for the rule being learned.

Object. Weighted-finite-automata-derived matrix recurrences inside attention.

Why it works. Regular-language structure is encoded as an explicit state update. The model gets a recurrence object that can extrapolate rather than memorizing finite positions.

Connection. It links sequence modeling to classical automata instead of treating extrapolation as a scaling accident.

Exact GNN Algorithms

Problem. A graph neural network may fit examples while being incapable of representing the intended algorithm.

Object. NTK-style learnability under bounded-degree and finite-precision constraints.

Why it works. The paper states when architecture, size, and precision are enough for exact algorithm recovery. It makes expressivity finite and operational instead of purely asymptotic.

Connection. It belongs with Floating-Point Neural Networks: computation claims must survive numeric reality.

Mamba Markov ICL

Problem. State-space models need a concrete account of what their recurrence estimates.

Object. Bayes- and minimax-optimal Laplacian smoothing estimators for Markov chains.

Why it works. The recurrence can be judged by whether it implements the right statistical update for a Markov source. That turns in-context learning into estimator analysis.

Connection. It links architecture, inference, and memory in one object.

OBJECT 04

Games And Constraints

Nash Equilibria With Coupling Constraints

Problem. Multiple players can share constraints, so one agent's action changes what others may do.

Object. A constrained game with feasible-set topology.

Why it works. Equilibrium existence must be proved before an algorithm can claim to converge. Shared constraints make optimality relational, not individual.

Connection. This is the strategic version of physical feasibility.

FTRL Lower Bounds

Problem. Common no-regret dynamics are often assumed to find equilibria quickly.

Object. Convergence time of FTRL in potential games.

Why it works. The lower bound shows that even structured games can create exponentially slow learning paths. It prevents a mistaken inference from regret guarantees to practical equilibrium reachability.

Connection. It is a warning for multi-agent AI: interaction can make optimization time the real bottleneck.

Fair Optimal Transport

Problem. Cheap distribution matching can produce unfair couplings.

Object. A transport plan constrained by fairness.

Why it works. Fairness is imposed on the mass movement itself, not audited after the cheapest coupling is chosen. Sinkhorn-style structure gives a computational handle on that constrained geometry.

Connection. It shares transport math with MAPF and distributional robustness.

Data Market Pricing And Bayesian Truthful Valuation

Problem. Data contributors and sellers may act strategically, so evidence quality depends on incentives.

Object. A mechanism over reusable information goods.

Why it works. Pricing and truthfulness conditions make distorted reporting less attractive. The learning pipeline becomes a game whose equilibrium determines what data reaches the model.

Connection. Governance becomes mathematical when the evidence source is strategic.

Robust Contextual Optimization With Missing Covariates

Problem. Decisions under missing covariates become overconfident if missing values are imputed as facts.

Object. An ambiguity set over plausible completions or distributions.

Why it works. The optimizer chooses actions that perform across the remaining plausible worlds. Uncertainty is kept alive until the decision instead of being erased upstream.

Connection. This is LIMSSR and partial-observation agents in decision-optimization form.

MORetro*

Problem. Retrosynthesis has cost, sustainability, toxicity, yield, and user criteria, not one universal objective.

Object. A Pareto front of feasible synthesis routes.

Why it works. The method aims to recover a tradeoff boundary rather than a single scalar optimum. This preserves choices that matter under different deployment priorities.

Connection. Pareto fronts also explain tool-use efficiency, safety-fidelity tradeoffs, and protein Best-of-N selection.

OBJECT 05

Sampling And Inference

GWF

Problem. Different distributional objectives imply different movement through probability space.

Object. Wasserstein flows for f-divergences, integral probability metrics, and squared MMD.

Why it works. A divergence is not just a score; it defines a direction for moving distributions. Generalizing the flow view explains what optimization path each objective induces.

Connection. This is the theory behind flow and diffusion samplers as controlled transport.

Path-Dependent DAI

Problem. Discrete construction choices change the meaning of future choices.

Object. A path-dependent Markov decision process with latent dynamics.

Why it works. The sampler conditions on the full construction history, so partial objects are treated as evidence. This avoids pretending each next choice has the same meaning independent of the path.

Connection. It matches molecule synthesis routes and agent traces: prefixes carry state.

CreDRO And CRC

Problem. Prediction sets can be calibrated but still not protect the downstream decision.

Object. Credal or robust prediction sets tied to decision loss.

Why it works. The uncertainty set is designed around the action it will support. This keeps the set from being a statistical ornament with no operational value.

Connection. It is evaluation uncertainty made actionable.

Weak Diffusion Priors

Problem. Inverse problems use powerful generative priors that are never exactly matched to the true data source.

Object. Posterior recovery under prior mismatch and measurement evidence.

Why it works. Consistency can hold when observations dominate the wrong parts of the prior. The theory says when a weak prior helps structure the solution without forcing the wrong answer.

Connection. It is the mathematical backbone for using diffusion models in science and medicine.

Linear Recurrent Memory

Problem. Partial observability requires memory, but not every recurrent state is a sufficient statistic.

Object. A compressed memory state preserving information needed for optimal policy learning.

Why it works. Recurrence helps only when hidden-state information relevant to future decisions can be represented linearly enough for learning. The paper states conditions where memory is not just capacity, but the right statistic.

Connection. It connects agents, Mamba-style models, and state estimation.

OU Identifiability And Latent Hawkes Causal Discovery

Problem. Scientific time series often hide causal or stochastic mechanisms behind incomplete observations.

Object. Identifiable drift or triggering structure under observational and interventional moments.

Why it works. The papers state which moments, intervals, or event patterns distinguish one latent process from another. The result is a boundary on what can be inferred, not only an algorithm for fitting.

Connection. This is causal discovery as probabilistic identification.

OBJECT 06

Scaling And Learnability

Language Generation In The Limit

Problem. Formal computability does not imply feasible learning from finite samples.

Object. Sample complexity of formal-language generation classes.

Why it works. The paper separates "there exists a generator" from "evidence can identify it within realistic data." That prevents architecture expressivity from being mistaken for learnability.

Connection. It is the caution paired with Softmax Turing.

Coverage Principle

Problem. Post-training and Best-of-N can only amplify responses reachable under the base distribution.

Object. Pretrained probability mass on high-quality responses.

Why it works. If good answers have near-zero base probability, selection and reward cannot reliably find them. Coverage makes base-model support a precondition for downstream improvement.

Connection. This is mode coverage for reasoning outputs.

Intrinsic Entropy And MrRoPE

Problem. Longer context is useful only if training statistics and positional geometry support it.

Object. Data entropy and positional extrapolation geometry.

Why it works. Intrinsic entropy says the data distribution bounds useful context length; MrRoPE asks whether position encodings preserve relations when extended. Both prevent context length from being treated as a free scalar.

Connection. Long context is a state-recovery problem under data and coordinate constraints.

Power Law Compositional Reasoning

Problem. Compositional generalization depends on whether reusable skills appear with the right frequency.

Object. A sampling distribution over composable skill pieces.

Why it works. Power-law sampling can expose reusable components often enough for the learner to combine them later. Data volume alone is not the key; the frequency shape controls the evidence for parts.

Connection. It links data distribution to reasoning structure.

Hallucination Rate-Distortion

Problem. Factual memory under limited capacity must trade storage cost against error.

Object. Sparse membership testing under lossy compression.

Why it works. The paper reframes hallucination as an expected error under capacity limits. That does not excuse hallucination; it clarifies which failures are predicted by compression pressure.

Connection. It links factuality to information theory rather than surface confidence.

QAT Scaling And WaterSIC

Problem. Low-bit models fail when quantization error is not allocated according to functional importance.

Object. Precision as an information budget over weights and activations.

Why it works. Separating weight and activation errors, or proving rate gaps for quantizers, turns compression into a controlled approximation problem. The method asks which numeric directions must survive.

Connection. This is the systems version of spectra: preserve the information-bearing directions.

OBJECT 07

Certificates

Finite Test Certification

Problem. Benchmark claims often overreach the amount of evidence in the test set.

Object. Sample complexity governed by distribution-family chi-square radius.

Why it works. The theorem states how many environments are needed before a generality claim is defensible. Evaluation becomes estimation with error bars, not a scoreboard ritual.

Connection. It is the theory version of UFEval and micro-benchmark reliability.

Pi-net

Problem. Neural outputs can violate hard convex constraints if feasibility is only penalized.

Object. Operator-splitting projection layers with implicit differentiation.

Why it works. The network output is mapped into the feasible set by construction. Gradients still flow through the projection, so learning respects the constraint rather than optimizing outside it and repairing afterward.

Connection. It is feasible-set theory turned into architecture.

Provable NAM Explanations

Problem. Explanations are often plausible narratives rather than certificates.

Object. Query-efficient sufficient explanations in neural additive models.

Why it works. Additive structure makes it possible to search for explanations with formal query bounds. Interpretability becomes a verifiable property of the model class.

Connection. It shows why architecture choice affects what can be explained.

Gaussian Certified Unlearning And Exact RL Unlearning

Problem. Deleting data is meaningless unless an auditor can certify what influence remains.

Object. Hypothesis-testing or total-variation-style guarantees after removal.

Why it works. The guarantee defines what evidence of the removed data should be statistically indistinguishable. In RL, deletion must cover policies shaped by trajectories, not only supervised labels.

Connection. Unlearning is privacy, optimization, and decision-process certification at once.

Floating-Point Neural Networks

Problem. Theoretical guarantees over exact real numbers may not describe deployed models.

Object. Depth, width, and computation under finite-precision arithmetic.

Why it works. Finite precision changes representable parameters and operations. A guarantee that survives floating point is closer to the system people actually run.

Connection. It makes numerical representation part of learning theory.

Catch-22

Problem. Watermarking cannot maximize stealth, robustness, and detectability independently.

Object. KL-based hypothesis-testing boundary between watermarked and unwatermarked text.

Why it works. Detection is a statistical test. The paper names the tradeoff frontier so safety claims can state which error rates and assumptions they pay for.

Connection. It links safety evaluation to classical decision theory.

SYNTHESIS

The Cross-Paper Shape

Geometry Before Descent

Adam Degeneracy, DiReCT, LoRA theory, Beyond Muon, Non-Euclidean EoS, Alignment-Sensitive Minimax Rates, and Jacobian-spectra papers all say that updates are meaningful only after the geometry is named. The same gradient can help, stall, or break a model depending on the metric and subspace in which it moves.

Expressivity Before Scaling

Transformer Circuits, Softmax Turing, HATSolver, Rational Transductors, Exact GNN Algorithms, and Language Generation in the Limit separate what a model class can express from what finite data and optimization can learn. This is the antidote to assuming that bigger systems automatically discover the right algorithm.

Guarantees Before Claims

Finite Test Certification, Pi-net, unlearning papers, Floating-Point Neural Networks, Catch-22, Fair OT, and mechanism-design work all force the claim to name its certificate. The big-picture problem is not proving everything; it is refusing to claim more than the state space, sample size, arithmetic, and incentive model can support.
