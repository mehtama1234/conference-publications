Problem

Scientific ML cannot ignore numerical implementation. A theorem over real numbers may fail in floating point, a PDE solver may violate time causality, and a model too expensive for hardware may never be used.

Approaches

The papers redesign PINNs, neural operators, Hamiltonian maps, reversible solvers, graph operators, number tokens, quantization, neuromorphic inference, and mechanistic simulators around real computational constraints.

Connecting read

This theme sits where algorithms meet machines: physics supplies structure, hardware supplies limits, and numerical design decides whether learning respects both.

FIRST PRINCIPLES

The core problem

Scientific computing is not just prediction. It is the controlled approximation of a process. The approximation must preserve causality, invariants, arithmetic meaning, stability, and hardware feasibility. If those constraints are lost, the model can be accurate on a benchmark while being unusable as a scientific tool.

The papers in this theme share a simple engineering stance: expose the real constraint and build around it. Time-dependent equations need causal temporal solvers. Molecular dynamics needs phase-space structure. Low-latency hardware needs event-driven propagation. Large models need low-bit conversion. Neural theory has to account for floating-point execution rather than ideal arithmetic.

SYNTHESIS

Four ways computation is made physical

1. Scientific solvers must preserve time, causality, and invariants

physical solvers

This cluster treats learned solvers as numerical methods. The hard part is not only fitting observed solutions, but preserving the structure that makes simulation reliable when steps get longer or conditions change.

ICLR 2026Frozen-PINN
Frozen-PINN uses frozen random spatial features and solves time coefficients with ODE machinery instead of optimizing a full PINN loss by gradient descent. Its second evidence row emphasizes separation of space and time, temporal causality, and decoupled PDE, boundary, and initial-condition losses. Together, the paper reframes PINNs as structured causal solvers rather than monolithic neural regressors.
ICML 2026IRNO
IRNO recasts neural-operator inference as iterative residual refinement with contraction-style convergence and fixed-point regularization. It treats inference itself as a solver. The model repeatedly corrects residual error, so the operator is judged by whether its refinement dynamics converge, not only by one forward pass accuracy.
ICML 2026Solving Time-Dependent Differential Equations with Physical Dynamical Systems
Solving Time-Dependent Differential Equations with Physical Dynamical Systems uses dynamical-system machines with state-aware nonlinearity and hierarchical temporal integration. The paper asks whether hardware-like dynamical processes can solve TDDEs faster by matching the temporal structure of the equation. Computation is organized as physical evolution.
ICML 2026Rex
Rex converts explicit ODE and SDE Runge-Kutta schemes into algebraically reversible exponential solvers for exact inversion. Reversibility matters when the solver must move backward without accumulating reconstruction error. The method makes a numerical property, exact inversion, part of the solver design rather than an afterthought.
ICML 2026Hamiltonian Flow Maps
Hamiltonian Flow Maps trains on independent machine-learned force-field phase-space samples without future states and supports larger integration timesteps. Molecular dynamics is governed by phase-space structure, not arbitrary trajectories. The method learns maps that respect that structure enough to step farther while staying physically meaningful.

2. Mechanistic models and graph operators carry domain structure

structured scientific models

Scientific domains often already contain strong structure: neural biophysics, meshes, graphs, and long-range relations. These papers use learning to parameterize that structure, not erase it.

ICML 2026Learning Biophysical Models for Neurostimulation
Learning Biophysical Models for Neurostimulation uses differentiable Hodgkin-Huxley simulation and simulation-based inference to infer biophysical parameters from scalable extracellular recordings. The model remains tied to retinal stimulation physics. That gives predictions a mechanistic route to generalize to unseen stimulation patterns.
ICML 2026S3GNN
S3GNN reports gains across long-range graph benchmarks, knowledge-graph question answering, and mesh-based fluid dynamics with fewer parameters. Its role here is showing why scientific graphs need both local message passing and global spectral mixing. Physical meshes can require long-range coordination that ordinary local GNNs compress poorly.

3. Machine arithmetic changes what models can represent

numbers and precision

The third cluster asks what happens when ideal mathematical models meet actual number systems. Tokens, floating-point operations, and low-bit weights are not implementation details; they define the computations the model can perform.

ICML 2026Floating-Point Neural Networks
Floating-Point Neural Networks shows that exact-arithmetic expressivity results do not directly transfer to implemented floating-point ReLU networks. That is a foundational warning. A network theorem over real numbers may overstate what the deployed model can compute under finite precision, rounding, and representable values.
ICML 2026BitTokens
BitTokens shows small language models can learn arithmetic nearly perfectly when numeric tokens preserve computational structure. Instead of scattering a number across subword pieces, it represents machine numbers directly. Arithmetic improves when the token interface matches the number system the model must reason over.
ICML 2026CAT-Q
CAT-Q reports ternary conversion for very large language models within practical GPU budgets. The importance is deployment pressure: if low-bit conversion can be done quickly and cheaply enough, compression becomes an operational tool rather than a research-only artifact. The question is how far precision can fall before useful behavior breaks.

4. Hardware-aware inference changes the computation schedule

event-driven hardware

The final cluster focuses on matching inference to hardware timing. Low-latency systems do not always want layer-by-layer dense evaluation; they may want sparse causal propagation driven by events.

ICML 2026ETTFS
ETTFS targets causal step-by-step time-to-first-spike propagation for low-latency neuromorphic hardware rather than ordinary layer-by-layer inference. The paper treats time as the computation carrier. If spikes arrive sparsely and causally, the hardware should propagate useful events immediately instead of waiting for dense synchronous layers.
FIELD READ

What this theme says about ICML and ICLR

The scientific-computing signal is that implementation is part of the theory. Causality, finite precision, reversibility, low-bit weights, event timing, and physical invariants all change what the model means.

That matters for the larger conference synthesis because many ML claims only become real when they survive the machine. This theme is the reminder that the benchmark object is not just the model; it is the model plus solver, arithmetic, hardware, and the scientific structure it is supposed to preserve.
