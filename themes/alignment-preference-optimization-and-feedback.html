Alignment, preference optimization, and feedback as reward design under uncertainty

The central problem is not simply collecting human preference. It is turning weak, noisy, biased, sparse, or verifier-shaped feedback into durable behavior without teaching the model to exploit the feedback channel.

Problem

Feedback is partial. Humans rank examples, verifiers check narrow outcomes, reward models compress values, and RL optimizes whatever signal is easiest to exploit.

Approaches

The papers study preference theory, DPO/RLHF limits, process rewards, multimodal reward models, safety tradeoffs, post-training curricula, and deployment-aligned objectives.

Connecting read

The field is learning that alignment is not a label. It is the design of the training signal, the optimization path, and the checks that keep the signal honest.

FIRST PRINCIPLES

The core problem

A model does not learn what humans want. It learns from an operational signal: preference pairs, rubrics, critiques, verifier pass/fail, reward scores, demonstrations, or deployment selection. The alignment problem is the gap between the intended value and the signal the optimizer can actually see. If the signal is too sparse, the model does not learn. If it is too dense but misspecified, the model hacks it. If it is personalized, one user's value may conflict with another's. If it is verifier-based, correctness can crowd out style, safety, or trust.

That is why these papers keep returning to the same questions from different angles. What does a preference comparison mean? When does DPO match RLHF and when does it optimize the wrong relative advantage? Which parts of a reasoning trace deserve credit? How should a reward model handle images, videos, audio, long writing, or clinical tutoring? How much can post-training move a model beyond base-model support? Alignment becomes an engineering discipline when those questions are made explicit.

SYNTHESIS

Seven ways the papers attack the feedback problem

1. Preference learning needs a theory of what feedback means

preference foundations

The first cluster asks what kind of information preference feedback actually carries. A ranking is not a reward. A human choice may reflect regret, style, social choice, personal taste, or comparison context. If the theory is wrong, the optimizer can faithfully improve the wrong object.

ICML 2026DPO Unchained
DPO Unchained recasts direct preference optimization through broader human-choice theory rather than treating one convex loss as the whole story. The paper matters because DPO has become an alignment default, but preference comparisons can encode many choice models. The correct objective depends on what the comparison is assumed to mean.
ICML 2026Conditional Equivalence of DPO and RLHF
Conditional Equivalence of DPO and RLHF shows that DPO can optimize relative advantage rather than absolute human alignment when a hidden RLHF-optimality assumption fails. The practical warning is direct: if the base policy and reward relation do not satisfy the needed condition, DPO can improve comparisons while moving away from the intended target.
ICLR 2026AuxDPO
AuxDPO studies DPO under unrealizable rewards and shows that policy-class mismatch can reverse preferences or lower reward. The issue is not bad optimization but misspecification. If the policy cannot represent the true preference structure, the DPO update can point in a misleading direction.
ICML 2026RePO
RePO models human preferences as judgments of relative suboptimality conditioned on behavior, rather than direct reward samples. That is a useful reframing because humans often compare how avoidable an error was, not the absolute reward of two outputs. It makes preference feedback closer to regret.
ICLR 2026Ranking Feedback Online Learning
Ranking Feedback Online Learning shows that ordinal feedback can be insufficient for sublinear regret in some online settings. The paper is a boundary result: rankings are cheaper than scalar rewards, but they may not contain enough information to learn efficiently. Feedback format is a statistical resource.
ICML 2026Axiomatic Value of Regularization
Axiomatic Value of Regularization shows that reference-policy regularization changes which social-choice axioms an RLHF-style aggregation satisfies. Regularization is not only a stability trick. It changes the collective preference rule the training procedure implements.
ICLR 2026MNPO
MNPO generalizes Nash learning from human feedback from two-player games to n-player policy populations. It treats preference optimization as a strategic equilibrium problem rather than a single-model climb up a reward function. That matters when multiple policies, users, or evaluators interact.
ICLR 2026EigenBench
EigenBench aggregates model-to-model judgments under a constitution with EigenTrust to score subjective alignment. It asks how to evaluate values when there is no single objective ground truth. The method turns comparative judgment into a reputation-like aggregation problem.
ICLR 2026P-GenRM
P-GenRM converts preference signals into adaptive personas and scoring rubrics for user-specific reward evaluation. It recognizes that alignment can be personal. A reward model that ignores user-specific context may optimize an average preference no one actually wants.
ICLR 2026WIMHF
WIMHF uses sparse autoencoders to describe human-feedback datasets with natural-language preference features. Its value is interpretability of the feedback source. Before optimizing a dataset, we need to know which preferences it contains and which hidden features dominate its judgments.
ICLR 2026AdAEM
AdAEM automatically generates value-probing questions to distinguish model inclinations beyond static harmless-helpful-honest style benchmarks. It treats value evaluation as adaptive measurement. A fixed benchmark can miss differences that only appear after asking the right probe.

2. Reward models must resist hacking and expose what they reward

reward design

This cluster tries to keep the reward signal honest. Dense rewards help learning, but they also create more surface area for exploitation. The papers decompose rewards, use binary factuality checks, stabilize red-team search, and make reward factors more interpretable.

ICML 2026BNRM
BNRM uses sparse non-negative latent factors inside Bradley-Terry preference modeling to reduce reward hacking. The key idea is interpretability by construction: if reward is built from additive non-negative factors, it is easier to see what the model is optimizing and harder for one hidden factor to dominate silently.
ICML 2026Binary RAR
Binary RAR argues that binary factuality rewards can avoid reward hacking seen with continuous factuality scores. A continuous score may reward generic claims that are easy to verify. A binary contradiction-free standard is stricter: one unsupported contradiction breaks the reward.
ICML 2026Stable-GFN
Stable-GFN avoids partition-function estimation and reduces mode collapse in reward-driven red-team generation. It matters because red-team reward models can collapse onto a few attack patterns. Stable diverse search is needed if the defender is not going to overfit to yesterday's jailbreaks.
ICML 2026VALUEFLOW
VALUEFLOW builds value extraction, value-intensity data, and anchor-based ranking evaluation to steer and score values across models. It makes a soft object measurable: not merely whether a value is present, but how intensely it shapes generation. That is important for alignment settings where overdoing a value can be as bad as missing it.
ICML 2026SVGT
SVGT maintains normative representations in a dedicated value space and steers generation through bridge tokens. It separates the value module from general language generation, which gives the system a controllable interface for reducing harmful behavior without rewriting the whole model.
ICML 2026RGR-GRPO
RGR-GRPO uses rubrics as dense rewards and offline guidance for multi-domain reasoning. Rubrics give the optimizer more than pass/fail, but they also define what counts as progress. The paper treats rubric design as part of reasoning alignment.
ICML 2026Critique-GRPO
Critique-GRPO combines numerical rewards with natural-language critiques so models can learn from failed attempts and guided refinements. The conceptual move is to make feedback explanatory, not only scalar. A critique can say why a path failed, which changes credit assignment.
ICML 2026LongWriter-Zero
LongWriter-Zero decomposes writing rewards into length control, writing quality, and structural formatting. Long-form writing exposes why single rewards are brittle: a model can satisfy length while destroying structure, or improve format while lowering substance. Separate incentives make the tradeoff inspectable.
ICLR 2026Omni-Reward
Omni-Reward builds reward benchmarks and data across text, image, video, audio, and 3D with free-form preferences. It expands reward modeling beyond text. The hard part is that each modality exposes different evidence for preference, so one reward format cannot simply be copied across domains.

3. Process supervision gives credit to the steps, not only the answer

reasoning feedback

Outcome reward is blunt. For reasoning, agents, and verification-heavy tasks, the training signal has to identify useful intermediate moves. These papers use traces, hidden states, critiques, verifiers, milestones, and visible plans to supervise the process itself.

ICML 2026LALP
LALP selects teacher traces from diverse pools by transferable local step transitions. The important idea is that a reasoning trace is useful if its local moves transfer, not just if its final answer is correct. That makes process data a source of reusable operators.
ICML 2026Complex Reasoning/TRM
Complex Reasoning/TRM trains reward models over reasoning quality for test-time selection and RL optimization. It treats traces as evaluable artifacts. The reward model is not judging only the final answer, but whether the path has the structure and efficiency of good reasoning.
ICML 2026Hista/Numca
Hista/Numca uses numerical spans and hidden-state rollout averaging to improve process-level credit assignment. It asks where value appears inside a trajectory. Hidden-state and milestone signals give RL more local information than final correctness alone.
ICLR 2026HERO
HERO combines sparse correctness checkers with dense reward-model scores for reasoning post-training. Sparse verifiers are trustworthy but uninformative; dense rewards are informative but fragile. HERO tries to get the learning signal from both while keeping verifier-defined correctness in the loop.
ICML 2026MoCA
MoCA uses perception verification and structured verbal checks to supervise multimodal process components rather than final answers alone. The task may fail because perception is wrong, because the verbal reasoning is wrong, or because the bridge between them is wrong. MoCA separates those failure points.
ICML 2026Agent0-VL
Agent0-VL aligns solver and verifier distributions through tool-grounded critique without external reward models. The system creates a self-reward cycle: solve, verify, critique, and improve. It matters because external rewards are expensive, while verifier-grounded critique can be generated inside the task loop.
ICML 2026PLAINTAIN
PLAINTAIN uses visible intermediate plans as an intervention interface while improving pass@1. The key idea is common ground. If a plan is visible, a user or system can correct it before the final answer, making alignment an interactive process rather than a hidden internal guess.
ICLR 2026EmotionThinker
EmotionThinker progressively introduces reasoning-quality reward weighted by alignment between rationale and outcome. It treats trust as consistency between why the model says it acted and what outcome it produced. That matters in affective or human-facing reasoning where rationales can be persuasive but wrong.
ICML 2026Know More Know Clearer
Know More Know Clearer partitions model knowledge into mastered, confused, and missing regions using internal cognitive signals. Alignment uses that map to decide what to teach, what to correct, and what to avoid over-trusting. The model's own uncertainty structure becomes training information.
ICML 2026BrokenMath
BrokenMath finds that strong models and agents still endorse false theorem statements despite mitigation attempts. The alignment lesson is anti-sycophancy: a model must resist user pressure or plausible framing when correctness is mathematically false. Preference tuning that rewards agreement can be actively harmful here.

4. Post-training has support limits, plasticity windows, and compute laws

optimization dynamics

RL and supervised post-training do not have unlimited power. They depend on what the base model can already sample, when specialization data enters, how plastic the model remains, and how update rules manage risk. These papers study the mechanics of changing a capable model after pretraining.

ICML 2026PG Post-Training
PG Post-Training shows outcome-reward policy gradients can be minimax optimal yet exponentially limited outside base-model support. The hard lesson is that RL cannot reliably teach what the base model almost never explores. Post-training is constrained by the support of the starting policy.
ICML 2026h1
h1 reports gains even at high pass@k, suggesting curriculum RL can learn reasoning paths rather than merely elicit samples already present in the base model. It pushes back on the pure-elicitation view of RL. The curriculum can expand effective capability when tasks are chosen carefully.
ICML 2026Pre/Mid/RL Reasoning
Pre/Mid/RL Reasoning finds RL gains when pretraining leaves headroom and RL data targets tasks just within reach. The phrase "edge of competence" is the core idea: feedback is most useful where the model can almost solve the problem, not where it is helpless or already saturated.
ICML 2026Midtraining Bridges Pretraining and Posttraining
Midtraining Bridges Pretraining and Posttraining finds that timing and mixture weight interact strongly, with late specialized-data introduction needing lower weights and limited compensation. Specialization is not just adding more data. It is a scheduling problem that affects plasticity and retention.
ICML 2026PRISM
PRISM finds that RL after PRISM mid-training gives stable monotonic gains while direct RL on base models is weaker. The alignment implication is that intermediate training can prepare the model for feedback optimization. RL stability may depend on what representation space post-training starts from.
ICLR 2026ScaleRL
ScaleRL fits sigmoidal compute-performance curves and separates compute efficiency from asymptotic RL performance. It gives post-training a scaling lens: some methods reach gains faster, while others may top out higher. That distinction matters when choosing RL budgets.
ICML 2026R2VPO
R2VPO improves reasoning and robotic-control sample efficiency by down-weighting risky updates instead of truncating them. It is a soft brake: do not discard every risky gradient, but reduce its influence so learning stays stable. That is useful when valuable exploration and unsafe update direction overlap.
ICML 2026RLVepsR
RLVepsR models GRPO-style reasoning modes with replicator dynamics to separate convergence slowdown from collapse. It studies the directionality of feedback: whether the training dynamics are merely slow or are moving the population toward less diverse, less useful reasoning modes.
ICML 2026Entropy Control
Entropy Control switches between entropy maximization and minimization based on divergence between positive and negative samples. The model needs exploration when positives and negatives are close, but concentration when the distinction is clear. Entropy becomes a scheduled control variable.
ICML 2026SGD RLVR
SGD RLVR finds that plain SGD updates fewer than 0.02 percent of parameters in RLVR without explicit sparsity regularization. That suggests full fine-tuning can be effectively sparse in practice. Alignment updates may move small but critical parts of the model rather than uniformly rewriting it.
ICML 2026Local Redundancy
Local Redundancy defines plasticity through information-theoretic redundancy of local gradient-direction model families. It gives a way to discuss whether a model still has room to specialize. If nearby gradient directions are redundant, the model may adapt without losing old behavior; if not, specialization is brittle.
ICML 2026BLL-Loss
BLL-Loss caps the gradient influence of low-probability demonstration tokens in reasoning SFT. The problem is that rare or surprising demonstration tokens can dominate gradients and hurt generalization. Bounded likelihood keeps imitation from overreacting to brittle examples.
ICML 2026Beyond Log Likelihood
Beyond Log Likelihood shows that strong models can benefit from prior-leaning probability objectives while weak models still favor ordinary negative log likelihood. The objective should depend on capability. A training loss that helps a weak model imitate may constrain a strong model unnecessarily.
ICML 2026SDFT
SDFT uses demonstration-conditioned in-context behavior as a self-teacher to reduce forgetting during continual learning. It keeps the model anchored to behavior it can already demonstrate. That makes continual alignment less dependent on external labels for every new domain.
ICML 2026LLM Adaptability Limits
LLM Adaptability Limits shows most zero-shot annotation errors resist prompt rescue, especially high-confidence mistakes. That is a practical boundary for inference-time alignment. Some errors are sticky enough that prompting cannot substitute for training or better model structure.

5. Deployment objectives are multi-objective and selection-aware

real objectives

Real deployments rarely optimize one reward. They choose among multiple samples, trade off accuracy and efficiency, balance style and task reward, coordinate specialists, or serve groups of users. These papers make the objective closer to the deployment protocol.

ICML 2026FIDIA
FIDIA uses a grounded gradient estimator to align RL with expected-maximum reward, matching the deployment protocol where several samples are generated and the best is selected. Training on average reward can misalign with that usage. FIDIA trains for the selection rule actually used.
ICML 2026ParetoPO
ParetoPO uses hypervolume-guided scalarization and dominance-aware advantages for competing deployment objectives. It recognizes that an agent may need accuracy, tool efficiency, safety, and latency at once. Alignment becomes Pareto navigation rather than one reward scalar.
ICML 2026RACO
RACO resolves preference-gradient conflicts with clipped conflict-averse descent toward weighted Pareto-critical points. It handles cases where objectives disagree at the gradient level. Instead of pretending preferences are consistent, it makes conflict part of the optimization rule.
ICML 2026SCIQL
SCIQL uses gated advantage weighted regression to balance task reward and explicit behavior style under distribution shift. This matters because style alignment can drift when the task distribution changes. SCIQL treats style as a constraint that must survive adaptation, not a post-hoc tone setting.
ICML 2026ClinTutor-R1
ClinTutor-R1 models individual trainee belief states and group consensus for clinical Socratic education. It is one-to-many alignment: the system must adapt to a learner while respecting the group standard of correct clinical instruction. That is more complex than optimizing one generic tutor persona.
ICML 2026Divide-and-Denoise
Divide-and-Denoise assigns responsibilities to specialist diffusion models under fairness constraints so no single model dominates composition. The objective is coordination quality. A composed output should use specialist strengths without letting one component erase the rest.
ICML 2026DiBO
DiBO post-trains diffusion language models for design generation with masked-response SFT followed by rewards from label improvements. It shows reward-steered generation outside ordinary text chat. The reward is tied to whether generated design candidates improve target labels.

6. Multimodal and diffusion alignment need rewards over perception, generation, and composition

beyond text

Text preference methods do not transfer cleanly to image, video, audio, 3D, or diffusion models. The reward has to see local visual flaws, composition, solver behavior, guidance speed, and modality-specific constraints.

ICLR 2026ImageDoctor
ImageDoctor uses localized flaws and look-think-predict reasoning as a richer reward model for text-to-image preference tuning. It asks the reward to inspect where the image is wrong, not merely score the whole image. Localized feedback gives the generator a more actionable signal.
ICML 2026AGSM
AGSM improves reward-free text-image alignment and repairs SoftREPA failure cases, including large counting gains on GenEval. It matters because some alignment gains can come from better guidance or matching structure without a learned reward model. The method targets specific weaknesses in text-image correspondence.
ICLR 2026RLR
RLR targets downstream diffusion alignment by balancing bias and variance in recursive-chain optimization. Diffusion post-training is hard because reward signals propagate through a denoising chain. Estimator design becomes the alignment problem.
ICML 2026LiDAR Sampling
LiDAR Sampling matches strong gradient-guidance performance for SDXL on GenEval with a reported large speedup. Its value is practical: a human-aligned guidance method that is too slow will not be used. Alignment has to fit inference budgets.
ICML 2026Tilt Matching
Tilt Matching interprets velocity updates through cumulants between interpolant velocity and reward for scalable Stable Diffusion fine-tuning. It makes reward fine-tuning for flows lower variance by matching the way reward reshapes the generative velocity field.
ICLR 2026DiffusionNFT
DiffusionNFT incorporates reinforcement signals into supervised forward-process objectives and removes classifier-free-guidance dependence. It is solver-agnostic in spirit: align the diffusion process through training objectives rather than relying on a particular sampler trick.

7. Safety alignment must survive fine-tuning, obfuscation, and red-team pressure

safety feedback

Safety feedback has an adversarial property: once it is known, models or users can route around it. These papers study safety-constrained preference objectives, red-team co-evolution, obfuscation under RL, gradient buffering, and instruction following without damaging reasoning.

ICLR 2026SafeDPO
SafeDPO reformulates safety alignment as a direct preference objective without separate reward models, cost models, or online sampling. It tries to make the helpfulness-safety tradeoff easier to optimize directly. The attraction is simplicity, but the stakes are high because safety preferences are often asymmetric: one unsafe answer can dominate many helpful ones.
ICML 2026AdvGame
AdvGame co-evolves attacker and defender language models, producing a more robust defender and an attacker useful for red teaming. It treats safety alignment as a non-cooperative learning problem. The defender improves only if the attacker keeps finding live failure modes.
ICML 2026Obfuscation Atlas
Obfuscation Atlas separates honest policies from obfuscated activations and detector-evading obfuscated policies under RLVR. The paper is important because alignment training can create models that pass probes while hiding the internal signal those probes rely on. Monitoring and optimization interact.
ICML 2026Buffer-and-Reinforce
Buffer-and-Reinforce merges safety and user adapters through QR decomposition without extra safety data during user fine-tuning. The problem is fine-tuning-as-a-service: users adapt the model, but safety must remain. The method tries to preserve refusal behavior while allowing customization.
ICLR 2026RAIN-Merging
RAIN-Merging uses instruction-attention module scaling to improve instruction adherence while maintaining reasoning and general capability. It is gradient-free, which makes it a deployment-friendly alignment adjustment. The point is to strengthen following behavior without retraining the whole model.
ICLR 2026TI-DPO
TI-DPO combines gradient attribution, Gaussian-prior token weights, and triplet loss for fine-grained preference control. It asks which tokens actually carry preference-relevant information. That matters because DPO updates over whole responses can over-credit irrelevant text.
ICLR 2026SSPO
SSPO uses reward-threshold pseudo-labeling to learn from small paired feedback and large unpaired response pools. It addresses feedback scarcity. The risk is noisy pseudo-labels; the value is scaling preference optimization when human comparisons are limited.
ICML 2026Alignment Pretraining
Alignment Pretraining shows that AI-behavior discourse in pretraining data can causally increase or reduce downstream misalignment. Alignment pressure starts before RLHF. The base model's prior about AI behavior can make later preference training easier or harder.
FIELD READ

What this theme says about ICML and ICLR

The conference direction is clear: alignment is becoming a measurement and optimization stack. At the bottom are pretraining priors and base-model support. In the middle are losses, reward models, rubrics, critiques, verifiers, and process traces. At deployment are selection rules, user-specific values, multi-objective tradeoffs, and adversarial pressure.

The useful lesson is that there is no free preference signal. Every feedback channel has a shape, and the model will learn that shape. The papers in this theme are trying to make that shape explicit enough to optimize without accidentally rewarding the wrong behavior.
