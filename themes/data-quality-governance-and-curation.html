Problem

Data quality is not a single score. A sample can be legally unusable, statistically harmful, missing key covariates, poorly timed in training, or valuable only because its gradient points toward a target behavior.

Approaches

The papers use licensed-corpus construction, gradient and Hessian geometry, midtraining gap analysis, missingness-aware optimization, prompt-guided imputation, and contrastive graph theory.

Connecting read

This page is the focused companion to the broader data governance page: it isolates the narrow mechanics of evidence quality.

FIRST PRINCIPLES

The core problem

A dataset only looks like a clean table from far away. Up close, each row has a status: it may be licensed or not, complete or missing, easy or harmful, early or late, aligned or off-target. The optimizer does not know those statuses unless the training process encodes them. Data quality work is the attempt to make those hidden statuses explicit enough to act on.

The six papers here cover the minimum set of failure modes. Common Corpus asks whether the data can be used at all. DiReCT asks whether the data moves the late-pretraining optimizer in the desired direction. Midtraining Bridges asks where data should sit between broad pretraining and narrow posttraining. LIMSSR and Robust Contextual Optimization ask what to do when the observed row is incomplete. Difficult Examples Hurt Unsupervised Contrastive Learning asks when hard data becomes anti-signal rather than useful challenge.

SYNTHESIS

Three pressure points in data quality

1. A corpus has to be usable before it can be useful

rights and provenance

The first quality gate is not statistical. It is whether the data can legally and operationally enter training, and whether future users can understand where it came from.

ICLR 2026Common Corpus
Common Corpus assembles roughly two trillion tokens of uncopyrighted or permissively licensed multilingual and code data, with provenance, filtering, and curation details. Its first-principles contribution is to treat legal usability as a technical constraint on pretraining, not an after-the-fact policy note. A corpus that cannot be audited, attributed, or reused safely is not a stable foundation for open model development.

2. Data quality depends on the training phase and optimizer state

optimization control

The same example can be good or bad depending on where the model is in training. These papers make data choice conditional on gradients, curvature, and the gap between general pretraining and specialized posttraining targets.

ICML 2026DiReCT
DiReCT recasts late-pretraining data choice as constrained sample selection using per-sample gradients and Hessian spectral geometry. The important move is to stop treating domain filtering as a proxy for usefulness. Late in training, the useful sample is the one whose update is compatible with the target direction and the local curvature of the model.
ICML 2026Midtraining Bridges Pretraining and Posttraining
Midtraining Bridges Pretraining and Posttraining shows that midtraining helps most when specialized data bridges a large distributional gap between broad pretraining and posttraining targets. The paper gives a concrete role to midtraining: it is not just extra data, but a transition layer. The best bridge data reduces the shock of specialization while preserving enough base-model support for posttraining to work.
ICLR 2026Difficult Examples Hurt Unsupervised Contrastive Learning
Difficult Examples Hurt Unsupervised Contrastive Learning models difficult examples through similarity graphs and shows why removal, margin tuning, or temperature scaling can improve bounds. The key lesson is that hard examples are not automatically good curriculum. In contrastive learning, some difficult samples create misleading neighborhood structure, so the data-quality rule has to depend on the objective.

3. Missing data is part of the problem, not a preprocessing nuisance

missingness

Missing covariates and missing modalities change what the learner can know. These papers treat incompleteness as uncertainty that must remain visible through prediction or decision-making.

ICML 2026LIMSSR
LIMSSR defines the training set itself as incomplete and uses prompt-guided imputation plus mask-aware uncertainty calibration. The conceptual point is that a missing modality is not just a blank input slot. The model needs to reason about what is absent, estimate what can be filled in, and keep uncertainty attached to the imputed signal.
ICML 2026Robust Contextual Optimization with Missing Covariates
Robust Contextual Optimization with Missing Covariates treats the missingness mechanism as part of the optimization model and preserves observation uncertainty through decisions. The paper matters because imputation alone can create false confidence. A decision system should know whether it is optimizing from observed facts or from uncertain reconstructed context.
FIELD READ

What this small theme adds

The larger data governance theme says data is a supply chain. This smaller page says quality is local and conditional. The usable corpus, the late-pretraining sample, the bridge dataset, the hard contrastive example, and the missing-covariate row each require a different quality test.

That is the practical takeaway: do not ask whether data is simply high quality. Ask high quality for which objective, at which training stage, under which rights, with which missing fields, and with what uncertainty carried forward.
