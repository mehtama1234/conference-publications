Probabilistic inference and uncertainty

The central problem is turning uncertainty into a usable object. A system needs to know not only what it predicts, but what alternatives remain plausible, which evidence changed the belief, and whether the uncertainty should alter sampling, filtering, verification, or action.

Problem

Point predictions hide the cases that matter most: ambiguous evidence, latent confounding, unnormalized targets, multiple correct answers, partial observability, distribution shift, and high-temperature generation.

Approaches

The papers use Bayesian transformers, credal sets, conformal prediction, particle filtering, MCMC, density-score estimation, causal decomposition, Hawkes processes, risk measures, and distribution-preserving verification.

Connecting read

The shared move is to preserve uncertainty long enough for it to change the downstream decision instead of collapsing it into one confident score too early.

READ

The Big Picture

Probabilistic inference is the machinery for asking what is still possible after seeing evidence. That sounds abstract, but it is a production concern. A retrieval system needs to know whether the answer actually used relevant context. A scientific sampler needs likelihoods accurate enough for thermodynamic estimates. A causal discovery method needs to know which graph features survive latent variables or unpaired environments. A decoder needs to accept draft tokens without changing the target distribution. A policy in an uncertain MDP needs a risk measure that still permits dynamic programming. Uncertainty is useful only when it changes what the system does next.

The papers on this page do not treat uncertainty as a decorative confidence number. They build objects that can be propagated: posterior mixture components, credal sets, particle clouds, latent construction histories, conformal prediction sets, recurrent sufficient statistics, density and score estimators, or causal equivalence classes. The practical question is whether the representation of uncertainty is calibrated to the operation that consumes it. Sampling, filtering, abstention, verification, causal inference, and robust decision-making all need different uncertainty interfaces.

STRUCTURE

What Uncertainty Is Used For

Posterior Updating

Bayesian transformers, mixture distances, empirical Bayes, and structured flow autoencoders keep posterior structure explicit instead of reducing evidence to a point estimate.

Sampling

MCMC, molecular flows, path-dependent samplers, diversity initialization, and high-temperature decoding all ask how to explore probability mass without collapse or bias.

Filtering

Particle filtering, partial-observability memory, diffusion emulators, and recurrent belief filters preserve uncertainty across time.

Calibration

Credal sets, conformal robustness, conditional-distribution scoring, RAG confidence, and pure-exploration stopping tie uncertainty to decision quality.

Causality

Latent-variable causal graphs, event processes, environments as instruments, selection mechanisms, and stochastic dynamics use uncertainty to distinguish structure from correlation.

Distribution Fidelity

Speculative decoding and multi-draft acceptance need to speed inference while preserving the exact or near-exact target distribution.

CLUSTERS

The Main Research Moves

1. Posterior structure is becoming a model input and output

Distribution Transformer maps Gaussian-mixture priors to posterior Gaussian mixtures using attention over components and data, making Bayesian updating itself the model's task. Structured Flow Autoencoders keep meaningful latent variables across continuous, mixture, and dynamical-system settings by combining flows with graphical structure. Gaussian Mixture Distances links mixture inequalities to robust empirical Bayes and minimax regret under contamination. InfoNCE Gaussian shows contrastive representations can be well approximated by high-dimensional Gaussians, giving representation learning a probabilistic geometry. LDM proves predictive latent distribution matching can recover identifiable representations under mild assumptions. The shared idea is that uncertainty should remain structured enough to update, compare, and identify.

2. Sampling methods need trustworthy probability mass

Sampling papers here focus on exploration without losing the target. Auxiliary MCMC introduces auxiliary variables into proposals and acceptance when unnormalized target evaluation is costly. FALCON provides fast molecular-flow samples with likelihoods accurate enough for thermodynamic equilibrium estimation. Path-dependent DAI conditions amortized discrete samplers on full construction history rather than a Markov state shortcut. DivIn expands the diversity-quality frontier through initialization-level diversity control. p-less improves reasoning and creative generation under high temperature by adapting truncation to the distribution rather than using a fixed hyperparameter. These papers are all about keeping exploration broad without making probability mass meaningless.

3. Filtering and memory preserve uncertainty over time

Sequential settings need uncertainty to persist across observations. Generative Filtering uses diffusion dynamical emulators to implement particle filtering without extra training. Linear Recurrent Memory explains when simple recurrent filters recover hidden Markov belief logits or reduce state ambiguity. OU Identifiability recovers linear stochastic dynamics from stationary observational and interventional moments. Hawkes Representer Theorem gives efficient nonparametric point-process estimation by fixing dual coefficients analytically. Latent Hawkes Causal Discovery uses a shrinking-interval discrete-time causal representation for latent subprocess and edge recovery. The common issue is that time turns uncertainty into state, not just error bars.

4. Calibration matters when uncertainty changes decisions

Calibration is useful only if it shapes a decision boundary. CreDRO learns credal prediction sets from distributionally robust disagreement rather than random seed variance. CRC optimizes conformal prediction sets directly under decision robustness constraints instead of stopping at coverage. MIRA scores conditional distributions through regional probability-mass agreement against joint samples. Information Flow UQ calibrates RAG confidence by checking focused, relevance-aligned evidence contributions. Sticky Track-and-Stop extends fixed-confidence pure exploration to cases with multiple correct answers, where stopping requires enough certainty about a set rather than one arm. The theme is decision-calibrated uncertainty, not generic confidence.

5. Causal uncertainty asks what structure survives missing information

Causal inference papers use uncertainty to avoid overstating structure. DiCoLa recursively decomposes causal discovery with latent variables and reconstructs global ancestral graphs. Unpaired Causal IV uses environments as instruments when covariates and outcomes are not jointly measured. Evolutionary Selection distinguishes repeated fitness-based selection from one-shot static selection bias. Risk Hypergraphs models EHR risk through latent disease subsets modulated by shared risk factors, preserving pathway uncertainty. Ambiguity-Averse MDPs characterizes which law-invariant risk measures preserve dynamic-programming decomposition under epistemic transition uncertainty. In these papers, uncertainty is the difference between a causal claim and a fragile association.

6. Fast decoding still has to preserve the target distribution

Speculative and accelerated decoding are uncertainty problems because accepting a draft changes the distribution unless handled exactly. HSD improves speculative decoding acceptance while maintaining target-model fidelity. Global Resolution achieves high multi-draft acceptance with negligible target deviation using max-flow and polymatroid structure. These papers sit naturally beside DR-Submodular Biased Gradients, which studies approximation-preserving optimization under biased gradients, because all three ask how much approximate computation can be introduced before guarantees break. The core lesson is that speedups are only valid if the output distribution remains the one the system claims to sample from.
