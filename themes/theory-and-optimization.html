Problem

Modern ML practice is full of procedures that work before we understand why: adaptive optimizers, LoRA, contrastive data selection, RL from verifiable rewards, speculative scaling laws, private analytics, and multi-agent learning. Theory asks which successes are structural and which are accidents of regime.

Approaches

The papers use convergence proofs, lower bounds, oracle complexity, spectral analysis, game theory, optimal transport, stochastic control, Lyapunov arguments, regret reductions, finite-sample statistics, and constructive impossibility results.

Connecting read

This theme is not separate from systems or agents. It supplies the audit layer: when a method should scale, when it cannot, what budget it needs, and which assumptions are silently carrying the result.

READ

The Big Picture

Theory and optimization matter because the field is increasingly built from large empirical recipes whose failure modes are expensive to discover late. A benchmark can say that a method worked once; a theorem, lower bound, or carefully solved model says what had to be true for it to work. That distinction is practical. It tells us whether hard examples should be removed from contrastive pretraining, whether LoRA should converge without smoothness shortcuts, whether Adam's behavior on degenerate objectives is accidental, whether a private analytics task is communication-limited, and whether a multi-agent system will approach equilibrium or stall for exponential time.

The papers here are broad, but they share one job: make the hidden budget visible. Sometimes the budget is samples, gradient calls, communication rounds, context length, memory bandwidth, independence queries, verifier queries, privacy loss, regret, or Wasserstein transport cost. Sometimes it is an assumption budget: smoothness, separability, Slater conditions, causal sufficiency, finite precision, unconfoundedness, or stability. The useful output is not only a better algorithm. It is a sharper statement of what the algorithm is buying and what it cannot buy.

STRUCTURE

What Theory Is Doing Here

Explaining Optimizers

Adaptive methods, low-rank updates, model averaging, spectral constraints, and low-precision subroutines are treated as mathematical dynamics, not only engineering recipes.

Finding Limits

Some work proves that a model class, feedback form, oracle, or embedding objective cannot achieve what naive intuition suggests, even with more compute or data.

Pricing Data

Data selection, reuse, valuation, source screening, and auctions become optimization problems with stability, welfare, and generalization tradeoffs.

Controlling Risk

Privacy, fairness, abstention, unlearning, causal discovery, and coverage require guarantees that survive nuisance errors, delayed observations, hidden confounding, or adaptive stopping.

Solving Games

Multi-agent learning, Stackelberg games, Nash equilibria, auctions, paging, and mean-field games expose dynamics where individual optimization does not automatically produce stable collective behavior.

Sampling And Transport

Diffusion sampling, optimal transport, Sinkhorn kernels, Schrödinger bridges, MCMC, and Wasserstein geodesics define how probability mass can be moved with provable cost and accuracy.

CLUSTERS

The Main Research Moves

1. Optimizers are dynamical systems with geometry

A large cluster tries to explain why practical optimizer recipes behave as they do. Adam Degeneracy shows Adam can naturally get local linear convergence on highly degenerate polynomial objectives, which challenges the idea that schedulers or nondegeneracy are always carrying convergence. LoRA Gradient Descent proves non-asymptotic convergence for original LoRA despite missing the usual smoothness assumptions, while LoRA-Pre compresses optimizer momentum by interpreting EMA state as online linear regression in low-rank subspaces. Beyond Muon places orthogonalized updates inside a broader spectral family, showing the popular endpoint is not universally superior to Adam. Polar Express makes the same spectral idea practical for low precision. Non-Euclidean EoS, Controlled LLM Training on Spectral Sphere, and Conservation Laws all say that optimizer behavior depends on the geometry in which updates are measured, not just on a scalar learning rate.

2. Spectra explain phases, thresholds, and delayed generalization

Spectral analysis is used here as a way to see training phases that ordinary loss curves hide. Phase Retrieval Dynamics derives learning phases controlled by spectral decay, including slow late-stage error from low-variance directions. Single-Head Attention in High Dimensions links query-key singular spectra to test error, interpolation thresholds, and perfect recovery in a solvable attention model. Why Deep Jacobian Spectra Separate explains how depth induces low-rank Jacobian structure through singular-value scaling. BBP Transitions appears from two angles: initialization informativeness and overparameterization's effect on Hessian spectral transitions. To Grok Grokking gives the familiar delayed-generalization story a cleaner model, proving overfitting followed by later generalization in ridge regression with weight decay.

3. Learnability has hard borders

Several papers are useful because they say no. Language Generation in the Limit shows that computable generation can still be sample-infeasible for formal-language classes, separating existence from practical learnability. Which Algorithms Can GNNs Learn proves standard message-passing GNNs cannot learn broad algorithm classes, then derives more expressive variants. Embedding Collapse gives hardness results for ordinal embedding, showing some triplet instances cannot efficiently beat trivial accuracy. FTRL Lower Bounds proves exponential time can be required to reach Nash equilibria in two-player potential games. KDE Kernel Algebra and Matroid Algorithms sharpen oracle and access-model limits, reminding us that a clean objective can still be expensive because the permitted queries are weak.

4. Data is an optimization variable, not just fuel

A major thread treats data choice, reuse, and valuation as first-class optimization problems. Difficult Examples Hurt Unsupervised Contrastive Learning shows that removing hard examples can improve downstream classification even with less data, which makes data difficulty a controllable bias rather than a moral preference for more data. DiReCT selects annealing samples by gradient alignment with flat validation-Hessian eigendirections while controlling stiff directions. Sequential Data Values gives a scalable submodular surrogate for data selection. Source Screening formalizes source-level selection for statistically optimal feature-extractor learning. Multi-Epoch Scaling defines an effective data reuse rate, while Scaling Laws Origin argues that scaling laws can emerge from learner-data interaction even without power-law data correlations. Data Market Pricing, Accuracy Auctions, and Bayesian Truthful Valuation add incentives: useful data pipelines must also price information in ways that are stable and truthful.

5. Guarantees for risk require the right stability notion

The risk-control papers ask what kind of stability is strong enough for deployment. CV Relative Instability argues that relative stability, not individual stability, is the relevant condition for valid cross-validation confidence intervals. Exact RL Unlearning uses rho-TV stability to get near-minimax tabular-MDP regret while satisfying exact unlearning constraints. TRECA builds doubly robust abstention risk estimates that tolerate nuisance-parameter error and possible assumption violations. UP-OCP reduces online coverage to regret control for linearized pinball loss. Anytime Trees protects tree growth from false splits under arbitrary streams through optional-stopping-safe tests. Noisy Sample Compression states minimax conditions for learning sample-compressible distribution families under additive independent noise.

6. Privacy, fairness, and causality are constrained optimization problems

Privacy and fairness show up here as mathematical constraints that change what is possible. Secret Memory Lower Bounds uses multi-player communication games to prove lower bounds for private medians, quantiles, and max-selection. IHM clarifies when Gaussian sketching mechanisms work for differentially private linear regression, and WGM Domain Discovery gives private utility guarantees for unknown-domain top-k and hitting-set problems. Fair OT combines fair Sinkhorn, penalized optimal transport guarantees, and learned costs for unseen-data fairness. Fair Causal Bandits handles unknown roundwise counterfactual fairness constraints without Slater conditions. On the causal side, Linear Causal Representation Learning, LC-PB-SCM, DiCoLa, Structure Learning CI, and Distributional Equivalence all ask what causal structure can be recovered under weakened assumptions, hidden confounding, or rank constraints.

7. Games and incentives make optimization collective

In games, optimization is no longer a single learner minimizing one loss. What Preferences Can and Cannot Predict shows that preference stability can predict dynamic stability in subgames but fails in general multi-agent dynamics. Nash Equilibria with Coupling Constraints proves existence under shared constraints using feasible-set topology. Asymmetric Perturbation perturbs one player's payoff so a perturbed equilibrium preserves the original equilibrium. Structured Stackelberg Games introduces a game-specific Littlestone dimension that characterizes leader regret. Feasible Payoffs estimates the full payoff set consistent with observed equilibrium play. Learning-Augmented Paging, First-Price Auctions, RQE Actor-Critic, and RSPG show the same issue in online algorithms, auctions, bounded-rational actor-critic learning, and mean-field games: guarantees depend on how agents react to each other, not only on the local gradient.

8. Sampling, transport, and control move probability mass under budget

The sampling and transport papers turn generation into numerical analysis. High-Accuracy Sampling and Dimension-Free Diffusion Sampling give polylogarithmic accuracy dependencies under score or collocation assumptions. SRMC uses score-history averages and exponential tilting to lower Monte Carlo variance. Auxiliary MCMC unifies noisy-gradient MCMC methods through guided proposals. FlashSinkhorn makes optimal transport practical by streaming tiles through SRAM and reducing HBM traffic. Loss-Aware OT-DRO learns decision-focused transport ambiguity sets through bilevel hypergradients. MAPF via MMOT and Schrödinger Bridges uses entropic regularization to guide multi-agent paths, while Control Consistency Losses treats rare-event diffusion bridge simulation as online control learning.

9. Optimization layers and constraints bring theory into models

A final group embeds optimization structure directly inside models and decision pipelines. Pi-net enforces convex output constraints with operator-splitting projections and implicit differentiation. FFOLayer computes approximate hypergradients for optimization layers using near-constant first-order information. Constrained Transformers enforce monotonic expected loss decreases across layers with primal-dual training. Deep Flow Networks turn minimum-cost-flow value functions into universal approximators for convex-extendable discrete functions. SC-MILP learns branch-and-bound node representations through dynamic contrastive training, and MORetro* gives admissibility-based guarantees for multi-objective chemical search. These papers are the bridge from analysis to software: the constraint is not an after-the-fact theorem, it becomes part of the architecture or solver.
