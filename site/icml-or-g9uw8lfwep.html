Efficient and Uncertainty-Aware Diffusion Framework for Offline-to-Online Reinforcement Learning

Pretrain a diffusion-based policy from offline data, distill it into a faster actor, and use uncertainty estimates plus shift detection to decide when online exploration is worth the risk.

Paper explainersAll themesOther

Offline-to-online reinforcement learning starts with a policy trained on a fixed dataset, then improves it with real interaction because the offline phase supplies a strong starting point before expensive online trials begin. The attraction is data efficiency: online learning starts from a competent policy instead of from scratch. The danger is distribution shift, meaning the states seen online can differ from the states covered by the offline data. Diffusion-based policies can represent complex action distributions, meaning weighted sets of plausible actions, but they are often slow to sample and they do not automatically say when they are uncertain. DUAL addresses both issues. In the offline phase it uses a diffusion planner to train a fast actor policy, meaning the network that outputs actions, and a transition model, meaning a predictor of the next state after an action. In the online phase it adds a Laplace approximation, meaning a local Gaussian approximation around learned weights, to estimate epistemic uncertainty, meaning uncertainty caused by limited knowledge rather than by random outcomes, and combines that with state-shift detection to decide how boldly the policy should explore.

Zero assumption version

Why offline-to-online RL needs speed and caution at the same time

An offline policy can give online learning a good starting point, but that starting point is only reliable where the offline dataset has support. As soon as online interaction moves the agent into less familiar states, the policy can make confident but poor action choices.

Diffusion policies are attractive because they can model complicated action choices, yet repeated diffusion sampling can be slow during online control. A useful method therefore needs two things together: a faster way to act and a principled way to notice when the agent has stepped outside what the offline phase really taught it, because speed without caution causes unsupported actions and caution without speed makes online control impractical.

The paper's move in one sentence. Distill a slow diffusion planner into a fast actor and guide its online updates with uncertainty and state-shift signals so exploration happens mainly when the offline policy is unreliable.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Train a diffusion planner offline

Fit a diffusion-based policy on the offline dataset so the model learns a broad starting action model for the states covered by the dataset, because the online phase should begin with many plausible actions already encoded instead of discovering them from scratch.

Distill the planner into a fast actor and transition model

Train a simpler actor to imitate the planner's actions and train a next-state predictor alongside it, because online control cannot afford many reverse-diffusion steps at every decision.

Estimate epistemic uncertainty from the actor

Approximate uncertainty around the actor weights with a Laplace method so action uncertainty is larger where the offline training signal was weak or inconsistent.

Measure transition-state shift

Compare current online transitions to the offline transition model's expected behavior so the algorithm can detect when the agent has moved into a less familiar part of state space.

Use both signals during online improvement

Increase exploration or caution when uncertainty and shift are large, and trust exploitation more when both signals remain small.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes the role of a diffusion policy in offline-to-online RL. Instead of using the full slow planner directly throughout online fine-tuning, it extracts a faster actor from that planner and then attaches uncertainty and shift-awareness to decide when the actor should be trusted, because the planner contains useful action knowledge but is too expensive to sample at every online step.

the object it moves  =  fast uncertainty-aware actor distilled from an offline diffusion planner
The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity

measured quantity  =  whether the current online state-action choice lies inside or outside the region where the offline policy has reliable support

The agent never directly observes support coverage. It only sees states, rewards, and its own predictions. The important hidden quantity is whether the current decision is still backed by the offline dataset or whether the policy is now extrapolating.

DUAL estimates that quantity in two ways. Weight-based uncertainty asks how sensitive the action prediction is to plausible changes in the trained actor. Shift detection asks whether predicted transitions now look unlike the offline transition patterns. These signals complement each other because one is about model confidence and the other is about state coverage.

Why it works

Why distillation plus uncertainty is a good fit for offline-to-online RL

Distillation matters because the diffusion planner contains useful action knowledge but is expensive to query repeatedly. Copying that knowledge into a faster actor preserves most of the offline phase's structure while making online control practical at every environment step because the actor can emit actions directly instead of running many reverse-diffusion updates first.

The Laplace approximation matters because it turns a single trained actor into a local uncertainty estimate around its learned weights. That estimate is not perfect, but it is informative where the actor has little evidence from the offline dataset. In those regions, small changes in plausible weights can change the chosen action more sharply.

Shift detection matters because an agent can be uncertain for two different reasons: the action mapping is weakly learned, or the current state is simply far from the offline data. The transition model gives a direct test for the second case. Combining the two signals lets the algorithm separate routine exploitation from the moments where online data collection should focus on unfamiliar states.

Math after the picture

The uncertainty-aware actor objective

First in words:

train the actor to match the distilled diffusion policy while carrying a penalty term that reflects uncertainty under the local weight approximation

Then the compact form:

L_actor = imitation_loss + beta uncertainty_penalty

Every symbol:

imitation_lossthe mismatch between the fast actor's action and the distilled diffusion planner's action
uncertainty_penaltya term derived from the local weight covariance that grows when the actor is uncertain
betathe weight that controls how strongly uncertainty changes the actor update
A tiny worked example
Suppose two states have the same imitation error of 0.2 relative to the diffusion planner.
In the first state, the uncertainty penalty is 0.1 because nearby plausible actor weights choose similar actions.
In the second state, the uncertainty penalty is 0.7 because nearby plausible actor weights disagree strongly.
With beta set to 0.5, the first total loss is 0.25 and the second is 0.55.
The larger loss marks the second state as one where the actor should be treated with more caution during online adaptation.

The uncertainty term is a proxy for how weakly supported the actor's decision is under the offline training signal.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Distillation can lose important action diversity. If the fast actor copies only the planner's average tendency, it may remove action modes that mattered for recovery or exploration in the full diffusion policy.
The uncertainty estimate can be poorly calibrated. A local Gaussian weight approximation is only a local model of uncertainty. If the actor is far from locally quadratic behavior, the estimate can understate or overstate risk.
Shift detection depends on the transition model. If the next-state predictor is weak, large transition error may reflect a poor model rather than a truly unfamiliar region of the online state distribution.
Done right. Check that distillation keeps policy quality high, validate uncertainty against held-out offline states, and use the transition model as one signal among several rather than as an unquestioned detector of unfamiliar states.

What a runnable demo should prove

Measure decision latency, online return over interaction steps, and how often the exploration signal activates in unfamiliar states. The demo should show that DUAL works because the fast actor reuses offline knowledge while the uncertainty and shift signals prevent blind reuse outside the offline support region.
