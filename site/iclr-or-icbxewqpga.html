ADM-v2: Direct Prediction for Full-Horizon Dynamics

ADM-v2 replaces step-by-step prediction with direct long-jump forecasts to stop error from piling up in long sequences.

Paper explainersAll themesReinforcement Learning

When a robot learns from past data (offline RL), it uses a dynamics model (a learned predictor of what happens next) to imagine trying new moves. The problem: if you guess step 1, then step 2, then step 3, each error compounds into the next, and by step 100 the predictions are nonsense. ADM-v2 fixes this by skipping intermediate steps — it learns to jump straight from the current state to what happens 50 steps ahead, sidestep the error avalanche.

Zero assumption version

Error piles up in long sequences

Offline reinforcement learning (learning a robot control strategy from recorded data) relies on a dynamics model — a learned function that predicts what the world does next. A robot says I will move my arm this way, and the dynamics model answers then the object will slide left and up. The model is trained on past observations to learn this prediction.

The naive approach: predict one step, feed that guess back in as input, predict the next step, and repeat 100 times to imagine a whole episode. This cascades errors. Step 1 might be 90% right. Step 2 uses that slightly-wrong step 1 as input, so now it is only 80% right. By step 50, the errors have stacked so high that the imagined future is fantasy, not useful for teaching the policy (the decision-making rule) how to act in the real world.

In one sentence. Direct prediction jumps straight to distant futures instead of chaining guesses, eliminating the cascade.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Learn direct mappings

Train the model to predict state at step 1, 5, 10, 50 directly from step 0.

Decouple the memory

Store a copy of the hidden state before chaining predictions, so direct paths do not mix with recurrent paths.

Estimate uncertainty in parallel

Measure confidence in each direct prediction without slowing down.

Roll out full episodes

Use direct predictions to imagine the robot moving for 100 steps without error pileup.

Train the policy

Teach the robot strategy from those cleaner imagined futures.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

ADM-v2 changes how the dynamics model makes predictions: instead of only learning state(t+1) from state(t), it learns state(t+k) directly from state(t) for many values of k (1 step ahead, 5 steps ahead, 50 steps ahead). The model still has a recurrent part (memory that persists), but ADM-v2 structurally separates the direct prediction pathways from the recurrent pathways so they do not interfere.

The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity: Prediction horizon confidence

measured quantity  =  Prediction horizon confidence

When you guess state at step 50 directly from step 0, how trustworthy is that guess? The hidden quantity is a confidence score for each direct prediction — a measure of how much the model doubts itself. Lower confidence means the training procedure should weight that prediction lightly (ignore noisy guesses); higher confidence means trust it.

ADM-v2 estimates this confidence by running parallel calculations: it predicts the state and also predicts how much it might be wrong. The paper measures this via uncertainty bands around each predicted state — the wider the band, the more the model says I am guessing here.

Why it works

Why direct prediction stops error cascade

Here is the causal chain. Step-by-step prediction forces each error forward: if step 1 is wrong, step 2 inherits that wrongness and adds its own. By step 50, the compounded error is larger than the useful signal in the data.

Direct prediction skips that chain. Instead of predict step 1, use it to predict step 2, use that to predict step 3, ADM-v2 says use the original state to predict step 50 directly. One prediction, one error injection. When you train the policy on sequences of length 50, every one of those 50 steps comes from independent direct predictions, each at roughly the same error level. The model's uncertainty estimates (the confidence scores) let the training procedure prioritize: I am very confident step 5 is right, but unsure about step 50, so I will focus more on step 5.

The structural decoupling (storing a backup copy of the recurrent state before branching off to direct paths) prevents the direct predictions from corrupting the recurrent memory, which would otherwise force later predictions to inherit errors from early direct attempts.

The prediction loss

First in words:

Total loss equals the sum of one-step loss plus weighted multi-step losses, where each multi-step prediction is weighted by its learned confidence, plus a regularization term that keeps confidence estimates honest.

Then the compact form:

L_total = L_1step + sum_k(1/sigma_k^2 * L_k + lambda * log(sigma_k^2))

Every symbol:

L_1steperror (squared difference) between predicted step 1 and real step 1
L_kerror between predicted step k and real step k
sigma_k^2uncertainty variance (squared width of confidence band) for step k
lambdastrength of the regularization that prevents the model from claiming false certainty
sum_kadd this up for k equals 1, 5, 10, 20, 50
A tiny worked example
Say the model predicts step 5 is at position 2.3, real is 2.0, so L_5 = (2.3 - 2.0)^2 = 0.09.
Model says confidence sigma_5^2 = 0.01 (it is sure). Then loss for this prediction = 0.09 / 0.01 + 0.5 * log(0.01) = 9.0 - 2.3 = 6.7.
Same error (0.09) but now sigma_5^2 = 0.5 (it is unsure). Loss = 0.09 / 0.5 + 0.5 * log(0.5) = 0.18 - 0.35 = -0.17.
Lesson: claim false confidence, lose badly. Stay honest about doubt, loss is small.

The loss teaches the model to predict accurately AND to say when it is uncertain, preventing overconfident wrong guesses that would derail long rollouts.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Training takes longer. ADM-v2 learns many prediction heads (one per horizon: steps 1, 5, 10, 50). More parameters, more gradient updates. Mitigated by parallel uncertainty estimation, which shares computation.
Uncertainty estimates can be overconfident early in training. The model might claim high confidence when it has not seen enough data yet. The regularization term (lambda in the equation) must be tuned to prevent this; too low and the model lies, too high and it distrusts itself too much.
Direct predictions for very long horizons (step 100+) may not have enough training data. If most collected episodes are only 50 steps long, the model has no ground truth for step 100, so it must interpolate and extrapolate, increasing error.
Done right: the model learns honest uncertainty bands and the policy learns to act within those bands. When the dynamics model is trained with ADM-v2 and the policy respects the uncertainty estimates, full-horizon rollouts stay accurate and the policy learns sound strategies.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Build a simple 2D navigation task where an agent learns to reach a goal from offline data (e.g. 100 collected trajectories, each 50 steps). Train two dynamics models: one with step-by-step prediction, one with ADM-v2 direct prediction. For each, use the model to imagine 50-step episodes 100 times and train a policy on those imagined futures. Then run both policies on the real simulator and measure how many times they reach the goal.
