Search-R2: Enhancing Search-Integrated Reasoning via Actor-Refiner Collaboration

Search-R2 trains a search agent with a second component that repairs weak reasoning steps and rewards useful retrieval, not just final correctness.

Paper explainersAll themesAgents & Tool Use

Search-based question answering agents often get only a final right-or-wrong reward. That is a weak training signal because a lucky guess can look good and a mostly correct search path can look bad if the final answer misses. Search-R2 fixes this by splitting the agent into an actor that drafts a reasoning path and a refiner that selectively repairs weak steps, while training with both final-answer reward and a process reward for useful retrieved evidence.

Why sparse reward misguides search-based reasoning

A search-based agent has to decide what to look up, how to combine evidence, and when to revise its current line of thought. If training watches only the final answer, then the system cannot tell which internal search and reasoning choices were helpful.

That creates a credit assignment problem across scales. A long trajectory may contain one useful retrieval and three wasteful ones, but a final correct answer rewards the whole path. A wrong final answer may hide the fact that early evidence gathering was good. Training becomes noisy because the reward does not point to the step that caused success or failure.

The paper's move in one sentence. Use an actor to draft the trajectory, a refiner to repair weak steps, and a hybrid reward that scores both final correctness and the usefulness of retrieved evidence.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Actor drafts a search-and-reason trajectory

The actor issues queries, reads retrieved evidence, and writes intermediate reasoning toward an answer.

Refiner diagnoses weak steps

A second component examines the draft and identifies steps where the search path or reasoning appears faulty.

Cut and regenerate from the weak point

Instead of rewriting everything, the system cuts the trajectory at the weak step and regenerates the rest from there.

Compute hybrid reward

Training uses final correctness plus a step-level reward for whether retrieved evidence is informative for the question.

Update both components jointly

The actor and refiner improve together because one produces better drafts and the other makes better targeted repairs.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes both the policy structure and the reward signal. It replaces a single search policy with an actor-refiner pair, and it adds process-level supervision so training can reward useful retrieval even before the final answer is decided.

The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity

measured quantity  =  Step usefulness inside a search trajectory

Step usefulness asks whether a particular retrieval or reasoning step genuinely moved the agent closer to the right answer. This quantity is hidden when training sees only final correctness.

Search-R2 makes usefulness more visible by adding a dense reward for informative evidence and by letting the refiner target weak points. That gives training a clearer signal about which steps should be repeated and which should be changed.

Why it works

Why it works

The actor-refiner split helps because diagnosis and generation are different jobs. A single component often commits to a bad early step and keeps building on it. The refiner breaks that pattern by examining the trajectory from the outside and rewriting only the weak suffix, so useful early work is preserved while bad later steps are replaced.

The hybrid reward helps because it gives the agent credit for retrieving evidence that actually informs the answer. That reduces reward noise from lucky final answers, and it also preserves signal when the final answer is wrong but some search steps were still good.

The hybrid reward

First in words:

The training reward combines final answer correctness with a process term that scores how informative the retrieved evidence was.

Then the compact form:

R_total = R_outcome + lambda * R_process

Every symbol:

R_totalthe total reward used for training
R_outcomereward for whether the final answer is correct
R_processreward for the usefulness of retrieved evidence and intermediate steps
lambdathe weight that balances process reward against final outcome reward
A tiny worked example
Question: Which city hosted the earlier of two events?
The actor first retrieves one relevant article and one irrelevant page.
The final answer is wrong because the later comparison step fails.
Outcome reward is low because the answer is wrong.
Process reward is still partly positive because one retrieved article contained the needed date.
The refiner cuts at the bad comparison step, keeps the useful retrieval, and rewrites the rest.

The paper formalizes the actor-refiner interaction more fully, but this equation captures the core training idea: final outcome plus process quality.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Refiner edits the wrong place. If the diagnosis step selects the wrong cut point, then regeneration may remove useful work and keep the real mistake because the repair targeted the wrong suffix.
Process reward is misaligned. If the step-level reward treats noisy evidence as useful, then the actor may learn to chase flashy but unhelpful retrieval because that behavior is being rewarded.
Too much rewriting overhead. If the refiner intervenes too often, training and answer-time execution can slow down because many trajectories are being partially regenerated.
Done right. The method is strongest on multi-step retrieval tasks, because those are exactly the cases where sparse final reward hides which intermediate steps mattered.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Build a retrieval question-answering task with multi-hop questions and a small document set. Compare two training setups: one that rewards only final answer correctness and one that adds a refiner plus process reward for useful evidence. Track answer accuracy, number of repeated searches, and whether repaired trajectories reuse good early evidence. The demo should show that the actor-refiner system learns cleaner search behavior because useful steps receive direct credit.
