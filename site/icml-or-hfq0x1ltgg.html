Learning Coupled Continuous-Time Latent Dynamics from Irregular Events

The paper learns smooth motion models of individual and crowd behavior from timestamped events that arrive unevenly in time.

Paper explainersAll themesTime Series & Sequential

Real-world data often arrives as scattered events (a person's location check-ins, a patient's test results, a stock price tick) rather than regular snapshots. Most learning methods flatten this into a discrete grid, losing important structure: how did the system evolve between events? This paper proposes CoCLD, which models the continuous smooth flow of an individual's state and the overall pattern of where all people are in abstract space, treating both as connected pieces of one unified motion.

Zero assumption version

Events don't arrive on a clock

Real systems produce events at unpredictable times: a patient visits the hospital on day 3, then day 21, then day 25. A person checks in somewhere on Monday, then Thursday, then the following Tuesday. Most machine-learning models expect data arranged in neat, equally-spaced columns (slot 1, slot 2, slot 3). When you force irregular events into this grid, you either pad huge gaps with zeros (wasting computation and hiding information) or you lose the fine structure of what happened between events.

A beginner might try: grab the last known value and hold it constant until the next event arrives. This clearly breaks—a patient's condition does not freeze between visits. Or: fit a simple trend line between points. This misses the complex dynamics: a person's movement patterns or a disease progression are not linear. The hidden challenge is that the state evolves continuously in an abstract space, and we see only sparse projections of it.

In one sentence. Learn smooth continuous motion in abstract space from events that arrive at random intervals.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

Instead of forcing events into time slots, the paper changes what gets modeled: the hidden state that evolves smoothly over time (a list of numbers in an abstract space). By doing so, the model can answer what the state was at any arbitrary time, even between events.

the object it moves  =  continuous hidden state
The hidden quantity

The alignment between individual and population

measured quantity  =  coupling strength

The hidden quantity is how tightly a single person's state evolution is locked to the crowd's overall statistical drift. Think of a concert: each musician plays their own part, but all parts stay synchronized to one tempo. The paper measures this alignment in abstract space: how well does the individual's path match the statistical pattern (the range and typical location) of all people's paths at each moment?

The method estimates this by running both the individual neural ODE and the population diffusion process, then checking whether they agree on where states should be. A strong alignment means the individual and population evolve together; weak alignment means one drifts away.

Why it works

How coupling continuous motion actually works

The mechanism has three moving pieces. First, diffusion fills the gaps: given an event at day 3 and an event at day 21, diffusion gradually adds randomness then removes it to generate plausible in-between states at days 4 through 20. This is like imagining multiple honest possible stories of what happened during the silence. Second, a neural ODE learns to reproduce this interpolated path, not by memorizing it, but by learning continuous differential equations that generate smooth motion through abstract space. Third, the coupling step ties the individual's neural ODE to the population's evolving statistics: if the crowd's typical state at day 10 is a point in abstract space, the individual's state should be nearby, pulled by a shared force.

Why this works: Ordinary machine learning locks the state into discrete time slots, missing the smooth motion between them. By learning a continuous equation (neural ODE), the model can predict at any time. By coupling individual to population, it avoids one person's path going wild, as the population mean acts as an anchor. Diffusion handles the hardest part: interpolating forward in an abstract space with no ground truth, because real dynamics often have many plausible paths.

Math after the picture

The coupling loss function

First in words:

total loss equals how well the individual ODE reproduces diffusion paths, plus a penalty for the individual drifting from the population mean

Then the compact form:

L = L_interp + lambda * KL(individual_dist || population_dist)

Every symbol:

L_interploss measuring how well the neural ODE trajectory matches the diffusion-interpolated path
lambdaweight controlling how much to penalize individual-population mismatch
KL(P||Q)divergence metric: how different one probability distribution is from another, measured in nats
A tiny worked example
Suppose a single person's inferred statistical pattern at time t has entropy 2.5 nats, and the population average has entropy 2.0 nats, meaning the population is tighter and more predictable.
The KL divergence, how far apart they are, might be 0.3 nats. With lambda equal to 1.0, the coupling penalty is 0.3.
If the interpolation loss, how well the ODE followed the diffusion path, is 1.2, then total L equals 1.2 plus 1.0 times 0.3, which is 1.5.
If we double lambda to 2.0, the coupling penalty doubles to 0.6, pushing the individual's path closer to the population mean. If the person is an outlier, high lambda keeps them from drifting away.

The formula ties the smooth ODE-generated path to both the observed events through diffusion and the crowd statistics through KL coupling.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Diffusion hallucination. The diffusion step generates plausible in-between states, but if the true dynamics are highly nonlinear or multimodal (many equally-valid paths), diffusion might miss rare trajectories or pick one mode arbitrarily.
Over-coupling. If lambda is too high, the individual's state is pulled so hard toward the population mean that idiosyncratic behavior gets erased. The penalty term (lambda times KL divergence) dominates the loss, forcing every person's path toward the crowd average. A patient who recovers faster than average disappears from the model because their unusual pattern conflicts with the population target; they get smoothed away. The result is a system that predicts only typical behavior, missing the valuable individual variation.
Sparse regime. If events are extremely rare, one every 100 time steps, diffusion has to interpolate huge gaps with minimal supervision, and the ODE has little signal to learn precise dynamics.
Done right. Tune lambda to balance individual realism and population consistency. Use domain knowledge to validate that interpolated paths are plausible. Test on held-out sequences with varied sparsity.
