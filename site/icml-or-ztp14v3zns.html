Structure-aware Granular-Ball based Information Bottleneck for Multi-modal Clustering

Cluster multi-modal data through adaptive groups of nearby samples instead of only point-to-point links, so the method keeps local structure while filtering noisy detail that harms clustering.

Paper explainersAll themesOther

Multi-modal clustering tries to group the same set of samples when each sample has several views, such as image features, text features, or other sensory measurements. The difficulty is that structure appears at more than one scale. Point-level relations capture local detail, but they are sensitive to noise and local irregularities. Very coarse summaries are quieter, but they can erase the neighborhood patterns that distinguish one cluster from another. The paper introduces granular-balls, which are adaptive groups of similar samples represented as hyperspheres, and uses them inside an information bottleneck style objective. The method starts with one large ball and keeps splitting it when the enclosed samples are too mixed according to a purity criterion derived from pseudo-label agreement across modalities. This creates a multi-scale representation of the data. The structure-aware objective then preserves useful local organization while compressing redundant information. The paper matters because it replaces one fixed granularity with an adaptive hierarchy that can follow the actual spread of the data.

Zero assumption version

Why one fixed scale is a bad fit for multi-modal clustering

Multi-modal clustering methods usually relate samples either at a very fine scale, such as pairwise neighborhoods, or at a very coarse scale, such as cluster prototypes. Fine-scale methods can preserve subtle local structure, but they are easily disturbed by noisy samples and inconsistent modalities.

Coarse methods suppress that noise, yet they often merge away the very local differences that define cluster boundaries. A single granularity therefore forces a bad tradeoff. The data may contain dense clean regions, mixed boundary regions, and uneven cluster shapes, so the right neighborhood size changes from place to place.

The paper's move. Represent the dataset with adaptive granular-balls that split only where purity is low, because different regions need different levels of detail.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Start with the whole dataset as one ball.

Treat all samples as one initial region, because the method wants to learn where finer detail is necessary rather than fixing that detail in advance.

Measure ball purity across modalities.

Estimate whether samples inside the ball support similar pseudo-label structure, because mixed regions should be split while consistent regions can stay compressed.

Recursively split impure balls.

Divide low-purity balls into smaller ones, because boundary regions and heterogeneous neighborhoods need finer granularity to preserve cluster structure.

Learn a structure-aware compressed representation.

Optimize a bottleneck objective that keeps cluster-relevant structure while discarding redundant detail, because the representation should preserve what separates groups and suppress what merely repeats across modalities.

Cluster from the resulting representation.

Use the learned multi-scale structure to assign samples to groups, because the granular-ball hierarchy has already adapted the neighborhood scale to each region.

The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity

measured quantity  =  regional purity that tells whether a neighborhood should stay coarse or be split

The key hidden quantity is the local purity of a region: whether the samples inside it behave as one coherent group across modalities or whether they mix several cluster tendencies. A pure region can be represented coarsely without much loss. An impure region needs finer subdivision.

This quantity matters because cluster structure is not evenly distributed. Interior regions of a cluster can tolerate compression, while boundary regions cannot. By estimating purity and splitting only where it drops, the method spends detail where detail changes the clustering outcome.

Why it works

Why granular-balls help more than fixed point neighborhoods

Point neighborhoods treat every region as if it needed the same resolution. That is rarely true in multi-modal data. Some parts of the space are clean and redundant, so point-level detail mostly repeats information and amplifies noise. Other parts sit near cluster boundaries or modality disagreement, so coarse averaging erases important distinctions.

Granular-balls give the method a controllable middle unit between individual samples and whole clusters. A large pure ball summarizes many similar samples at once, which reduces sensitivity to local perturbations. A mixed ball is split, which restores detail exactly where the coarse summary would be harmful. The information bottleneck objective then uses this adaptive structure to keep cluster-relevant content and discard variation that does not help separate groups, because redundant variation consumes capacity without changing which samples belong together.

The structure-aware part matters because the method is not compressing blindly. It preserves neighborhood organization while removing redundancy. That balance allows the representation to stay discriminative after compression because boundary information is retained while repetitive within-cluster variation is reduced.

Math after the picture

Split where purity is low

First in words:

A ball is kept coarse when the samples inside it agree strongly on their pseudo-label structure across modalities. When that agreement falls, the ball is split and the method keeps a finer description there.

Then the compact form:

split(ball) = purity(ball) < purity_threshold

Every symbol:

purity(ball)how consistently the samples inside a granular-ball support the same pseudo-label structure across modalities
purity_thresholdthe cutoff below which the region is considered too mixed to stay as one ball
split(ball)whether the method divides the current ball into smaller balls
A tiny worked example
One ball contains samples from a dense cluster interior and most modalities assign similar pseudo-label tendencies there.
Its purity is high, so the method keeps that region coarse.
Another ball lies near the boundary between two clusters and modalities disagree more inside it.
Its purity is low, so the method splits it into smaller balls.
The final clustering therefore uses fine detail at the boundary and coarse compression in the stable interior.

The threshold controls where the method spends detail, not the final number of clusters directly.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Pseudo-label purity can be misleading early on. If the pseudo-label signal is poor at the start, the method may split the wrong regions or keep mixed regions coarse, because the purity estimate is built from those provisional labels.
Very irregular cluster shapes can challenge spherical regions. A hypersphere is a convenient summary, but some cluster segments may be elongated or curved, so several splits may be needed before the ball structure matches the local shape well enough.
Modal disagreement can reflect real ambiguity. When different modalities encode genuinely different grouping signals, splitting alone may not resolve the conflict, because the data may support more than one plausible clustering structure.
Done right. The approach is strongest when cluster interiors and boundaries differ clearly in purity, because the recursive ball refinement can then place detail exactly where a fixed-scale method would blur or overfit.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Create a multi-view synthetic dataset with two kinds of regions: clean cluster interiors and noisy mixed boundaries. Compare a point-neighborhood clustering baseline, a coarse prototype baseline, and a granular-ball method that recursively splits regions based on purity. Visualize where the granular-ball method keeps large regions and where it creates smaller ones.

The demo should show that adaptive splitting concentrates fine structure near boundaries and yields better final clustering under noise because extra refinement is being spent where cluster membership is most mixed. A convincing result is not only a better clustering score. It is a visible match between low-purity regions and extra refinement, because that is the direct mechanism the method claims.

boundary_focus = refined_boundary_regions / all_refined_regions

The main proof is that the method spends its complexity where the data are mixed, because that is what lets it preserve boundaries without carrying point-level noise everywhere.
