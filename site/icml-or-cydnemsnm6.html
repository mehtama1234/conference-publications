Learning the ESG Geometry with Domain Aware Language Models

Learn a shared representation for ESG, returns, news, and sentiment so companies can be forecast, grouped by trajectory, and searched in a common space.

Paper explainersAll themesOther

ESG analysis combines several streams that do not naturally live on the same scale: numeric risk series, financial returns, text-derived sentiment, and news signals. A model that simply concatenates them can blur together differences that should stay distinct, such as raw value closeness and signal type. This paper builds a domain-aware sequence model that encodes each stream with value-aware tokens, meaning tokenized inputs that preserve the actual numeric level, and with block-wise orthogonal embeddings, meaning separate feature subspaces that reduce interference between signal types. The model then adds FACET tokens to summarize whole trajectories and trains with a loss that keeps companies with similar multi-stream evolution near each other in the learned space.

Why ESG modeling needs more than plain sequence prediction

Responsible investing depends on several linked signals: environmental risk, social events, governance behavior, price movement, and changing public sentiment. These streams evolve over time and affect each other, so a useful model must forecast future values and also capture which companies follow similar multi-stream paths.

A generic language-model style encoder can process sequences, but it can lose two important distinctions. First, raw numeric magnitude can disappear when numbers are converted too crudely into symbols. Second, heterogeneous streams can interfere with each other if they share one undifferentiated feature space. In that case the learned representation may be poor for forecasting, grouping, and asset search.

The paper's move in one sentence. Encode each time-series block in a way that preserves both numeric value and signal type, then train a shared representation that respects whole-trajectory similarity across ESG, returns, news, and sentiment.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Turn heterogeneous observations into value-aware tokens

Each time step keeps the actual observed level together with stream identity so the model does not discard magnitude information, because raw value size often carries financial meaning that coarse symbolization can blur.

Assign block-wise orthogonal embeddings

Different signal families receive separate feature blocks, which means their representations begin in mutually separated subspaces and interfere less during training.

Insert FACET trajectory tokens

Add summary tokens that aggregate information across longer horizons so the model can encode company-level evolution, not only local transitions, because many investment judgments depend on long-run path shape rather than one-step changes.

Train for both forecasting and structure preservation

Optimize future-value prediction together with a structure-preserving objective, meaning an objective that keeps similar trajectories close and dissimilar trajectories apart in the learned space.

Use the learned space downstream

Forecast ESG values, group companies by behavior over time, and search for assets with related trajectory profiles.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes the role of the sequence model. It is not only a forecaster. It becomes a representation learner whose output space is designed to preserve cross-stream structure, so one model can support prediction, grouping, and search.

The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity

measured quantity  =  whether the learned space preserves trajectory similarity that matches real multi-stream behavior

The important quantity is not just one-step forecast error. It is whether companies that evolve similarly across ESG, returns, news, and sentiment end up near each other in the learned space. If that structure is lost, downstream search and grouping become unreliable even when short-horizon prediction looks acceptable.

Preserving that structure requires two things at once. Numeric closeness should matter, because a small change in a risk score should not look identical to a large change. Signal identity should also matter, because a news sentiment jump should not be treated as the same kind of evidence as a governance metric jump.

Why it works

Why the domain-aware encoding improves the learned space

Value-aware tokens matter because the raw magnitude of a series carries financial meaning. A company whose emissions score changes from 10 to 11 is not experiencing the same event as one that changes from 10 to 80. If the encoding treats both changes too similarly, the model loses useful scale information.

Block-wise orthogonal embeddings matter because heterogeneous streams should not overwrite one another too early. By giving each stream family its own starting subspace, the model can learn interactions later while preserving which type of signal produced each feature pattern.

FACET tokens matter because local step-by-step encoding is not enough for investment use. Investors care about paths over time, such as steady improvement, repeated shocks, or divergence between sentiment and fundamentals. The trajectory tokens collect that longer-range history, and the structure-preserving loss trains the representation space so those histories determine neighborhood relations.

The joint objective

First in words:

The model learns from two signals at once: forecast future values accurately and preserve trajectory relations in the representation space.

Then the compact form:

L = L_forecast + lambda L_structure

Every symbol:

L_forecastthe prediction loss for future ESG and related values
L_structurethe loss that keeps similar company trajectories close and dissimilar ones separated in the learned space
lambdathe weight that balances prediction and structure preservation
A tiny worked example
Suppose two companies have similar return trends, similar governance drift, and similar sentiment shocks across a year.
If the model predicts their next quarter well but places them far apart in the learned space, the structure term rises.
If it places them near each other but predicts the next values poorly, the forecast term rises.
Training lowers both terms so the representation is useful for search and for prediction.

The added structure term matters because a pure forecasting objective can ignore whether the learned space itself is organized in a way that downstream investors can use.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Separate subspaces can block useful interaction. If the stream-specific blocks are kept too isolated, the model may miss real cross-stream dependencies such as sentiment leading returns or governance events affecting ESG scores.
Trajectory summaries can wash out recent shocks. If FACET tokens dominate too strongly, abrupt recent changes may be averaged away even when those changes matter for forecasting and trading decisions.
The learned space may reflect historical bias. If the training data overrepresents one region, sector, or reporting style, nearest-neighbor search in the learned space may inherit those imbalances.
Done right. Keep numeric scale explicit, preserve stream identity without fully blocking interaction, and validate the learned neighborhoods against real downstream tasks because the representation is meant to serve more than one objective.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Assemble a panel dataset with company ESG indicators, returns, news-derived sentiment, and governance events. Train one plain multi-stream sequence model and one domain-aware model with value-aware tokens, separated stream blocks, and trajectory summary tokens.

Compare them on next-period forecasting, nearest-neighbor company retrieval, and clustering by long-horizon behavior. The demo should show that the domain-aware model wins on all three because it preserves value scale and stream identity while still capturing trajectory-level structure.
