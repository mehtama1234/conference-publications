T$^2$PO: Uncertainty-Guided Exploration Control for Stable Multi-Turn Agentic Reinforcement Learning

Stabilize multi-turn agent training by detecting when the model's uncertainty stops improving and then intervening at the token and turn levels so training does not waste effort on uninformative exploration.

Paper explainersAll themesReinforcement Learning

Multi-turn reinforcement learning for language-model agents is unstable because the agent can spend many actions exploring without learning anything useful. This paper argues that the main failure mode is not simply bad credit assignment; it is wasted exploration. The agent keeps producing low-information tokens (the small text pieces the model emits one step at a time) and whole turns that neither reduce uncertainty nor move the task forward. T2PO addresses that with two control points. At the token level, it monitors how uncertainty changes as the model generates and triggers an extra thinking step when uncertainty reduction stalls. At the turn level, it measures whether a full interaction made enough exploration progress to justify its cost and resamples turns that did not. The result is a training loop that concentrates rollouts on places where the policy still has something to learn.

Zero assumption version

Why multi-turn agent training collapses

In a multi-turn environment, the agent does not solve the task with one answer. It observes, reasons, acts, gets new observations, and repeats. That makes exploration expensive because a poor choice early in the turn can lead to many later tokens and actions that are no longer informative.

Older stabilization tricks improve how reward is assigned after a rollout, but they do not decide whether the rollout was worth continuing in the first place. The paper argues that collapse happens because training keeps paying for tokens and turns whose uncertainty profile shows they are no longer teaching the policy anything useful.

The paper's diagnosis. Training becomes unstable because the agent spends too much exploration budget on tokens and turns that fail to reduce uncertainty or reveal new task information.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Measure token-level uncertainty

Track uncertainty, which is the spread in the model's next-token belief, across generated tokens because a stalled spread signals that the model is no longer refining its belief.

Trigger a thinking intervention

When marginal uncertainty change falls below a threshold, insert extra reasoning so that the model can reconsider before committing to more low-information tokens.

Score turn-level exploration progress

After each interaction turn, estimate whether the turn reduced uncertainty or revealed useful new state information, because long turns should justify their training cost.

Resample weak turns

If a turn shows negligible progress, regenerate or replace it so the replay buffer is not dominated by stale exploration.

Optimize the policy on cleaner rollouts

Run reinforcement learning on trajectories that contain more informative tokens and turns, because higher-quality exploration produces more stable policy updates.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes exploration control granularity. Instead of treating an entire rollout as one indivisible training sample, it introduces decisions about whether to continue at the current token and whether to keep the current turn.

the object it moves  =  where the training loop decides that exploration is still worth paying for
The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity: exploration progress

measured quantity  =  how much uncertainty reduction a token or turn actually produces

The key hidden quantity is exploration progress. A rollout looks busy on the surface because it contains many generated tokens and environment steps, but the real question is whether those actions reduce the agent's uncertainty about what to do next.

The paper estimates progress at two scales. At the token scale, it checks the marginal change in uncertainty from one generated step to the next. At the turn scale, it aggregates whether the interaction changed the policy's knowledge enough to justify keeping that turn. If uncertainty barely moves, the method treats the behavior as low-information even if the text is long.

Why it works

Why it works: stop paying for stagnant exploration

The method works because reinforcement learning quality depends on trajectory quality. A policy update is only as useful as the evidence inside the sampled rollout. If many tokens are generated after the model has already stopped refining its belief, those tokens add variance and cost without adding learning signal.

The token-level controller addresses local stagnation. As the model generates a reasoning trace, it monitors whether uncertainty is still changing. When the marginal change becomes tiny, the current continuation is no longer sharpening the policy's belief. Triggering a thinking intervention helps because it gives the model a chance to branch into a more informative internal computation before more low-value text accumulates.

The turn-level controller addresses global stagnation. Some whole interactions fail to discover anything new even if individual steps looked active. Resampling those turns helps because it replaces low-information trajectories with alternatives that may visit more informative states. Combining both levels improves stability because the buffer receives fewer dead-end traces, so policy optimization is driven by rollouts that actually change the agent's knowledge.

Math after the picture

The equation: exploration gain at token and turn scales

First in words:

A simple summary is to measure progress as the uncertainty before a token or turn minus the uncertainty after it. Small gain means the step was not informative enough to justify its cost.

Then the compact form:

gain(unit) = uncertainty_before(unit) - uncertainty_after(unit)

Every symbol:

gain(unit)how much useful uncertainty reduction one token or one turn produced
uncertainty_before(unit)the policy's uncertainty right before that token or turn
uncertainty_after(unit)the policy's uncertainty right after that token or turn
A tiny worked example
Token 1 starts with uncertainty 0.70 and ends at 0.50, so gain is 0.20.
Token 2 starts at 0.50 and ends at 0.49, so gain is 0.01.
If the threshold is 0.02, token 2 triggers the thinking intervention because its gain is too small.
For the full turn, suppose uncertainty before the turn is 0.80 and after the turn is 0.78. Gain is 0.02, so the turn may be resampled because the interaction barely taught the policy anything.

The paper uses a richer uncertainty-aware controller, but this gain view captures the main decision rule: keep exploration only when uncertainty is still moving.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Bad uncertainty estimates break the controller. If the model's uncertainty signal is poorly calibrated, the method may interrupt useful reasoning or keep useless reasoning, because the gain calculation depends on uncertainty tracking the real information state.
Thresholds can be too aggressive. A threshold set too high may cut off exploration before the agent reaches a hard but informative branch, because difficult tasks often require several low-immediate-gain steps before the payoff appears.
Resampling can reduce diversity. If the method discards too many slow-progress turns, the policy may stop visiting rare states that matter later, because short-term gain is not always the same as long-term discovery.
Done right. Calibrate uncertainty carefully, tune thresholds on several environments, and verify that resampling removes stagnant traces without collapsing the diversity of visited states.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Build a small multi-turn environment such as WebShop-style browsing or a text navigation task. Train one agent with ordinary reinforcement learning and one with token-level and turn-level exploration control. Log uncertainty, intervention triggers, resampled turns, return, and training variance.

The test should show two things. First, many long reasoning traces contain late tokens with almost no uncertainty reduction. Second, removing or redirecting those traces improves stability and final score because the replay buffer carries more informative evidence per unit of rollout cost.

efficiency = reward / rollout_tokens ; stability_gain = variance_baseline / variance_t2po

The demo is convincing only if the controlled agent earns better reward with fewer stagnant tokens and lower training variance over repeated runs, because that combination shows the controller is improving both sample quality and update stability.
