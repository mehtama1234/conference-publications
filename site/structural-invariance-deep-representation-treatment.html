Self-Supervised Learning from Structural Invariance

This page treats structural invariance as a deeper version of the representation problem: when appearances keep changing, what stable skeleton is the model supposed to discover?

Core idea

Find the skeleton that survives many changes

Structural invariance is not just "make two views similar." It asks what hidden arrangement stays true when color, crop, viewpoint, style, background, or time changes.

A chair can be wooden, metal, red, blue, photographed from the side, photographed from the front, in a kitchen, or in a lab. The surface changes. The structure remains: a seat, a back, support legs, and relations among parts. A model that learns only surface appearance will fail when the surface changes. A model that learns the stable part-relation structure can generalize.

surface changes: color, lighting, texture, background, viewpoint structure survives: parts, relations, roles, constraints, possible transformations
Concrete example

The chair example

Imagine four photos of chairs. One is red, one is black, one is wooden, one is partly cropped. A weak model may learn "chair means brown texture" or "chair means object in a living room." That works until the chair is plastic, white, or outdoors.

A stronger model learns the stable structure: a sitting surface, a back support, legs or a base, and the way these parts connect. The exact pixels change, but the part relations survive.

different surface
same part structure

This is the first-principles point: the model should not only ask whether two pictures look similar. It should ask whether the same underlying arrangement is present.

Why this is hard

The model does not know which facts are structural

Without labels, the model has to infer what matters from repeated changes. If the same kind of object appears under many surfaces, the model can learn that surface is not the core. If the same behavior appears with different animals, the model can learn the motion pattern. If the same graph role appears under different node names, the model can learn the relation pattern.

The danger is that the model may preserve the wrong thing. It may treat background as structure because the background repeats often. It may erase viewpoint even when viewpoint matters. It may preserve color for object identity even when color is accidental. Structural invariance is hard because it is a bet about what will remain useful beyond the current view.

wrong stable thing: snow background -> wolf better stable thing: animal body, face, fur pattern, shape, movement
Paper questions

The detailed treatment

1. What raw mess is the paper starting from?

The raw mess is unlabeled data with many changing factors. In images, this means object, pose, lighting, crop, texture, background, and camera. In video, it means object, motion, viewpoint, time, and event phase. The model sees many views, but it is not told which changes are harmless and which reveal structure.

2. What hidden structure is it trying to recover?

It is trying to recover the stable relation pattern underneath appearances. That can mean object parts and their relations, motion pattern across time, graph structure under renamed nodes, or a world state that stays meaningful under viewpoint changes.

hidden structure = stable relations among parts, states, or roles

3. What should stay the same?

The structure should remain. A chair remains chair-like if color changes. A walking action remains walking if clothing changes. A graph community remains a community if node IDs are renamed. A melody remains the melody if played louder.

4. What should stay different?

Different structures should not collapse. A chair and a table both have legs, but their part roles differ. Walking and falling both involve body motion, but the temporal relation differs. A molecule ring and a chain both contain atoms, but the connectivity differs.

5. What should change in a controlled way?

Surface factors can change without changing the structural representation. But transformations that expose structural state should remain trackable. A chair seen from the side should still preserve which part is the back and which part is the seat. A video frame later in time should preserve how the state evolved.

6. What shortcut is blocked?

The blocked shortcut is surface agreement. The model should not decide sameness by color, background, texture, or one easy pixel cue. It should need evidence that the deeper arrangement matches.

How it solves it

Use repeated changes to separate structure from surface

The solving pressure is: if many views change but one relation pattern keeps recurring, make the representation keep that recurring pattern.

The method family works by creating or using transformations that change surface appearance while preserving the intended structure. The model is then trained so the stable structural part remains aligned across views. If the paper also supports predictive SSL, the model may need to predict missing parts in a way that respects the same structure.

The actual learning pressure

Suppose two images are different in color, crop, and background but share the same part relation. The model is penalized if their structural representations are far apart. Suppose two examples share color and background but have different part relations. The model should not pull them together just because the surface matches.

pull together: same structure + different surface keep apart: same surface + different structure

That is the deeper solve. The model is not merely learning invariance to a list of augmentations. It is learning that some recurring relations are more trustworthy than surface features.

Concrete numbers

A tiny structural example

Represent an object with four numbers: color score, background score, seat-back relation, and leg-support relation.

wood chair indoors: [color brown=1.0, indoor=1.0, seat-back=1.0, leg-support=1.0] red chair outdoors: [color brown=0.0, indoor=0.0, seat-back=1.0, leg-support=1.0] brown table indoors: [color brown=1.0, indoor=1.0, seat-back=0.0, leg-support=1.0]

A surface model thinks wood chair and brown table are close because color and background match. A structural model thinks wood chair and red chair are close because seat-back and leg-support relations match.

Bad similaritywood chair close to brown table because color/background match
Good similaritywood chair close to red chair because structure matches
Training goalmake stable relations dominate over accidental surface cues

This is how structural invariance differs from blind invariance. It does not say "ignore everything that changes." It says "use changes to discover what was never the important part."

Bigger connection

How this connects to topology and soft equivariance

Soft equivariance asks whether a change should be tracked. Structural invariance asks what stable relation remains across many such changes. Topology asks whether those stable relations form a larger shape that should not be torn.

soft equivariance: when input moves, should features move? structural invariance: when surface changes, what relation survives? topology-aware ML: when many examples move, does the learned map keep its shape?

Together, they form one larger view: representation learning is not just compressing data. It is deciding which facts are stable, which facts move in a lawful way, and which larger shapes those facts create.

Failure cases

What would make this fail?

Wrong transformations.

If the chosen changes do not actually preserve structure, the model learns a false invariance.

Surface repeats too often.

If every wolf appears in snow, the model may treat snow as structural.

Structure is task-dependent.

Color is nuisance for dog identity, but it may be structural for fruit ripeness or traffic lights.

Too much alignment.

If different structures are forced together, the model loses meaningful differences.

Takeaway

The first-principles line

A model generalizes when it learns the structure that survives changes, not the surface details that happen to come with one view.

This page is the bridge between invariance, equivariance, and topology. Structural invariance says the model should discover the stable skeleton. Soft equivariance says useful motion should not be erased. Topology says the learned skeletons across many examples should keep their larger shape.
