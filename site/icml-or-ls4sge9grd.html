Characterizing the Predictive Impact of Modalities with Supervised Latent-Variable Modeling

PRIMO predicts with missing modalities and measures, for each case, how much the absent modality could still change the answer.

Paper explainersAll themesMultimodal

A multimodal predictor often trains on pairs such as image plus text or audio plus video, then silently breaks when one side is absent. PRIMO is built for that setting. It learns from complete and partial training examples, treats the missing modality as an unknown variable tied to the observed modality and label, and then samples many plausible completions at test time. Those samples do two jobs at once: they let the model average over missing information for prediction, and they reveal whether the absent modality would have mattered for this specific case.

Zero assumption version

Multimodal systems usually assume every channel is present

Suppose a hospital model uses notes, lab values, and sensor traces to predict risk. In real records, one of those channels is often absent. A standard multimodal model has two bad options in that case. It can discard incomplete examples during training, which wastes data, or it can fill the gap with one fixed guess, which hides uncertainty.

Both choices erase an important distinction: some missing channels barely affect the answer, while others could change it a lot. A model that inserts one average completion cannot tell those cases apart, because it never asks how much the answer would move across different plausible completions. PRIMO is built to keep that variation visible, because the spread across plausible completions is exactly the evidence needed to judge how much the missing channel matters.

Core problem. When a modality is missing, you need both a prediction and a way to measure whether the absent information would have changed that prediction.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Train on complete and partial records

Use all available examples instead of filtering to only fully observed multimodal pairs.

Model the absent channel with a hidden variable

Introduce a latent variable, which is a compact unknown code, that links the observed modality, the missing modality, and the target label.

Sample many plausible completions

At test time, draw many candidate versions of the missing modality from the learned conditional model.

Predict under each completion

Run the label predictor on every completed example and collect the resulting class probabilities or scores.

Aggregate for prediction and impact

Average the predictions for the final answer, then compute how much those predictions vary across completions.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes missing-modality handling from single imputation to conditional simulation. Instead of pretending there is one best replacement for the absent channel, PRIMO keeps a full conditional model of what that channel could have been in the context of the observed data and the prediction task.

the object it moves  =  task-aware missing-modality completion
The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity: predictive impact of the missing modality

measured quantity  =  predictive impact

Predictive impact asks a direct question: if you could reveal the missing modality, how much could the answer still move? PRIMO estimates that by drawing many plausible completions for the absent channel and checking how much the prediction changes from one completion to another.

If nearly all completions produce the same answer, the missing modality has low impact for this case. If the completions push the predictor toward different classes, the impact is high, because the absent channel carries decision-making information that the observed modality alone does not pin down. This is why PRIMO uses a variance-based summary across sampled predictions rather than only reporting the average.

Why it works

Why the method works

The method works because missing data creates two separate jobs. First, you still need a label prediction. Second, you need to know whether the absent modality could have changed that prediction. A single filled-in value can support the first job a little, but it cannot support the second one, because there is no full spread of plausible alternatives to compare.

PRIMO builds that full spread explicitly. The observed modality restricts which completions are plausible, and the supervised objective further shapes those completions toward versions that matter for the target task. This matters because the goal is not to rebuild the missing channel in every visual or acoustic detail. The goal is to rebuild the parts of it that affect the label. By tying completion learning to prediction, PRIMO spends modeling capacity on task-relevant uncertainty.

At test time, repeated sampling turns hidden uncertainty into measurable output variation. If all reasonable completions lead to the same prediction, the answer is stable because the observed modality already determines the label. If different completions lead to different labels, the answer is unstable because the missing modality still contains unresolved evidence. The average prediction is useful because it integrates over uncertainty, and the spread is useful because it exposes how much unresolved evidence remains.

Math after the picture

Prediction and impact from sampled completions

First in words:

PRIMO averages predictions over many sampled completions of the missing modality, then uses the variance across those predictions as the impact score.

Then the compact form:

predictive_impact = variance of p(y | observed modality, sampled completion_k) across k samples

Every symbol:

ythe label to predict
observed modalitythe modality that is present at test time
sampled completion_kthe k-th plausible reconstruction of the missing modality
p(y | ...)the model's predicted class probabilities
predictive_impacthow much the prediction changes across plausible completions
A tiny worked example
Suppose three sampled completions of the missing modality produce class probabilities for mortality of 0.18, 0.20, and 0.19.
The average is about 0.19 and the variance is tiny, so the absent modality has low impact for this patient.
Now suppose three completions produce 0.15, 0.54, and 0.81.
The average is about 0.50 but the variance is large, so the final decision is unstable because the missing modality could push the answer in very different directions.

The average gives the best prediction under uncertainty; the variance says whether that prediction is dependable without the missing channel.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

The conditional completion model is weak. If the learned completion model does not match the true relation between modalities, the sampled completions are unrealistic. Then both the average prediction and the impact score are unreliable, because they are computed from the wrong alternatives.
Too few samples. If you draw only a small number of completions, the estimated average and variance are noisy. The impact score may look small or large just because sampling happened to miss important alternatives.
Task supervision is misaligned. If the training label does not reflect the decision you care about, the model will learn task-aware completions for the wrong target. PRIMO will still focus on label-relevant uncertainty, but it will be the wrong label.
Done right. PRIMO is most useful when the observed modality narrows the missing channel to a small set of believable possibilities and the label depends on differences among those possibilities.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Build a toy two-modality classifier where one modality is occasionally hidden. For example, use paired handwritten digit images and short audio clips. Train a baseline that fills the missing channel with one average guess and train PRIMO with sampled conditional completions. For each test example with one missing modality, record the average prediction and the prediction variance across completions.

impact_error = absolute difference between estimated predictive impact and the true change in prediction after revealing the hidden modality

The demo should show two things at once: PRIMO keeps prediction quality near the full-modality model when one channel is missing, and its variance score is highest exactly on the cases where revealing the hidden modality changes the decision.
