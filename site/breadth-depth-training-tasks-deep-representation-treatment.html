connective page - training pressure

Breadth and depth of training tasks

This page explains how training tasks shape representations: broad tasks expose many kinds of variation, while deep tasks force the model to preserve structure that cannot be learned from surface matching alone.

Core idea

The training task decides what information survives

A representation is shaped by pressure: the training task decides what information survives.

Choosing a unit tells the model what it can treat as one thing. Training tells that unit what it must carry. A token, patch, clip, sparse dimension, codebook entry, or latent state does not become meaningful just because we named it. It becomes meaningful when the training task makes useless information fail and useful information succeed.

This is why self-supervised learning is not only "learning without labels." It is the design of pressure. Hide something and the model must recover it. Show two views and it must find what stays the same. Give a teacher and it must imitate a stable target. Force a bottleneck and it must choose what deserves the limited space. Ask for future prediction and it must carry state, cause, and time.

unit choice asks: what is one piece? training task asks: what must that piece learn to carry?

The deep issue is breadth and depth. Breadth asks whether the model sees enough different cases to learn what varies across the world. Depth asks whether the task is hard enough to force structure inside each case. A wide but shallow task can teach broad familiarity without real understanding. A deep but narrow task can teach strong structure that does not transfer. The best training pressure is broad enough to avoid narrow shortcuts and deep enough to make shallow shortcuts fail.

Breadth

Breadth teaches what can vary across the world

Breadth means the training task sees many examples, domains, views, augmentations, modalities, languages, scenes, patients, molecules, documents, or behaviors. It matters because a model trained on a narrow slice can mistake local habits for general truth. If every wolf appears in snow, the model may learn snow. If every medical scan comes from one hospital, the model may learn scanner style. If every code example uses one library, the model may learn that library instead of the programming idea.

Broad training asks the representation to survive many changes. The same object appears under many lights. The same meaning appears in many phrasings. The same disease appears across many patients. The same action appears from many camera views. The same graph role appears in many graph sizes. The model learns that some differences are surface variation, not the core thing.

Many views

Crops, colors, camera angles, modalities, languages, sensors, or time points.

Many domains

Different datasets, users, hospitals, robots, document types, or environments.

Many negatives

Close but wrong examples that force the model to keep real distinctions.

But breadth by itself can be shallow. A model can see millions of images and still learn texture. It can see many captions and still learn common word associations. It can see many patient records and still learn hospital artifacts. Breadth gives opportunity. It does not guarantee depth.

Depth

Depth teaches what structure must survive inside one case

Depth means the training task cannot be solved by a cheap surface clue. It forces the representation to carry internal structure: object layout, syntax, timing, causal state, physical constraint, graph relation, patient trajectory, or mathematical dependency. A shallow task asks for recognition. A deep task asks for the hidden organization that makes recognition reliable.

Predicting a masked word may be shallow if the answer follows from a nearby phrase. It becomes deeper when the model must use long-range grammar, entity identity, or world knowledge. Predicting a masked image patch may be shallow if texture is enough. It becomes deeper when the missing part requires object shape, scene layout, or text recognition. Predicting a future video frame may be shallow if the scene barely changes. It becomes deeper when the model must track motion, intent, contact, and time.

Depth is not the same as difficulty for its own sake. A task can be hard because it is noisy or impossible. Useful depth means the task is hard in the same way the downstream problem is hard. If the downstream problem needs temporal state, the training task should force temporal state. If it needs exact copying, the training task should punish approximate identity. If it needs physical action, the training task should punish motion that cannot be executed.

useful depth: the shortcut fails, the needed structure succeeds, and the learned structure transfers to the real task
Four cases

Training pressure can be broad, deep, both, or neither

Broad and shallowMany examples, but the task can be solved by easy surface clues. The model becomes familiar with the world but may keep shortcuts. Example: many image crops where texture is enough to match views.
Narrow and deepA task forces real structure, but only in one small domain. The model may become strong there and brittle elsewhere. Example: a robot policy trained deeply in one room with one object set.
Narrow and shallowThe weakest case. The model sees little variation and faces little pressure to learn structure. It may memorize formatting, background, frequency, or dataset habits.
Broad and deepThe strongest case. The model sees many variations and each training signal forces structure. Example: video tasks across many animals and scenes that require both appearance and motion, or multimodal tasks that require exact alignment instead of keyword matching.

The research question is often how to move a method from broad-but-shallow toward broad-and-deep without making training too expensive or unstable.

Masking

Hide part, predict it

Masking creates pressure by removing information and asking the model to recover it. The missing piece becomes a test of what the visible context contains. If a word is hidden, the model must use sentence structure. If an image patch is hidden, it must use surrounding visual structure. If a graph node is hidden, it must use relations. If a time-series value is hidden, it must use trend and timing.

Masking has breadth when many kinds of parts are hidden across many examples. It has depth when the hidden part cannot be recovered from local hints alone. A masked patch task is shallow if nearby texture gives the answer. It is deeper if the model must understand object boundary, layout, or written text. A masked language task is shallow if a common phrase gives the word. It is deeper if the model must track an entity across a long document.

masking pressure: visible context must contain enough structure to recover the missing unit
Contrast

Match same thing, separate different things

Contrastive learning creates pressure by comparison. Two views of the same underlying thing should be close. Different things should be apart. This teaches the representation what sameness means. A crop and a color-shifted image may still be the same object. Two different patients, documents, actions, or graph roles may need to stay separate.

Contrast gets breadth from many positives and negatives. It gets depth from hard comparisons. Easy negatives teach little because the model can separate them by surface cues. Hard negatives force precision: two similar drug names, two near-duplicate documents, two visually similar species, two actions with the same pose but different motion, or two graph nodes with similar labels but different roles.

The risk is false sameness or false difference. Two views may be treated as the same even though the change matters. Two examples may be pushed apart even though they share the same concept. The training task must choose comparisons that match the real use case.

Teacher-student targets

A stable teacher tells the student what to keep

A teacher-student task creates pressure by giving the student a target representation. The teacher may be larger, slower, higher resolution, more stable, or trained with more context. The student learns to carry the teacher's useful information in a cheaper or more constrained form.

This is depth when the teacher target contains structure the student would not easily discover alone. SALT uses stable targets so video learning does not chase a moving signal. SigLIP-HD uses high-resolution supervision to teach cheaper visual units what fine detail matters. Discrete communication turns the teacher signal into a compact message, forcing the student to preserve only what helps predict the teacher's view.

The danger is copying the teacher's weaknesses. If the teacher relies on shortcuts, the student may inherit them. Stability helps learning, but it does not make the target true. The teacher has to be judged by downstream behavior.

Bottlenecks

Limited space forces the representation to choose

A bottleneck makes the message smaller than the input. Sparse dimensions, binary codes, codebooks, smaller vocabularies, fewer visual tokens, and short clips all create bottlenecks. This pressure asks: if the representation cannot say everything, what should it say?

Bottlenecks are useful because they can remove noise and make representations cheaper to store, search, or transmit. CSRv2 asks whether only a few active dimensions can carry retrieval meaning. Lossless vocabulary reduction asks whether a smaller vocabulary can preserve next-token behavior. Discrete communication asks whether a short binary message can carry the teacher's useful information.

The risk is that the bottleneck removes the wrong thing. A sparse code can become generic. A small vocabulary can make names fragile. A codebook can collapse to a few entries. A short clip can miss long-range intent. Bottleneck training needs tests that expose what was lost.

Reconstruction

Rebuild enough detail to prove the representation kept it

Reconstruction asks the representation to rebuild an input, a missing part, or a useful target. It is a direct pressure to keep detail. If the model must reconstruct text, it must keep order and identity. If it must reconstruct visual detail, it must keep layout and local structure. If it must reconstruct a graph, it must keep relations.

Reconstruction can be too shallow when the rebuilt detail is low-level and local. A model may learn pixels without objects, wording without meaning, or sensor values without patient state. It becomes deeper when reconstruction requires hidden structure: the object behind the pixels, the claim behind the words, the disease process behind the labs, or the action state behind the frames.

Prediction

Forecasting forces the representation to carry state

Prediction asks the representation to say what comes next. This is deeper than reconstruction when the future depends on hidden state. A video model predicting future motion must know what is moving and why. A time-series model predicting future risk must know the current patient state. A robot model predicting next action must know contact, goal, and physical constraint.

Prediction is powerful because it punishes representations that only describe the present surface. A still frame may show a hand near a cup. Future prediction asks whether the hand is reaching, pushing, lifting, or avoiding. The representation must carry direction, intent, and state, not just appearance.

Collapse prevention

Stop all units from becoming the same

Many self-supervised tasks have a lazy solution: make many inputs share the same representation. If everything is close to everything, matching becomes easy but useless. If a codebook uses only a few entries, most discrete units are dead. If sparse dimensions rarely activate, the representation looks efficient but carries little meaning.

Collapse prevention adds pressure for diversity, use, and separation. Contrastive negatives keep different examples apart. Codebook losses encourage many entries to be used. Sparse methods keep active dimensions meaningful. Topology-aware methods check whether the larger shape survives rather than only local pairs. The point is not diversity for decoration. It is to ensure different real states have different representational homes.

Behavior tests

The final test is whether the learned representation behaves correctly

A representation can look good by its training loss and still fail the real task. So the final question is behavioral: did the pressure teach the right information to survive?

MaskingDoes recovered content require real structure, or can the model guess from local texture and frequency?
ContrastDo positives and negatives match the true task, or do they teach false sameness and false difference?
Teacher targetDoes the student inherit useful structure, or only copy teacher shortcuts?
BottleneckDoes compression preserve retrieval, exact copy, OCR, code, medicine, graph transfer, or smooth control?
ReconstructionDoes rebuilding force meaning, or only local surface detail?
PredictionDoes forecasting require hidden state, or can the model extrapolate shallow patterns?
Collapse preventionAre units actually used to separate real states, or do they only satisfy a regularizer?
Paper map

How the papers fit this pressure view

SALTUses stable teacher targets for video self-supervision. Breadth comes from many unlabeled videos; depth comes from making patch and frame representations learn from a stable target rather than noisy moving labels.
BEASTUses appearance and temporal pressure. Breadth comes from animal video variation; depth comes from forcing the representation to carry behavior over time, not just frame appearance.
Topology-aware contrastive learningAdds structure to contrast. Breadth comes from many pairs; depth comes from preserving paths, loops, and larger shape instead of only local similarity.
Latent distribution matchingPushes representations toward hidden causes. Depth comes from asking the representation to match the underlying distribution, not just reconstruct surface observations.
Discrete communicationUses a limited message between teacher and student. The bottleneck makes the model choose which information is worth transmitting.
CSRv2Uses extreme sparsity. The pressure is that a few active dimensions must carry enough meaning for retrieval and transfer.
Lossless vocabulary reductionTests whether smaller token units preserve behavior. The pressure is behavioral: remove a token only if next-token performance survives.
SigLIP-HDUses high-resolution supervision to teach cheaper visual units. The pressure is to preserve fine detail without always paying high-resolution cost.
Continuous-time and action-field papersUse prediction and smoothness pressure. The representation must carry state through time and support valid behavior between observed points.
Takeaway

The first-principles line

A representation is shaped by pressure: the training task decides what information survives.

Breadth teaches the model what varies across the world. Depth teaches the model what structure must survive inside one case. Masking, contrast, teacher targets, bottlenecks, reconstruction, prediction, collapse prevention, and behavior tests are different ways of applying pressure to the unit.

good training pressure = broad enough to avoid narrow shortcuts deep enough to make shallow shortcuts fail tested by the behavior the representation is supposed to enable
