Bayesian Tensor Decomposition with Diffusion Model Prior

Combine low-rank Bayesian CP decomposition with a pretrained diffusion prior so the model can fill missing or noisy multi-way data using both factor structure and realistic data statistics.

Paper explainersAll themesOther

A tensor, meaning data arranged along three or more axes, can represent height, width, and color channel or user, item, and time. A low-rank CP decomposition represents that data as a sum of a small number of rank-one components, which often works when corruption is mild. Under heavy missingness or strong noise, low rank alone is too weak: many reconstructions fit the observations, and most of them do not look like real images or real signals. This paper builds DiffBCP, a Bayesian method that keeps the low-rank factorization but adds a diffusion prior, meaning a learned preference for reconstructions that resemble real data according to a pretrained diffusion model. The method matters because it injects learned data statistics into posterior inference, meaning estimation of plausible hidden reconstructions after observing corrupted entries, without giving up tractable updates for the factor matrices, meaning the lists of numbers that define each CP component.

Zero assumption version

Low rank is not enough when the data are badly corrupted

Bayesian tensor decomposition is attractive because it gives a probabilistic model of missing data, noise, and latent structure, where latent means hidden but inferred from the observations. The standard assumption is that the clean tensor can be expressed with only a few CP components. That assumption captures global structure, but it misses the local statistics that make an image edge look sharp or a texture look natural.

Handcrafted priors such as sparsity or smoothness add some extra bias, yet they still encode only a narrow class of patterns. The paper asks how to import a learned prior from a strong image model into Bayesian decomposition without destroying the conditional updates that make Bayesian inference practical.

The paper's move in one sentence. Keep the Bayesian CP model for low-rank structure, add a diffusion prior on the reconstructed tensor, and split inference so factor updates and data-prior updates take turns.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Write a Bayesian CP model

Represent the clean tensor as a sum of CP components and place shrinkage on the components so unnecessary rank is driven toward zero.

Attach a diffusion prior to the reconstruction

Use a pretrained diffusion model to express which complete tensors look plausible beyond the low-rank assumption.

Condition on the observed corrupted entries

Use the likelihood, meaning the rule that scores how probable the observations are under a candidate reconstruction, to keep the reconstruction tied to the measured data.

Alternate split Gibbs updates

Update the CP factors with closed-form conditional steps, then update the reconstructed tensor through denoising moves guided by both the diffusion prior and the current low-rank estimate.

Average posterior samples

Use the sampled reconstructions to estimate the final completion and to reflect uncertainty where the data are sparse.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes the prior knowledge used during Bayesian tensor decomposition. It no longer relies only on low rank and simple handcrafted penalties; it adds a learned prior on the full reconstruction.

the object it moves  =  prior over the clean reconstructed tensor
The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity

measured quantity  =  The full clean tensor together with its effective rank

The observations reveal only part of the clean tensor, and the number of CP components that should remain active is also unknown. Those two hidden quantities interact. If the rank is too small, the model cannot fit real structure. If the rank is too large, the model can fit corruption and missing-data artifacts.

DiffBCP addresses both sides at once. The cumulative shrinkage prior pushes weak components down, while the diffusion prior constrains the completed tensor toward shapes and textures that appear in real training data. The sampler then searches over reconstructions that satisfy both pressures.

Why it works

Why the hybrid prior works

Low-rank decomposition and diffusion priors contribute different forms of information. The CP factors capture long-range structure through a small number of separable components. That is useful for completion because it lets the model generalize from sparse entries. The diffusion prior contributes local realism because it has learned the patterns that real images or signals tend to contain. Either source alone leaves a gap: low rank can be overly smooth, and the diffusion prior alone has no compact explanation of the observation pattern.

The split Gibbs sampler makes the combination practical. The factor update block stays simple because the CP model still provides conditional weighted rules for each update step. The diffusion block then adjusts the reconstructed tensor in directions favored by the pretrained denoiser while staying tethered to the current low-rank state. The low-rank-guided denoising step matters because it prevents the diffusion prior from drifting toward a visually plausible sample that ignores the decomposition structure.

Math after the picture

The hybrid posterior

First in words:

The posterior combines three ingredients: how well the reconstruction matches observed entries, how well a low-rank CP model explains the tensor, and how plausible the whole tensor is under the pretrained diffusion prior.

Then the compact form:

p(X, A | Y) proportional to p(Y | X) p(X | A) p(A) p_diff(X)

Every symbol:

Xthe clean tensor to be reconstructed
Athe collection of CP factor matrices
Ythe observed noisy or incomplete tensor
p(Y | X)the likelihood that ties the reconstruction to the observations
p(X | A)the CP decomposition model that links the tensor to the factors
p(A)the shrinkage prior over factor matrices and effective rank
p_diff(X)the diffusion-model prior over realistic complete tensors
A tiny worked example
Suppose two candidate reconstructions fit the visible pixels equally well.
Candidate 1 uses many weak CP components and creates blurry edges.
Candidate 2 uses two strong components and produces edges that the diffusion model considers natural.
The likelihood does not separate them, but the shrinkage prior favors fewer active components and the diffusion prior favors the sharper edge pattern.
The hybrid posterior therefore places more weight on Candidate 2.

The paper implements this through alternating sampling rather than direct evaluation of the full posterior, because the diffusion prior is implicit and enters through denoising-based updates.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

The diffusion prior can be mismatched. If the pretrained diffusion model was learned on data unlike the target domain, it can pull reconstructions toward the wrong textures or shapes.
Severe observation gaps can still dominate. When almost no entries are observed, even a strong prior leaves high uncertainty because many reconstructions remain consistent with the evidence.
Sampling can be expensive. Alternating between CP updates and denoising steps may require many iterations, so the method can be slower than a single forward-pass completion model.
Done right. The method is most compelling when low-rank structure is real but incomplete as a prior, because that is exactly the setting where the diffusion model adds information that the CP factors cannot express alone.
