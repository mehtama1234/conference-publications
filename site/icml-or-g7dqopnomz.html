FLARE-AI: Flaw Reporting for AI

A reporting system that turns one AI flaw submission into structured, triage-ready reports that can be shared across multiple recipients.

Paper explainersAll themesOther

When a researcher finds an AI system failure, the next step should be straightforward: describe the flaw, send it to the right parties, and give each recipient enough detail to triage and act. The current reporting ecosystem makes that hard. Different organizations ask for different fields, many reports are not shareable, and the reporter often has to guess where the issue belongs. FLARE-AI treats reporting as an information-routing problem and designs a common intake format with conditional questioning and optional multi-party dissemination.

Zero assumption version

AI flaw reports are hard to route and hard to act on

A useful flaw report must answer concrete questions: what system was tested, what failed, what evidence supports the claim, how severe is the issue, and who needs to know. Today those questions are asked through many separate forms with different scopes and different field names. A reporter with one real finding may have to re-enter the same content many times.

The recipients suffer too. If each form collects different details, triage teams receive inconsistent reports that are harder to compare and harder to forward. Some reports belong with a model developer, some with an ecosystem coordinator, some with an incident registry, and some with more than one of these at once. Without a shared structure, coordination slows down.

The actual bottleneck. The ecosystem does not only lack willingness to report. It lacks a common way to collect, classify, and distribute triage-ready flaw information.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes the reporting workflow. Instead of one report being trapped inside one local form, FLARE-AI creates an interoperable report object whose contents can travel across organizations. It also changes the user experience of reporting by using conditional logic to ask for necessary detail without showing every possible field to every reporter.

The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity: triage-ready interoperability

measured quantity  =  triage-ready interoperability

A report is not useful merely because it exists. It is useful when another party can act on it without first rewriting it. That requires interoperability: the information must be standardized enough that multiple recipients can parse the same fields, understand the report scope, and connect it to their own triage process.

The paper also emphasizes readiness for triage. A free-text complaint may be descriptive but still hard to process. Triage-ready means the report carries the minimum structured information needed to assess severity, reproduce the flaw, identify affected systems, and determine whether strict-liability or emergency handling rules apply.

Why it works

Why conditional structured reporting helps

Reporting systems fail in two opposite ways. Some are too short, so they collect vague narrative text that triage teams cannot compare or route. Others are too long, so reporters face a heavy form that asks many irrelevant questions and discourages submission. FLARE-AI tries to avoid both errors by classifying early and then branching the intake path.

Early classification matters because not every flaw needs the same evidence. A harmful content case, a privacy leak, and a behavior that triggers strict liability rules can each require different supporting fields. Conditional logic narrows the form to the relevant questions, so the reporter spends effort on the details that change triage decisions rather than on a generic checklist.

The standardized report object is the second key mechanism. Once the information is in one shared schema, dissemination becomes a routing choice rather than a rewriting exercise. That reduces duplicate effort for the reporter and helps recipients coordinate because they start from the same structured record instead of several slightly different narrative summaries.

The equation: reporting quality is completeness plus routing fit

First in words:

A useful report maximizes relevant information collected while minimizing unnecessary burden and maximizing how many appropriate recipients can use the same submission directly.

Then the compact form:

report_utility = triage_completeness - reporter_burden + interoperability_gain

Every symbol:

report_utilitythe practical value of the reporting workflow
triage_completenesshow well the report captures the facts recipients need to assess and reproduce the flaw
reporter_burdentime and effort spent answering irrelevant or duplicate questions

A reporter finds one model jailbreak that affects two developers and one registry.
Under fragmented reporting, the reporter fills three different forms and rewrites the evidence each time.
Under FLARE-AI, the report is collected once with the right category-specific fields, then sent in one structured format to all relevant parties.
Utility rises because triage completeness stays high while duplicate entry falls.

This compact formula captures the design goal even though the real system operates through schemas, branching logic, and recipient workflows rather than through one numeric score.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Wrong early classification. If the system assigns the report to the wrong category at the start, later conditional questions may omit evidence that another category would have required.
Organizations reject the common schema. Interoperability only works when recipients accept or map the shared fields into their own workflows. Without that adoption, the report still has to be rewritten.
Sensitive cases need exceptional handling. Some reports involve strict-liability or emergency concerns, so automatic sharing may be inappropriate unless the workflow includes explicit safeguards and routing constraints.
Done right. The system is strongest when the category logic is accurate, the schema covers triage-critical fields, recipient organizations support structured intake, and special high-risk cases have clear separate handling rules.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Build a web form prototype with three flaw categories and three recipient types. The demo should begin with a short classification page, branch into category-specific follow-up questions, and then generate one machine-readable report that can be previewed for each recipient. Include a baseline version that uses one static long form for all cases.

Measure completion time, number of irrelevant fields shown, and whether simulated triage teams can correctly route and assess the report without asking for missing information. The difference should show whether conditional logic and a shared schema really reduce burden while keeping reports actionable.

routing_success = reports_triaged_correctly_without_followup / total_reports

A convincing demo shows higher routing success and lower reporter effort because one standardized submission now carries enough structured detail for several stakeholders to act on it directly.
