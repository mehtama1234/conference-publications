CaRe-BN: Precise Moving Statistics for Stabilizing SNNs in RL

A new batch normalization method that adapts dynamically during reinforcement learning to keep spiking neural networks stable and efficient on neuromorphic hardware.

Paper explainersAll themesRobotics & Control

Spiking Neural Networks (SNNs) compute using discrete, event-driven pulses like biological brains, making them far more energy-efficient than traditional neural networks. But this energy advantage comes with a cost: the discrete pulses are hard to optimize during training. Batch Normalization, which smooths out the numbers flowing through the network, becomes essential for SNNs to train stably. The problem: in online reinforcement learning, where the network is learning and acting simultaneously on a single stream of experience, the statistics that batch normalization relies on become stale and inaccurate, slowing learning and producing weaker strategies. This paper proposes CaRe-BN, which makes batch normalization adapt confidently to online learning scenarios, preserving SNNs' energy advantage without sacrificing performance.

Why SNNs need adaptive normalization in reinforcement learning

Spiking neural networks compute by sending discrete electrical pulses between neurons, much like real brains. This means they use far less energy than traditional networks, which compute smooth values at every step. For robotics and control tasks running on resource-constrained devices, this energy savings is huge.

The catch: discrete pulses are mathematically awkward during training. The spikes themselves are not differentiable (you cannot compute a nudge-direction with respect to a yes-or-no event), so SNNs are notoriously hard to optimize. Batch normalization—a technique that normalizes (scales and shifts) the values flowing through each layer—turns out to be critical for SNN training stability. It smooths out the wild swings in spike counts that would otherwise derail learning.

In reinforcement learning, the network is learning and acting at the same time, taking actions in the environment and updating its weights from a single stream of experience. Unlike supervised learning, where you process large batches of independent examples, RL operates online: each interaction is fresh, and the batch statistics (the average and spread of values in a batch) become stale and inaccurate very quickly. When batch normalization uses stale statistics, the normalized values no longer match the actual pattern of how often each value appears in the network, causing unstable training and slower convergence to good strategies.

A naive approach: run batch normalization on the current batch alone, even if it is small. But small batches give noisy statistics, and the normalization becomes unreliable.

In one sentence. Batch normalization relies on accurate statistics, but in online RL those statistics become stale, destabilizing SNN training and slowing learning.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper modifies how batch normalization statistics (the mean and standard deviation of values in each layer) are updated during online reinforcement learning. Instead of rigidly trusting either the current batch alone or the running history, CaRe-BN makes the update adaptive: it measures confidence in the current batch and weights the blend accordingly.

the object it moves  =  Batch normalization statistics
The hidden quantity

The confidence signal: measuring batch reliability

measured quantity  =  Batch confidence score

The hidden quantity is a confidence score for each batch—a number that says how much we should trust the fresh batch statistics versus relying on the history. Confidence is high when the batch statistics look consistent and representative; it is low when they are noisy or anomalous.

CaRe-BN estimates confidence by checking alignment: it compares the current batch's mean and variance against the running history and the actual distribution of activations. A batch that aligns well with the history (meaning the environment is not shifting dramatically) gets high confidence; a batch that deviates sharply gets low confidence. This score then gates the update: high confidence means blend more of the fresh batch, low confidence means trust the history more.

Why it works

Why confidence-adaptive recalibration stabilizes SNNs in RL

The core insight: in online learning, the environment and the network's behavior change continuously. Blindly trusting a tiny batch gives noisy normalization; blindly trusting old history gives stale normalization. CaRe-BN solves this by mixing the two, weighted by a confidence signal.

Here is why it works. When the current batch is representative (high confidence), the network uses fresher statistics, staying in sync with the actual pattern of spikes and activations. This keeps normalized values centered and scaled correctly, so learning step directions flow smoothly and training nudges are well-aimed. When the batch is noisy or the environment has shifted (low confidence), the method relies more on the running history, avoiding wild swings that would destabilize how the network updates its weights.

The recalibration step is essential: it applies an additional correction that aligns the normalized values with the actual pattern of values CaRe-BN observes. This prevents drift from building up over time and ensures that the network's internal activations stay in a healthy range for training—not too small (learning signals become too faint) and not too large (learning signals explode).

Because this adaptation happens only during training, inference remains unchanged: the deployed SNN uses frozen, pre-learned statistics, incurring no extra computation or energy cost. The energy advantage persists end-to-end.

Math after the picture

The blend equation

First in words:

The batch mean is updated as a weighted average: the new mean equals the confidence score times the fresh batch mean, plus (one minus the confidence score) times the running mean. The same blend is applied to variance.

Then the compact form:

mu_t = conf * mu_batch + (1 - conf) * mu_running; sigma_t = conf * sigma_batch + (1 - conf) * sigma_running

Every symbol:

mu_tupdated mean (the average) at step t
confconfidence score, between 0 and 1
mu_batchmean computed from the current batch
mu_runningmean saved from all previous steps
sigma_tupdated variance (spread) at step t
sigma_batchvariance computed from the current batch
sigma_runningvariance saved from all previous steps
A tiny worked example
Suppose the running mean is 5.0 and the running variance is 0.2 (fairly stable).
The current batch mean is 4.8 and variance is 0.25 (slightly different, consistent).
CaRe-BN computes confidence: alignment looks good, so conf = 0.7 (favor the fresh batch).
Updated mean: 0.7 × 4.8 + 0.3 × 5.0 = 3.36 + 1.5 = 4.86.
Updated variance: 0.7 × 0.25 + 0.3 × 0.2 = 0.175 + 0.06 = 0.235.
The new statistics are close to the running values but nudged toward the fresh batch, smoothly tracking the environment without over-reacting.

Higher confidence gives the fresh batch more weight, allowing the network to adapt quickly; lower confidence trusts the history, providing stability when the current batch is unreliable.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Confidence overestimated. If the confidence score is computed naively (e.g., only from batch size), it may be high when statistics are actually noisy, causing instability. CaRe-BN guards this by measuring actual alignment to the running history and to the broader distribution.
Recalibration mismatch. If the recalibration correction is too aggressive, it can overcorrect and introduce new drift. Tuning the recalibration strength is necessary; the paper uses an adaptive scheme that respects the data.
Environmental nonstationarity ignored. If the environment changes rapidly (e.g., sudden rule shift), a high historical memory can cause the statistics to lag. CaRe-BN adapts the confidence threshold to detect these shifts, but fast changes may still require manual tuning.
Done right. When confidence is measured from true statistical alignment and recalibration is calibrated to the task, CaRe-BN tracks both stable and shifting environments smoothly, and deployment remains unchanged.

If CaRe-BN is working, the confidence scores should rise when batch statistics align with history (stable environment) and drop during shifts. Because fresh, accurate statistics guide training better, the reward curve should climb faster for CaRe-BN, proving that adapting batch statistics based on confidence beats static statistics in online learning.
