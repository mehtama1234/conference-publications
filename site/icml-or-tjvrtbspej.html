Reading Between the Tokens: Improving Preference Predictions through Mechanistic Forecasting

Instead of predicting preferences only from a model's final output probabilities, the paper reads internal features associated with political parties and aggregates those hidden signals across many synthetic personas.

Paper explainersAll themesLLMs & Foundation Models

Many studies use language models to estimate human preferences by prompting synthetic personas and then reading the model's final answer probabilities. This paper argues that the final answer is not the only place where preference information lives. Inside the model, intermediate features already encode associations with political parties. The authors build probes, which are small classifiers trained to detect specific internal directions, to identify party-related features in multilayer perceptron value vectors, which are stored directions inside the feed-forward blocks. They then measure how strongly different personas turn on those features. Aggregating those internal signals gives a new estimate of population preference patterns. In election forecasting, that representation-based estimate is often closer to survey data than the estimate built from final word probabilities alone.

Zero assumption version

Output probabilities may hide useful preference structure

Suppose you ask a language model to act as many different voters and estimate support for political parties. A direct method is to read the probability it assigns to each party word. That treats the model's final output layer as the whole story.

The paper argues that this misses internal evidence. A model can carry distributed signals about a party throughout its hidden computation and still produce a muted or distorted final probability because instruction tuning, answer formatting, or decoding constraints reshape the last step. If that happens, final probabilities are a noisy readout of a richer internal state.

The core problem. Preference information may exist inside the model even when the final output probabilities do not express it cleanly.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Construct persona prompts

Create synthetic people from combinations of demographic and ideological attributes, with multiple prompt templates for each persona.

Identify party-related internal features

Train probes to detect which internal value vectors are associated with each political party.

Run the personas through the model

For each prompt, record how strongly the party-related internal features are activated.

Weight and aggregate those signals

Combine the activation strengths, meaning how strongly the internal features turn on, into party-level shares across personas because one prompt alone is noisy but the aggregate pattern is informative.

Compare with survey data

Measure whether the hidden-feature forecast or the output-probability forecast is closer to the observed conditional party shares.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes the readout location. Standard preference prediction looks only at the final output layer. Mechanistic forecasting moves the readout inside the model and treats internal party-coded features as the object to measure. The forecast is then built from those features rather than from the last-word probabilities alone.

the object it moves  =  the preference readout
The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity: party-aligned internal activation

measured quantity  =  how strongly a persona prompt turns on internal features linked to a party

Inside a transformer, which is a sequence model built from repeated attention steps, meaning weighted mixing over earlier text positions, and feed-forward blocks, each multilayer perceptron block contains value vectors that contribute content to the residual stream. The paper identifies value vectors that are systematically associated with particular parties. For a given persona prompt, it measures the scaling coefficient placed on each of those value vectors.

This quantity matters because it is a more direct measure of internal association than the final output probability. A persona may activate several weak party-related features spread across layers. Individually those signals are small. Taken together they can reveal a preference pattern even when the final output layer spreads probability mass broadly or suppresses some of those signals.

Why it works

Why it works: internal evidence can survive output distortion

A language model does not form a preference estimate in one step. Information is transformed layer by layer, and the final output layer is only the last readout. That last readout can be distorted by prompt wording, calibration effects, or instruction-following behavior. For example, a model may internally associate a persona with one party, yet spread final probability across several party words because the prompt is framed as an uncertain survey question.

Mechanistic forecasting helps because it measures the internal evidence before that final compression. The probes identify internal features that are genuinely tied to parties by checking whether removing those features changes how likely the model is to produce party words in held-out data. Then the forecast uses activation strength across many prompts, not a single answer string. That reduces sensitivity to one prompt form and makes the estimate depend on stable internal structure rather than on one decoded response.

The paper also finds that these gains are selective. They are strongest for attributes with high entropy, which means the output probabilities are spread across many categories and do not strongly favor one option. In those cases, weak internal cues remain useful because the final output is especially uninformative.

Math after the picture

The equation: weighted party activation

First in words:

For each internal party feature, the paper multiplies the prompt-specific scaling value by how well that feature aligns with the party probe.

Then the compact form:

a_i = m_i times alignment_i

Every symbol:

a_iweighted activation for internal feature i
m_ithe scaling value induced by the current persona prompt
alignment_ihow strongly that internal feature matches the party probe
A tiny worked example
Suppose a persona prompt activates three party-related internal features with scaling values 0.8, 0.3, and 0.5.
Their alignment strengths with Party A are 0.9, 0.4, and 0.7.
Weighted activations are 0.72, 0.12, and 0.35.
Total Party A hidden-feature score for this prompt is 0.72 + 0.12 + 0.35 = 1.19.
Repeat this over many personas and average within each demographic category to estimate Party A support in that category.

The weighting step matters because not every detected internal feature is equally specific to the same party.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

The probes do not isolate real party features. If the probes latch onto incidental wording or country-specific artifacts, the measured activations will not reflect genuine party association. Then the hidden-feature forecast is only a more complicated noise source.
Synthetic personas miss the target population. The aggregation only makes sense if the persona mix resembles the real population. If important groups or attribute combinations are absent, the forecast can be biased even when the internal readout is accurate.
Hidden-feature gains are assumed to be universal. The paper explicitly finds selective gains. When output probabilities are already sharp and informative, the hidden-feature readout may add little or even hurt. Practitioners need a diagnostic such as attribute-level entropy before deciding which forecast to trust.
Done right. When party-coded internal features can be identified reliably and output probabilities are diffuse, the internal readout can recover preference information that the final answer layer only expresses weakly.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Use a small open model and a toy political-choice task with two or three parties. Create persona prompts from a few demographic and issue attributes. Train simple probes to identify internal features associated with each party word, then compare two forecasts for each attribute group: one from final word probabilities and one from aggregated internal activations. Measure absolute error against a held-out synthetic survey target pattern, then repeat separately for low-entropy and high-entropy attribute groups.

latent_gain = output_based_absolute_error - latent_based_absolute_error

The demo should show that the internal readout helps precisely when the final output is spread thin and therefore fails to express the model's internal association cleanly because the final readout distributes probability too broadly.
