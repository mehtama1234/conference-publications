Mitigating Mask Prior Drift and Positional Attention Collapse in Large Diffusion Vision-Language Models

Repair long-form decoding in diffusion vision-language models by suppressing the shared mask-token bias and rescaling positional encoding so visual evidence stays readable across unmasking steps.

Paper explainersAll themesMultimodal

Large diffusion vision-language models generate many text positions in parallel by starting from mask tokens (special placeholder tokens that mean 'content not decided yet') and repeatedly replacing them. This decoding style is fast, but it introduces two structural failures during long responses. First, many undecided positions begin from the same mask-token prior (the shared default hidden-state direction inherited from the mask token), so their representations drift toward a common direction and produce repetitive text. Second, the positional read bias becomes misaligned with iterative unmasking, so later decoding steps under-read the informative visual tokens. This paper identifies both failures and fixes them without retraining the model by adding Mask Prior Suppression and Monotonic RoPE Scaling during decoding.

Zero assumption version

Why parallel masked decoding degrades on long outputs

In a left-to-right decoder, each new token is generated from a growing prefix, so resolved words break symmetry between positions as decoding proceeds. In a diffusion language decoder, many positions remain masked and are refined together over multiple rounds. That means unresolved positions keep inheriting information from the same mask-token state for longer.

This produces repetition because hidden states at different positions drift toward the same prior direction instead of differentiating into distinct content. At the same time, positional attention (the weighting rule that decides which positions to read from, combined with position-dependent bias) was designed for ordered sequence processing, not for iterative unmasking where positions are progressively resolved out of step with their index. The result is weaker grounding to image content during long generation, since informative visual tokens receive less effective attention exactly when text positions need fresh external evidence.

The method in one sentence. During decoding, subtract the shared mask-token bias from unresolved positions and rescale rotary position encoding monotonically so text positions keep reading useful visual evidence instead of collapsing toward repetition.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Inspect unresolved text positions.

At each diffusion decoding round, identify the positions that still carry mask-token influence because those are the positions most at risk of collapsing toward shared content.

Suppress the mask prior.

Remove or downweight the common mask-token direction from their hidden states so unresolved positions can separate according to context instead of copying one shared template.

Rescale rotary position encoding.

Apply Monotonic RoPE Scaling to the position encoding because the raw position bias was designed for ordered decoding, while iterative unmasking needs a schedule that tracks which positions are still unresolved.

Recompute attention with the adjusted states.

Use the modified hidden states and position encoding to update token-token and token-image interactions, which restores access to informative visual features during later rounds.

Repeat until all positions are resolved.

Continue the corrected diffusion steps so long-form outputs can diversify across positions and remain visually grounded.

The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity

measured quantity  =  how much unresolved positions are pulled toward shared mask bias instead of distinct content

The hidden quantity is the balance between shared mask bias and content-specific signal at each unresolved position. When the shared component dominates, many positions carry nearly the same hidden direction, so the decoder has little basis for writing different words at different places.

A second linked hidden quantity is the usable strength of visual evidence across rounds. If the positional scheme increasingly favors text-side self-reinforcement over image reads, the decoder will keep refining text from its own partial output rather than from the picture. The two failures interact: shared mask bias promotes repetition, and weakened image access removes a source of differentiation that could have broken that repetition.

Why it works

Why the two decoding fixes address the two observed failures

Mask Prior Suppression works because the repetition problem starts from a shared initialization bias. If many unresolved positions inherit the same dominant hidden component, the decoder keeps proposing similar updates at different positions. Subtracting or damping that component reduces the coupling between positions, so local context and image evidence can differentiate them.

Monotonic RoPE Scaling works because rotary position encoding determines how similarity between positions changes with distance and order. In iterative unmasking, the semantic maturity of a position no longer matches its raw index: a later position may already be resolved while an earlier one is still masked, or the reverse. A monotonic rescaling makes the positional bias evolve with the decoding schedule, so image-conditioned reads do not fade in a way that is inconsistent with which positions still need information.

The fixes reinforce each other. Suppressing the shared mask bias gives positions room to diverge. Preserving usable visual attention supplies external evidence that drives that divergence toward image-grounded content instead of random variation. Since both interventions act at test time, the method can be applied to existing diffusion vision-language models without retraining.

Math after the picture

Write the corrected hidden state as content minus shared mask bias

First in words:

A simple view is to decompose an unresolved hidden state into its current value minus a scaled shared mask direction before the next attention update.

Then the compact form:

h'_t = h_t - beta_t * m

Every symbol:

h_tthe hidden state at one unresolved text position during diffusion round t
mthe shared mask-token direction estimated from the model's mask representation
beta_tthe suppression strength used at round t
h'_tthe corrected hidden state after mask prior suppression
A tiny worked example
Suppose two unresolved positions have hidden states that differ only slightly because both still contain a large copy of the mask direction.
If beta_t is set so that half of the shared mask component is removed, the remaining hidden states differ more in their context-specific parts.
Those remaining differences then produce different attention updates and different candidate words.
Without the subtraction, the next round would keep pushing both positions toward similar content.

The second intervention changes the positional encoding used inside attention. The main structural idea is the same: reduce shared bias and restore position handling that matches iterative unmasking.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Over-suppressing the mask prior can remove useful uncertainty structure. The mask token is not purely harmful. If the suppression strength is too large, the decoder can lose a stabilizing default state and produce noisy updates.
One rescaling schedule may not fit every architecture. Different diffusion vision-language models can use distinct layer depths, token layouts, and unmasking schedules. A fixed monotonic scaling rule may under-correct one model and over-correct another.
Short outputs may not need the fix. When only a few rounds are needed or the answer is short, the original model may not show strong prior drift. In that case the added decoding logic yields little change.
Done right. The method is strongest on long-form generation where repeated mask inheritance and late-round visual under-reading are visible, and the suppression and scaling strengths are tuned per architecture.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Run a diffusion vision-language model on long image description prompts and log hidden-state similarity across unresolved positions over decoding rounds. Then add mask prior suppression and monotonic RoPE scaling and repeat the run.

The demo should track repetition rate, attention mass on image tokens, and image-grounded factual details in the output. It should also show the normalized dot-product similarity between unresolved positions, because that directly tests whether the shared mask direction is no longer dominating the later rounds.

drift_score = average_pairwise_similarity(unresolved_hidden_states)

A convincing result is lower drift score together with stronger image-token reading and fewer repeated phrases, because that shows the fixes addressed the diagnosed decoding failures rather than merely changing style.
