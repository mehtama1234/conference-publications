TreeDebater: Strategic Planning and Rationalizing on Trees for Better LLM Debates

A debate system that uses tree-shaped plans to decide which arguments to focus on and how to revise claims in real-time competitive debate.

Paper explainersAll themesAgents & Tool Use

Winning a debate means choosing the right arguments to press and responding cleverly to your opponent's counterarguments. Current AI debaters treat each argument in isolation and do not account for how arguments interact in a back-and-forth exchange. This paper introduces TreeDebater, which uses two interlocking tree structures to anticipate the opponent's response, allocate its limited speaking time to the most impactful points, and revise its claims based on audience feedback.

Zero assumption version

The debate problem: limited time, interdependent arguments

In competitive debate, each debater gets a fixed amount of speaking time and must decide which arguments to raise and how deeply to develop them. A naive approach is to present as many arguments as possible, hoping some will land. But real debate is not a list — it is a conversation. An argument raised early invites a rebuttal. That rebuttal then forces you to defend or concede that point. Miss that back-and-forth, and you lose the debate even if your initial arguments looked strong in isolation.

Existing AI debaters either treat arguments as independent claims or use a single fixed debate script. They do not model the flow of the debate — which of my arguments will my opponent attack, how should I defend them, and which points are truly worth my limited time?

The method in one sentence. TreeDebater builds two tree structures: one that rehearses possible attack-and-defense sequences to evaluate each argument's strength, and one that tracks which arguments are currently live in the debate, then allocates speaking time to the highest-impact points and revises claims based on audience reactions.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes: time allocation and claim revision

Instead of treating each argument equally or following a fixed debate script, TreeDebater dynamically reweights speaking time based on which arguments are currently under attack and which will likely be challenged. It also revises the text of claims based on audience feedback, not just their selection.

the object it moves  =  Speaking time allocation and claim wording
The hidden quantity

The hidden quantity: argument strength under attack

measured quantity  =  Defensibility score (how well a claim survives opponent counterargument)

TreeDebater must measure something invisible: how well will a claim hold up if the opponent attacks it? It cannot wait for a real rebuttal. Instead, it simulates the opponent's likely rebuttal by generating plausible counterarguments and evaluating how persuasive they are. This gives each claim a defensibility score — not just how true it sounds, but how well it can withstand scrutiny.

The Rehearsal Tree estimates this by playing out attack-defense pairs and rating which claims sound bulletproof and which crumble quickly. The Debate Flow Tree then updates this score in real-time as the debate progresses and the audience reacts.

Why it works

Why it works: trees separate planning from execution

Debate is a conversation that unfolds in real time, but it is also a game with strategy. The core move is to separate two kinds of trees. The Rehearsal Tree is private planning — it works out all the ways the debate could go, before you actually speak. This lets you ask ahead of time: which of my arguments can I actually win if pressed? The Debate Flow Tree is public tracking — it records which points are live right now and which have been resolved. This tells you where to focus remaining speaking time.

The mechanism works in three stages. First, before the debate, the Rehearsal Tree simulates opponent attacks on each claim and scores how well the defense holds. This separates weak claims (which will crumble) from strong ones (which survive). Second, during the debate, the Debate Flow Tree marks which claims are under active attack and which are moving in your favor. Third, the time controller allocates remaining speaking time to claims that are both under fire and defensible — the high-stakes battlegrounds. This focuses effort where it matters most.

Audience feedback closes the loop. After each stage of debate, a simulated audience rates how persuasive each move was. Claims that convince the audience get more time in the next round. Claims that fall flat get revised. This replaces the static plan with a dynamic one that learns from reaction in real-time.

Math after the picture

The allocation equation: weighted time assignment

First in words:

Speaking time for claim i is proportional to defensibility score times current debate importance times remaining time budget.

Then the compact form:

t_i = (D_i * W_i / sum_j(D_j * W_j)) * T_remaining

Every symbol:

t_iSpeaking time allocated to claim i
D_iDefensibility score for claim i (how well it survives attack), from the Rehearsal Tree
W_iDebate flow weight for claim i (how active or contested is it right now)
T_remainingTotal speaking time budget left
sum_j(D_j * W_j)Sum of all weighted defensibilities (used to normalize)
A tiny worked example
Say we have three claims with defensibility scores: Claim A = 8, Claim B = 3, Claim C = 6.
Opponent is attacking A and B but not C yet, so debate flow weights: W_A = 2, W_B = 2, W_C = 0.5.
Weighted scores: D_A * W_A = 8 * 2 = 16; D_B * W_B = 3 * 2 = 6; D_C * W_C = 6 * 0.5 = 3.
Sum = 16 + 6 + 3 = 25. Remaining time = 10 seconds.
Time allocation: Claim A gets (16/25) * 10 = 6.4 sec; Claim B gets (6/25) * 10 = 2.4 sec; Claim C gets (3/25) * 10 = 1.2 sec.
Strong defensibility + active debate = large time share. Weak or dormant = little time. This focuses effort where it will be most effective.

This equation captures the core trade-off: spend time on claims you can defend and that are currently contested, not on weak points or settled issues.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Weak Rehearsal Tree predictions. If the simulated opponent attacks are not realistic, the defensibility scores will be wrong. The debater will allocate time to claims that look strong in simulation but crumble against a real attack with a different strategy.
Time budget too tight. If the debater runs out of speaking time before addressing key challenges, the Flow Tree will mark important claims as unresolved, damaging persuasiveness even if those claims were defensible.
Audience feedback misaligned with debate rules. If the simulated audience rating does not match actual judge scoring (e.g., the audience rewards flowery language but judges reward logical precision), the real-time revision will optimize for the wrong signal.
Opponent exploits predictable strategy. If the opponent infers from the debater's speaking-time pattern which claims are weak (low defensibility), they can focus their fire on those points.
Done right. Rehearsal Tree built with realistic attack simulations, Flow Tree updated frequently, time allocator responsive to stage outcomes, and audience feedback calibrated to actual judge preferences.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Build a simple three-round debate on a concrete topic (e.g., a policy claim with clear pros and cons). Implement the Rehearsal Tree by generating 2-3 plausible opponent counterarguments for each of your three claims and scoring how well your defense beats the rebuttal. Implement the Debate Flow Tree as a table that marks which claims have been attacked, defended, or remain uncontested. Then run the debate twice: once with equal time per claim (baseline) and once with TreeDebater time allocation. Measure stage-level persuasiveness (did the audience find each stage convincing?) and track which claims were addressed in depth versus left thin.

improvement = (TreeDebater_persuasiveness - Baseline_persuasiveness) / Baseline_persuasiveness

The demo must make visible that TreeDebater spends time on claims that are both defensible and active, whereas baseline wastes time on weak or dormant points.
