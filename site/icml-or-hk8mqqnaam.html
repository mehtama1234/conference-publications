Autobidding Auctions with LLM-Powered Creatives

Treat ad creative enhancement and bidder response as one coupled decision problem so the platform spends on creative improvement only when the later auction response justifies the cost.

Paper explainersAll themesAgents & Tool Use

Large language models can rewrite or improve ad creatives before the auction happens. That can improve user response and click behavior, but it also costs money because each enhancement call consumes compute. This paper studies the platform and the bidders as a coupled system. The platform chooses how much to invest in creative improvement, and autobidding agents respond with new bids under their budgets. The main goal is to choose improvement levels that raise overall value enough to cover their cost.

Why creative improvement breaks the old auction picture

In a standard ad auction, the platform ranks ads and bidders respond with spending rules under budget constraints. If the platform now improves creatives on the fly, ad quality is no longer fixed before bidding because the platform has altered the ad before bidders respond. The platform's own spending decision changes user response and therefore changes the bidders' best actions too.

That creates a moving target. If the platform spends too little, it may miss profitable quality gains. If it spends too much, revenue can fall because the added compute cost outweighs the gain in auction performance. Heuristic rules are unreliable because bidder response changes as quality changes.

The paper's move in one sentence. Model creative improvement and bidder response as a Stackelberg game, then learn the platform's spending rule together with bidder adaptation so that improvement cost and auction response are balanced.

The algorithm as a flow

What it actually does

Model the platform as the leader

The platform chooses how much to spend on creative improvement before bidders react.

Model autobidders as followers

Each bidder adjusts bids online under budget limits after seeing how ad quality changes.

Charge the platform for compute use

Improvement is not free, so the objective subtracts the cost of the creative-enhancement calls.

Learn both sides over time

Use separate learning procedures for the platform and the bidders so that the coupled system moves toward a stable operating point.

Measure market outcomes

Evaluate revenue, welfare, and engagement after both spending and bidding adapt.

What the paper changes

What the paper changes

The paper changes the auction design problem from a fixed-quality bidding problem into a joint spending-and-bidding control problem where the platform's improvement choice becomes part of the mechanism.

the object it moves  =  The platform's pre-auction spending rule for creative enhancement
The hidden quantity

Net marginal value of extra creative improvement

measured quantity  =  Improvement value after cost and bidder response

The hidden quantity is the net value of one more unit of creative improvement. That value is not just the direct gain in ad quality. It must also include how bidders respond, how budgets shift over time, and how much the extra compute call costs.

The paper estimates this quantity through repeated interaction in a leader-follower game. The platform learns whether extra enhancement pays off after observing auction outcomes and bidder adaptation, while bidders learn how to bid as ad quality changes over time.

Why it works

Why the game view is necessary

A static heuristic fails because creative quality and bidder behavior co-determine each other. If the platform raises quality, bidders may bid differently, spend budgets earlier, or shift emphasis across ads. Those changes alter whether the original spending decision was worthwhile.

A leader-follower formulation helps because it makes the dependence explicit. The platform can learn a spending rule by observing the downstream market response, and bidders can learn their response under that evolving rule. The result is a better long-run operating point because the platform is no longer pretending that enhancement cost and bidder response are separate problems.

A simplified platform objective

First in words:

Choose enhancement spend to maximize auction value after subtracting compute cost, while bidders choose bids that maximize their own payoff under budgets.

Then the compact form:

platform_value = revenue + alpha * welfare + beta * engagement - enhancement_cost ; bidder_value_j = click_value_j - spend_j

Every symbol:

platform_valuethe platform's total objective for one auction period
revenuemoney collected by the platform
welfarecombined value created for participants
engagementuser response quality such as clicks or conversions
enhancement_costcompute cost of improving creatives
alphaweight on welfare
betaweight on engagement
bidder_value_jpayoff for bidder j after auction results and spend
click_value_jvalue bidder j gets from user responses
spend_jmoney bidder j pays under its bidding policy
A tiny worked example
No enhancement: revenue = 10, welfare = 6, engagement = 4, enhancement_cost = 0.
With alpha = 0.3 and beta = 0.2, platform_value_no = 10 + 0.3 * 6 + 0.2 * 4 = 12.6.
Moderate enhancement raises outcomes to revenue = 11, welfare = 7, engagement = 5, with enhancement_cost = 1.
platform_value_mid = 11 + 0.3 * 7 + 0.2 * 5 - 1 = 13.1.
Heavy enhancement raises outcomes slightly to revenue = 11.2, welfare = 7.2, engagement = 5.2, but enhancement_cost = 2.5.
platform_value_high = 11.2 + 0.3 * 7.2 + 0.2 * 5.2 - 2.5 = 11.9.
The platform should choose the moderate level because it gives the best net value after cost.

The real algorithms are more detailed, but this simplified objective captures the main decision: spend on creative improvement only when downstream gains exceed its cost.

What can go wrong

What can go wrong

Bidder learning can be slow. If followers adapt slowly, the platform may optimize against outdated behavior because the market has not yet stabilized.
Cost estimates can drift. If enhancement compute cost changes across models or traffic conditions, the learned spending rule may overspend because its cost picture is stale.
Offline data can hide feedback loops. Historical logs may understate how bidders would react to new enhancement policies, so deployment still needs careful online monitoring.
Done right. Track live cost, retrain with current bidder behavior, and evaluate net value rather than raw engagement so that enhancement spending stays economically justified.
What a runnable demo should prove

What a runnable demo should prove

Build a small auction simulator with a platform that can improve ad creatives at a chosen cost before each round so that later bidder responses depend on the platform's spending choice. Let several bidders follow budgeted bidding policies that react to changed ad quality. Compare a no-enhancement baseline, a fixed enhancement rule, and a learned leader policy that adapts spend. Record platform value, bidder value, welfare, and enhancement cost over time.

The demo should prove that the learned leader policy beats fixed heuristics because it spends only when the downstream auction gains justify the compute cost after bidder response is accounted for.
