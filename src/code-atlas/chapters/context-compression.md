# Context Compression

## The Big Idea

Compression chooses what future work is allowed to remember. It is not just
making text shorter. A shorter record is good only when it keeps the facts that
can change the later answer.

A claim file may have eighty pages. Seventy-nine pages may repeat normal
coverage details. One early sentence may exclude international claims. If the
summary drops that early sentence, the summary did not merely save space. It
changed the problem.

## What Must Stay True

The protected thing is the answer supported by the full record. The compressed
record should let the system reach the same decision on facts that matter.
Unimportant wording can be dropped. Repeated details can be shortened. But a
quiet decisive fact must survive.

## The Failure

The hidden failure is summary as rewriting. A compression method keeps recent,
loud, repeated, or easy-to-summarize facts and drops the rare fact that decides
the answer. The shorter memory then sounds clean while supporting the wrong
decision.

## Why This Matters Outside The Demo

This matters in long chats, patient records, legal discovery, claim files,
meeting notes, video review, incident reports, and audit logs. The issue is not
length by itself. The issue is whether the shorter record preserves the facts
future work needs.

In topology and geometry, a compressed shape description must preserve the
features that decide later questions: connected parts, holes, boundary points,
crossings, and inside-outside relationships. A small summary that drops a tiny
hole may be compact, but it is wrong for any later task that depends on that
hole.

## What Client Data Makes It Real

Use long records, marked decisive facts, compressed records, full-record
answers, short-record answers, and disagreement analysis. The proof is that the
compressed record gives the same answer as the full record when the decisive
facts are needed.
