# Same-Evidence Cause Stories

## The Big Idea

Sometimes the same records fit more than one cause story. When that happens,
the honest answer is not to choose the story that sounds best. The honest answer
is to say what the records do and do not force.

Ice cream sales and sunburn can rise together because hot weather drives both.
The shared pattern alone does not prove ice cream causes sunburn. To make a
cause claim, you need more structure: hold background facts fixed, measure the
shared driver, run a deliberate test, or reduce noise.

## What Must Stay True

The protected thing is honesty about what the evidence supports. The claim must
not say more than the records and assumptions allow. If rival stories still fit,
the answer must keep that uncertainty visible.

## The Failure

The hidden failure is story overreach. A method chooses one cause diagram or
one business explanation while another story still explains the same records.
The output looks decisive, but the decision was not earned.

## Why This Matters Outside The Demo

This matters in medicine, product launches, policy, education, marketing,
training programs, economics, and scientific discovery. People often want to
know what caused an outcome, but the available records may only show that two
things moved together.

In topology, a final visible shape can sometimes be produced by different
histories of bending, cutting, gluing, or projection. Seeing the final object is
not always enough to know the path that made it. The same warning applies to
cause claims: a story that fits is not the same as a story forced by evidence.

## What Client Data Makes It Real

Use treatment records, outcome records, background facts, overlap checks,
assumed rules, sensitivity tests, and abstention cases. The proof is that rival
stories have been separated enough for the claim being made, or that the system
correctly refuses to overclaim.
